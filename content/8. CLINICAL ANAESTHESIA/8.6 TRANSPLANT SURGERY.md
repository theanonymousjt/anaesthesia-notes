# 2. BRAIN-DEAD DONORS (DONATION AFTER NEUROLOGIC DETERMINATION OF DEATH)

💡 **Brain death is declared when the clinical picture is consistent with irreversible cessation of all brain functions** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]].

## 2.1. BRAIN DEATH CRITERIA

**Legal and medical brain death criteria differ from state to state, but all require permanent absence of both cerebral and brainstem functions** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]].

### 2.1.1. PREREQUISITES FOR DECLARATION

* Correction of reversible causes of coma:
  * Hypothermia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
  * Hypotension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
  * Hypoglycemia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
  * Drug or toxin intoxications [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
  * Acid-base or electrolyte imbalances [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* Clinical or neurologic imaging evidence of irreversible brain damage [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]

### 2.1.2. CLINICAL EXAMINATION FINDINGS

* Unresponsive to sensory stimuli [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* Absence of brainstem reflexes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* No ventilatory drive with apnea testing [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* May have complex motor activity from spinal cord or peripheral nerves [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]

### 2.1.3. ANCILLARY TESTING

When clinical criteria are incomplete:
* Cerebral angiography [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* Transcranial Doppler [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* Electroencephalography [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Physicians involved in transplant recipient process should not be involved in brain death declaration** [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]].

**Bottom Line:** Brain death requires irreversible cessation of all brain functions with correction of reversible causes and may need ancillary testing when clinical criteria are incomplete.

## 2.2. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY AND HEMODYNAMIC MANAGEMENT

**Brain death is associated with two distinct hemodynamic stages with wide swings in hormone levels, systemic inflammation, and oxidant stress** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]], [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]].

### 2.2.1. STAGE 1: AUTONOMIC STORM

* **Catecholamine surge** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]] leads to:
  * Tachycardia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
  * Hypertension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
  * Acute myocardial dysfunction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
  * Ischemia and ischemic-reperfusion injury [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]

### 2.2.2. STAGE 2: CARDIOVASCULAR COLLAPSE

* **Profound hypotension and relative hypovolemia** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]] due to:
  * Pituitary failure [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
  * Deactivation of sympathetic nervous system [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
  * Brain herniation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]

### 2.2.3. HEMODYNAMIC GOALS

💡 **Recommended fluid resuscitation goals include:**
* Mean arterial pressure >60 mmHg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
* Minimum urine output of 1 mL/kg/h [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
* Maintenance of left ventricular ejection fraction ≥45% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
* Only low doses of vasopressors [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]

### 2.2.4. VASOPRESSOR MANAGEMENT

* **First-line:** Dopamine (traditional recommendation) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]
* **Alternative:** Vasopressin - reduces pressor requirement, protects lung function, increases successful organ procurement rate [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]], [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
* **Reserved for severe shock:** Norepinephrine or phenylephrine [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **High-dose catecholamines can be detrimental to organ procurement** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]].

**Bottom Line:** Brain death creates biphasic hemodynamic instability requiring careful fluid management and judicious vasopressor use to optimize organ procurement.

## 2.3. HORMONE THERAPY AND MEDICATION MANAGEMENT

**Once pituitary failure ensues, hormone therapy may help stabilize donors hemodynamically and extend the donor pool** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]], [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]].

### 2.3.1. HORMONE REPLACEMENT REGIMENS

* **Triiodothyronine:** 4 μg IV bolus then 3 μg/h infusion [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
  * ⚠️ Use not well supported by meta-analyses [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
* **Desmopressin:** 1 unit IV bolus then 0.5-4 units/h infusion [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
  * Target: Maintain SVR at 800-1,200 dyne-sec/cm5 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
* **Vasopressin:** Recommended for donors with hypotension despite adequate volume [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]

### 2.3.2. SPECIFIC INDICATIONS

* **Central diabetes insipidus:** Common due to pituitary compression [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
* **Cardiac donors with LV ejection fraction <45%:** Thyroid hormone replacement recommended [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
* **Corticosteroids:** Methylprednisolone reduces inflammatory response, improves lung procurement rates [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]

### 2.3.3. ADDITIONAL MEDICATIONS

* **Insulin infusion:** Maintain blood glucose 120-180 mg/dL [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
  * 💡 Recent studies support glucose control for donor kidney graft quality [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
* **Broad-spectrum antibiotics** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
* **Mannitol and loop diuretics** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
* **Heparin for procurement** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
* **Thromboprophylaxis:** Important due to high DVT/PE incidence [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Comprehensive hormone replacement and medication management is essential to maintain organ viability and optimize procurement success.

## 2.4. ORGAN-SPECIFIC CONSIDERATIONS

### 2.4.1. LUNG DONORS

💡 **Lung-protective ventilatory strategy important:**
* Tidal volumes 6-8 mL/kg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Ventilation|ⓘ]]
* PEEP adjusted to minimize required FiO2 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Ventilation|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ Donor lungs more susceptible to injury than other organs [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Ventilation|ⓘ]]

**Extended Criteria for Lung Donors:**
* Age <64 years (no major differences from <55 years) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Ventilation|ⓘ]]
* Acceptance of donors with smoking history [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Ventilation|ⓘ]]
* Sputum cultures and targeted antibiotics [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Ventilation|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ Ischemic times should still be <6.5 hours [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Ventilation|ⓘ]]

### 2.4.2. HEART DONORS

**Ideal heart donor characteristics:**
* Age <50 years [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
* Hemodynamically stable [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
* No major chest trauma, cardiac disease, active infection [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
* No prolonged cardiac arrest, malignancy, HIV, or hepatitis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]

**Procurement Considerations:**
* HLA typing and ABO compatibility required [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
* Donor heart size within 20-30% of recipient size [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]
* Systemic heparinization prior to exsanguination [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S1 Critical Care Medicine|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Organ-specific management protocols optimize procurement success while maintaining strict criteria for transplant suitability.

# 3. DONATION AFTER CARDIAC DEATH (DONATION AFTER CIRCULATORY DETERMINATION OF DEATH)


💡 **In 2007, U.S. hospitals were mandated by The Joint Commission to develop DCD policies in response to organ donor shortages** [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6.1 Clinical Governance|ⓘ]].

## 3.1. DCD PROTOCOLS AND REQUIREMENTS

**DCD donors accounted for 30.2% of donors in the United States in 2021** [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6.1 Clinical Governance|ⓘ]], highlighting the need for optimized protocols.

### 3.1.1. KEY PROTOCOL ELEMENTS

* **Decision Independence:** Withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment must be made by patient's surrogate independently [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]
* **OPO Responsibilities:**
  * Obtaining informed consent for organ donation [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]
  * Facilitating preorgan recovery procedures [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]
  * Drug administration or vascular cannulation [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]
* **Team Separation:** Organ recovery team prohibited from:
  * Being present for withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]
  * Guiding the palliative process [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]
  * Declaring death of donor [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]

### 3.1.2. DEATH DECLARATION REQUIREMENTS

**Death defined by cessation of respiration and circulation:**
* Arterial monitoring showing no pulse pressure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* Doppler monitoring showing no flow [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* Minimum 2 minutes absence before organ recovery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ Recovery started >5 minutes after arrest may compromise organ quality [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** DCD protocols require strict separation of end-of-life care from organ procurement teams with mandatory waiting periods.

## 3.2. PREDICTION OF DEATH TIMING

**Suitable DCD donors are those in whom death is anticipated within 1-2 hours of withdrawal** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]].

### 3.2.1. UNOS PREDICTION CRITERIA FOR DEATH WITHIN 60 MINUTES

* Apnea [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* Respiratory rate <8 or >30 breaths/min [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* Dopamine ≥15 μg/kg/min [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* Left or right ventricular assist device [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* VA or VV ECMO [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* PEEP ≥10 and SaO2 ≤92% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* FiO2 ≥0.5 and SaO2 ≤92% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* Norepinephrine or phenylephrine ≥0.2 μg/kg/min [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* Pacemaker-unassisted heart rate <30 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]
* IABP 1:1 or dobutamine/dopamine ≥10 μg/kg/min and CI ≤2.2 L/min/m2 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]]

💡 **Pediatric DCD donors also provide quality organs, with validated pediatric prediction scoring systems available** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4 Trauma Resuscitation and Crisis Management|ⓘ]].

**Bottom Line:** Accurate prediction tools help identify suitable DCD donors and optimize procurement timing.

## 3.3. ORGAN QUALITY AND OUTCOMES

### 3.3.1. DCD VS. DBD OUTCOMES

* **Kidney transplants:** No major differences in intraoperative events [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * ⚠️ DCD kidneys have increased risk of delayed graft function [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Long-term function not reduced [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Liver transplants:** DCD livers generally have worse survival than DBD livers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Heart transplants:** DCD hearts gaining popularity with promising preliminary results [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** DCD organs provide viable alternatives despite some increased complications, particularly important given ongoing shortages.

# 4. LIVING KIDNEY DONORS


💡 **Safety and comfort are primary considerations in care of living donors** [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]].

## 4.1. DONOR SELECTION CRITERIA

**Living donors must be healthy and without significant:**
* Cardiopulmonary disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Neurologic or psychiatric disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Diabetes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Obesity [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Hypertension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Renal dysfunction - normal function required [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* No history of renal stones or proteinuria [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 4.1.1. PROCEDURAL STATISTICS

* **Laparoscopic approach:** Vast majority of living kidney grafts [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Complications:** 21% experience perioperative complications, ~3% classified as severe [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* 💡 **Enhanced recovery protocols and diligent anesthetic practice reduce complications** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]].

**Bottom Line:** Strict donor selection criteria ensure safety while enhanced recovery protocols minimize complications.

## 4.2. ANESTHETIC MANAGEMENT AND MONITORING

### 4.2.1. FLUID MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES

**Renal blood flow decreased by both anesthesia and laparoscopic insufflation** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.1 Cardiovascular System|ⓘ]], [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]:
* **Fluid loading overnight before surgery** (vs. starting with surgery) associated with better creatinine clearance [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Some suggest **colloid bolus just before pneumoperitoneum** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Central venous access rarely warranted** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **CVP does not accurately reflect volume status** with lateral decubitus position and pneumoperitoneum [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 4.2.2. ANESTHETIC TECHNIQUE CONSIDERATIONS

* **General anesthesia standard** for donor nephrectomy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **Nitrous oxide contraindicated** - distended bowel crowds surgical field [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Regional techniques:** Epidural-spinal combinations largely replaced by fascial plane blocks [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Extubation:** Most donors extubated in OR at case end [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 4.2.3. POSITIONING

* **Open nephrectomy:** Lateral decubitus with bed flexed to expose flank [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Careful fluid management and anesthetic technique selection optimize donor safety and renal function preservation.

## 4.3. POSTOPERATIVE CARE AND COMPLICATIONS

### 4.3.1. PAIN MANAGEMENT

* **Severe postoperative pain:** Can limit respiratory effort and mobilization [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Patient-controlled analgesia often used** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Chronic pain statistics:** Survey of 333 donors found:
  * 25% reported pain >1 year after donation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * 17% reported functional limitation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 4.3.2. COMPLICATIONS

**Early Complications:**
* Pulmonary conditions (atelectasis, pneumothorax, pneumonia) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Urinary tract infections [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Wound problems [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Long-term Complications:**
* Reduced renal function [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Hypertension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Albuminuria [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Psychiatric issues (anxiety, depression) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 4.3.3. RECOVERY TIMELINE

* **Hospital stay:** Usually 2-4 days [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Bladder catheter removal:** Postoperative day 1 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Full recovery:** 4-6 weeks, especially after open procedure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **Perioperative mortality rare but possible** - must be discussed preoperatively [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Comprehensive perioperative care and realistic recovery expectations are essential for donor safety and satisfaction.

# 5. LIVING LIVER DONORS


## 5.1. TYPES OF LIVER DONATION

### 5.1.1. LEFT LOBE DONATION (SEGMENTS II AND III)

* **Indication:** Adult-to-pediatric donation with recipients <15 kg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Tolerance:** Left lateral segmentectomy generally well tolerated [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Requirements:** Healthy donors without history or risk of thromboembolic disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

![Figure 52-1 Left lateral segment (segments II and III) living donor transplantation. A: Donor operation. B: Recipient operation complete.](https://5252.ngrok-free.app/figures/fig_52_1.png)

### 5.1.2. RIGHT HEPATECTOMY (ADULT-TO-ADULT)

* **Complexity:** Major procedure with significant risk [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Donor Safety Requirements:**
  * Residual liver volume >35% of original to prevent "small for size" syndrome [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * No liver disease in donors [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Risk increased in older donors or cholestatic/hepatocellular disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

![Figure 52-2 Right lobe (segments V to VIII) living donor transplantation. A: Donor operation. B: Recipient operation completed.](https://5252.ngrok-free.app/figures/fig_52_2.png)

### 5.1.3. MORTALITY AND COMPLICATIONS

* **U.S. mortality:** Estimated as high as 1.7% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Korean experience:** >10,000 donations, 53 mortalities over ~6 years follow-up [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Most common cause: Suicide (35% of mortality) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Followed by cancer and traffic accidents [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Serious complication rates:** Up to one-third of right liver donors [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Air embolism [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Atelectasis, pneumonia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Respiratory depression [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Biliary tract damage [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Right liver donation carries significant risks requiring careful donor selection and comprehensive perioperative management.

## 5.2. INTRAOPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

### 5.2.1. HEMODYNAMIC CONSIDERATIONS

**Large liver resections may require complete hepatic vascular occlusion:**
* Cross-clamping hepatic pedicle without cava clamping [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Venous return falls significantly** - normal donors lack collaterals [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Significant hypotension** when hepatic pedicle cross-clamped [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **BP maintained** through reflex increases in endogenous vasopressin and norepinephrine [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]

### 5.2.2. VOLUME MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES

* **Volume loading reasonable prior to clamping** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Institutional practices vary widely** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]:
  * Some centers: Reduce blood loss by maintaining low CVP [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Other centers: CVP monitoring not routine [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Vasopressor choices:** Vasopressin and norepinephrine reasonable to enhance normal reflexes [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]

### 5.2.3. BLOOD CONSERVATION

* **Experienced centers:** Blood loss usually <1 L, transfusion rare [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Isovolemic hemodilution:** Reported to reduce transfusion requirements [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Blood salvage useful** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Autologous donation programs** available at some centers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 5.2.4. MONITORING AND ACCESS

* **TEE availability:** May obviate need for central venous access in challenging cases [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Central line avoidance:** Many centers avoid if adequate peripheral access obtained [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Extubation:** Most donors can be safely extubated in OR [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **Hypothermia:** Preventable reason for not extubating in OR [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Careful hemodynamic management and blood conservation strategies are essential for living liver donor safety.

## 5.3. ENHANCED RECOVERY AND ANALGESIA

### 5.3.1. REGIONAL ANALGESIA TECHNIQUES

**Important aspect of enhanced recovery protocols:**
* **Epidural analgesia:** Demonstrates superior pain control but concerns about:
  * Postoperative coagulopathy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Epidural hematoma risk [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Alternative techniques:**
  * Intrathecal narcotics [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Subcostal TAP blocks [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Erector spinae plane blocks [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **No quality studies exist comparing techniques** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 5.3.2. MINIMALLY INVASIVE APPROACHES

* **Laparoscopic and robot-assisted techniques becoming more common** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Barrier:** Prolonged warm ischemia time remains concern [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 5.3.3. POSTOPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

**Metabolic Considerations:**
* **Hypophosphatemia common** after hepatectomy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Treat with sodium phosphate infusions [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Maintain phosphate levels 3.5-5.4 mg/dL [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * ⚠️ **Contraindicated if creatinine clearance <50 mL/h** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Laboratory Changes:**
* **INR abnormal** postoperatively, returns to baseline 5-7 days [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Liver function tests:** Small changes can persist up to 3 months [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Chronic low platelet counts:** Some donors experience after hepatectomy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Enhanced recovery protocols with multimodal analgesia improve outcomes, but evidence-based technique selection needs further study.

# 7. CORNEAL TRANSPLANTATION


**Corneas are the most common tissue transplanted in the United States, with around 49,000 transplants performed in 2021** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]].

## 7.1. TYPES AND INDICATIONS

### 7.1.1. SURGICAL APPROACHES

* **Full thickness (penetrating keratoplasty):** Involving all five corneal layers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Partial thickness:** Involving anterior or deep layers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 7.1.2. PATIENT POPULATION

* **Recipients often elderly** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* 💡 **Routine preoperative testing does not decrease adverse events or improve outcomes** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Anticoagulation management:** Perioperative discontinuation should be undertaken in consultation with prescribing provider [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Corneal transplantation encompasses various techniques with elderly populations requiring individualized perioperative assessment.

## 7.2. ANESTHETIC GOALS AND CONSIDERATIONS

### 7.2.1. PRIMARY ANESTHETIC GOALS

* **Analgesia and akinesia** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Avoidance of increased intraocular pressure** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Prevention of oculocardiac reflex** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **PONV prevention** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 7.2.2. ANESTHETIC OPTIONS

**General Anesthesia:**
* **Preferred for:** Cognitive dysfunction, tremor, claustrophobia, inability to lie flat, longer surgeries [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Disadvantage:** Increased PONV risk [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **LMA vs. ETT:** LMA may reduce potential for ocular hypertension from postoperative coughing [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Regional Anesthesia (Retrobulbar, Peribulbar, Sub-Tenon):**
* **Advantages:**
  * Analgesia and akinesia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Ablation of oculocardiac reflex [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Reduced PONV [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Quicker recovery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Disadvantages/Risks:**
  * Sight-threatening hemorrhage [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Infection [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Total spinal anesthesia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Increased IOP from local anesthetic volume [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Topical Anesthesia with IV Sedation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 7.2.3. POSITIONING CONSIDERATIONS

* **Head-up positioning:** May be required to avoid IOP elevations [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Trendelenburg position:** May rarely be requested for partial thickness keratoplasty [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 7.2.4. COMPLICATIONS

**Intraoperative:**
* Extrusion of ocular content [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Expulsive choroidal hemorrhage [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Postoperative:**
* Rejection of corneal allografts - most common cause of graft loss in first year [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Corneal transplantation requires careful anesthetic planning to balance surgical requirements with avoidance of increased intraocular pressure.

# 8. RENAL TRANSPLANTATION


## 8.1. PREOPERATIVE CONSIDERATIONS

### 8.1.1. ALLOCATION SYSTEM

💡 **Kidney Donor Profile Index (KDPI) implemented in 2014:**
* Percentile score 0-100% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Lower scores associated with longer estimated graft function [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Based on Kidney Donor Risk Index (KDRI) combining 10 donor factors [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**10 KDRI Factors:**
* Age, height, weight, ethnicity [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* History of hypertension, diabetes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Cause of death, creatinine [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Hepatitis C status, DCD status [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 8.1.2. PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS AND DIAGNOSES

**2021 Statistics:**
* Total kidney transplants: >24,000 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* 76% from deceased donors, 24% from living donors [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Most common age group: 50-64 years [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Primary Diagnoses (2018):**
* Diabetes: 25.6% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Hypertension: 22.9% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Glomerulonephritis: 21.6% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Other/unknown: 29.9% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 8.1.3. CARDIOVASCULAR ASSESSMENT

💡 **About half the mortality of dialysis patients is due to heart failure** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]].

**Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death after renal transplantation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]:
* **Risk factor modification imperative:** Hypertension and hyperlipidemia management [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Screening:** Patients >50 years generally screened with dobutamine stress tests or myocardial scintigraphy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Anemia common:** With hyperdynamic cardiac indices [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 8.1.4. ADDITIONAL ASSESSMENTS

* **Hypercoagulable states:** Common in renal disease, detailed evaluation required [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Graft thrombosis:** Main cause of graft failure in first year [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Screening required:** Tumors, infections, dental evaluation, viral serologies [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Dialysis timing:** Should be dialyzed before surgery to avoid hyperkalemia, fluid overload, acidosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Comprehensive preoperative assessment focuses on cardiovascular risk stratification and optimization of metabolic status.

## 8.2. INTRAOPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

### 8.2.1. ANESTHETIC TECHNIQUE SELECTION

* **General anesthesia most common** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Regional anesthesia (epidural/spinal) technically possible** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Concerns limiting regional use:**
  * Uremic platelet dysfunction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Residual heparin from preoperative dialysis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Surgery duration:** Should take <3 hours [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 8.2.2. PREMEDICATION AND INDUCTION

* **Antihypertensives:** Common in this population [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Consider maintaining β-blockers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Consider withholding ARBs [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Rapid-sequence induction:** Indicated for diabetic patients with gastroparesis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Preceded by oral sodium bicarbonate [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 8.2.3. NEUROMUSCULAR BLOCKADE

* **Rocuronium useful** for rapid-sequence induction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * ⚠️ **Duration of block may be prolonged in ESRD** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Avoid long-acting nondepolarizing relaxants** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Preferred:** Cisatracurium (cleared by Hoffman elimination) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 8.2.4. SURGICAL APPROACH

* **Incision:** Usually lower right abdomen for iliac fossa placement [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Vascular anastomoses:** Recipient iliac artery and vein used [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Ureteral connection:** To recipient bladder [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Alternative positioning:** Retroperitoneal space if kidney too large [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Anesthetic management emphasizes cardiovascular stability with attention to altered drug pharmacokinetics in ESRD.

### 8.2.5. FLUID AND HEMODYNAMIC MANAGEMENT

💡 **The major anesthetic consideration is maintenance of renal blood flow** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]].

**Hemodynamic Goals:**
* Systolic pressure >90 mmHg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Mean pressure >60 mmHg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Often CVP >10 mmHg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **CVP not reliable marker of fluid status** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Fluid Management:**
* **Balanced salt solutions preferred:** Ringer's lactate, Plasma Lyte [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Associated with less acidosis and hyperkalemia vs. normal saline [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Avoid starch solutions:** Increase risk of renal injury [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Evidence lacking for routine albumin use** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Volume Assessment:**
* **Pulse pressure variation >13%** likely to respond to fluid [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Systolic blood pressure variations** on arterial waveform during mechanical ventilation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Vasoactive Agents:**
* **Dopamine:** Does not reliably improve renal function [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Fenoldopam:** Selective dopamine-1 agonist used in some centers, but evidence for improved outcomes lacking [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 8.2.6. OPIOID CONSIDERATIONS

* **Morphine effect prolonged in ESRD** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **High-dose meperidine:** Can cause toxic metabolite (normeperidine) accumulation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Normal pharmacokinetics:** Fentanyl, alfentanil, sufentanil [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * ⚠️ **Increased vigilance needed for postoperative respiratory depression** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Remifentanil:** Metabolite accumulation may occur but low opioid activity [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 8.2.7. IMMUNOSUPPRESSION PROTOCOL

**Timing of Administration:**
* **Glucocorticoid bolus:** Before reperfusion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Antilymphocyte preparations:** Shortly before or after reperfusion via slow infusion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Induction Agents:**
* **High rejection risk:** Rabbit antithymocyte globulin (rATG) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Lower rejection risk or rATG intolerance:** IL-2 antibody basiliximab [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**High Rejection Risk Factors:**
* HLA mismatch ≥1 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Panel reactive antibody >20% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Donor-specific antibody [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 8.2.8. GLUCOSE MANAGEMENT

* **Target range:** 100-180 mg/dL reasonable during transplantation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Avoid hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Comprehensive intraoperative management prioritizes renal perfusion while managing complex immunosuppression protocols.

### 8.3.2. TRANSFUSION CONSIDERATIONS

* **Transfusion rarely required in OR** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Unique Aspects:**
* **Most common ESRD causes:** Congenital anatomic anomalies [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Postoperative care emphasizes adequate analgesia while avoiding nephrotoxic medications and managing complex immunosuppression.

### 9.1.1. MELD SCORING AND ALLOCATION

💡 **Share 35 was initiated in June 2013** to allocate donor livers to higher acuity patients (MELD >35) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]].

**MELD Calculation:**
```
10 × [(0.957 × ln(serum creatinine)] + [(0.378 × ln(serum bilirubin)] 
+ [1.12 × ln(INR)] + 6.43
```

**MELD-Na (modified January 2016):**
```
MELD − Na − [0.025 × MELD × (140 − Na)] + 140
```
* Sodium values capped at 125-140 mmol/L [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* 💡 **Mathematical modeling suggests saves ~60 lives per year** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Alternative MELD Formula (Singapore National Organ Transplant Unit):**
```
MELD = 11.2 × ln(INR) + 9.57 × ln(Cr) + 3.78 × ln(bilirubin) + 6.43
```
[NUH:14]

### 9.1.2A. CHILD-PUGH SCORING

💡 **The Child-Pugh scoring system (Child-Pugh-Turcotte score)** was originally conceptualised to predict postoperative mortality after portocaval shunt surgery; now used to predict perioperative mortality for abdominal surgeries [NUH:7].

| Measure | 1 point | 2 points | 3 points |
|---------|---------|----------|----------|
| **Serum albumin** (g/L) | >35 | 28-35 | <28 |
| **Total bilirubin** (μmol/L) | <34 | 34-50 | >50 |
| **INR** | <1.7 | 1.7-2.3 | >2.3 |
| **Ascites** | None | Mild (or suppressed with medication) | Moderate-severe (or refractory) |
| **Hepatic encephalopathy** | None | Grade I-II | Grade III-IV |

**Child-Pugh Classification and Perioperative Mortality:**
| Points | Class | Perioperative Mortality (Abdominal Surgery) |
|--------|-------|---------------------------------------------|
| 5-6 | A | 10% |
| 7-9 | B | 30% |
| 10-15 | C | 82% |
[NUH:7, Mansour et al. Surgery 1997]

**West Haven Classification of Hepatic Encephalopathy:**
* **Grade I:** Trivial lack of awareness, euphoria or anxiety, shortened attention span, impaired addition/subtraction, altered sleep rhythm [NUH:7]
* **Grade II:** Lethargy or apathy, disorientation for time, obvious personality change, inappropriate behavior, dyspraxia, asterixis [NUH:7]
* **Grade III:** Somnolence to semi-stupor, responsive to stimuli, confused, gross disorientation, bizarre behavior [NUH:7]
* **Grade IV:** Coma [NUH:7]

* Liver cancer (hepatocellular carcinoma) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

* ⚠️ **BMI <18 and BMI >40 are relative contraindications** to liver transplantation [NUH:14]

* BMI <18 associated with increased haemorrhage and cerebrovascular events [NUH:14]
* All patients with BMI >35 should have dietary counselling prior to transplant [NUH:14]

### 9.1.4. SPECIALIZED ASSESSMENTS

**Cardiac Evaluation:**
💡 **Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death early after liver transplant and the third leading cause of death beyond the first year** [NUH:8].

* **Critical importance:** Cardiovascular disease most common cause of 30-day mortality [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Patients with known CAD** who undergo liver transplant tend to have poorer outcomes [NUH:8]

**Cardiac Conditions to Evaluate and Optimise:**
* **Coronary artery disease:** NASH patients particularly predisposed [NUH:8]
* **Cardiomyopathy:**
  * Chronic alcohol → dilated cardiomyopathy [NUH:8]
  * Haemochromatosis → restrictive cardiomyopathy [NUH:8]
  * Liver cirrhosis → cirrhotic cardiomyopathy (QT prolongation common) [NUH:8,9]
* **Pericardial disease** [NUH:8]
* **Heart failure** [NUH:8]
* **Arrhythmia** [NUH:8]
* **Valvular heart disease** [NUH:8]
* **Pulmonary hypertension** (portopulmonary) [NUH:8]
* **Congenital heart disease** [NUH:8]

**Recommended Cardiac Investigations:**
* **12-lead ECG:** QT interval may be prolonged in cirrhotic cardiomyopathy [NUH:9]
* **Transthoracic echocardiogram:** Features suggestive of pulmonary hypertension (PASP ≥40 mmHg, dilated RV, RV dysfunction) → prompt RHC [NUH:9]
* **Stress echocardiogram/myocardial perfusion imaging** [NUH:9]
* **Coronary CTA or coronary angiography:** Recommended for high CAD risk patients [NUH:9]

**Standard tests:** Dobutamine stress echocardiography or myocardial stress scintigraphy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Cardiac catheterization:** Required for significant coronary lesions amenable to angioplasty [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Functional assessment:** 6-minute walk test <250 meters independent predictor of waiting list death [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**CAD Risk Factors Requiring Further Investigation:**
* Dyslipidemia, history of hypertension, chronic kidney disease [NUH:10]
* Left ventricular hypertrophy, family history of premature CHD [NUH:10]
* Active or past tobacco use, coronary artery calcification score >0 [NUH:10]
* Diabetes or NASH [NUH:10]
* Age ≥40 years [NUH:10]
* Inability to achieve ≥4 METs (cannot climb ≥1 flight of stairs without stopping) [NUH:10]

**Anaesthesia Evaluation Required If:**
* Portopulmonary hypertension [NUH:5]
* Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy [NUH:5]
* Previous anaesthesia complications [NUH:5]

### 9.1.5. PULMONARY ASSESSMENT

💡 **Patients with chronic liver disease can have pulmonary vascular complications (POPH, HPS) which further worsen perioperative risks** [NUH:11].

⚠️ **Cigarette smoking** is implicated in cardiovascular mortality and increased hepatic artery thrombosis - smoking should be prohibited in liver transplant candidates [NUH:11].

**Recommended Pulmonary Investigations:**
* **Chest X-ray:** Evaluate for pleural effusions [NUH:11]
  * 💡 **Hepatic hydrothorax:** Movement of ascitic fluid into pleural space through diaphragmatic defects - usually right-sided; chest tube drainage NOT advised [NUH:11]
* **Lung function test:**
  * May show restrictive pattern due to ascites [NUH:11]
  * ⚠️ **Severely depressed lung function** (obstructive or interstitial disease) may be contraindication to transplant [NUH:11]
* **Contrast-enhanced echocardiogram and lung perfusion scan:** If HPS suspected [NUH:11]
* **Right heart catheterisation:** For suspected POPH [NUH:11]

**Hepatopulmonary Syndrome (HPS):**
* **Triad:** (1) Abnormal arterial oxygenation + (2) Intrapulmonary vascular dilatations (IPVD) + (3) Advanced liver disease/portal hypertension [NUH:12]
* **Abnormal oxygenation defined as:** A-a gradient ≥15 mmHg (or ≥20 mmHg if age >64) breathing room air, sitting position at rest [NUH:12]
* **IPVD detection:** Contrast-enhanced transthoracic echocardiography (CETTE) - agitated saline microbubbles (>10 μm) that normally don't pass pulmonary capillaries (<8-15 μm); **delayed appearance in left heart (≥3 cardiac cycles)** = intrapulmonary shunt [NUH:12]
  * 💡 **Early appearance (1-2 cardiac cycles)** = intracardiac shunt (PFO, ASD) [NUH:12]

**HPS Severity Classification (ERS Task Force, PaO₂ on room air, sitting):**
| Severity | PaO₂ |
|----------|------|
| Mild | ≥80 mmHg |
| Moderate | 60-79 mmHg |
| Severe | 50-59 mmHg |
| Very severe | <50 mmHg |
[NUH:12]

💡 **All HPS patients should have a trial of oxygen therapy** - those with improvement in saturation can be considered for listing [NUH:12].

**Portopulmonary Hypertension (POPH):**
💡 **POPH is pulmonary artery hypertension in the setting of portal hypertension, after exclusion of other causes** [NUH:13].

**ERS Task Force Diagnostic Criteria (Right Heart Catheterisation):**
* Mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP) >25 mmHg [NUH:13]
* Pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) >3 Wood units (240 dynes·sec·cm⁻⁵) [NUH:13]
* Pulmonary artery wedge pressure (PAWP) <15 mmHg [NUH:13]
* **2018 Update:** mPAP cut-off lowered to 20 mmHg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Portal Hypertension Diagnosis:** Clinical signs (gastroesophageal varices, splenomegaly, ascites) or portal pressure measurement [NUH:13].

**POPH Severity Classification (mPAP, assuming increased PVR):**
| Severity | mPAP | Clinical Implication |
|----------|------|---------------------|
| **Mild** | 25 ≤ mPAP < 35 mmHg | Can usually proceed safely with transplant |
| **Moderate** | 35 ≤ mPAP < 45 mmHg | Co-manage with cardiologist; trial of vasodilator therapy to reduce severity before transplant discussion |
| **Severe** | mPAP ≥ 45 mmHg | **Contraindication** to transplant; may be reconsidered if controlled to moderate/mild |
[NUH:13]

* **Absolute contraindication:** mPAP >50 mmHg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **High risk:** PA pressure 35-50 mmHg with PVR >250 dyne-sec/cm5 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

💡 **International Liver Transplant Society recommends PAC monitoring prior to and during liver transplantation for all adult patients with portopulmonary hypertension** [NUH:23].

**POPH Treatment Options:**
* **First-line:** Epoprostenol (complicates surgery due to platelet inhibition) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Alternatives:** Inhaled iloprost, sildenafil, bosentan, ambrisentan, imatinib [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Intraoperative:** iNO useful for PA pressure management [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Comprehensive preoperative assessment addresses multisystem dysfunction with particular attention to cardiovascular and pulmonary complications.

## 9.2. THREE-PHASE INTRAOPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

💡 **Liver transplantation is traditionally described in three phases: dissection, anhepatic phase, and neohepatic phase** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]].

**Summary of Phases - Surgical Details and Anaesthetic Problems:**

| Phase | Surgical Details | Common Anaesthetic Problems |
|-------|-----------------|----------------------------|
| **❶ Dissection** | • Transverse upper abdominal incision with midline extension to xiphoid • Mobilisation from ligamentous attachments • Porta hepatis skeletonised • Mobilisation from IVC • Bile ducts ligated [NUH:15] | • CV instability from ascitic decompression • Haemorrhage from dissection, varices, adhesions • Haemorrhage exacerbated by coagulopathy • Low SVR hypotension (maldistribution to splanchnic circulation) • ⚠️ Over-treatment with fluid/blood may cause splanchnic congestion and exacerbate bleeding [NUH:15] |
| **❷ Anhepatic** | • Begins when arterial supply compromised (hepatic arteries divided) • Continued mobilisation • Portal vein divided • Hepatectomy completed (side-clamping IVC = piggyback OR total caval excision = classical) • Explantation • New liver inserted (start warm ischaemia) • Caval and portal anastomoses [NUH:15] | • Side/full IVC clamping → ↓venous return/preload • Absent citrate and lactate metabolism, ↓gluconeogenesis • Worsening metabolic acidosis • Progressive hypocalcaemia • Hypothermia • No clotting factor/fibrinogen production • **Anaesthetist's role:** mitigate derangements and prepare for reperfusion [NUH:15] |
| **❸ Neohepatic** | • Begins with graft reperfusion (IVC clamp release, then portal vein) • Hepatic artery anastomosis • Biliary reconstruction [NUH:16] | • Abrupt preload increase on IVC clamp release • Release of cold, acidotic, hyperkalaemic fluid and inflammatory mediators → pulmonary vasoconstriction, systemic vasodilatation, arrhythmias, cardiac arrest • **Post-reperfusion syndrome:** MAP decrease >30% within 5 min of reperfusion, lasting >1 min [NUH:16] |

**Signs of Good Graft Function:**
* Production of bile [NUH:16]
* Decreasing serum lactate [NUH:16]
* Normalisation of serum calcium [NUH:16]
* Increasing serum glucose [NUH:16]
* Increasing temperature [NUH:16]

### 9.2.0. DONOR GRAFT CONSIDERATIONS

**Cadaveric Donor Factors:**
* Age of donor [NUH:17]
* Degree of fatty liver [NUH:17]
* Cold ischaemia time [NUH:17]
* Retrieval damage, split liver [NUH:17]
* Donation after brain death (DBD) vs donation after circulatory death (DCD) [NUH:17]

💡 **These factors impact reperfusion haemodynamic changes, bleeding, and initial graft function post-reperfusion** [NUH:17].

**Living Donor Grafts:**
* Usually associated with good graft function and less haemodynamic instability at reperfusion [NUH:17]
* Vascular anastomoses may be challenging (especially in children) - monitor initial graft function [NUH:17]

**Graft Implantation Techniques:**

| Classical Technique | Piggyback Technique |
|---------------------|---------------------|
| Involves clamping supra- and infra-hepatic IVC | IVC is partially clamped |
| Venous return significantly reduced | Venous return partially maintained |
| Increased insult to kidneys | Less insult to kidneys |
| Not usually done for living donor grafts | Standard in most centres |
| IVC should be **test clamped** before division; if hypotensive → remove clamp, give volume/vasopressor; occasionally may require venovenous bypass [NUH:18] | |

⚠️ **Variations in surgical technique are possible - good communication between surgical and anaesthesia teams is essential** [NUH:18].

**Potentially Challenging Surgical Situations:**
* Severe portal hypertension [NUH:19]
* Portal vein thrombosis [NUH:19]
* Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis [NUH:19]
* Previous abdominal surgery (adhesions) [NUH:19]
* Redo-liver transplants [NUH:19]

**Potential Intraoperative Crises:**
* Rapid and unabated haemorrhage [NUH:19]
* Acute myocardial dysfunction/failure [NUH:19]
* Right heart dysfunction/failure [NUH:19]
* Acute renal dysfunction/failure [NUH:19]
* Hyperkalaemia [NUH:19]
* Post-reperfusion syndrome [NUH:19]
* Coagulopathy [NUH:19]
* Intracardiac and/or pulmonary thromboembolism [NUH:19]
* Cardiac arrest [NUH:19]
* Initial poor graft function [NUH:19]

### 9.2.1. ANESTHETIC SETUP AND MONITORING

**Theatre Preparation:**
* Key equipment: rapid transfusion devices, cell saver, warming devices, haemodynamic monitoring, point-of-care coagulation monitoring [NUH:20]
* Equipment on standby: venovenous bypass pump, intraoperative dialysis [NUH:20]
* Key personnel: transplant-trained anaesthetic nurses; dialysis nurses and perfusionists may be on standby [NUH:20]

**General:**
* **Duration:** 6+ hours on average [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Rapid-sequence induction:** Indicated due to gastroparesis and ascites [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]] [NUH:20]
* **Blood products:** Availability must be confirmed before incision [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Warming:** Essential for optimal hemostasis - normothermia maintenance [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Vascular Access and Monitoring:**
* Standard anaesthesia monitoring including temperature and BIS [NUH:20]
* **At least one large-bore peripheral IV** [NUH:20]
* **2× Intra-arterial lines** (preferably bilateral radial arteries): one for sampling, one for monitoring [NUH:20]
* **Central venous catheter** [NUH:20]
* **Swan sheath or rapid infusion catheter (RIC):** For rapid volume infusion; Swan sheath allows insertion of PAC if indicated [NUH:20]
* **KIV venovenous bypass lines / dialysis catheters** [NUH:20]

💡 **Unique pathophysiological CVS state in cirrhosis:** Hyperdynamic circulation with peripheral, pulmonary, and splanchnic shunting → total volume increased but centrally dry → portal hypertension, hepatorenal syndrome, reduced stroke volume response to fluid loading, cirrhotic cardiomyopathy [NUH:21].

**Haemodynamic Monitoring Options:**
| Monitor | Benefits | Limitations |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| **Invasive BP** | Beat-to-beat monitoring | Peripheral arteries may underestimate central MAP during reperfusion or high-dose vasopressors [NUH:21] |
| **PAC** | Accurately determines CO via thermodilution; directly measures PA pressures | Invasive; static pressures imperfect for fluid status/stroke volume [NUH:21] |
| **Arterial pulse wave analysis (CO)** | Less invasive CO calculation | Does not reliably calculate CO in advanced cirrhosis or during OLT [NUH:21] |
| **Arterial pulse wave analysis (SVV)** | Predicts fluid responsiveness in OLT | Requires sinus rhythm; no spontaneous respiratory efforts; most accurate at tidal volumes 8-12 mL/kg [NUH:21] |
| **TEE** | Direct assessment of cardiac filling; monitors myocardial ischaemia/strain; diagnoses PE, shunts, effusions, valvular pathology | Requires advanced training; limited views intraop; risk of oesophageal varix rupture/injury [NUH:21] |

**FloTrac System Algorithm:**
* **During dissection and parenchymal resection:** Avoid hypervolaemia; accept SVV up to 20% if CI >2.2 L/min/m² and MAP within 20% of baseline [NUH:22]
* **Post parenchymal resection:** Judicious fluid loading to restore circulating blood volume [NUH:22]

| SVV >20% | → Fluid challenge (balanced crystalloid 250 mL) |
|----------|------------------------------------------------|
| **SVV <20%** | Assess mean arterial pressure |

| MAP | Cardiac Index | Action |
|-----|---------------|--------|
| Within 20% baseline | Low | Consider inotrope or inodilator |
| Within 20% baseline | Normal/High | Do nothing |
| Lower than 80% baseline | High/Normal | Consider vasoconstrictor |
| Lower than 80% baseline | Low | Consider inotrope |
| Higher than 120% baseline | Low | Consider arteriodilator |
| Higher than 120% baseline | Normal/High | Consider beta-blocker, GTN, or diuretic |
[NUH:22]

**Maintenance Targets:**
* Cardiac index >2.2 L/min/m² [NUH:22]
* Heart rate >60 beats/min [NUH:22]
* Oxygenation PaO₂ >100 mmHg [NUH:22]
* Correct anaemia if haemoglobin <8 g/dL [NUH:22]
* Temperature >36°C [NUH:22]

**Pulmonary Artery Catheter:**
* **Particularly useful in patients with pulmonary hypertension** [NUH:23]
* ILTS recommends PAC monitoring for all adult patients with POPH [NUH:23]

**PAC Contraindications:**
* **Absolute:** Right-sided endocarditis, tumours, masses (may dislodge tissue) [NUH:23]
* **Relative:** Severe coagulopathy, thrombocytopenia; left bundle-branch block (may induce complete heart block); right-sided valve disease (difficult passage) [NUH:23]

**PAC Insertion Distances:**
| Location | Distance to VC/RA junction | Distance to PA |
|----------|---------------------------|----------------|
| Internal jugular | 15-20 cm | 40-55 cm |
| Subclavian vein | 10-15 cm | 35-50 cm |
| Femoral vein | 30 cm | 60 cm |
| Right antecubital fossa | 40 cm | 75 cm |
| Left antecubital fossa | 50 cm | 80 cm |
[NUH:34]

**Normal PAC Parameters:**
| Parameter | Normal Range |
|-----------|-------------|
| CO | 4.0-8.0 L/min |
| CI | 2.5-4.0 L/min/m² |
| SV | 60-100 mL/beat |
| SVI | 33-47 mL/beat/m² |
| SVR | 800-1200 dynes·sec·cm⁻⁵ |
| SvO₂ | 60-80% |
| PAOP | 6-12 mmHg |
| PADP | 8-15 mmHg |
| RVEF | 40-60% |
| PVR | <250 dynes·sec·cm⁵ |
[NUH:23]

**Transoesophageal Echocardiography (TEE):**
* Allows visualisation of contractility and volume status in real time [NUH:25]
* Studies show TEE can be used safely with low incidence of major haemorrhagic complications even with documented gastroesophageal varices [NUH:25]

**TEE Contraindications:**
* **Absolute:** Perforated viscus, oesophageal pathology or previous oesophageal surgery, active or recent upper GI bleeding [NUH:25]

**Grading of Oesophageal Varices:**
* **Grade 1:** Small, straight varices that do not disappear with insufflation [NUH:25]
* **Grade 2:** Medium, tortuous varices occupying <1/3 of oesophageal lumen [NUH:25]
* **Grade 3:** Large, coiled-shaped varices occupying >1/3 of oesophageal lumen [NUH:25]

**TEE Safety Measures with Varices:**
* Lubricate TEE probe generously [NUH:26]
* Use a smaller TEE probe [NUH:26]
* ⚠️ **Avoid manipulating TEE probe during anhepatic phase** (IVC/hepatic vein clamping causes variceal engorgement) [NUH:26]
* ⚠️ **Avoid transgastric and deep transgastric views** in high-risk patients (Grade 3 varices or history of UGI bleeding) - varices near GOJ are most superficial and vulnerable [NUH:26]

**Useful TEE Views in Liver Transplantation:**
| Phase | TEE Views | Possible Findings |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Dissection** | ME AA SAX, ME 4C, TG Mid SAX | Guidewire placement during CVC insertion; hyperdynamic left heart, pericardial/pleural effusion; differentiate systolic dysfunction vs hypovolaemia [NUH:38] |
| **Anhepatic** | ME 4C, TG Mid SAX, ME AA SAX | Ventricular dysfunction (Right>Left); hypovolaemia; rarely pulmonary or cardiac emboli [NUH:38] |
| **Neohepatic** | ME 4C, ME RV Inflow/outflow, TG Mid SAX | RV failure (RV dilatation, leftward IVS shift, new TR); worsening RV dysfunction/PH; intracardiac/pulmonary embolism; new RWMA [NUH:38] |

**Biochemical Monitoring:**
Routine measurements include: ABG, electrolytes (Na, K), ionised calcium, lactate, glucose [NUH:26]

**ABG Timing:**
* Every hour after induction [NUH:26]
* Every 30 minutes during anhepatic phase and immediate post-reperfusion period [NUH:26]
* Hourly until closure [NUH:26]

**Fluid Management and Renoprotection:**
* Judicious use of fluids and blood products [NUH:27]
* Aim for MAP >60 mmHg to maintain perfusion pressure - vasopressors (e.g., noradrenaline) may be used [NUH:27]
* **Renal protection strategies** (none backed by compelling evidence):
  * Mannitol [NUH:27]
  * Frusemide [NUH:27]
  * Continue terlipressin infusion till native liver explanted (if already started preop for HRS) [NUH:27]
  * Aim for ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr urine output where possible [NUH:27]

### 9.2.3. COAGULATION MANAGEMENT

💡 **Coagulation in cirrhosis is a fine balance between anticoagulant and procoagulant states** [NUH:27]:
* Although some clotting factors are reduced, anticoagulant factors (Protein C, antithrombin III) are also reduced [NUH:27]
* Reduction in platelet numbers/function may be compensated by high factor VIII and vWF levels [NUH:27]

⚠️ **This balance may be disrupted by minor insults, illness, and especially major surgery** [NUH:28]:
* Be mindful of apparent vs real coagulopathy [NUH:28]
* Use point-of-care coagulation monitoring (ROTEM/TEG) [NUH:28]
* Be careful of post-reperfusion fibrinolysis [NUH:28]
* ⚠️ **Be careful of hypercoagulability** - risk of hepatic artery thrombosis [NUH:28]

**Standard Laboratory Tests:**
* Not predictive of bleeding but valuable for management [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Viscoelastic testing (TEG/ROTEM) widespread use** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**ROTEM Parameters:**
| Parameter | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **CT (Coagulation Time)** | Time until start of clot formation (detection of 2 mm clot firmness) |
| **CFT (Clot Formation Time)** | Increasing polymerisation of fibrin and platelets (time between 2-20 mm firmness) |
| **α-angle** | Slope of tangent at 20 mm amplitude - measures rate of clot formation |
| **A10** | Amplitude 10 min after CT |
| **MCF (Maximum Clot Firmness)** | Ultimate strength of clot (fibrin and platelet interaction) |
| **CLI (Clot Lysis Index)** | Physiological lysis within 60 min <15% of MCF |
[NUH:29]

**ROTEM Timing in Liver Transplant:**
* Baseline [NUH:30]
* After 1 hour or anytime when bleeding during pre-anhepatic [NUH:30]
* 10 min after cava clamping (early anhepatic) [NUH:30]
* 5-10 min after reperfusion [NUH:30]
* 30-45 min after reperfusion [NUH:30]
* Skin closure [NUH:30]
* (Or 15 min after any specific haemostatic intervention) [NUH:30]

**ROTEM-Guided Transfusion Algorithm for Liver Transplant:**

**1. Manage Hyperfibrinolysis:**
| Trigger | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| At start of Op: A5 EX <35 mm OR CT FIB >600 s OR CT EX + CFT EX >280 s | **TXA 25 mg/kg IV bolus** |
| Pre-anhepatic: LI 60 <85% (associated with increased mortality) | **TXA 25 mg/kg IV bolus** |
| Anhepatic/reperfusion: LI 30 <50% (usually self-limiting) | **TXA 25 mg/kg IV bolus** |
[NUH:30]

**2. Replace Fibrinogen:**
| Trigger | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| A5 EX <25 mm **AND** A5 FIB <8 mm (12 mm) | **Fibrinogen concentrate (FC) or Cryoprecipitate** |
| Target: A5 FIB ≥10 mm (14 mm) [NUH:30] | |

**3. Replace Platelets:**
| Trigger | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| A5 EX <25 mm **AND** A5 FIB ≥8 mm (12 mm) **OR** Platelet dysfunction: TRAPTEM <50 Ω·min **AND/OR** ADPTEM <40 Ω·min | **Platelet concentrate** |
[NUH:30]

**4. Thrombin Generation:**
| Trigger | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| CT EX >75 s **AND** A5 FIB ≥8 mm | **PCC 15-20 IU/kg BW** (or FFP 10-15 mL/kg BW); Consider AT substitution if high thrombotic risk |
[NUH:30]

**5. Heparin Effect or Other Factor Deficiency:**
| Trigger | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| Do INTEM & HEPTEM → CT IN >280 s with CT IN/CT HEP ≥1.25 | **Protamine 25-50 mg** |
| CT IN + CT HEP >280 s | **FFP 10 mL/kg** |
[NUH:30]

**Conditions with Increased Thrombotic Risk:**
* Primary biliary cirrhosis [NUH:30]
* Budd-Chiari syndrome [NUH:30]
* Portal vein thrombosis [NUH:30]
* Malignancies [NUH:30]
* Pre-existing AT deficiency [NUH:30]

**Transfusion Protocols:**
* **Fresh frozen plasma:** Maintain INR <2.0 with anticipated/ongoing bleeding [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **Rapid FFP infusion:** Leads to ionized hypocalcemia due to citrate load [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.1 Cardiovascular System|ⓘ]]
* **Calcium management:** Continuous CaCl2 infusion better than intermittent boluses [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.1 Cardiovascular System|ⓘ]]
* **Platelets:** Traditionally maintain >50,000/mm3 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * ⚠️ **Platelet transfusion associated with worse graft and patient survival** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Fibrinogen:** Maintaining >150 mg/dL with cryoprecipitate critical for hemostasis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Cell saver:** May be used except in hepatocellular carcinoma patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Complex Coagulation States:**
* **"Rebalanced" coagulation:** Reduced procoagulant factors offset by reduced anticoagulant factors [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Simultaneous bleeding and thrombosis possible** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Viscoelastic testing guides:** Complex coagulation disturbances during transplantation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Antifibrinolytics:** Both epsilon-aminocaproic acid and tranexamic acid documented safe in most patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Recombinant factor VII:** Reserved for refractory critical bleeding, expensive, thrombosis risk [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 9.2.4. PHASE 2: ANHEPATIC PHASE

**Technical Considerations:**
* **Traditional approach:** Complete caval cross-clamping above and below liver [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Hemodynamic impact:** Venous return falls 50-60%, often causing hypotension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Venovenous bypass (VVB):** May increase venous return and decompress portal pressures [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Rarely used with cava-sparing techniques [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Potential complications: arm lymphedema, air embolism, vascular injury [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Piggyback technique:** Standard in most U.S. centers, preserves caval flow [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 9.2.5. PHASE 3: NEOHEPATIC PHASE - REPERFUSION

**Preparation for Reperfusion:**  
💡 **Critical preparation includes:**
* **Hyperventilation:** Induce respiratory alkalemia to offset acid load from graft [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Calcium administration:** 1,000 mg CaCl2 precisely at portal reperfusion time [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Emergency medications available:** Lidocaine, atropine, norepinephrine, epinephrine [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Reperfusion Syndrome:**
* **Original descriptions:** Severe hypotension and bradycardia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Current presentation:** Less bradycardia due to flushing techniques and preservation solution changes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Typical response:** Hypotension from further SVR drop [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Reperfusion Complications:**
* **Microemboli and RV dysfunction common** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Intracardiac thromboemboli not rare** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Biventricular dysfunction:** Associated with adverse postoperative cardiac events [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Graft Function Assessment:**
* **Early metabolic signs:**
  * No ongoing calcium requirement after acute reperfusion effects [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Base deficit improvement with citrate and lactate metabolism [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * CO decreases as SVR increases to prereperfusion levels [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Physical signs:**
  * Smooth graft edge without engorgement [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Bile production within first 30 minutes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Renal function:** May improve due to graft metabolism of renal vasoconstrictors [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

## 9.3. SPECIALIZED LIVER TRANSPLANT CONSIDERATIONS

### 9.3.1. HEPATORENAL SYNDROME (HRS) MANAGEMENT

**HRS Definition and Criteria:**
* **Renal dysfunction due to chronic liver disease or ALF** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Diagnosis of exclusion** with specific criteria [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

* **HRS-AKI:** Increase in serum Cr 0.3 mg/dL from baseline in 48 hours, or 1.5× baseline within 7 days [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Management Strategies:**
* **Prevention:** SBP prophylaxis, IV albumin for SBP, antibiotic prophylaxis for GI bleeding [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Large volume paracentesis:** Albumin therapy 6-8 g/L of ascites drained [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Pharmacologic support:** Norepinephrine, midodrine with octreotide [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]
* **Volume replacement:** Most important consideration before instituting diuresis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 9.3.2. PEDIATRIC LIVER TRANSPLANTATION

**Unique Indications:**
* **Biliary atresia:** 44% of pediatric cases [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Inborn errors of metabolism:** 34% of cases [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Cardiac associations:** Biliary atresia associated with cardiac defects and situs inversus [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Technical Considerations:**
* **Small children (<1 year):** Often very small for age with inherited disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Vascular access:** Radial artery catheter and ≥18-g peripheral IV after induction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Central access:** Surgeons may place tunneled central lines before incision [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Previous Kasai operations:** May have massive bleeding due to adhesions [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Respiratory compromise:** With abdominal closure in small children receiving large grafts [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Anticoagulation Management:**
* **Target INR:** 1.8-2.0 range at surgery end to prevent hepatic artery thrombosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Postoperative therapy:** Aspirin and alprostadil often used [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Hepatic artery thrombosis (HAT):** More common in children than adults [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 9.3.3. SPLIT LIVER TRANSPLANTS

* **One liver for two patients:** Extends donor pool but strains transplant teams [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Increased complexity:** Requires additional surgical coordination [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Pediatric liver transplantation requires specialized techniques with particular attention to size mismatch and vascular complications.

## 9.4. ACUTE LIVER FAILURE (ALF) MANAGEMENT

💡 **Anesthetic considerations for ALF focus on brain protection** - opposite approach to chronic liver failure where kidneys are most fragile [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]].

### 9.4.1. CRITICAL CARE MANAGEMENT

* **ICU management essential:** Rapidly progressive ICP elevation leading to herniation and death [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Multidisciplinary protocol required:** Single centers may have limited experience [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 9.4.2. INTRACRANIAL PRESSURE MANAGEMENT

**ICP Monitoring Controversy:**
* **Some centers find useful** for patient management [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **No demonstrated outcome improvement** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Placement risks:** Intracranial bleeding in coagulopathic patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Alternative:** Transcranial Doppler monitoring [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Therapeutic Interventions:**
* **Positioning:** Head midline, head of bed raised [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Mild hyperventilation:** As rescue therapy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Osmotherapy:** Mannitol to serum osmolality 310-315 mOsm/L [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Hypertonic saline:** Target serum Na 145-155 mEq/L [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Hypothermia:** Mild hypothermia (34-35°C) used in some centers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * ⚠️ **Recent analysis suggests no benefit** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 9.4.3. ANESTHETIC MANAGEMENT FOR ALF

**Cerebral Protection Priority:**
* ⚠️ **Avoid all cerebral vasodilating anesthetics, including all inhaled agents** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Propofol commonly used** (with decreased barbiturate availability) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Antihypertensive therapy:** Labetalol does not cause significant cerebral vasodilation [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]
* **Reperfusion management:** Acute cerebral vasodilation often occurs [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 9.4.4. LIVER-ASSIST DEVICES

* **Bridge to transplantation:** Attempted for ALF patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **Generally not shown benefit** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** ALF management prioritizes cerebral protection over renal considerations, requiring specialized anesthetic techniques and intensive monitoring.

# 10. PANCREAS AND ISLET TRANSPLANTATION


## 10.1. TYPES AND INDICATIONS

### 10.1.1. PANCREAS TRANSPLANT TYPES

* **Simultaneous pancreas-kidney (SPK):** ~85% of pancreas transplants from single deceased donor [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Pancreas after kidney (PAK):** Done after kidney transplantation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Pancreas transplant alone (PTA):** For type 1 diabetes with preserved renal function [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

💡 **SPK grafts have historically had better long-term survival than PAK or PTA** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]].

### 10.1.2. DECLINING USE AND ALTERNATIVES

* **Pancreas transplantation becoming less frequent** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Islet transplantation increased** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Reasons:** Pancreas transplants associated with higher surgical and postoperative complication rates [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 10.1.3. IMMUNOSUPPRESSION CHALLENGES

* **Islet transplantation:** Optimal immunosuppression to ward off alloand autoimmunity remains significant problem [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Islet encapsulation:** Create barrier to immune cells - surprisingly difficult problem [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Pancreas transplantation provides excellent glycemic control but declining use due to complications, with islet transplantation offering alternative approach.

## 10.2. ANESTHETIC MANAGEMENT

### 10.2.1. PREOPERATIVE ASSESSMENT

* **Focus on diabetic end-organ complications** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Cardiovascular disease present in many patients** but tend to be younger than liver recipients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Monitoring:** Generally do not require PA catheters [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Cardiac evaluation:** Part of transplant workup [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 10.2.2. CRITICAL GLUCOSE MANAGEMENT

💡 **Strict attention to glucose control indicated to protect newly transplanted β cells from hyperglycemic damage** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]].

**Insulin Protocol:**
* **Initial glucose >250 mg/dL:** Give 10 units insulin IV [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
* **Insulin infusion:** Starting rate varies with initial glucose level [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
* **Target glucose <140 mg/dL:** Then initiate 5% dextrose ~100 mL/h [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
* **Frequent monitoring:** Check response and adjust frequently [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]

### 10.2.3. INSULIN PUMP MANAGEMENT

* **>400,000 pumps implanted in United States** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Protocol recommendations:**
  * Preadmission contact with endocrinologist [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Documentation of pump status preoperatively to postoperatively [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Surgical management:** Continue pump at basal rates with regular glucose monitoring [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 10.2.4. ISLET TRANSPLANT SPECIFICS

* **Edmonton protocol (2000):** Revived islet transplants [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Glucocorticoid-free immunosuppression [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
  * Immediate transplantation after isolation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Current practice:** Islets often cultured after isolation for easier scheduling [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Portal infusion:** May cause acute portal hypertension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Blood loss:** Should not be significant [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Pancreas and islet transplantation requires meticulous glucose management to protect transplanted β cells from hyperglycemic damage.

# 11. SMALL BOWEL AND MULTIVISCERAL TRANSPLANTATION


## 11.1. INDICATIONS AND PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS

### 11.1.1. PRIMARY INDICATIONS

* **Impending liver failure:** In patients with intestinal failure requiring TPN [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Frequent severe dehydration:** In intestinal failure patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Severe complications of central lines:** Sepsis, thrombosis of central veins for TPN [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 11.1.2. COMBINED TRANSPLANTS

* **Liver-intestine transplantation:** For patients who develop liver failure from TPN [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Protective effect:** Presence of liver in graft may protect bowel graft [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Biopsy confirmation:** Required to confirm irreversible liver failure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 11.1.3. PATIENT POPULATION

* **Mostly performed in children** but increasingly in adult recipients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Life-threatening complications:** Usually only performed for severe intestinal failure complications [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Intestinal transplantation reserved for life-threatening complications of intestinal failure, often requiring combined organ transplants.

## 11.2. ANESTHETIC CHALLENGES

### 11.2.1. VASCULAR ACCESS ISSUES

**Major hurdle for adequate transfusion capability:**
* **Review angiographic studies:** Determine venous patency before central line attempts [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Ultrasound guidance:** Helpful for identifying patent vessels [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Alternative access methods:**
  * Surgical cutdowns [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Interventional radiologic techniques [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Transhepatic catheterization [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Intraoperative renal vein catheterization [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **SVC/IVC obstruction:** May require preoperative intervention [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 11.2.2. ANESTHETIC MANAGEMENT

* **Antibiotic regimens:** Continue during surgery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **Avoid nitrous oxide** (as in liver transplantation) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Electrolyte monitoring:** Continuous monitoring with appropriate replacement [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **TPN continuation:** Required perioperatively as enteral feeding not possible for weeks [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 11.2.3. COMPLICATIONS OF INTESTINAL FAILURE

* Dehydration and electrolyte abnormalities [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Gastric acid hypersecretion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Pancreatic insufficiency [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Bone disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* TPN-induced liver failure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Intestinal transplantation presents significant vascular access challenges requiring creative solutions and careful metabolic management.

## 11.3. REPERFUSION AND POSTOPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

### 11.3.1. REPERFUSION SYNDROME

**Similar to liver graft reperfusion:**
* **Acute release of acid and potassium from graft** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Postreperfusion syndrome occurs** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Management:** Anticipatory bicarbonate and CaCl2 administration useful [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.1 Cardiovascular System|ⓘ]]

### 11.3.2. COAGULATION MANAGEMENT

* **Coagulopathy may worsen after reperfusion** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Management approach:** Reassess INR, fibrinogen, platelet counts and correct with blood products [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 11.3.3. PAIN MANAGEMENT

* **Epidural anesthesia may be useful** for postoperative pain management [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Intestinal graft reperfusion creates similar metabolic challenges to liver transplantation requiring proactive management.

# 12. COMPOSITE TISSUE ALLOGRAFTS


## 12.1. UPPER EXTREMITY TRANSPLANTS

### 12.1.1. CURRENT EXPERIENCE

### 12.1.2. ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS

* **Complex procedures:** Multiple surgeons involved in both graft recovery and implantation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Airway management:** Recipients with nose and mouth deformities require individualized care [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Regional techniques:** Well-planned nerve blocks very useful for upper extremity transplants [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Heparinization:** Limb transplant recipients may be heparinized [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Bilateral transplants:** Expose recipient to two reperfusion events [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 12.1.3. UNIQUE FEATURES

* **Multiple organ content:** Blood vessels, nerves, muscle, skin [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Multiple anastomoses:** Imply very long surgical procedures [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Intense immunosuppression:** Required due to highly antigenic skin [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Unfamiliar immunosuppressants:** May be administered intraoperatively [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Donor marrow infusions:** May be used to induce tolerance [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Upper extremity transplants require specialized anesthetic planning for complex, prolonged procedures with intensive immunosuppression.

## 12.2. FACE TRANSPLANTATION

* **Immunologic complexity:** Extremely complex procedures requiring specialized management [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 12.2.4. COMMON COMPLICATIONS

* Jugular vein thrombosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Face transplantation represents the most complex form of composite tissue transplantation requiring extensive perioperative planning and monitoring.

# 14. LUNG TRANSPLANTATION


## 14.1. EPIDEMIOLOGY AND OUTCOMES

### 14.1.1. CURRENT STATISTICS

* **>46,000 lung transplants** in United States since 1988 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **69,200 lung transplants** performed worldwide since 1988 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **2020 survival rates:**
  * 1-year: 87.7% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * 3-year: 71.8% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * 5-year: 58.6% (double-lung transplant recipients) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Median survival:** 6.2 years overall, 7.1 years bilateral vs. 4.5 years single-lung [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

### 14.1.2. CHANGING INDICATIONS

**Historical vs. Current:**
* **Past most common:** Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Current leading indication (since 2007):** Interstitial lung disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * ILD including idiopathic interstitial pneumonia: 32.4% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * Non-IIP ILD: 8.1% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * North America: 47% vs. 38% a decade ago [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Other Current Indications:**
* COPD: 30.1% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Cystic fibrosis: 15.2% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* α-1-antitrypsin deficiency: 4.7% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

### 14.1.3. SURGICAL OPTIONS

* **Double-lung transplants increasing** over past two decades [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Single-lung transplants relatively stable** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Options include:**
  * Single-lung transplant [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * En bloc double-lung [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * Sequential double-lung [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * Cadaveric or living donor lobar [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * Combined heart-lung transplantation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Lung transplantation outcomes continue to improve with interstitial lung disease now the leading indication and double-lung procedures increasingly preferred.

## 14.2. RECIPIENT SELECTION CRITERIA

### 14.2.1. GENERAL INDICATIONS (2021 INTERNATIONAL GUIDELINES)

**Patients should be considered if they meet:**
* Clinically and physiologically advanced lung disease with insufficient medical therapy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* High risk (>50%) of death from lung disease without transplantation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* High likelihood (>80%) of surviving ≥5 years post-transplant with adequate graft function [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

💡 **Ideally, referral should begin prior to urgent need** to allow adequate evaluation and address modifiable risk factors [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]].

### 14.2.2. DISEASE-SPECIFIC CRITERIA

**Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease:**
* BODE index score 5-6 with increased mortality risk factors [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* FEV1 <20-25% predicted [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Pulmonary artery to aorta diameter >1 on CT [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Clinical deterioration despite maximal medical therapy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Interstitial Lung Disease:**
* Any pulmonary fibrosis with FVC <80% predicted or DLCO <40% predicted [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Chronic oxygen requirement at rest or exertion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Rapid disease progression despite medical therapy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Cystic Fibrosis:**
* FEV1 <40% with limited 6-minute walk, hypoxemia/hypercapnia, pulmonary hypertension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* FEV1 <25% predicted [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Chronic respiratory failure, mechanical ventilation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Worsening nutritional status, BMI <18 kg/m2 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Pulmonary Hypertension:**
* ESC/ERS intermediate or high risk, REVEAL risk score >8 despite medical therapy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Severe RV dysfunction despite medical therapy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* IV or SC prostacyclin therapy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

### 14.2.3. CONTRAINDICATIONS AND RISK FACTORS

**Absolute Contraindications:**
* Malignancy with high recurrence/death risk [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* GFR <40 mL/min/1.73 m2 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* ACS or MI within 30 days [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Stroke within 30 days [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Liver cirrhosis, acute liver failure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Active infection, septic shock [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**High Risk Factors:**
* Age >70 years [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Cardiac comorbidity (CAD, LVEF <40%, severe CVD) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* BMI >35 or <16 kg/m2 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Infection with specific species (Burkholderia cenocepacia, Mycobacterium abscessus) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

💡 **Age limits evolved:** Age >65 years now considered general risk factor rather than absolute contraindication [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]].

**Bottom Line:** Recipient selection balances disease severity with transplant benefit potential using comprehensive risk stratification.

## 14.3. INTRAOPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

### 14.3.1. PREOPERATIVE PREPARATION

* **Anxious patients with minimal pulmonary reserve** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Sedation:** Careful incremental dosing (0.25-2.0 mg midazolam) under monitoring [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **Narcotic premedication:** Extreme caution due to ventilatory depression [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Full stomach considerations:** Metoclopramide, H2 antagonists, nonparticulate antacid [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

### 14.3.2. HEMODYNAMIC CONSIDERATIONS

* **Chronic intravascular volume depletion common** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Chronic pulmonary hypertension common** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Preinduction arterial line crucial** for invasive BP monitoring [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Position limitations:** Many unable to lie supine or Trendelenburg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

### 14.3.3. INDUCTION AND AIRWAY MANAGEMENT

* **Induction agent:** Etomidate advised for hemodynamic stability [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
* **Rapid sequence induction often performed** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Lung isolation necessary:** For non-CPB approaches [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Double-Lumen Tube vs. Bronchial Blocker:**
* **DLT advantages:**
  * Less prone to dislodgement [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * Better operative lung deflation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * Better secretion suctioning [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * Allows CPAP to operative lung [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Bronchial blocker disadvantages:**
  * More easily dislodged with surgical manipulation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * Requires repositioning for bilateral sequential procedure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Left-sided DLT preferred** due to right upper lobe anatomy considerations [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

### 14.3.4. MONITORING AND ACCESS

* **TEE probe typically inserted** for cardiac monitoring throughout [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Central venous access achieved after induction** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **PAC often warranted:** For CO and PVR monitoring, prefer continuous CO and MVO2 capability [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Lung transplant anesthetic management requires careful attention to limited pulmonary reserve and complex hemodynamic considerations.

### 14.3.5. VENTILATORY MANAGEMENT

**Two-Lung Ventilation Phase:**
* **Normocarbia maintained** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Lung-protective strategy:** 6-8 mL/kg ideal body weight [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **100% FiO2 in anticipation** of one-lung ventilation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**One-Lung Ventilation Phase:**
* **Tidal volume:** Decrease to 4-5 mL/kg ideal body weight [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Peak airway pressure:** Not to exceed 30 cmH2O [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **FiO2 titration:** Maintain SpO2 >88%, but hypoxia common [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Strategies to Improve Oxygenation:**
* PEEP to dependent lung [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* CPAP to nondependent lung [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Adjust I:E ratio [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Pressure-controlled ventilation modes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* PA clamping of nonventilated lung [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

### 14.3.6. MECHANICAL CIRCULATORY SUPPORT

**Indications for CPB/ECMO:**
* Inadequate oxygenation despite ventilatory and pharmacologic maneuvers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Inability to provide adequate ventilation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* RV failure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**ECMO Options:**
* **VV ECMO:** For normal cardiac function with refractory hypoxemia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **VA ECMO or CPB:** For limited cardiac reserve, severe hemodynamic instability [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

💡 **VA ECMO advantages over CPB:** Lower PGD rates, less bleeding, less dialysis need, shorter intubation and hospital stay [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]].

### 14.3.7. HEMODYNAMIC MANAGEMENT

**Vasopressor Selection:**
* **α-agonists:** Norepinephrine, phenylephrine for hypotension [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]
* **Vasopressin preferred:** Does not increase PVR [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]
* **RV dysfunction management:** Epinephrine, dobutamine, milrinone [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]
* **iNO or epoprostenol:** For suspected RV dysfunction from PA pressure rise [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]

**Fluid Management:**  
💡 **Conservative strategy recommended:**
* **Prefer colloid over crystalloid** for volume resuscitation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Avoid large volume blood product transfusion** - high PGD risk [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Intermittent fluid boluses** while keeping CVP <7 cmH2O [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

### 14.3.8. REPERFUSION AND GRAFT MANAGEMENT

**Post-Anastomosis Care:**
* **Donor lungs kept cold** until reperfusion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Reinflation with recruitment breaths** followed by lung protective ventilation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Bronchoscopic check** of anastomotic site [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Methylprednisolone prior to reperfusion** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]

**TEE Assessment:**
* Pulmonary venous drainage [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* PA blood flow patterns and velocities [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**FiO2 Management:**
* ⚠️ **High FiO2 associated with PGD** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Quick wean to 40-50% FiO2** and titrate to SpO2 >92% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Postoperative Tube Management:**
* **Exchange DLT to single-lumen tube** unless contraindicated [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Large diameter preferred** (>8.0 mm) for ICU management [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Indications to leave DLT:** Significant oropharyngeal edema, high PEEP requirement, need for differential ventilation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Lung transplant anesthetic management emphasizes lung protection strategies and conservative fluid management to minimize primary graft dysfunction.

## 14.4. SINGLE VS. DOUBLE LUNG TRANSPLANTATION

### 14.4.1. SINGLE-LUNG TRANSPLANTATION

* **15-20% of all lung transplants** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Surgical approaches:** Anterior or posterior lateral thoracotomy, midline sternotomy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Preferred approach:** Lateral thoracotomy for better hilar exposure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**PA Clamping Assessment:**
* **Temporary PA clamp:** Assess PA pressure rise before ligation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **If excessive PA pressure rise with RV dysfunction:** MCS needed [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Positioning requirements:** Rapid access to aorta/right atrium or femoral vessels [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Operative Side Selection:**
* Based on preoperative V/Q studies and prior thoracic surgeries [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Poorer functioning lung typically replaced [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

### 14.4.2. DOUBLE-LUNG TRANSPLANTATION

* **80% of adult lung transplants, 97% of pediatric** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Positioning:** Supine with transverse thoracosternotomy ("clamshell") [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Arm positioning:** Suspended on padded bar or tucked at sides [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
  * ⚠️ **Avoid brachial plexus stretching** with suspended arms [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Sequential vs. En Bloc:**
* **Sequential preferred:** No tracheal anastomosis, less bleeding [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **En bloc:** Requires CPB and single-lumen ETT [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **More diseased lung transplanted first** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Elective ECMO/CPB:** If preoperative pulmonary hypertension present [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Postoperative Considerations:**
* **Clamshell incision extensive:** Significant postoperative pain [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Thoracic epidural very useful** for pain management [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Tube exchange over airway exchange catheter** recommended due to airway swelling [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Choice between single and double lung transplantation depends on underlying disease with sequential bilateral approach now preferred for double lung procedures.

## 14.5. PRIMARY GRAFT DYSFUNCTION (PGD)

### 14.5.1. DEFINITION AND GRADING

💡 **PGD defined as allograft dysfunction within 72 hours of transplantation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]].

**PGD Grading Scale (0-3):**
* **Grade 0:** PaO2/FiO2 >300, no infiltrates [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Grade 1:** PaO2/FiO2 >300, infiltrates present [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Grade 2:** PaO2/FiO2 200-300, infiltrates present [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Grade 3:** PaO2/FiO2 <200, infiltrates consistent with pulmonary edema [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Grade 3 PGD associated with significantly high 30-day mortality** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]].

### 14.5.2. RISK FACTORS

**Recipient Risk Factors:**
* Female sex [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* African American race [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Obesity [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Sarcoidosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Donor Risk Factors:**
* History of smoking [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Heavy alcohol use [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Lung contusions or aspiration [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Older age [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Surgical Risk Factors:**
* Long ischemic time [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* Massive transfusion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **CPB role as contributing factor unclear** with conflicting results [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

### 14.5.3. MANAGEMENT

* **Protective ventilatory strategy recommended** similar to acute lung injury management [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **No correlation between OR fluid management and outcome** shown in literature [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Severe, life-threatening PGD:** Successfully managed with ECMO [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** PGD is a major cause of morbidity and mortality requiring lung protective strategies and may necessitate ECMO support in severe cases.

## 14.6. PEDIATRIC LUNG TRANSPLANTATION

### 14.6.1. DEMOGRAPHICS AND OUTCOMES

* **2,323 pediatric lung transplants** between 1992-2018 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Increased over past 15 years** with adolescents receiving majority [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Survival similar to adult populations** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Age-related differences:** Infants historically better than adolescents, but adolescent survival improved significantly [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

### 14.6.2. COMMON DIAGNOSES

* **Cystic fibrosis:** Most common but declining in Europe between 2010-2018 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Congenital heart disease** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **COPD** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis/hypertension** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

### 14.6.3. TECHNICAL CONSIDERATIONS

* **Most receive double-lung transplantation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **CPB via clamshell incision** with single-lumen ETT [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Central and arterial access necessary** for perioperative monitoring [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Pediatric lung transplantation shows improving outcomes with most patients receiving bilateral procedures under cardiopulmonary bypass.

## 14.7. INHALED NITRIC OXIDE IN LUNG TRANSPLANTATION

### 14.7.1. MECHANISM AND EFFECTS

* **Extremely short duration of action** - rapidly inactivated by heme reaction [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Preferential delivery to ventilated areas** leads to improved V/Q matching [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Rapid pulmonary inactivation** prevents systemic distribution and hypotension [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]

### 14.7.2. POTENTIAL BENEFITS

* **Decrease PVR and improve oxygenation** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Decrease inflammatory response** to surgery or trauma [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Impede microbial growth** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Immunomodulatory and antimicrobial activities** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Antiplatelet effects** through guanylate cyclase activation [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]

### 14.7.3. CLINICAL USE DEBATE

**Proponents argue:**
* Use in recipient and possibly donor takes advantage of NO benefits [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* Early initiation post-transplant in pulmonary hypertension recipients prevents acute rejection [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]

**Opponents argue:**
* Data show no benefit in preventing reperfusion injury or PGD [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* Should limit use to specific indications [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]

💡 **Authors recommend:** iNO use should be based on specific clinical circumstances [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]].

**Bottom Line:** iNO offers theoretical benefits for lung transplant patients but routine use remains controversial with recommendation for individualized application.

# 15. HEART-LUNG TRANSPLANTATION


## 15.1. CURRENT STATUS AND INDICATIONS

### 15.1.1. HISTORICAL CONTEXT AND CURRENT PRACTICE

* **First successful combined heart-lung transplant:** 1981 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Least common intrathoracic transplant procedure** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **ISHLT reports:** 1,532 adult heart-lung transplants (1992-2018) and 733 pediatric (same period) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 15.1.2. INDICATION EVOLUTION

**Bilateral sequential lung transplant has largely replaced heart-lung transplantation:**
* Improved pharmacologic management of pulmonary hypertension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Better management of RV failure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Current Primary Indications:**
* Congenital heart disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Idiopathic pulmonary hypertension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Heart-lung transplantation is now rarely performed due to advances in medical management and alternative surgical approaches.

## 15.2. ANESTHETIC MANAGEMENT

### 15.2.1. TECHNICAL CONSIDERATIONS

* **Anesthetic management similar** to isolated heart or lung transplant patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Single-lumen ETT sufficient** due to tracheal anastomosis requirement [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **ETT management during CPB:** Removed or withdrawn above suture line for tracheal anastomosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 15.2.2. POSTOPERATIVE CONSIDERATIONS

* **Inotropes may be needed** for RV dysfunction immediately after bypass [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]
* **Pulmonary reperfusion injury possible** requiring acute lung injury management [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Heart-lung transplantation anesthetic management combines principles of both cardiac and pulmonary transplantation.

# 16. HEART TRANSPLANTATION


## 16.1. OVERVIEW AND CURRENT STATISTICS

### 16.1.1. HISTORICAL AND CURRENT CONTEXT

* **First successful heart transplant:** Christian Barnard, South Africa, 1967 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **>84,297 heart transplants** in United States since 1988 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Current waiting list:** >3,400 patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.1.2. SURVIVAL IMPROVEMENTS

* **1-year survival:** Improved from 74% (early 1980s) to 90% currently [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **5-year survival:** 79% for primary transplantation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.1.3. WAIT TIMES

* **Status 1A patients:** Median 87 days [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Status 1B and 2 patients:** 250-726 days [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.1.4. LEADING INDICATIONS

💡 **Nonischemic cardiomyopathy has replaced ischemic cardiomyopathy** as most common indication [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]:
* **Increasing presentations:** Congenital heart disease, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, restrictive cardiomyopathy from amyloidosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Heart transplantation outcomes continue to improve with changing demographics of recipient populations.

## 16.2. MECHANICAL CIRCULATORY SUPPORT SYSTEMS

💡 **Mechanical circulatory support devices are increasingly common in patients presenting for heart transplantation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 16.2.1. SHORT-TERM MCS DEVICES

**Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump (IABP):**
* **Location:** Descending thoracic aorta [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Mechanism:** Augments coronary perfusion during diastole, provides afterload reduction during systole [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Percutaneous MCS:**
* **Impella:** Catheter-based coaxial pump across aortic valve, pumps blood from LV to aorta [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **TandemHeart:** Left atrial to iliac artery bypass via femoral access with transseptal puncture [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

![Figure 52-3 TandemHeart percutaneous ventricular assist device. Cannulas are placed percutaneously into the femoral vein and femoral artery, and the drive mechanism and power supply are external. The femoral venous line is placed across the atrial septum so as to drain the left atrium. Minimally invasive placement is a significant advantage of this system.](https://5252.ngrok-free.app/figures/fig_52_3.png)

**ECMO:**
* **Cannulation:** Percutaneous femoral vein/artery, internal jugular vein, or central via sternotomy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Advantage:** Provides oxygenation and ventilation in addition to circulatory support [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.2.2. LONG-TERM VAD SYSTEMS

**Three Generations of LVADs:**

**First-Generation (Pulsatile):**
* **Examples:** HeartMate I, Thoratec PVAD, Novacor [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Status:** Rarely used due to limited durability and complications [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Second-Generation (Continuous Axial Flow):**
* **HeartMate II:** Flow pump outside LV, generates up to 10 L/min [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Jarvik 2000:** Pump at LV apex, generates up to 7 L/min [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Characteristics:** Silent, increased durability, easier implantation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Third-Generation (Continuous Centrifugal Flow):**
* **HeartMate 3:** Magnetically levitating rotor, 3,000-9,000 rpm, up to 10 L/min [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **HeartWare:** Single moving impeller, up to 4,000 rpm, up to 10 L/min [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Benefits:** Long durability (5-10 years), low thromboembolic risk, compact size [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

![Figure 52-4 A: Axial flow pump VAD (HeartMate II). The axial flow pump sits outside the heart. B: Centrifugal flow pump VAD (HeartMate 3 and HeartWare). The centrifugal pump is attached to the apex of the left ventricle.](https://5252.ngrok-free.app/figures/fig_52_4.png)

### 16.2.3. LVAD ANESTHETIC MANAGEMENT

**Preoperative Assessment:**
* **LVAD parameters:** Make/model, implantation date, pump speed, flow, power, pulsatility index [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Anticoagulation status** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Comorbidities** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Intraoperative Management:**
* **Power source:** Switch from battery to wall power via power base unit [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Blood pressure:** Noninvasive may work if some pulsatility; arterial line with ultrasound guidance if needed [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Hemodynamic goals:** Maintain preload and afterload for proper LVAD function [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Target MAP:** Within 10% of baseline or ≥70 mmHg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Key Parameters:**
* **Pump Speed:** Only adjustable variable (RPM) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Pump Flow:** Calculated from speed and pressure gradient (L/min) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Pump Power:** Dependent on flow (watts) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Pulsatility Index:** Describes native LV contribution to CO [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Critical Considerations:**
* **Avoid "suction events":** Inadequate preload causes LV walls to collapse into inflow cannula [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Expert availability:** Individual familiar with device should assist if clinician lacks experience [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** VADs require specialized understanding of device mechanics and careful hemodynamic management to avoid complications.

## 16.3. HEART TRANSPLANT RECIPIENT SELECTION

### 16.3.1. CURRENT DEMOGRAPHICS

* **>5 million Americans have CHF** with increasing incidence with age [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Only ~3,000 per year listed** for heart transplantation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **1-year mortality rates:** 21% (men), 17% (women) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **5-year mortality rates:** 50% (men), 46% (women) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.3.2. ISHLT SELECTION GUIDELINES (UPDATED 2016)

**Referral Indications:**
* NYHA Class IV heart failure despite optimal medical therapy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Cardiogenic shock on maximal pharmacologic and MCS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Restrictive or hypertrophic cardiomyopathy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Intractable life-threatening arrhythmias [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Severe ischemic heart disease not amenable to intervention [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Congenital heart disease not amenable to palliative surgery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.3.3. UPDATED CRITERIA

* **Age limit:** Remains at 70, but carefully selected patients >70 may be considered [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **BMI limit:** Increased from 30 kg/m2 to 35 kg/m2 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Frailty assessment:** Newly added criterion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **MCS as bridge to candidacy** advocated [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.3.4. FUNCTIONAL ASSESSMENT

**Exercise Testing:**
* **Maximal VO2 <10-12 mL/kg/min:** Poor 1-year prognosis, referral recommended [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Maximal VO2 >14 mL/kg/min:** Better prognosis with medical therapy than transplantation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Heart Failure Survival Prognosis Score (HFSS):** May be better for risk stratification [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.3.5. CONTRAINDICATIONS

**Pulmonary Hypertension:**
* **Relative contraindication:** Severe irreversible pulmonary hypertension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **High-risk criteria:** Transpulmonary gradient >16 mmHg or PVR >5 Woods units [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Optimization attempts:** Pulmonary vasodilators, inotropes, diuretics to decrease PVR <3 Woods units [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Other Contraindications:**
* Significant noncardiac disease (renal, hepatic) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* FEV1 <50% predicted despite CHF management [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Significant atherosclerosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Severe cerebrovascular disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Malignancy with limited life expectancy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Inadequate social support or substance abuse [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Heart transplant candidate selection requires comprehensive assessment balancing multiple risk factors with potential transplant benefit.

## 16.4. INTRAOPERATIVE HEART TRANSPLANT MANAGEMENT

### 16.4.1. PREOPERATIVE CONSIDERATIONS

**Timing Constraints:**
* **Donor heart function worsens** with cold ischemia times >6 hours [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Night hour procedures common** due to organ availability timing [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Coordination essential:** Between donor and recipient teams for optimal organ use [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Preoperative Assessment:**
* **Fasting status** - often inadequate time, RSI may be needed [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Cardiovascular support:** Inotropic infusions, chronic heart failure medications, MCS presence [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Anticoagulation status** and reversal needs [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Immunosuppressive regimen** confirmation [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]

### 16.4.2. ANESTHETIC MANAGEMENT

**Monitoring and Access:**
* **Invasive arterial pressure** prior to induction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Defibrillator pads** placement [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Large-bore IV access** for resuscitation medications and volume [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Inotrope availability:** Dobutamine, epinephrine, milrinone, norepinephrine, dopamine, vasopressin, phenylephrine [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]

**Induction and Maintenance:**
* **Balanced induction technique** emphasizing hemodynamic stability [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Continue chronic inotropic therapy** during induction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **PAC placement:** Favored by many centers, typically placed postinduction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **High-dose narcotic or balanced techniques** both acceptable [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.4.3. SURGICAL PROCEDURE

**Surgical Approach:**
* **Median sternotomy** for orthotopic transplantation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Bicaval technique preferred** over classic biatrial approach [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
  * Preserves tricuspid valve function [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
  * Maintains normal right atrial conduction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
  * Meta-analysis shows favorable early and late outcomes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

![Figure 52-5 Anatomy of bicaval orthotopic heart transplant. A: A recipient left atrial patch including the four pulmonary veins remains in the recipient. Anastomoses are performed at the superior and inferior vena cava, aorta, pulmonary artery, and left atrium. B: The completed transplantation.](https://5252.ngrok-free.app/figures/fig_52_5.png)

**CPB Management:**
* **Heparin dosing** similar to other CPB procedures [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **High aortic cannulation** near aortic arch [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Individual caval cannulation** with tourniquets for bloodless field [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **PAC withdrawal** during resection, readvanced after superior cava cannula removal [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.4.4. POST-CPB MANAGEMENT

**Denervated Heart Considerations:**  
💡 **Donor heart is denervated** - normal physiologic feedback for inotropy and chronotropy is lost:
* **Direct-acting β-receptor agonists:** Isoproterenol or epinephrine frequently used [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]
* **Temporary epicardial pacing** sometimes needed [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Severe vasoplegia:** May require multiple vasopressor agents [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]

**TEE Assessment:**
* **Comprehensive examination** after induction and post-CPB [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Evaluate:** Ventricular and valvular function, intracardiac shunts, anastomotic sites [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**High-Risk Situations:**
* **Recipients with preoperative pulmonary hypertension** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Donor hearts with long ischemic times** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Marginal donor hearts** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Risk:** Donor right heart failure due to unaccustomed high pulmonary resistance [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**RV Failure Management:**
* **Goal:** Improve contractility and decrease PVR [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Pharmacologic:** Standard inotropes and vasodilators [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]
* **Inhaled therapies:** iNO and inhaled prostacyclin (iloprost) beneficial [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **MCS:** ECMO or IABP if weaning from CPB fails [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.4.5. ECG CONSIDERATIONS

* **Two P waves possible:** Native atrial tissue (no physiologic effect) and donor [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Only donor P wave affects donor heart function** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Heart transplantation requires specialized management of the denervated heart with particular attention to right heart function and pulmonary vascular resistance.

## 16.5. PEDIATRIC HEART TRANSPLANTATION

### 16.5.1. DEMOGRAPHICS AND OUTCOMES

* **>15,726 pediatric heart transplants** worldwide since 1992 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **>11,200 pediatric heart transplants** in United States since 1988 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Age distribution:** ~60% performed in children <1 year or >11 years old [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Median age:** Increased from 6 to 7 years over past decade [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.5.2. INDICATIONS AND TRENDS

**Leading Causes (~75% of cases):**
* Congenital heart disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Idiopathic/viral cardiomyopathy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Increasing:** Retransplantation due to graft failure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Current Trends:**
* **Higher rates** of prior cardiac surgery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Increased use** of intravenous inotropes and MCS (including ECMO and VADs) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.5.3. SURVIVAL IMPROVEMENTS

* **1-year survival:** Risen from 87% (2000-2005) to 92% (2012-2017) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.5.4. ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS

* **Preoperative focus:** Cardiopulmonary status and congenital heart disease physiology [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S13 Ophthalmic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Palliative procedures:** May have been performed prior to transplant, increasing surgical risk [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Anesthetic technique:** High-dose narcotics and intermittent benzodiazepines after inhalation induction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Monitoring:** Central venous and arterial catheters placed routinely after induction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.5.5. MARGINAL DONORS AND ABO MISMATCH

**Marginal Donor Use Increasing:**
* Size mismatches [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* High donor inotrope requirement [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Prolonged ischemic time [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* ABO mismatch [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

💡 **ABO-Incompatible Transplantation:**
* **Contraindicated in adults** but more successful in infants [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Success factors:** Immune system immaturity, absence of blood group antibodies [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Management:** Plasma exchange during CPB, isohemagglutinin titer monitoring [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Outcomes:** Similar 4-year morbidity and mortality to ABO-compatible recipients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Pediatric heart transplantation shows improving outcomes with expanding use of marginal donors including ABO-incompatible grafts in infants.

## 16.6. DCD HEART TRANSPLANTATION

### 16.6.1. CURRENT STATUS AND GROWTH

* **2010-2019:** Only 12 DCD cardiac transplants in United States [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **2020-2021:** 349 DCD cardiac transplants performed [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Professional support:** UNOS, ISHLT, and SCCM published statements supporting DCD heart transplants [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6.1 Clinical Governance|ⓘ]]

### 16.6.2. PROCUREMENT METHODS

**Direct Procurement and Perfusion (DPP):**
* **Process:** Heart removal after death declaration, evaluation ex situ in organ perfusion machine [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Technology:** TransMedics Organ Care System [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Normothermic Regional Perfusion (NRP):**
* **Process:** Ligation of aortic arch vessels after death declaration, reestablish circulation via CPB/ECMO [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Evaluation:** In situ organ function assessment [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.6.3. INSTITUTIONAL VARIATIONS

* **Method selection:** Dependent on institutional preference and local regulations [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Donor management:** Under ICU team responsibility until death declaration [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Procurement initiation:** Only after death declaration by ICU team [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 16.6.4. OUTCOMES

* **Promising results** from both DPP and NRP methods [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **International experience:** Successful programs in European countries [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** DCD heart transplantation is rapidly expanding in the United States with promising early results using different procurement methods.

# 18. GENERAL PRINCIPLES FOR TRANSPLANT RECIPIENTS

💡 **As the population of transplant recipients has increased, the incidence of elective or emergent nontransplant surgery in these patients has grown** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]].

## 18.1. ASSESSMENT FOCUS BY ORGAN SYSTEM

### 18.1.1. ORGAN-SPECIFIC CONSIDERATIONS

* **Solid-organ recipients:** Evaluation centered on function of grafted organ [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Renal/liver transplant patients:** Level of dysfunction determines drug choice and dosing [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * May require dose modification or avoidance of renally excreted drugs [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Major consideration for renal recipients:** Maintenance of renal perfusion with adequate arterial pressure and volume replacement [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 18.1.2. ADVANCED MONITORING RECOMMENDATIONS

**Low threshold for advanced monitoring:**
* **Intra-arterial pressure** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Minimally invasive CO with stroke volume variation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **CVP monitoring:** For major surgeries with large fluid shifts or tenuous organ function [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **TEE:** Very useful for cardiac function and volume status monitoring [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **PA pressure monitoring:** Reserved for significant pulmonary hypertension requiring iNO or prostaglandins [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 18.1.3. INFECTION RECOGNITION AND MANAGEMENT

⚠️ **Signs of infection may be masked by immunosuppression** [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]:
* **Any signs of sepsis or SIRS** should be addressed aggressively [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Infectious disease specialists** important consultants for perioperative transplant patients [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]

## 18.2. DRUG AND GRAFT CONSIDERATIONS

### 18.2.1. FAILING GRAFTS

* **Rejected, reinfected, or failing liver grafts:** Often accompanied by renal function deterioration [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Renal function preservation challenging:** Every effort should be made to preserve function [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

💡 **For all transplant recipients, antibiotic, antiviral, antifungal, and immunosuppression regimens should be disrupted as little as possible in the perioperative period** [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]].

### 18.2.2. INFECTION PATTERNS OVER TIME

**Timeline-dependent infection susceptibility:**
* **First posttransplant month:** Donor-derived and hospital-acquired infections predominate [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]
* **Months 2-6:** Distinct infection patterns vs. later posttransplant [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]
* **These patterns should guide:** Antibiotic prophylaxis and perioperative diagnostic procedures [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Transplant recipients require specialized perioperative management with focus on graft function preservation and infection prevention.

# 19. IMMUNOSUPPRESSIVE MEDICATION MANAGEMENT

## 19.1. PHASES OF IMMUNOSUPPRESSION

### 19.1.1. THREE PHASES

* **Induction:** At time of transplant [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Maintenance:** Ongoing, indefinite [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Rejection treatment:** High "pulse" dose immunosuppression [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]

💡 **Most recipients presenting for nontransplant surgery will be in maintenance phase** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]].

### 19.1.2. PERIOPERATIVE INFECTION RISK

**Immunosuppressive medications increase infection risk:**
* Surgical wound infections [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]
* Intravascular catheter infections [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]
* Urinary catheter infections [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]
* Bronchopulmonary system infections [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Strict aseptic technique should be used** for placement of all vascular catheters, especially central catheters [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6.1 Clinical Governance|ⓘ]].

## 19.2. COMMON IMMUNOSUPPRESSIVE AGENTS

### 19.2.1. CORTICOSTEROIDS

* **Mechanism:** Immune suppression by CD4 T cell sequestration, cytokine transcription inhibition [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Example:** Prednisone [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Side effects:** Electrolyte abnormalities, impaired wound healing, GI bleeding, hyperglycemia [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]

### 19.2.2. CALCINEURIN INHIBITORS

* **Mechanism:** Decrease IL-2 production by activated T cells [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Examples:** Tacrolimus, Cyclosporine [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Side effects:** Hypertension, renal dysfunction, enhanced neuromuscular blockade [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Metabolism:** Cytochrome P-450 system (CYP3A4) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
  * May affect metabolism of benzodiazepines, fentanyl, propofol [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]

### 19.2.3. ANTIMETABOLITES

* **Mechanism:** Cell cycle arrest [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Examples:** Azathioprine, Cyclophosphamide [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Side effects:** Hepatic dysfunction, pancreatitis, renal dysfunction, enhanced neuromuscular blockade [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]

### 19.2.4. MONOCLONAL ANTIBODIES

* **Anti-CD3:** Muromonab-CD3 - less commonly used due to side effects [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Anti-IL-2 receptor:** Daclizumab [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Anti-CD52:** Alemtuzumab [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]

### 19.2.5. DRUG LEVEL MONITORING

**Narrow therapeutic windows:**
* **Significant fluid shifts** can cause acute decrease in cyclosporine or tacrolimus levels [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Consideration:** Repeat drug level testing on day of surgery [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* **Consultation with primary transplant team advised** [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Understanding immunosuppressive medication effects and interactions is crucial for safe perioperative management.

# 20. ORGAN-SPECIFIC ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS

## 20.1. MEDICATION CONSIDERATIONS

### 20.1.1. SAFE MEDICATIONS

* **Most anesthetic and antinociceptive medications** can be safely provided to transplant recipients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 20.1.2. MEDICATIONS TO AVOID

⚠️ **NSAIDs (e.g., ketorolac) should be avoided:**
* Many recipients have underlying renal dysfunction from immunosuppressive agents [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]
* NSAIDs could exacerbate renal dysfunction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* Increased GI hemorrhage risk in patients on chronic steroids [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Pharmacokinetics|ⓘ]]

### 20.1.3. MUSCLE RELAXANT CONSIDERATIONS

* **Hepatic and renal function attention required** when administering muscle relaxants [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Benzylisoquinoline relaxants (cisatracurium):** May be advisable in impaired hepatic or renal function [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]
  * Do not rely on renal or hepatic elimination [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#Endocrine|ⓘ]]

## 20.2. ANESTHETIC TECHNIQUE SELECTION

### 20.2.1. REGIONAL VS. GENERAL ANESTHESIA

* **Both techniques used successfully** in posttransplant patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Choice guided by usual factors** governing anesthetic selection [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 20.2.2. REGIONAL ANESTHESIA ADVANTAGES

**Lung transplant recipients:**
* **Minimization of airway manipulation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Decreased infection risk** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 20.2.3. AIRWAY CONSIDERATIONS

* ⚠️ **Avoid nasal intubation:** Potential infection risk from nasal flora [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6.1 Clinical Governance|ⓘ]]

## 20.3. ORGAN-SPECIFIC CONSIDERATIONS

### 20.3.1. LUNG TRANSPLANT RECIPIENTS

**Tracheal vs. Bronchial Anastomosis:**
* **Most current transplants:** Bronchial anastomoses reduce tracheal complications [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S7 Thoracic Surgery|ⓘ]]
* **Tracheal anastomosis patients:** Diminished/absent cough reflex below suture line [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Increased risk of retained secretions and pneumonia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Increased airway hyperreactivity and bronchospasm [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Diagnostic Assessment:**
* **Compare preoperative studies** with prior results [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Pulmonary function testing [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Arterial blood gas [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
  * Chest x-ray [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Significant decreases suggest:** Acute rejection or other pulmonary problems [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **ABG changes with rejection:** Increased A-a gradient, perihilar infiltrates [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Pulmonologist Consultation:**
* **Consider for suspected active pulmonary process** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Diagnostic bronchoscopy** may be needed [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]
* **Surgery delay** may benefit patients with active rejection or infection [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

### 20.3.2. HEART TRANSPLANT RECIPIENTS

💡 **Transplanted hearts are denervated** - significantly affects perioperative management:

**Medication Response Alterations:**
* **Indirect-acting agents:** Cannot respond (e.g., dopamine, ephedrine have reduced efficacy) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]
* **Direct β-receptor effects:** Exaggerated vs. α effects with epinephrine/norepinephrine [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]
* **Isoproterenol particularly useful:** Direct β1 agonist for bradydysrhythmias and myocardial depression [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]

**Clinical Maneuvers:**
* **Carotid massage and Valsalva:** No immediate hemodynamic effect [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**ECG Considerations:**
* **Two P waves possible:** Residual native atrial tissue and transplanted heart [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Native P wave:** Does not conduct to implanted heart [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Should not be confused** with complete heart block [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Hemodynamic Management:**
* **Denervated heart cannot reflexively compensate** for anesthetic-induced changes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Direct-acting sympathomimetics:** Most effective for hemodynamic depression [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#CVS: Contractility, Rate, Rhythm, Vascular Tone|ⓘ]]

### 20.3.3. CARDIAC FUNCTION ASSESSMENT

**Preoperative Evaluation:**
* **Focus on cardiac functional status** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Significant rejection:** Presents with heart failure symptoms [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Required studies:** ECG and TTE prior to surgery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **New findings:** Discuss with cardiology for stress testing or biopsy needs [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#Medical Ethics|ⓘ]]

**Advanced Monitoring:**
* **Place invasive monitors only when warranted** by clinical status and procedure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Useful options:** TEE, CVP, minimally invasive CO monitoring [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S8 General Surgical Urological Gynaecological and Endoscopic Procedures|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Each organ system requires specific considerations based on altered anatomy, physiology, and pharmacology post-transplantation.
