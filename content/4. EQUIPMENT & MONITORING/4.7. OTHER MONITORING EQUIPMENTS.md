# 1. DIATHERMY

> **Syllabus**: [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Electrical Principles|Part A: Electrical Principles]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3: Equipment & Monitoring|Part B: Equipment & Monitoring]]

## 1.2. PRINCIPLES OF DIATHERMY

**A high-frequency electrical current used to generate localized heat for cutting tissue and coagulating blood vessels.**

* Diathermy uses an electrical current to generate heat energy at the point of contact of the surgical instrument
  * Heat generated is sufficient to cut through tissue and coagulate blood vessels
* Functions by establishing a circuit that includes the patient's body
  * For **monopolar** diathermy, there is a live/active electrode (the instrument) and a passive electrode (diathermy pad attached to patient's skin distant from the operative site)
* The key operating principle is the generation of very high **current density**, which allows it to coagulate and cut through tissue

## 1.3. FREQUENCY USED IN DIATHERMY

* **Cutting mode:** 0.5MHz continuous sine wave
* **Coagulation mode:** 1-1.5MHz pulsed wave
* **Blended mode:** Combines both cutting and coagulation

![nse-5363029923522182303-114553.jpg](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_OTHER_EQUIPMENT_nse-5363029923522182303-114553.jpg_1e0b26.jpg_7885e6.jpg)

* 💡 The heart is most sensitive to arrhythmias around 50Hz
  * Much higher frequencies (0.5-1.5MHz) reduce arrhythmia risk
  * Higher frequencies have poorer tissue penetration
  * These properties greatly reduce the risk of muscle spasm, pain or ventricular fibrillation

## 1.4. MONOPOLAR VS BIPOLAR DIATHERMY

**Monopolar Diathermy:**
* Has two points of connection with the patient:
  * A large diathermy pad (return electrode)
  * The surgical tool itself (active electrode)
* Uses 100-400 Watts of power
* Patient is earthed
* ⚠️ Current travels through the patient between active electrode and return pad

**Bipolar Diathermy:**
* Diathermy passes current from one side of the forceps to the other, via a single point of contact with the patient
* Uses 40 Watts of power (much lower than monopolar)
* More suitable for coagulation than cutting
* Circuit is not earthed
* 💡 Safer option as current only passes through tissue between the forceps tips

## 1.5. SAFETY IMPLICATIONS

**Potential hazards can be categorized as follows:**

**Burns**
* Metal-related burns:
  * Metalwork in the body can generate areas of high current density and cause accidental burns
  * ⚠️ Surgical implants, jewelry, ECG electrodes, and other metal objects can create alternate current pathways
* Poor plate contact:
  * If the return electrode (plate) isn't properly attached, it risks generating higher current density and causing skin burns
  * 💡 Modern machines have return electrode monitoring systems to prevent this

**Electric Shock**
* Safety features include:
  * Safety noise: The machine generates an audible signal when in use to alert staff
  * Floating circuit: Isolates the patient from the earth to prevent unwanted current paths
  * Isolating capacitor: Has high impedance to low frequency signals (which carry a higher risk of electric shock) and blocks alternating current from flowing

**Interference**
* Even with electrical filters present in devices, monitoring such as ECG and pulse oximetry can suffer from interference by diathermy
* 💡 Consider temporarily pausing diathermy use if critical monitoring is compromised

**Fire and Smoke**
* Flammable materials such as alcohol-based skin preparations (e.g., chlorhexidine) can risk igniting if present in high concentrations
* ⚠️ Allow alcohol-based solutions to dry completely before using diathermy
* Smoke produced during diathermy contains potentially harmful particles and should be evacuated

## 1.6. DIATHERMY IN PATIENTS WITH PACEMAKERS

**Risks associated with diathermy in patients with pacemakers include:**

**Damage to the device itself**
* Avoid diathermy if possible in patients with pacemakers or ICDs
* Use bipolar diathermy preferentially as it's safer
* If monopolar is necessary:
  * ⚠️ Position the return electrode (pad) as far as possible from the pacemaker
  * Ensure the current pathway doesn't cross the pacemaker/ICD or its leads
  * Ensure adequate monitoring and have an external pacemaker available in case of device malfunction

**Heating of tissue around the device**
* Only use diathermy in short bursts to avoid prolonged heating of tissue around leads
* 💡 Keep bursts under 5 seconds with pauses between applications

**Electric interference**
* Ensure the pacemaker has been checked recently (within 3 months)
* Check the exact model and functionality
* Consider contacting electrophysiology team to determine whether anything else needs doing prior to surgery
  * This may include turning ICD off or reprogramming pacemakers to asynchronous mode

**Bottom Line:** Diathermy is an essential surgical tool that uses high-frequency electrical current to cut tissue and coagulate blood vessels. Safety considerations include preventing burns, electrical shocks, interference with monitoring, and special precautions for patients with implanted electronic devices like pacemakers.

# 2. VENTILATORS

> **Syllabus**: [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.2 Physics of Gases|Part A: Physics of Gases]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C1: Airway, Oxygenation & Ventilation|Part B: Airway, Oxygenation & Ventilation]]

## 2.2. BASIC FUNCTIONS AND CLASSIFICATION

**Ventilators are devices used to provide controlled ventilation to maintain oxygenation and removal of carbon dioxide.**

**Basic Variables in Any Ventilator:**
* **Tidal Volume (mL/breath)** - can be delivered as:
  * Fixed volume with variable peak airway pressures
  * Fixed pressure with variable volumes
* **Respiratory Rate (breaths/min)** - can be:
  * Set by ventilator (mandatory breaths)
  * Set by patient (triggered breaths)
  * These variations allow different ventilatory modes
* **Minute Volume** - product of tidal volume and respiratory rate
  * Some advanced ventilators allow direct setting of minute volume
* **I:E Ratio** - ratio of inspiratory time to expiratory time
  * Can be set directly or through adjusting inspiratory time or respiratory rate
  * Critical for conditions like ARDS or acute severe asthma

**Common Ventilatory Modes:**
* **Volume Control (VC)** - desired tidal volume delivered regardless of airway pressure
  * Peak pressure depends on lung compliance
* **Pressure Control (PC)** - desired positive pressure reached, delivering variable volume
  * Volume depends on lung compliance
* **Pressure Support (PS)** - ventilator supports patient's spontaneous breaths
  * Pressure setting defines applied pressure while patient determines breath timing

**Classification of Ventilators:**

1. **Power Source:**
   * Electrically powered
   * Pneumatically powered
   * Pneumatically powered microprocessor-controlled

2. **Pressure Generation:**
   * Positive pressure - gas pushed into lungs
   * Negative pressure - vacuum created around chest
	 * Tank ventilator ("iron lung")
	 * Cuirass ventilator

3. **Control System:**
   * Closed-loop vs. open-loop systems
   * Pneumatic circuit:
	 * Internal (single or double)
	 * External

4. **Usage Context:**
   * Operating theatre
   * Intensive care unit
   * Transport
   * Home use

5. **Patient Type:**
   * Adult
   * Pediatric

6. **Drive Mechanism:**
   * Flow devices
   * Volume displacement devices

7. **Output Control Mechanism:**
   * Proportional solenoid valve
   * Digital on/off valves

**Bottom Line:** Ventilators are complex devices with multiple classification systems and operating modes. Understanding the basic variables (tidal volume, respiratory rate, I:E ratio) and modes of ventilation (volume control, pressure control, pressure support) is essential for safe and effective ventilatory support.

## 2.3. CHARACTERISTICS OF THE IDEAL VENTILATOR

**The ideal ventilator should have these key characteristics:**

1. **Practical Design**
   * Simple, portable, robust
   * Economical to purchase and use
   * Reduced compressed gas wastage
   * Some ventilators use Venturi to drive bellows to reduce oxygen consumption

2. **Versatility**
   * Supply tidal volumes up to 1500 mL
   * Respiratory rates up to 60 breaths/min
   * Variable I:E ratio
   * Compatibility with different breathing systems
   * Ability to deliver various gas/vapor mixtures
   * PEEP capability

3. **Comprehensive Monitoring**
   * Airway pressure
   * Inspired and exhaled minute/tidal volume
   * Respiratory rate
   * Inspired oxygen concentration

4. **Additional Features**
   * Humidification capabilities
   * Drug nebulization

5. **Safety Features**
   * Disconnection alarm
   * High airway pressure alarm
   * Power failure alarm

6. **Ventilatory Mode Options**
   * SIMV
   * CPAP
   * Pressure Support

7. **Maintenance**
   * Easy to clean and sterilize

**Bottom Line:** The ideal ventilator balances versatility, monitoring capability, safety features, and practical design considerations to provide effective ventilatory support while being reliable and easy to use in various clinical settings.

## 2.4. BAG IN BOTTLE VENTILATOR

**A common ventilator incorporated into modern anesthetic machines, using a bellows system inside a rigid chamber.**

**Components:**
1. **Driving Unit:**
   * Chamber with tidal volume range of 0-1500 mL (pediatric version: 0-400 mL)
   * Ascending bellows accommodating fresh gas flow
   * 💡 "Ascending" means the bellows rests in an expanded position and moves downward during expiration

2. **Control Unit:**
   * Controls for tidal volume, respiratory rate (6-40 breaths/min), I:E ratio
   * Displays for airway pressure and power supply

![Bag in bottle ventilator](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p8_img1_aa7f88.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. Time-cycled ventilator: pneumatically powered and electronically controlled
2. Fresh gas accommodated in the bellows
3. Compressed air used as driving gas enters the chamber, forcing bellows down and delivering fresh gas to patient
4. Driving gas and fresh gas remain separate
5. Volume of driving gas reaching chamber equals tidal volume

![Mechanism of action](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p10_img1_dbddcf.jpeg)

**Problems and Safety Features:**
1. Positive pressure in standing bellows causes PEEP of 2-4 cm H₂O
2. ⚠️ Safety feature of ascending bellows: collapses to empty position and remains stationary when disconnection or leak occurs, making the problem obvious
3. ⚠️ Descending bellows designs are less safe: they hang down to fully expanded position during disconnection and may continue to move almost normally during leakage

**Bottom Line:** Bag in bottle ventilators use an ascending bellows system that provides a visual indicator of disconnection. The driving gas and the delivered gas remain separate, with the volume of driving gas determining the tidal volume. This time-cycled ventilator design is common in modern anesthetic machines.

## 2.5. PENLON NUFFIELD VENTILATOR

**An intermittent blower ventilator that is compact, versatile, and usable with different breathing systems and patient populations.**

**Components:**
1. **Control Module:**
   * Airway pressure gauge (cmH₂O)
   * Inspiratory and expiratory time dials (seconds)
   * Inspiratory flow rate dial (L/s)
   * On/off switch
   * Connections for driving gas supply and valve block

2. **Valve Block:**
   * Port for tubing connection to breathing system reservoir bag mount
   * Exhaust port (can connect to scavenging system)
   * Pressure relief valve (opens at 60 cmH₂O)
   * Can be changed to pediatric (Newton) valve

![Penlon Nuffield 200](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p12_img1_bf303d.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. Powered by driving gas (typically oxygen at ~400 kPa) from anesthetic machine
2. Compatible with multiple breathing systems (Bain, Humphrey ADE, T-piece, circle)
   * For Bain and circle systems, reservoir bag is replaced by tubing from ventilator
   * APL valve must be fully closed during ventilation
3. Adjustable inspiratory/expiratory times for desired I:E ratio
4. Tidal volume determined by inspiratory time and flow rate settings
5. With standard valve: acts as time-cycled flow generator (minimum tidal volume 50 mL)
6. With Newton valve: acts as time-cycled pressure generator (tidal volumes 10-300 mL)
   * 💡 Recommended for children <20 kg
7. PEEP valve can be fitted to exhaust port

**Problems and Safety Features:**
1. ⚠️ Continues to cycle despite breathing system disconnection with no alarm
2. Requires high flows of driving gas

**Bottom Line:** The Penlon Nuffield ventilator is a versatile, compact device that can accommodate both adult and pediatric patients. It functions as either a flow generator or pressure generator depending on valve selection. While flexible in use, it lacks disconnection alarms and requires high driving gas flows.

## 2.6. MANLEY MP3 VENTILATOR

**A minute volume divider (time-cycled pressure generator) that delivers the entire fresh gas flow to the patient divided into set tidal volumes.**

**Components:**
1. Rubber tubing delivers fresh gas flow from anesthetic machine to ventilator
2. Two sets of bellows:
   * Smaller time-cycling bellows receives fresh gas directly then empties into main bellows
   * Main bellows delivers gas to patient
3. Three unidirectional valves
4. APL valve with tubing and reservoir bag for spontaneous/manual ventilation
5. Controls:
   * Pressure gauge (up to 100 cmH₂O)
   * Inspiratory time dial
   * Tidal volume adjuster (up to 1000 mL)
   * Mode selection knobs (controlled vs. spontaneous/manual)
   * Sliding weight to adjust inflation pressure

![Manley MP3](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p16_img1_13256f.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. Fresh gas flow drives the ventilator
2. During inspiration:
   * Smaller bellows receives fresh gas while main bellows delivers contents to patient
   * Inspiratory time dial controls extent of smaller bellows filling before emptying into main bellows
3. During expiration:
   * Smaller bellows delivers contents to main bellows until predetermined tidal volume is reached
   * Then inspiration starts again
4. In spontaneous/manual mode, functions as Mapleson D breathing system

**Problems and Safety Features:**
1. 💡 Safety feature: Ventilator ceases to cycle and function when fresh gas flow is disconnected, allowing rapid detection of gas supply failure
2. ⚠️ Difficulty ventilating patients with poor pulmonary compliance
3. Generates back pressure in back bar during cycling
4. ⚠️ Emergency oxygen flush in anesthetic machine should not be activated while ventilating with Manley

**Bottom Line:** The Manley MP3 is a minute volume divider that uses fresh gas flow as its driving force. Its unique design provides a built-in safety feature as it stops cycling if gas flow is disconnected. However, it has limitations when ventilating patients with poor lung compliance and caution is needed with emergency oxygen flush.

## 2.7. SERVO-U VENTILATOR

**A versatile intensive care ventilator for both pediatric and adult patients, fully transportable with battery backup.**

**Components:**
1. **Patient Unit:**
   * Where gases are mixed and administered
2. **Graphical User Interface:**
   * For configuring settings and monitoring ventilation

![Servo-U ventilator](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p18_img1_29f83d.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. Gas flow from oxygen and air inlets regulated by respective gas modules
2. Oxygen concentration measured by oxygen cell
3. Delivered gas mixture pressure measured by inspiratory pressure transducer
4. Patient's expiratory gas flow measured by ultrasonic transducers
5. PEEP regulated by expiratory valve

**Ventilation Modes:**
1. **SIMV (Synchronized Intermittent Mandatory Ventilation):**
   * Provides synchronized mandatory breaths with patient effort
   * Options include:
	 * **Pressure-Regulated Volume Control (PRVC):** Preset tidal volume with pressure limitation
	 * **Volume Control:** Preset tidal volume and rate with constant flow
	 * **Pressure Control:** Set pressure level above PEEP, volume depends on compliance and resistance

2. **Supported Ventilation Modes:**
   * **Volume Support:** Assures set tidal volume by supplying required pressure support
   * **Pressure Support:** Patient's breath supported with constant pressure above PEEP
   * **CPAP:** Maintains continuous positive pressure with measurement capabilities

3. **Advanced Features:**
   * **NAVA (Neurally Adjusted Ventilatory Assist):** Uses specially adapted nasogastric tube to detect phrenic nerve impulses
   * **Lung Recruitment Tools:** Software that regulates PEEP to maintain lung compliance

**Problems and Safety Features:**
1. Comprehensive alarm system
2. Optional mainstream CO₂ analyzer for continuous monitoring
3. Multiple battery modules (2-6) for extended transport use
4. ⚠️ Heavier (23 kg) than dedicated transport ventilators

**Bottom Line:** The Servo-U ventilator is a sophisticated ICU ventilator with multiple ventilation modes including advanced features like NAVA and lung recruitment tools. Its comprehensive monitoring and battery backup make it suitable for transport, though its weight is a disadvantage compared to dedicated transport ventilators.

## 2.8. HIGH-FREQUENCY JET VENTILATOR

**A specialized ventilator delivering small tidal volumes at high frequency, producing lower peak airway pressures with various physiological benefits.**

**Components:**
1. **Venturi Injector:**
   * Can be attached to:
	 * Surgical suspension laryngoscope
	 * Cannula positioned in tracheal tube
	 * Cannula via cricothyroid membrane
	 * Modified tracheal tube with additional small lumens
2. **Solenoid Valves:**
   * Deliver the jet gas
3. **Controls and Display:**
   * Dials for driving pressure, frequency and inspiratory time
   * These parameters determine gas volume delivered to patient
4. **Peristaltic Pump:**
   * For nebulizing drugs or humidifying the jet gas
5. **Gas Blender:**
   * High-flow air/oxygen or nitrous oxide/oxygen blender

![High-frequency jet ventilator](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p22_img1_81586f.jpeg)

![Jet tracheal tubes](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p23_img1_220a38.jpeg)

![Hi-Lo Jet tube](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p24_img1_01aa20.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. Frequencies of 20-500 cycles/min with minute volumes of 5-60 L/min
   * 💡 Typical starting frequency is 100 cycles/min
2. Time-cycled ventilator delivering gas in small jet pulsations
   * Adjustable inspiratory time (20-50% of cycle)
3. High-velocity gas from narrow injector causes entrainment of additional gas
   * Amount of entrained gas is uncertain, making tidal volume and FiO₂ measurement difficult
4. Jet and entrained gases impact larger volume of relatively immobile gases in airway, pushing them forward
5. Expiration is passive
   * PEEP occurs automatically at respiratory rates >100 breaths/min
   * Additional PEEP can be added via PEEP valve

**Benefits:**
1. Reduced side effects compared to conventional IPPV
2. Lower peak airway pressures
3. Better maintenance of cardiac output
4. Less antidiuretic hormone production and fluid retention
5. Better tolerated by alert patients than conventional IPPV
6. Frequently used in ENT surgery

**Problems and Safety Features:**
1. ⚠️ Barotrauma risk still exists as expiration depends on passive lung/chest wall recoil
2. High-pressure (35-40 cmH₂O) and system malfunction alarms

**Bottom Line:** High-frequency jet ventilation delivers small tidal volumes at high frequencies, resulting in lower peak airway pressures and better cardiac output maintenance. It's valuable in ENT surgery and when conventional ventilation is problematic. Despite benefits, barotrauma risk remains and careful monitoring is essential.

## 2.9. TRANSPORT VENTILATORS

### 2.9.1. PARAPAC PLUS VENTILATOR

**A portable, gas-powered, time-cycled ventilator for transport of critically ill patients and emergencies.**

**Components:**
1. **Controls:**
   * Function switch (ventilator, demand, CPAP)
   * Tidal volume (70-1000 mL)
   * Frequency (8-40/min)
   * CPAP flow (0.5-35 L/min O₂, up to 16 cmH₂O)
   * Adjustable inspiratory relief pressure with alarm (20-60 cmH₂O)
   * Air mix dial (FiO₂ at 0.5 or 1.0)
   * PEEP control dial (up to 20 cmH₂O)
2. **Display and Connections:**
   * Luminous inflation pressure manometer
   * 120-cm polyester/silicone corrugated tubing with one-way valve
   * Separate port for CPAP/oxygen delivery
   * Oxygen input tubing

![ParaPAC Plus ventilator](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p28_img1_8ac385.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. Powered by dry, filtered, oil-free pressurized gas (280-600 kPa) at 65 L/min
   * 💡 Using FiO₂ of 0.5 reduces gas consumption by almost 70%
2. In FiO₂ 0.5 mode, uses entrainment device to mix ambient air with supply gas (approximately 2:1 ratio)
3. Function switch allows:
   * Controlled ventilation
   * Demand mode
   * CPAP mode
   * Single breath delivery when pressed
4. Visual indicator flashes when spontaneous breath is detected
5. During controlled ventilation, patient's spontaneous breath triggers SMMV mode
   * Mandatory ventilatory attempts synchronized with breathing pattern
6. Built-in PEEP valve facility

**Problems and Safety Features:**
1. Color-coded tidal volume and frequency controls for quick setup
2. Adjustable inspiratory pressure relief (20-60 cmH₂O) reduces barotrauma risk
3. Audible and visual alarms:
   * Low-pressure (disconnection)
   * High-pressure (obstruction)
   * Color-coded supply gas failure
4. MRI compatible

### 2.9.2. PNEUPAC VR1 EMERGENCY VENTILATOR

**A lightweight, handheld, time-cycled, gas-powered flow generator ventilator for emergency and transport use.**

**Components:**
1. Tidal volume/frequency control
2. Auto/manual control with manual trigger and push button
3. Air mix switch (100% or 50% oxygen)
4. Patient valve connecting to catheter mount/filter or face mask
5. Gas supply input

![Pneupac VR1](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p30_img1_944adc.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. Powered by pressurized oxygen (280-1034 kPa)
   * Air mix prolongs cylinder oxygen duration
2. Constant I:E ratio of 1:2 with flow rates of 11-32 L/min
3. Optional patient demand facility for synchronization
4. Manual controls allow triggering of single controlled ventilation
   * Useful for various chest compression/ventilation options in cardiac life support
5. Suitable for children (>10 kg) and adults

**Problems and Safety Features:**
1. Pressure relief valve operates at 40 cmH₂O
2. 💡 Manual control triggers equivalent volume to automatic ventilation
   * Not a purge action, cannot stack breaths (inherently safer)
3. MRI compatible up to 3 Tesla

**Bottom Line:** Transport ventilators like the ParaPAC Plus and Pneupac VR1 provide portable ventilation during patient transport and emergencies. They are gas-powered, relatively simple to operate, and include essential safety features. Their MRI compatibility adds versatility in hospital environments.

## 2.10. VENTURI INJECTOR DEVICE

**A manually controlled ventilation device used during rigid bronchoscopy and emergency ventilation in "can't intubate, can't oxygenate" (CICO) scenarios.**

**Components:**
1. High-pressure oxygen source (~400 kPa) from anesthetic machine or pipeline
2. On/off trigger
3. High-pressure-resistant connection tubing
4. Needle of suitable gauge for good air entrainment without excessive airway pressures

![Manually controlled injector](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p31_img1_1a39c9.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. High-pressure oxygen injected intermittently through needle placed at bronchoscope's proximal end or cricothyroid needle
2. Creates Venturi effect, entraining atmospheric air and inflating lungs with oxygen-enriched air
3. Achieves oxygenation and carbon dioxide elimination with airway pressures of 25-30 cmH₂O

**Problems and Safety Features:**
1. ⚠️ Barotrauma risk - no airway pressure monitoring available
   * Essential to allow full exhalation before delivering next breath to prevent air stacking
2. ⚠️ Risk of gastric distension if ventilation begins before bronchoscope passes beyond larynx

**Bottom Line:** The Venturi injector device provides a simple emergency ventilation method during rigid bronchoscopy or CICO scenarios. While effective, it lacks pressure monitoring and requires careful timing of ventilation to prevent barotrauma and gastric distension.

## 2.11. SELF-INFLATING BAG AND MASK

**A disposable manual ventilation device for resuscitation, transport and short-term ventilation, usable with or without pressurized gas supply.**

**Components:**
1. **Self-inflating bag:**
   * Usually made of clear silicone
   * Connection for added oxygen
2. **One-way valve with three ports:**
   * Inspiratory inlet for fresh gas entry during inspiration
   * Expiratory outlet for exhaled gas exit
   * Patient connection to face mask or tracheal tube
3. **Oxygen reservoir:**
   * Increases FiO₂ delivered to patient

![Self-inflating bags](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p33_img1_75d7e5.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. **Non-rebreathing valve (Ambu valve):**
   * Incorporates silicone rubber membrane
   * Low resistance to flow (inspiratory resistance 0.4 cmH₂O and expiratory resistance 0.6 cmH₂O at 25 L/min)
   * Acts as spillover valve, channeling excess inspiratory gas directly to expiratory outlet
   * Suitable for both IPPV and spontaneous ventilation

2. **Self-inflating mechanism:**
   * Bag automatically restores shape after compression
   * Draws fresh gas from reservoir
   * Pediatric version has smaller bag and pressure relief valve

3. **Optional features:**
   * Pressure manometer to monitor airway pressure and reduce barotrauma risk

![Ambu valve disassembled](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p35_img1_3a811a.jpeg)

![Pressure manometer](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_VENTHME.pdf_p36_img1_d44a48.jpeg)

**Bottom Line:** Self-inflating bag and mask systems provide a reliable, portable means of manual ventilation for emergency situations, transport, and short-term ventilation. Their self-inflating design allows use without pressurized gas, though oxygen enrichment is possible with an attached reservoir.
# 3. CPAP AND RESPIRATORY SUPPORT

> **Syllabus**: [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.2 Physics of Gases|Part A: Physics of Gases]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C1: Airway, Oxygenation & Ventilation|Part B: Airway, Oxygenation & Ventilation]]

## 3.2. COMPONENTS AND MECHANISM OF CPAP

**A spontaneous breathing mode that maintains continuous positive airway pressure throughout the respiratory cycle, used in intensive care, anesthesia, and for home respiratory support.**

**Purpose:**
* Increases functional residual capacity (FRC)
* Improves oxygenation
* Prevents alveolar collapse
* May recruit already collapsed alveoli

**Components:**
1. **Flow Generator:**
   * Produces high flows of gas
   * Alternative: large reservoir bag

2. **Connecting Tubing:**
   * From flow generator to mask's inspiratory port
   * Includes oxygen analyzer to determine inspired oxygen concentration

3. **Interface:**
   * Tight-fitting mask or hood with inspiratory and expiratory ports
   * CPAP valve fitted to expiratory port
   * For intubated patients: T-piece with CPAP valve on expiratory limb

![CPAP setup](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p2_img1_1d363f.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. Maintains positive pressure throughout breathing cycle
2. Must meet patient's peak inspiratory flow rate
3. Typical CPAP levels: 5-15 cmH₂O based on patient requirements
4. Applications:
   * Weaning patients off ventilators, especially when PEEP was used
   * Improving oxygenation in type 1 respiratory failure (where CO₂ elimination isn't problematic)

5. **BiPAP (Bilevel Positive Airway Pressure) Variant:**
   * Provides two pressure levels:
	 * IPAP (Inspiratory Positive Airway Pressure): Pressure during inspiration
	 * EPAP (Expiratory Positive Airway Pressure): Pressure during expiration
   * Airway pressure during inspiration independent of expiratory pressure
   * Useful for type 2 respiratory failure - reduces work of breathing, improves tidal volume and CO₂ removal

## 3.3. PROBLEMS AND SAFETY FEATURES

**Potential complications and considerations when using CPAP:**

1. **Cardiovascular Effects:**
   * Similar to PEEP but generally less severe
   * ⚠️ Can reduce cardiac output
   * Risk of reduced oxygen delivery to tissues despite improved arterial oxygenation

2. **Respiratory Complications:**
   * ⚠️ Barotrauma risk

3. **Interface Issues:**
   * Loose-fitting mask allows leakage and pressure loss
   * ⚠️ Skin erosion from tight-fitting mask - mitigated by soft silicone masks, protective dressings, or CPAP hood
   * Rhinorrhea and nasal dryness can occur
   * Nasal masks better tolerated but mouth breathing reduces effectiveness

4. **Gastrointestinal Considerations:**
   * 💡 Nasogastric tube insertion recommended for patients with depressed consciousness to prevent gastric distension

**Bottom Line:** CPAP is a non-invasive ventilation mode that maintains positive airway pressure throughout the respiratory cycle to improve oxygenation, particularly in type 1 respiratory failure. BiPAP extends this concept by providing different pressure levels during inspiration and expiration, making it useful for type 2 respiratory failure. Both require careful monitoring for cardiovascular effects, potential barotrauma, and interface-related complications.

## 4.2. ARTERIAL BLOOD GAS ANALYZER

**A device that measures arterial blood gases, requiring a sample of heparinized, anaerobic, and fresh arterial blood.**

**Sample Requirements:**
1. **Heparinization:**
   * Prevents clotting during analysis
   * ⚠️ Heparin should only fill dead space of syringe and coat interior
   * ⚠️ Excess heparin (acidic) lowers sample pH

2. **Anaerobic Sampling:**
   * ⚠️ Air bubbles increase oxygen partial pressure and decrease carbon dioxide partial pressure

3. **Sample Freshness:**
   * ⚠️ Old samples have lower pH and oxygen partial pressure, higher carbon dioxide partial pressure
   * If analysis must be delayed, keep sample on ice

**Parameters Measured:**
1. Arterial blood oxygen partial pressure (PaO₂)
2. Arterial carbon dioxide partial pressure (PaCO₂)
3. Arterial blood pH

**Calculated Parameters:**
* Actual bicarbonate
* Standard bicarbonate
* Base excess
* Oxygen saturation

![ABL800 Flex analyzer](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p12_img1_aa19db.jpeg)

**Bottom Line:** Arterial blood gas analyzers provide crucial information about a patient's acid-base status and gas exchange. Sample integrity is critical—blood must be collected anaerobically, with minimal heparin, and analyzed promptly to avoid measurement inaccuracies.

## 4.3. POLAROGRAPHIC OXYGEN ELECTRODE

**Also known as the Clark electrode, this device measures oxygen partial pressure in blood or gas samples.**

**Components:**
1. Platinum cathode sealed in glass body
2. Silver/silver chloride anode
3. Sodium chloride electrolyte solution
4. Oxygen-permeable Teflon membrane separating solution from sample
5. Power source of 700 mV

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. Oxygen molecules cross membrane into electrolyte solution at rate proportional to partial pressure in sample
2. Small electric current flows when polarization potential applied - electrons donated by anode and accepted by cathode
3. Electrode reactions:
   * Cathode: O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻
   * Electrolyte: 4Ag + 4Cl⁻ → 4AgCl + 4e⁻
   * Anode: 4Ag → 4Ag⁺ + 4e⁻
4. Current is linearly proportional to oxygen partial pressure in sample
5. Electrode maintained at constant temperature of 37°C

![Oxygen electrode](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p14_img1_e8da84.jpeg)

**Problems in Practice:**
1. ⚠️ Membrane deterioration/perforation affects performance - regular maintenance essential
2. ⚠️ Protein particles can precipitate on membrane affecting performance

**Bottom Line:** The polarographic oxygen electrode measures oxygen partial pressure by generating a current proportional to oxygen concentration. It requires careful maintenance, particularly of the membrane, to ensure accurate and reliable measurements.

## 4.4. PH ELECTRODE

**A device that measures the activity of hydrogen ions in blood and other physiological fluids.**

**Components:**
1. Glass electrode (silver/silver chloride) with pH-sensitive glass bulb containing buffer solution
2. Calomel reference electrode (mercury/mercury chloride) in contact with potassium chloride solution
3. Cotton plug connecting KCl solution to sample
4. Meter displaying potential difference across electrodes

![pH electrode](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p16_img1_bcb881.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. Reference electrode maintains constant potential
2. pH gradient between sample and buffer creates electrical potential
3. Electrical circuit measures potential between electrodes
4. Linear electrical output of ~60 mV per unit pH
5. Electrodes maintained at constant temperature of 37°C

**Problems in Practice:**
1. Requires calibration before use with two buffer solutions
2. Electrodes must be kept clean

**Bottom Line:** The pH electrode uses a glass membrane that's sensitive to hydrogen ion concentration, creating a potential difference proportional to pH. Maintaining proper calibration and clean electrodes is essential for accurate measurements.

## 4.5. CARBON DIOXIDE ELECTRODE

**Also known as the Severinghaus electrode, this modified pH electrode measures carbon dioxide partial pressure by detecting pH changes in an electrolyte solution.**

**Components:**
1. pH-sensitive glass electrode with silver/silver chloride reference electrode
2. Thin film of electrolyte solution (sodium bicarbonate) surrounding electrodes
3. Carbon dioxide permeable rubber or Teflon membrane

![Carbon dioxide electrode](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p19_img1_c027c2.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. Carbon dioxide (not hydrogen ions) diffuses across membrane between sample and electrolyte solution
2. CO₂ reacts with water in electrolyte solution: CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
3. Resulting pH change measured by glass electrode
4. Electrode maintained at temperature of 37°C
5. Regular calibration required

**Problems in Practice:**
1. ⚠️ Membrane integrity crucial for accuracy
2. ⚠️ Slow response time (2-3 minutes) due to diffusion time of carbon dioxide

**Bottom Line:** The carbon dioxide electrode indirectly measures CO₂ by detecting pH changes caused by carbon dioxide diffusion into a bicarbonate solution. While effective, it has a relatively slow response time and depends on membrane integrity for accurate results.

## 5.4. TEG/ROTEM SYSTEMS

**Thromboelastography (TEG) and Rotational Thromboelastometry (ROTEM) are point-of-care technologies that assess the viscoelastic properties of whole blood during clot formation.**

**Background and Applications:**
* Assess speed to clot formation and clot firmness/quality
* Differ in technical approach:
  * TEG: Spins cup around fixed pin
  * ROTEM: Spins pin in fixed cup
* Advantages over laboratory tests (PT, aPTT, TT):
  * Significantly faster results
  * Whole blood assessment
* Gold standard for bleeding management in:
  * Major trauma
  * Cardiac surgery
  * Liver transplantation
  * Massive hemorrhage from other causes

**Original TEG/ROTEM Principle:**
1. Small volume of whole blood added to cup with clot activator
2. Sensor pin inserted into blood
3. Rotational movement between pin and cup with brief pauses
4. Back and forth rotation continues throughout analysis (represents sluggish venous flow)
5. System maintained at 37°C
6. As clot forms, blood becomes more viscous and exerts more resistance to rotation
7. Resistance detected and plotted on graph of clot strength vs. time

### 5.4.1. TEG 6S

**A modern, portable point-of-care device using resonance technology instead of traditional cup and pin to assess clot formation.**

**Components:**
1. Compact, portable unit with interactive display screen
2. Microfluidic cartridge primed with fresh whole blood
   * Multiple assay configurations available

![TEG 6s](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT2.pdf_p7_img1_25b242.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. **Resonance Method:**
   * Sample exposed to fixed vibration frequency range (20-500 Hz)
   * LED illumination allows detector to measure up/down motion of blood meniscus
   * Fast Fourier transform identifies resonance frequency
   * Mapping function converts to TEG trace
   * Stiffer (stronger) clots have higher resonant frequencies and higher TEG traces
2. Can run up to four assays simultaneously

![TEG patterns](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT2.pdf_p9_img1_c4c4a9.jpeg)

**Cartridge Options:**
1. **Global Hemostasis Cartridge (Citrated Multi-channel):**
   * Uses kaolin for coagulation activation
   * Measures time to first clot, clot formation kinetics, clot strength, and fibrinolysis

2. **Kaolin TEG with Heparinase (CKH):**
   * Heparinase eliminates heparin effects in test sample
   * Used with kaolin TEG to assess presence of systemic heparin

3. **RapidTEG (CRT):**
   * Accelerated assay with tissue factor and kaolin activation
   * Provides rapid assessment for prompt intervention

4. **TEG Functional Fibrinogen (CFF):**
   * Uses tissue factor with platelet function inhibition (GPIIb/IIIa inhibitor)
   * Examines fibrinogen contribution to clot strength
   * Can extrapolate platelet contribution by comparison with overall clot strength

**Problems in Practice:**
1. Requires regular quality control checks
2. Results not instantaneous and require interpretation
3. Blood must be citrated (added to citrated blood test bottle)

**Bottom Line:** TEG and ROTEM systems provide comprehensive assessment of coagulation by measuring the viscoelastic properties of blood during clot formation. They offer rapid, whole-blood evaluation of coagulation status, allowing targeted therapy for bleeding disorders. The TEG 6s represents an evolution of this technology, using resonance methods for more portable and user-friendly testing.

> **Syllabus**: [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.2 Physics of Gases|Part A: Physics of Gases]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C4: Trauma, Resuscitation & Crisis|Part B: Trauma, Resuscitation & Crisis]]

## 6.2. INTRA-AORTIC BALLOON PUMP

**A catheter-based counterpulsation device inserted into the aorta to support patients with severe cardiac failure.**

**Insertion and Positioning:**
* Usually inserted via percutaneous femoral approach over guidewire
* Placed under fluoroscopic or transesophageal echo guidance
* Correct position: descending aorta, just distal to left subclavian artery

![IABP in situ](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p21_img1_445253.jpeg)

**Components:**
1. **Catheter:**
   * 7-FG to 8-FG catheter with balloon
   * Two coaxial lumens:
	 * Outer lumen for helium gas exchange to/from balloon
	 * Fluid-filled central lumen for continuous aortic pressure monitoring via transducer
	 * Modern versions may use fiber optics for faster, more sensitive pressure monitoring
   * Balloon volume typically 40 mL (smaller 34 mL available for small individuals)
   * Balloon size should be 80-90% of aortic diameter

2. **Console:**
   * Controls helium flow in/out of balloon
   * Monitors blood pressure and ECG
   * Allows parameter adjustment to optimize counterpulsation

![IABP console](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p24_img1_80cc39.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. **Inflation (Early Diastole):**
   * Balloon inflates immediately after aortic valve closure
   * Increases peak diastolic blood pressure (diastolic augmentation)
   * Increases coronary artery perfusion pressure and myocardial oxygen supply
   * Occurs at dicrotic notch on arterial pressure waveform
   * Corresponds to peak of T-wave on ECG

2. **Deflation (End Diastole):**
   * Balloon deflates just before aortic valve opens and remains deflated during systole
   * Decreases aortic end-diastolic pressure, reducing left ventricular afterload
   * Decreases myocardial oxygen demand
   * Increases left ventricular performance, stroke work, and ejection fraction
   * Should occur at lowest point of arterial diastolic pressure

3. **Physiological Benefits:**
   * During myocardial ischemia:
	 * Reduced myocardial oxygen demand (lower LV pressure)
	 * Increased myocardial oxygen supply (higher coronary perfusion)
   * Effectiveness depends on balloon-to-aorta size ratio, heart rate/rhythm, aortic compliance, precise timing
   * Correctly timed IABP should increase augmented diastolic pressure higher than systolic pressure
   * Expected improvements:
	 * 30% increase in diastolic pressure
	 * 20% decrease in systolic pressure
	 * 20% improvement in cardiac output

4. **Use of Helium:**
   * Low density promotes laminar flow (reduced Reynold's number)
   * Improves flow rate compared to other gases
   * Flow principles:
	 * Laminar flow: Flow rate ∝ driving pressure
	 * Turbulent flow: Flow rate ∝ √driving pressure

![IABP waveform effects](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p24_img2_3550d2.png)

**Indications:**
* Refractory ventricular failure
* Acute myocardial infarction complicated by:
  * Cardiogenic shock
  * Mitral regurgitation
  * Ventricular septal defect
* Impending myocardial infarction
* Unstable angina refractory to medical treatment
* High-risk angioplasty
* Ischemia-related ventricular arrhythmias
* Preand post-coronary bypass surgery, including weaning from cardiopulmonary bypass

**Contraindications:**
* Severe aortic regurgitation
* Aortic dissection
* Major coagulopathy
* Severe bilateral peripheral arterial disease
* Bilateral femoral-popliteal bypass graft
* Sepsis

**Problems in Practice:**
1. ⚠️ Balloon sizing issues - too large may damage aorta; too small gives ineffective counterpulsation
2. Dyssynchrony with cardiac cycle
3. Limb ischemia
4. Thrombosis and embolism - often managed with low-dose heparinization
5. Arterial dissection or perforation
6. Bleeding
7. ⚠️ Balloon rupture causing gas embolism (helium not readily absorbed)
8. Infection

**Bottom Line:** The intra-aortic balloon pump provides mechanical circulatory support through counterpulsation, increasing coronary perfusion while decreasing cardiac workload. Precise timing of inflation (early diastole) and deflation (end diastole) is crucial for optimal effect. While effective for short-term support in various cardiac conditions, it carries risks including limb ischemia, vascular injury, and potential balloon rupture.

## 6.3. EXTRACORPOREAL MEMBRANE OXYGENATION (ECMO)

**A modified cardiopulmonary bypass technology adapted for bedside use that provides prolonged cardiopulmonary support for days to weeks.**

**Indications:**
* Hypoxia, especially due to potentially recoverable viral pneumonias (e.g., SARS CoV 1 and 2)
* Cardiogenic shock or cardiac arrest
* Failure to wean from CPB following heart surgery
* Bridge to transplantation or long-term mechanical support (e.g., ventricular assist device)

**Types:**
1. **Veno-Venous (VV) ECMO:**
   * Supports only the lungs
   * Adequate systemic circulation provided by patient's own cardiac output
   * Deoxygenated blood drained from venous circulation, oxygenated, and returned to right atrium
   * Cannula position: drains from major vein and returns to major vein

2. **Veno-Arterial (VA) ECMO:**
   * Supports both heart and lungs
   * Deoxygenated blood drained from venous circulation, oxygenated, and pumped directly to arterial circulation
   * Cannula position: drains from major vein and returns to major artery

**Components:**
1. Large-bore cannulae allowing large blood flows through circuit
2. Polycarbonate semipermeable membrane (oxygenator) for gas exchange
3. Electric centrifugal pump capable of up to 12 L/min flow

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. Oxygenator provides oxygen to drained venous blood, supplementing lung-protective conventional ventilation
2. Carbon dioxide removal depends on gas flow across membrane oxygenator
   * Typically starts with 3 L/min oxygen delivery to membrane
   * Increasing flow removes more carbon dioxide
3. Circuit usually primed with approximately 1 L of crystalloid (e.g., Hartman's solution)
4. Heparinization essential, typically via continuous infusion
   * Monitored using ACT machine and TEG

**Problems in Practice and Safety Features:**
1. ⚠️ Localized bleeding:
   * Vessel cannulation requires precise technique
   * VA-ECMO involves higher-pressure arterial vessels, increasing bleeding risk
2. ⚠️ Bleeding elsewhere:
   * Cerebral (10%) and gastrointestinal bleeding most common serious sites
3. ⚠️ Femoral artery cannulation may block distal arterial flow:
   * Can cause leg ischemia
   * Small bypass cannula into distal femoral artery can reestablish perfusion
4. Flow and pressure alarms on centrifugal pump
5. Hand crank backup if centrifugal pump fails
6. Battery backup for power failures
7. ⚠️ Transmembrane pressure >50 mmHg suggests clot formation within oxygenator

**Bottom Line:** ECMO provides advanced cardiopulmonary support for patients with severe respiratory failure (VV-ECMO) or combined cardiac and respiratory failure (VA-ECMO). While effective at supporting gas exchange and circulation, it requires meticulous management to prevent complications such as bleeding, thrombosis, and limb ischemia.

## 7.3. CHEST DRAINS

**Used for drainage of air, blood, and fluids from the pleural space.**

**Components:**
1. Drainage tubing with distal ports
2. Underwater seal and collection chamber (~20 cm diameter)

![Seldinger chest drain kit](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p47_img1_0a963b.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. **System Requirements:**
   * Air-tight system to maintain subatmospheric intrapleural pressure
   * Underwater seal acts as one-way valve:
	 * Allows air expulsion from pleural space
	 * Prevents reentry during inspiration
   * Enables lung reexpansion after pneumothorax
   * Minimizes mediastinal shift for hemodynamic stability

2. **Insertion Technique:**
   * Aseptic technique at 4th-5th intercostal space in midaxillary line
   * Local anesthetic infiltration of skin and deeper tissues
   * Incision and blunt dissection to pleural cavity
   * Manual sweeping to ensure lung is not adherent to insertion site
   * Drain inserted and positioned (usually toward apex)
   * Connection to underwater seal device
   * Some designs include flexible trocar to reduce trauma risk

3. **Underwater Seal System:**
   * Drainage tube submerged 1-2 cm in collection chamber
   * Ensures minimal drainage resistance while maintaining seal against inspiratory efforts
   * Collection chamber positioned ~100 cm below chest (accommodates subatmospheric pressures up to -80 cmH₂O during obstructed inspiration)
   * Alternative: Heimlich flutter one-way valve for better patient mobility
   * Drainage occurs via gravity or applied suction (-15-20 mmHg)

![Chest drain underwater seal](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p48_img1_467b3a.jpeg)

**Problems in Practice and Safety Features:**
1. ⚠️ Retrograde fluid flow risk if collection chamber raised above patient level
   * Keep collection chamber below patient at all times to prevent siphoning into pleural space
2. Absence of oscillations may indicate:
   * Obstruction (clots, kinks)
   * Loss of subatmospheric pressure
   * Complete lung reexpansion
3. Persistent bubbling indicates continuing bronchopleural air leak
4. ⚠️ Never clamp a pleural drain with continuing air leak - may cause tension pneumothorax

**Bottom Line:** Chest drains provide a closed, one-way system for evacuating air and fluid from the pleural space. The underwater seal principle prevents air reentry during inspiration while allowing drainage. Key considerations include maintaining the collection chamber below the patient level and monitoring for oscillations and bubbling to assess system function and lung status.

## 7.4. HAEMOFILTRATION

**A process of acute renal support for critically ill patients involving ultrafiltration of blood.**

**Principles:**
* Ultrafiltration: passage of fluid under pressure across semipermeable membrane
* Low molecular weight solutes (up to 20,000 Da) carried with fluid by solvent drag (convection) rather than diffusion
* Preserves larger molecules like plasma proteins, albumin (62,000 Da), and cellular elements
* Advantageous in critical care for:
  * Relative ease of use
  * Better tolerability in cardiovascularly unstable patients

**Delivery Methods:**
1. **Continuous Low Flow Therapy:**
   * Typically runs 24h/day for >1 day
   * May be stopped for procedures and circuit changes
   * Offers better cardiovascular stability and efficient solute removal
   * Gradual biochemical correction and fluid removal
   * Better suited for frequently changing fluid balance in multiorgan dysfunction

2. **Intermittent High Flow Systems:**
   * Often 4-hour sessions
   * New filter and circuit for each session

![Haemofiltration system](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p5_img1_f56510.jpeg)

**Components:**
1. **Intravascular Access:**
   * Arteriovenous lines (femoral artery and vein, brachial artery and femoral vein)
   * Venovenous lines (femoral vein, subclavian vein using double-lumen catheter)
   * Extracorporeal circuit connected to these lines
   * Lines kept as short as possible to minimize resistance

2. **Filter/Membrane:**
   * Synthetic materials ideal: polyacrylonitrile, polysulfone, polymethyl methacrylate
   * Large pore size for efficient diffusion (larger than dialysis filters)

3. **Pumps:**
   * Two roller pumps - one on each side of circuit
   * Each peristaltically propels ~10 mL blood per revolution
   * Positioned slightly below patient's heart level

4. **Collection Vessel:**
   * For ultrafiltrate
   * Positioned below pump level

![Haemofilter set](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p6_img1_9731c2.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. **Basic Circuit:**
   * Links artery to vein with filter between
   * Patient's blood pressure provides hydrostatic pressure for ultrafiltration
   * Suitable for fluid-overloaded patients with stable, normal blood pressure
   * ⚠️ Mean BP <60-70 mmHg reduces flow/filtrate volume and increases clotting risk

2. **Venovenous System:**
   * Pump added to eliminate need for arterial cannulation
   * Pump speed controls transmembrane pressure maintenance
   * Reduces clotting risk
   * Most common method
   * Blood flows 30-750 mL/min possible (typically 150-300 mL/min used)
   * Ultrafiltration rate 25-40 mL/min

![Venovenous haemofiltration](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p9_img1_e535bf.jpeg)

1. **Fluid Balance:**
   * Simultaneous reinfusion of sterile crystalloid fluid
   * Contains normal plasma electrolyte values (Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Cl⁻, glucose, lactate/bicarbonate buffers)
   * Osmolality 285-335 mOsmol/kg
   * Large volumes needed (2-3 L/h)
   * Some systems automatically weigh filtrate and supply appropriate reinfusion fluid

2. **Monitoring and Safety:**
   * Pressure transducers monitor blood pressure in access and return lines
   * Air bubble detection included
   * Low inflow pressure may indicate line occlusion
   * High/low postpump pressures may indicate line occlusion/disconnection

3. **Anticoagulation:**
   * Unfractionated heparin typically used:
	 * Loading dose ~3000 IU
	 * Infusion ~10 IU/kg body weight
   * Monitored by aPTT or ACT
   * Alternatives: citrate, prostacyclin, low-molecular-weight heparin

4. **Filter Design:**
   * Cylinder or flat box casing
   * High surface area-to-volume ratio
   * Usually parallel collection of hollow fibers packed in plastic canister
   * Blood passes through tubules; filtrate collected via ports in outer casing
   * Key properties:
	 * Highly biocompatible - minimal complement/leukocyte activation
	 * Highly wettable - high ultrafiltration rates
	 * Large pore size - efficient diffusion
	 * Optimal surface area 0.3-1.9 m²
   * Clears small molecules (urea, creatinine, potassium) and some larger molecules (myoglobin, some antibiotics)
   * Proteins excluded due to size

**Problems in Practice and Safety Features:**
1. Extracorporeal circuit requires 2L normal saline priming to remove ethylene oxide sterilization gas
2. Risk of hemolysis from roller pump
3. Risk of tubing cracks after long-term use
4. Bleeding risk must be controlled by optimizing anticoagulant dose
5. ⚠️ Citrate can be used as alternative to heparin, usually delivered as prefilter infusion

**Bottom Line:** Haemofiltration provides effective renal support for critically ill patients through convective removal of fluid and solutes across a semipermeable membrane. The venovenous approach is most common in current practice, offering cardiovascular stability and efficient solute clearance. Key considerations include vascular access, anticoagulation management, and fluid balance maintenance.

## 7.5. RED BLOOD CELL SALVAGE (CELL SAVER)

**A device that enables autologous transfusion of red blood cells from blood lost during surgery or trauma.**

**Applications:**
* Valuable alternative to allogeneic blood transfusion (reduces complications and costs)
* Used in various surgical settings:
  * Cardiac surgery
  * Major vascular surgery
  * Major hepatobiliary surgery
  * Major spinal surgery
  * Arthroplasty (particularly revision hip replacement)
  * Major urological surgery
  * Thoracic, abdominal, and pelvic trauma surgery
  * Obstetric procedures and major obstetric hemorrhage

![Cell Saver Elite](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p56_img1_534199.jpeg)

**Components:**
1. Suction tube with wide-bore tip (>4 mm)
2. Anticoagulant (ACDA or heparinized saline)
3. Reservoir with 40-150 μm mesh filter
4. Bowl and centrifuge
5. Blood collection bag for salvaged RBCs
6. Waste bag

![Cell saver schematic](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p58_img1_e8de6c.jpeg)

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. **Blood Collection:**
   * Wide-bore tip suction with minimal negative pressure (−100 to −150 mmHg) to reduce hemolysis
   * System autoregulates pressure to maximize red cell recovery
   * Anticoagulant added in collection reservoir:
	 * Heparin (1:5 ratio - 20 mL anticoagulant to 100 mL blood)
	 * Citrate (1:7 ratio - 15 mL anticoagulant to 100 mL blood)
	 * Heparin saline typically prepared with 30,000 units in 1L normal saline
	 * Flow rate: 60-80 drops/min for heparin, 40-60 drops/min for citrate

2. **Processing:**
   * Reservoir contents filtered to remove large clots and debris
   * Blood enters centrifuge where sterile isotonic saline is pumped into bowl
   * Bowl size varies based on collected blood volume
   * RBCs (being denser) held against outer wall by centrifugal force
   * Plasma and less dense components move toward center, spilling into waste bag
   * Some RBC loss occurs in this process
   * Photo-optics measure maximum cell density to initiate washing of packed cells with saline
   
3. **Reinfusion:**
   * Packed RBCs collected in separate blood collection bag
   * Can be reinfused immediately or kept up to 4 hours at room temperature
   * Standard blood infusion set used
   * Transfused blood has 60-70% hematocrit
   * 💡 Salvaged RBCs have higher concentrations of 2,3-DPG and ATP compared to stored allogeneic RBCs
   * Waste products (plasma, platelets, clotting factors, WBCs, fat) discarded as clinical waste
   * "Collect-only" mode recommended when blood loss exceeds 10% of total blood volume

**Problems in Practice and Safety Features:**
1. No absolute contraindications, but relative contraindications include:
   * Bowel content contamination
   * Infection
   * Tumor cells
2. 💡 For cancer or infected field surgery, leucodepletion filters recommended during reinfusion
3. ⚠️ Retransfused blood lacks platelets and clotting factors
4. ⚠️ Certain substances should not be aspirated:
   * Iodine skin preparations
   * Chlorhexidine
   * Antibiotics not licensed for IV use
   * Fibrin-based glues
   * Topical clotting agents
   * Unset orthopedic cement

**Bottom Line:** RBC salvage provides a valuable method for autologous blood transfusion, reducing allogeneic transfusion requirements in cases with significant blood loss. The system collects, washes, and concentrates RBCs while removing potentially harmful components. The resulting product has high hematocrit and improved oxygen-carrying capabilities compared to stored blood, though it lacks platelets and clotting factors.

## 8.2. COMPONENTS AND MECHANISM

**A device that delivers electrical energy to the heart, causing simultaneous depolarization of myocardial cells to allow stable rhythm establishment.**

**Types:**
* Automated external defibrillators (AEDs) - offer interaction with rescuer through voice and visual prompts
* Manual defibrillators - provide full control over energy selection and delivery

![AED](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p38_img1_94770a.jpeg)

![Manual defibrillator](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p39_img1_511668.jpeg)

**Components:**
1. Controls:
   * On/off switch
   * Joules setting control
   * Charge and discharge buttons
2. Paddles:
   * External (applied to chest wall)
   * Internal (applied directly to heart)
   * External paddles/pads typically 8-8.5 cm in size

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. **Energy Source:**
   * Direct current (DC) used rather than alternating current (AC)
   * DC causes less myocardial damage and is less arrhythmogenic
   * Lower energy causes less heart damage

2. **Electrical Circuit:**
   * Transformers step up mains voltage from 240V AC to 5000-9000V AC
   * Rectifier converts to 5000V DC
   * Variable voltage transformer allows different charge selection
   * Internal rechargeable batteries provide DC when mains unavailable
   * For battery operation, inverter converts DC to AC before transformation

![Defibrillator circuit](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p40_img1_256ae5.jpeg)

- **Rectifier** - converts AC to DC, allows current to flow in one direction only
- **Capacitor** - stores electrical charge
- **Inductor** - stores energy in the changing magnetic field in the coil 
	- Opposes sudden change in current flow and slows down rapid discharge from capacitor 
	- Smooths out rapid change in current 
	- Impt as high peak current can result in myocardial injury - and hence this inductor allows for an optimal duration and shape of current 
	- Prolongs duration to reduce burns and allow effective defib

1. **Discharge Mechanism:**
   * Capacitor stores electrical charge, releases over short period
   * Current maintained for several milliseconds for successful defibrillation
   * Inductors prolong current flow duration (counteracts rapid exponential decay)

2. **Paddle Placement:**
   * Standard positions:
	 * One on sternum
	 * One on left midaxillary line (5th-6th rib)
   * Alternative placement:
	 * One anterior over left precordium
	 * One posterior behind heart
   * Firm pressure reduces transthoracic impedance for higher peak current
   * Conductive gel pads reduce impedance
   * Modern systems often use disposable adhesive defibrillator pads for hands-free operation

3. **Current Flow:**
   * Most current dissipated through skin and tissue resistance
   * Small portion (~35A) flows through heart
   * Impedance typically 50-150 Ohms, reduces with repeated shocks in quick succession

4. **Waveform Types:**
   * **Monophasic:** Unipolar current flow (one direction)
	 * Older technology
	 * Waveform affected by transthoracic impedance
	 * Larger patients received less transmyocardial current
   
   * **Biphasic:** Two-phased current flow
	 * Current flows in one direction then reverses
	 * Two main types:
	   * Biphasic truncated exponential
	   * Rectilinear biphasic
	   ![[Pasted image 20250715194443.png]]
	 * Compensates for transthoracic impedance by electronically adjusting waveform
	 * Ensures optimal current delivery regardless of patient size

5. **Peak Current Consideration:**
   * Biphasic delivers lower peak current than monophasic
   * Lower peak current reduces risk of heart injury while maintaining effectiveness
   * Goal: Deliver sufficient current to stop lethal rhythm with minimal peak current

![Defibrillator current comparison](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p44_img1_820bf1.jpeg)

1. **Special Features:**
   * Some include ECG display and recording capabilities
   * Synchronized cardioversion capability times shock with R-wave peak for treating certain arrhythmias (e.g., atrial fibrillation)

2. **Implantable Cardiac Defibrillators (ICDs):**
   * Self-contained diagnostic and therapeutic device
   * Components: battery and pulse generator (sealed together) plus insulated wires
   * Implanted under skin near shoulder
   * Wires threaded through blood vessels to heart muscle
   * Continuously monitors rhythm; delivers shock when malignant tachyarrhythmias detected
   * Modern devices include:
	 * Overdrive pacing capability
	 * Backup pacing for bradycardia
	 * Storage of detected arrhythmic events
	 * Non-invasive electrophysiologic testing capability

![Implantable cardiac defibrillator](OTHER_EQUIPMENT_pdf_OTHEREQUIPMENT.pdf_p45_img1_9fc0d8.jpeg)

## 8.3. SAFETY CONSIDERATIONS

**Potential complications and safety features of defibrillator use:**

1. **Patient Complications:**
   * Skin burns
   * Further arrhythmias

2. **Device Concerns:**
   * ICD malfunction:
	 * Internal short circuit when delivering electrical shock
	 * Memory errors

3. **Protocol Issues:**
   * Synchronization errors during cardioversion
   * Paddle positioning affects current distribution
   * Energy selection must be appropriate for the patient and arrhythmia

**Bottom Line:** Defibrillators deliver electrical current to terminate lethal arrhythmias. Modern devices use biphasic waveforms that adjust for patient impedance, delivering more effective defibrillation with lower energy. Proper paddle/pad placement, appropriate energy selection, and awareness of potential complications are essential for safe and effective use.
