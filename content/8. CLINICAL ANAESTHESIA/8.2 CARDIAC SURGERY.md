# 1. INTRODUCTION
<!-- EPA-10:K1:basic_science EPA-10:K6:cv_procedures -->

> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|Part B: S6 Cardiac Surgery and Interventional Cardiology]]

**Cardiac anesthesia represents one of the most complex and intellectually demanding subspecialties in anesthesiology.**

Anesthetizing patients who undergo cardiac surgery is a dynamic, intellectually challenging, and demanding hands-on experience [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. The cardiac anesthesiologist should have a thorough understanding of normal and altered cardiac physiology, cardiovascular and anesthetic pharmacology and be cognizant of the physiologic alterations associated with cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) and attendant surgical procedures [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

## 1.1. CLINICAL POSTING OVERVIEW

During a one-month cardiac anesthesia posting, residents should obtain exposure to most of the following:

**Adult Cardiac Anaesthesia:**
* CABG – off pump and on pump
* Valve repair/replacement  
* TAVI
* Minimally invasive cardiac surgery
* Aortic Surgery
* Adult congenital cardiac surgery
* Redo operations
* Elective, urgent and emergency

**Neonatal & Paediatric Cardiac Anaesthesia**

**Additional Skills:**
* Cardiopulmonary Bypass
* Basic Transthoracic and Transesophageal Echocardiography

💡 **Key Principles:**
* Thorough understanding of cardiovascular physiology and pharmacology
* Knowledge of cardiopulmonary bypass effects
* Integration of surgical and anesthetic considerations
* Dynamic hemodynamic management

⚠️ **Important Note:** Please discuss all management issues with the consultant in charge.

**Bottom Line:** Cardiac anesthesia requires comprehensive knowledge of cardiovascular physiology, pharmacology, and surgical considerations to provide optimal perioperative care for complex cardiac patients.

# 2. CORONARY ARTERY DISEASE
<!-- EPA-10:K8:cv_diseases EPA-10:K6:cabg -->

> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Anaesthesia considerations/technique for cardiac surgical procedures|Part B: Anaesthesia Considerations for Cardiac Surgical Procedures]]

**The primary goal in coronary artery disease management is prevention or treatment of myocardial ischemia and reduction of perioperative myocardial infarction.**

The goal of perioperative care for the patient undergoing coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery is the prevention or treatment of myocardial ischemia and to decrease the incidence of perioperative myocardial infarction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. Hemodynamic management should avoid factors that increase myocardial oxygen demand (M O2), particularly during the vulnerable pre-CPB period [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

## 2.1. MYOCARDIAL OXYGEN BALANCE

![Figure 39-1 Determinants of myocardial oxygen balance. P, intracavitary pressure; R, ventricular radius; h, wall thickness; AoDP, diastolic arterial pressure; LVEDP, left ventricular end-diastolic pressure; Hb, hemoglobin; SatO2, arterial oxygen saturation.](https://4c37f570e5dd-4080-9df0-ac4ec6c67e0c.ngrok-free.app/img/fig39-1.png)

### 2.1.1. MYOCARDIAL OXYGEN DEMAND

The principal determinants of M O2 are wall stress, heart rate, and contractility [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. According to Laplace's law, wall stress is directly proportional to intracavitary pressure and radius and inversely proportional to wall thickness [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

💡 **Key Determinants:**
* **Wall stress** (most important factor)
* **Heart rate** (linear relationship with oxygen consumption)
* **Contractility** (inotropic state)

⚠️ **Clinical Implications:**
* M O2 is reduced by decreasing afterload (blood pressure) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Preventing or promptly treating ventricular distention (hypervolemia) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Tachycardia is the main cause of intraoperative and perioperative increases in M O2 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 2.1.2. MYOCARDIAL OXYGEN SUPPLY

Increases in M O2 can be addressed by augmenting coronary blood flow [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. Arterial blood oxygen content and myocardial oxygen extraction are infrequent reasons for intraoperative myocardial ischemia because blood volume and oxygenation are usually well controlled during anesthesia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

💡 **Supply Factors:**
* **Coronary blood flow** (primary determinant)
* **Arterial oxygen content** (usually adequate intraoperatively)
* **Myocardial oxygen extraction** (limited reserve - coronary sinus blood PO2 15-20 mmHg) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 2.1.3. CORONARY BLOOD FLOW REGULATION

Coronary blood flow is regulated by several factors including heart rate, perfusion pressure, vascular tone of the coronary circulation, and metabolism [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 2.1.3.1. LEFT VENTRICULAR PERFUSION

* **Coronary perfusion pressure** = Aortic diastolic pressure (AoDP) - LV end diastolic pressure (LVEDP) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* LV subendocardium is most vulnerable to ischemia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Perfusion occurs almost entirely during diastole [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 2.1.3.2. AUTOREGULATION AND CORONARY RESERVE

* Alterations in tone of small intramyocardial arterioles regulate diastolic vascular resistance [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Coronary vascular reserve** = difference between autoregulated flow and maximal vasodilated flow [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Normal reserve is 3-5 times higher than basal flow [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Autoregulation lost when perfusion pressure <40 mmHg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

![Figure 39-2 The pressure relationships between the aorta (1) and the left ventricle (2) determine coronary perfusion pressure. In coronary artery disease, myocardial perfusion may be compromised by decreased pressure distal to a significant stenosis (1a) (not quantifiable clinically) and/or by an increase in left ventricular end-diastolic pressure (2a).](https://4c37f570e5dd-4080-9df0-ac4ec6c67e0c.ngrok-free.app/img/fig39-2.png)

#### 2.1.3.3. ISCHEMIA TYPES

* **Supply-induced ischemia:** Transient coronary occlusion from vasospasm or thrombus [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Demand-induced ischemia:** Inability to increase coronary blood flow in response to increased M O2 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Stunned myocardium:** Temporary dysfunction due to ischemia, normalizes once perfusion restored [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Hibernating myocardium:** Abnormal function from chronically reduced flow due to fixed stenosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 2.1.3.4. CRITICAL STENOSIS CONCEPT

💡 **Critical Stenosis Definition:** Coronary stenosis that significantly impairs distal flow, typically >70% diameter reduction in epicardial vessels.

**Effects on Distal Vascular Bed at Rest:**
* **Compensatory vasodilation** of distal resistance vessels to maintain resting flow
* **Reduced coronary reserve** - limited ability to increase flow during increased demand
* **Flow becomes pressure-dependent** beyond the stenosis
* **Collateral circulation development** may partially compensate for reduced flow

**Bottom Line:** Myocardial ischemia results from imbalance between oxygen supply and demand. The key to prevention is controlling heart rate, afterload, and preload while maintaining adequate coronary perfusion pressure.

## 2.2. HEMODYNAMIC GOALS

The primary goal is prevention of myocardial ischemia and prompt identification and treatment of new ischemic episodes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

| Parameter | Goal | Comment |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Preload | ↓ | Smaller heart size (TEE dimensions) decreases wall tension and PADP (LVEDP) and increases perfusion pressure gradient |
| Afterload | Maintain | Hypertension is better than hypotension |
| Contractility | ↓ | If LV function is normal |
| Rate | ↓ | |
| Rhythm | Sinus | Correct arrhythmias |
| M O2 | | Monitor for and treat "supply"-related disturbances |
| Post-CPB | | No need for increased PADP (LVEDP) |

### 2.2.2. ANESTHETIC GOALS BEFORE CPB

**Hemodynamic Stability Goals:**
* Maintain hemodynamic stability - avoid extremes of heart rate and blood pressure
* **Bradycardia is tolerated** so long as MAP and cardiac output are not compromised as this allows increased diastolic time for coronary perfusion
* Maintain adequate perfusion pressure based on coronary, cerebrovascular, renal, and other involved organ systems, based on examination of preoperative values and reduction in demand during stable anesthetic

💡 **Essential Medications:**
* **Statins and β-blockers** are essential preoperatively [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* β-Blockers associated with decreased incidence of atrial and ventricular dysrhythmias [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Critical Points:**
* Slower heart rate, avoidance of ventricular distention, and adequate blood pressure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Peripheral arterial systolic pressure is different (usually higher) than aortic root pressure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Hemodynamic management focuses on controlling heart rate, maintaining adequate afterload, and preventing ventricular distention while optimizing coronary perfusion pressure.

## 2.3. ANESTHETIC SELECTION

The choice of anesthetic should be based on known hemodynamic, pharmacologic, and pharmacokinetic effects of each drug [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. Hemodynamic changes are minimized if drugs are titrated to the desired effect [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 2.3.1. OPIOIDS

**Primary advantages:** Lack of myocardial depression, maintenance of stable hemodynamic state, and reduction of heart rate [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

* Supplemented with benzodiazepines and volatile agents [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Shorter-acting opioids** (sufentanil and remifentanil) preferred to decrease mechanical ventilation time [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Opioid-sparing techniques:** Preoperative acetaminophen, intraoperative nonopioid analgesic infusions, regional anesthesia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Common Induction Combinations:**
* Various combinations of opioids can be used – bolus or infusions
* Examples include alfentanil, remifentanil, fentanyl
* **Reduced doses** of opioids are used to facilitate fast tracking of recovery/post-op early extubation (Example - Fentanyl 10-20mcg/kg)

### 2.3.2. INHALATION ANESTHETICS

💡 **Key Benefits:**
* Dose-dependent hemodynamic effects (titratable) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Easy to reverse [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Provide amnesia and suppress sympathetic responses [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Volatile anesthetics precondition the myocardium against reperfusion injury** and help reduce myocardial infarct size [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 2.3.2.1. SPECIFIC AGENTS

* **Desflurane and sevoflurane:** Fastest recovery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Isoflurane:** Greatest coronary vasodilation (clinically insignificant <1 MAC) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Desflurane:** Rapid uptake/distribution, useful for rapid hemodynamic changes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Important Considerations:**
* When compared to TIVA, desflurane or sevoflurane associated with decreased mortality and morbidity after cardiac surgery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Desflurane as sole agent can increase sympathetic activity and myocardial ischemia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Nitrous Oxide is avoided** in cardiac anesthesia

#### 2.3.2.2. ISCHEMIC PRECONDITIONING WITH VOLATILE AGENTS

**Definition:** An adaptive mechanism by which brief period of reversible ischemia increases the heart tolerance to a subsequent longer period of ischemia.

**Mechanisms:** When volatile anesthetics are administered before, during, or after periods of organ ischemia, they exert cell-protective effects through multiple mechanisms:
* Modulation of G-protein–coupled receptors
* Intracellular signaling pathways
* Gene expression modification
* Potassium channels modulation
* Mitochondrial function enhancement

**Types of Preconditioning:**

**Early/Classical Preconditioning:**
* Onset is immediate
* Duration: 2–3 hours after ischemic insult
* Robust infarct-sparing effect
* Duration cannot increase by continuous infusion (adenosine receptor desensitization)

**Late Preconditioning/Second Window:**
* Changes in gene expression lead to synthesis of stress proteins
* Duration: 12-24 hours after ischemia up to 72 hours
* Infarct-sparing effect plus anti-stunning effect

💡 **Clinical Evidence:** Volatile anesthetics reduce myocardial infarct size in animal models and several randomized, controlled trials have shown that volatile anesthetics reduce biomarkers of myocardial injury.

**MYRIAD Trial:** RCT of 5400 patients comparing outcomes with volatile agents vs TIVA. Among patients undergoing elective CABG, anesthesia with a volatile agent did not result in significantly fewer deaths at 1 year than total intravenous anesthesia.

### 2.3.3. INTRAVENOUS SEDATIVE HYPNOTICS

**Options:** Midazolam, propofol, ketamine, or dexmedetomidine [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

* Can be continued postoperatively in ICU [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Provide predictable and rapid awakening after discontinuation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Benzodiazepines** are generally avoided or used in reduced doses due to increased incidence of post-op delirium

⚠️ **Comparative Effects:**
* **Propofol:** Associated with less favorable cardiac function, higher inotropic support need, elevated troponins in elderly [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Dexmedetomidine:** May provide faster emergence but should not be used to reduce atrial fibrillation or delirium [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Maintenance Options:**
* Generally with volatile agents (sevoflurane & ischemic pre-conditioning effect)
* Infusions can be used – TIVA (TCI Propofol), IV Dexmedetomidine infusion

**Bottom Line:** Volatile anesthetics are preferred for their cardioprotective effects, while opioid-sparing multimodal techniques facilitate early recovery.

## 2.4. TREATMENT OF ISCHEMIA

Principal vasoactive drugs include nitrates, β-blockers, peripheral vasoconstrictors, and calcium entry blockers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

| Clinical Manifestation | Suggested Intervention |
|------------------------|------------------------|
| **Increased Demand** |  |

| ↑ PCWP | Nitroglycerin |
| **Decreased Supply** |  |

### 2.4.2. NITRATES

**Nitroglycerin** is the drug of choice for acute myocardial ischemia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

💡 **Mechanism and Effects:**
* Acts via systemic venodilation and coronary arterial dilation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Effective in stenosed coronaries and collateral beds [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Prophylactic use does not prevent intraoperative ischemia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Adverse Effects:**
* Higher doses cause systemic hypotension and compensatory tachycardia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Can cause methemoglobinemia at higher doses/prolonged administration [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Sodium Nitroprusside:**
* Decreases peripheral vascular resistance via nitric oxide [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Improves ventricular compliance in ischemic myocardium [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Cyanide Toxicity Risk:**
* Toxic levels (>100 μg/dL) occur with >1.0 mg/kg in 2 hours or >0.5 mg/kg/hr in 24 hours [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Signs:** Elevated mixed venous O2 (SVO2), tachyphylaxis, metabolic acidosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Treatment:** Discontinue infusion, 100% O2, amyl nitrate, sodium nitrite, thiosulfate [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 2.4.3. VASOCONSTRICTORS

**Agents:** Phenylephrine, norepinephrine, vasopressin [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

💡 **Benefits:**
* Increase coronary perfusion pressure by raising blood pressure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Increase in perfusion pressure usually outweighs increased wall tension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Essential during episodes of systemic hypotension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 2.4.4. Β-BLOCKERS

**Mechanism:** Improve myocardial oxygen balance by decreasing chronotropy and inotropy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Indications:**
* Sinus tachycardia unrelated to light anesthesia or hypovolemia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Decreasing ventricular response to supraventricular dysrhythmias [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Control of ventricular dysrhythmias [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Available Agents:**
* **Metoprolol:** β1-selective, less likely to trigger bronchospasm [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Labetalol:** Combined β- and α-blocking properties [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Esmolol:** Short-acting β1-blocker, half-life 9.5 minutes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **POISE Study Warning:** Death and stroke were side effects of perioperative β-blockade [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Treatment of ischemia requires targeted therapy based on the underlying mechanism - increasing supply or decreasing demand through appropriate pharmacologic interventions.

# 3. VALVULAR HEART DISEASE
<!-- EPA-10:K6:open_heart_procedures EPA-10:K8:cv_diseases -->

> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Perioperative assessment of|Part B: Perioperative Assessment - Valve Pathologies]]

**Valvular heart disease management remains challenging due to primary valvular pathology and secondary compensatory mechanisms.**

The prevalence of valvular heart disease (VHD) continues to be a source of patient morbidity and mortality. In 2021 the American Heart Association reported that the incidence of VHD was 65 per 100,000 people-years [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. Aortic stenosis remains the most prevalent diagnosis at 47.2% followed by mitral regurgitation at 24.2% and aortic regurgitation at 18% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

💡 **Key Epidemiology:**
* **Aortic stenosis:** 47.2% of VHD cases
* **Mitral regurgitation:** 24.2% of cases  
* **Aortic regurgitation:** 18% of cases

The challenge in VHD management is that primary valvular disease impacts cardiac output and secondary compensatory mechanisms also contribute to morbidity and mortality [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

## 3.1. MITRAL STENOSIS

**Pathophysiology:** Due to leaflet thickening and commissural fusion, typically from rheumatic heart disease or mitral annular calcification [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 3.1.1. CAUSES

* **Rheumatic disease:** Most common cause worldwide [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Other causes:** Radiation valvulitis, congenital (parachute mitral valve, cor triatriatum), atrial myxoma, prosthetic valve degeneration [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.1.2. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

![Figure 39-3 Pathophysiology of mitral stenosis. RV, right ventricle; PA, pulmonary artery; LA, left atrium; AFib, atrial fibrillation; LV, left ventricle.](https://4c37f570e5dd-4080-9df0-ac4ec6c67e0c.ngrok-free.app/img/fig39-3.png)

* **Normal MV area:** 4-6 cm² [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Gradient develops:** When area <2 cm² [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Consequences:** Increased LAP → pulmonary hypertension → RV hypertrophy/failure → TR [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Atrial fibrillation:** Present in ~40% of MS patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 3.1.2.1. GORLIN FORMULA FOR MVA CALCULATION [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

MVA = Flow / (K × √Pressure Gradient)

Where:
* Flow = cardiac output/diastolic filling time
* Pressure gradient = LAP - LVDP  
* K = hydraulic pressure constant

💡 **Clinical Implications:**
* Anything increasing cardiac output or decreasing diastolic filling time increases LAP [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* High output states (pregnancy, infection) can cause pulmonary edema [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Atrial fibrillation decreases diastolic filling time → increased LAP [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.1.3. TREATMENT

* **Definitive:** MV replacement, repair, or balloon mitral valvotomy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Medical:** Slow heart rate with β- or calcium channel blockers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Acute AF:** Immediate rate control ± synchronized cardioversion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.1.4. TABLE 39-3: MITRAL STENOSIS HEMODYNAMIC GOALS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

| Parameter | Goal | Comment |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Preload | Maintain | Maintain, avoid hypovolemia |
| Afterload |  | Prevent ↑ RV afterload; treat systemic hypotension with vasopressors |
| Contractility | ↑ RV | May be decreased in long-standing pulmonary hypertension (LV usually intact) |
| Rate | Maintain | At low end of normal; avoid and treat tachycardia |
| Rhythm |  | Control ventricular response in atrial fibrillation |
| M O2 |  | Not a problem |
| Post-CPB |  | LV preload and filling pressures may be elevated; function does not improve immediately |

### 3.1.5. ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS

⚠️ **Key Risks:**
* Pulmonary edema and RV failure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Avoid tachyarrhythmias, hypervolemia, increased RV afterload [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Hypoxemia, hypercapnia, acidosis, hypothermia increase PVR → RV failure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

💡 **Management Principles:**
* Many induction agents cause hypotension with reflex tachycardia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Fluid bolus may not be ideal due to fixed stenotic MVA [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Post-CPB: Chronically under-filled LV at risk of distension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Mitral stenosis management focuses on controlling heart rate, maintaining preload, and preventing increases in pulmonary vascular resistance while preparing for potential RV dysfunction.

## 3.2. MITRAL REGURGITATION

**Classification:** Primary (intrinsic valve pathology) vs Secondary (LV dysfunction) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 3.2.1. MECHANISMS OF MR

#### 3.2.1.1. TABLE 39-4: MECHANISMS OF MITRAL REGURGITATION [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

| Cause | Characteristics | Location |
|-------|----------------|-----------|
| Degeneration | Excessive motion due to prolapse or flail, redundant tissue, ruptured chordae | Leaflet |
| Rheumatic | Thickened, calcified, restricted leaflets, commissural fusion | Leaflet |
| Congenital | Cleft mitral valve, double orifice mitral valve | Leaflet |
| Miscellaneous | Drug-related (fenfluramine) | Leaflet |
| Endocarditis | Perforation(s), vegetations | Leaflet |
| Myocardial infarction | Papillary muscle rupture | Tensor apparatus |
| Dilated cardiomyopathy | Annular dilatation | Annulus |
| Ischemic heart disease | Papillary muscle dysfunction | Tensor apparatus |

### 3.2.2. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

![Figure 39-4 Pathophysiology of mitral regurgitation. LA, left atrial; LV, left ventricular.](https://4c37f570e5dd-4080-9df0-ac4ec6c67e0c.ngrok-free.app/img/fig39-4.png)

#### 3.2.2.1. ACUTE VS CHRONIC MR

* **Acute MR:** LA/LV haven't adapted → pulmonary edema, increased LVEDP, decreased stroke volume [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Chronic MR:** LA dilates, LV hypertrophies → initial compensation, eventual LV dysfunction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 3.2.2.2. UNIQUE LOADING CONDITIONS

* **Two LV outflow tracts:** Aortic valve (normal impedance) and mitral valve (low impedance) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **EF may overestimate function** due to low impedance regurgitant pathway [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Post-repair: Unmasked LV dysfunction when only normal impedance pathway remains [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.2.3. TABLE 39-5: MITRAL REGURGITATION HEMODYNAMIC GOALS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

| Parameter | Goal | Comment |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Preload | ↑ Slightly |  |
| Afterload | ↓ | With anesthetics, arterial vasodilators |
| Contractility |  | (May be ↑) titrate myocardial depressants carefully |
| Rate | ↑ Slightly | Avoid bradycardia |
| Rhythm |  | Control ventricular response if atrial fibrillation present |
| M O2 |  | Compromised if MR coexists with ischemic heart disease |
| Post-CPB |  | ↑ Inotropy (afterload is increased with competent valve) |

### 3.2.4. ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS

💡 **Key Management Principles:**
* **Decrease afterload** and **faster heart rate** promote forward flow [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Minimize afterload with anesthetics and vasodilators [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Faster heart rate decreases systolic duration → less time for MR [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Systolic Anterior Motion (SAM) Risk Post-MVr:**
* **Risk factors:** Bileaflet prolapse, posterior leaflet height >15 mm, C-sept distance <25 mm, LVEDD <45 mm [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Can cause LVOT obstruction if anterior leaflet drawn toward septum [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** MR management emphasizes promoting forward flow through afterload reduction and appropriate heart rate control, while anticipating potential systolic dysfunction after valve repair.

## 3.3. TRICUSPID STENOSIS

**Incidence:** Relatively rare (~3% worldwide) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Causes:**
* **Most common:** Rheumatic heart disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Other:** Congenital lesions (Ebstein anomaly), infective endocarditis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Treatment:** Surgical valve replacement or repair [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Hemodynamic Goals:**
* Maintain higher preload and contractility [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Sinus rhythm crucial** - control arrhythmias promptly [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

## 3.4. TRICUSPID REGURGITATION

**Classification:** Primary (10-15%) vs Functional [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 3.4.1. CAUSES

* **Primary:** Ebstein anomaly, rheumatic disease, carcinoid disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Functional:** Annular dilation/leaflet tethering secondary to left heart disease → pulmonary hypertension → RV failure/AF [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.4.2. CONSEQUENCES

* RV volume overload and dysfunction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Hepatic congestion, peripheral edema, ascites [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.4.3. TREATMENT

* **Surgical:** Replacement or repair [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Transcatheter:** Emerging tricuspid valve repair techniques [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Hemodynamic Goals:**
* Maintain preload, sinus rhythm, faster heart rate [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Inotropic agents for failure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]  
⚠️ **Avoid factors increasing PVR:** Hypoxemia, hypercapnia, acidosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Tricuspid valve disease management focuses on optimizing RV function and avoiding increases in pulmonary vascular resistance.

## 3.5. HYPERTROPHIC CARDIOMYOPATHY

**Characteristics:** Autosomal dominant condition with asymmetric LV hypertrophy, typically involving basal interventricular septum [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. Prevalence: 0.16-0.29% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 3.5.1. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

![Figure 39-5 Pathophysiology of primary left ventricular (LV) hypertrophy in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. BP, blood pressure.](https://4c37f570e5dd-4080-9df0-ac4ec6c67e0c.ngrok-free.app/img/fig39-5.png)

#### 3.5.1.1. KEY FEATURES

* **Myocyte hypertrophy and interstitial fibrosis** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Dynamic LVOT obstruction** in ~1/3 of patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Mechanism:** Hypertrophied septum + SAM of anterior mitral leaflet [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 3.5.1.2. LVOT GRADIENT INCREASES WITH

* **Decreased preload** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Decreased afterload** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]  
* **Increased contractility** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 3.5.1.3. OXYGEN SUPPLY-DEMAND IMBALANCE

* **Decreased supply:** Coronary arteries show luminal narrowing [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Increased demand:** LV hypertrophy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Result: Ischemic symptoms without atherosclerotic CAD [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.5.2. TREATMENT OPTIONS

* **Alcohol septal ablation:** Ethanol injection into septal perforator → septal infarction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Surgical septal myectomy:** Goal of resting peak gradient <30 mmHg across LVOT [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.5.3. TABLE 39-6: HYPERTROPHIC CARDIOMYOPATHY HEMODYNAMIC GOALS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

| Parameter | Goal | Comment |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Preload | Maintain or ↑ | Treat as in aortic stenosis |
| Afterload | ↑ | Treat hypotension aggressively with α-adrenergic agonists |
| Contractility | ↓ |  |
| Rate | Maintain | β-Blockers |
| Rhythm | Sinus | Consider atrial pacing modalities |
| M O2 |  | Not a problem |
| Post-CPB |  | Start with volume and vasoconstrictors; avoid inotropes. Check for residual LVOT gradient and SAM |

### 3.5.4. ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS

💡 **Management Principles:**
* **Maintain preload** (adequate filling) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Maintain/increase afterload** (α-adrenergic agonists) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Avoid increasing contractility** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Avoid tachycardia** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Maintain sinus rhythm** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Distinguish from Aortic Stenosis:** Some patients have both conditions - echocardiography shows distinct systolic velocity patterns [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy management requires opposite hemodynamic goals from most cardiac conditions - maintain preload and afterload while avoiding increased contractility.

## 3.6. AORTIC VALVE STENOSIS

**Anatomy:** Normal AV has three symmetrical semilunar cusps (right, left, noncoronary) within aortic annulus [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

![Figure 39-6 Transesophageal echocardiography mid-esophageal short axis of the aortic valve. NCC, aortic valve non-coronary cusp; RCC, aortic valve right coronary cusp; LCC, aortic valve left coronary cusp.](https://4c37f570e5dd-4080-9df0-ac4ec6c67e0c.ngrok-free.app/img/fig39-6.png)

### 3.6.1. CAUSES

* **Most common:** Degenerative process with calcium deposits [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Second most common:** Congenital (bicuspid AV) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Rheumatic heart disease:** Commissural fusion (seen in underdeveloped countries) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.6.2. CLASSIFICATION

#### 3.6.2.1. TABLE 39-7: AORTIC STENOSIS CLASSIFICATIONS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

| Severity | Peak AV Velocity | Transvalvular Pressure Gradient | AV Area |
|----------|------------------|--------------------------------|---------|
| Mild | 2.6-3.0 m/s | <20 mmHg | >1.5 cm² |
| Moderate | 3-4 m/s | 20-40 mmHg | 1.0-1.5 cm² |
| Severe | >4 m/s | >40 mmHg | <1.0 cm² |

💡 **Special Considerations:**
* **Normal AV area:** 2-4 cm² [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **"Pseudo" or "low flow, low gradient" AS:** May occur with LV dysfunction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Dobutamine stress testing:** Helps distinguish true severe AS from pseudo-AS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.6.3. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

Life expectancy after symptom development: 5 years for angina, 3 years for syncope, 2 years for dyspnea [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 3.6.3.1. LV ADAPTATION

* **Concentric hypertrophy** develops to reduce wall stress per LaPlace's law [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Wall Stress = (Pressure × Volume) / Wall Thickness** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

![Figure 39-7 The early and late stages of aortic stenosis pathophysiology.](https://4c37f570e5dd-4080-9df0-ac4ec6c67e0c.ngrok-free.app/img/fig39-7.png)

#### 3.6.3.2. COMPLICATIONS OF LV HYPERTROPHY

* **Increased myocardial oxygen demand** (increased LV mass) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Diastolic dysfunction** (decreased ventricular relaxation) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Increased LVEDP** (decreased compliance) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Coronary perfusion compromise:** CPP = AoDP - LVEDP [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

💡 **Key Physiologic Points:**
* **"Preload dependent" state:** Stroke volume depends on preload [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Fixed afterload:** Determined by stenotic valve [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Atrial kick crucial:** Progressive diastolic dysfunction increases AF risk [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.6.4. TABLE 39-8: AORTIC STENOSIS HEMODYNAMIC GOALS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

| Parameter | Goal | Comment |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Preload | Maintain or ↑ | Preload dependent condition |
| Afterload | Maintain or ↑ | Coronary perfusion gradient |
| Contractility | Normal | Normal in early stage, may decrease in late stages |
| Rate | Normal | Bradycardia decreases cardiac output; Tachycardia increases O2 requirement |
| Rhythm | Sinus | Consider cardioversion or rate control if nonsinus rhythms |
| M O2 | Normal | Increases with disease progression; Avoid tachycardia and treat hypotension |

### 3.6.5. ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS

⚠️ **High-Risk Population:**
* Patients with severe and moderate AS have increased 30-day mortality and MI risk for noncardiac surgery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Minimally invasive procedures reasonable with invasive monitoring ± PAC or TEE [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

💡 **Critical Management Points:**
* **Immediate detection/treatment of hypotension** to preserve coronary perfusion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Avoid heart rate extremes:** Tachycardia ↑ O2 demand; bradycardia ↓ CO [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Heart rate contributes significantly to CO** due to fixed afterload and limited stroke volume reserve [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**TAVR Option:** Transcatheter aortic valve replacement for high-risk surgical patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Aortic stenosis creates a preload-dependent, fixed-afterload state requiring careful maintenance of filling pressures, heart rate, and systemic blood pressure.

## 3.7. AORTIC INSUFFICIENCY

**Classification:** Based on leaflet motion - normal, prolapse, or restriction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 3.7.1. CAUSES

* **Normal leaflet motion:** Annular dilation (aortopathies) or cusp perforation (endocarditis) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Leaflet prolapse:** Bicuspid AV, aortic dissection, trauma, infection [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Leaflet restriction:** Calcification, thrombosis, vegetations, rheumatic disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.7.2. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

![Figure 39-8 The acute and chronic pathophysiology of aortic insufficiency.](https://4c37f570e5dd-4080-9df0-ac4ec6c67e0c.ngrok-free.app/img/fig39-8.png)

#### 3.7.2.1. ACUTE VS CHRONIC AI

**Acute AI:**
* **Minimal adaptation** → decompensated HF [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Coronary perfusion compromise:** ↓ diastolic BP + ↑ LVEDP [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Compensatory mechanisms:** Tachycardia, vasoconstriction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Treatment:** Emergent valve intervention for severe cases [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Chronic AI:**
* **LV remodeling:** Gradual increase in cavity size, eccentric hypertrophy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Increased compliance:** Prevents significant LVEDP changes initially [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **May eject >2× normal stroke volume** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Eventually:** Decompensation when compliance decreases (myocardial fibrosis) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 3.7.3. TABLE 39-9: AORTIC INSUFFICIENCY HEMODYNAMIC GOALS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

| Parameter | Goal | Comment |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Preload | ↑ Slightly |  |
| Afterload | ↓ | Vasodilation (to decrease regurgitant fraction) |
| Contractility |  | Usually adequate |
| Rate | ↑ | Reduces left ventricular volume and increases diastolic aortic pressure |
| Rhythm | Normal |  |
| M O2 | Normal | Increases as aortic insufficiency progresses |

### 3.7.4. ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS

💡 **Management Principles:**
* **Promote cardiac output** and **decrease regurgitant volume** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Prevent increased afterload:** Use vasodilators (nicardipine, nitroprusside) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Above-normal heart rate beneficial:** Reduces diastolic runoff, increases diastolic BP [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Avoid bradycardia:** Causes LV distention and ↑ LAP [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **CPB Challenges:**
* **Ventricular distention** may occur until aortic cross-clamp applied or LV vent placed [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Cardioplegia delivery problem:** Incompetent AV prevents antegrade delivery to coronaries [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Solutions:** Direct coronary ostial injection or retrograde via coronary sinus [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** AI management focuses on promoting forward flow through afterload reduction and increased heart rate while preventing LV distention and optimizing coronary perfusion.

# 4. AORTIC DISEASES
<!-- EPA-10:K6:aortic_surgery EPA-10:K8:aortic_aneurysm EPA-10:K8:aortic_dissection -->

> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Initial medical management of specific conditions|Part B: Initial Medical Management - Acute Aortic Dissection]]

**Aortic pathology represents some of the most time-sensitive and technically challenging conditions in cardiac surgery.**

Pathology of the aorta originates from many different conditions: inflammation, trauma, genetic disorders, wall stress alterations from flow through a bicuspid AV, chronic hypertension, and others [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. Aortopathies develop due to stiffening of aortic tissue secondary to alterations in wall composition - decreased smooth muscle cells and elastin fibers with increasingly erratic arrangement [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

## 4.1. AORTIC DISSECTION

**Definition:** Intimal tear allowing blood to escape from true lumen and create false lumen within aortic media layers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 4.1.1. RISK FACTORS

* **Elderly patients:** Hypertension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Younger patients:** Connective tissue disorders (Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos, Loeys-Dietz syndromes) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.1.2. CLASSIFICATION SYSTEMS

#### 4.1.2.1. DEBAKEY CLASSIFICATION [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* **Type I:** Intimal tear in ascending aorta extending to descending aorta
* **Type II:** Tear in ascending aorta confined to ascending aorta  
* **Type III:** Tear beginning in descending aorta

#### 4.1.2.2. STANFORD CLASSIFICATION [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* **Type A:** Dissections involve ascending aorta
* **Type B:** Dissections do not involve ascending aorta

### 4.1.3. CLINICAL URGENCY

⚠️ **Type A Dissection:**
* **Mortality rate:** 1-2% per hour after onset [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **True surgical emergency** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Type B Dissection:**
* **30-day mortality:** 10% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Management:** Medical unless organ malperfusion or persistent pain [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.1.4. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY AND COMPLICATIONS

* **False lumen expansion** can extend proximally or distally [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Branch vessel compromise** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Free rupture** into pericardium or thoracic cavity [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **AV leaflet detachment** → acute AI [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Cardiac tamponade** if aortic wall ruptures into pericardium [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.1.5. CLINICAL PRESENTATION

* **Pain:** Chest (type A) or back/abdomen (type B) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Pulse deficits** in extremities [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Blood pressure differences** between limbs [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Cerebral/coronary perfusion compromise** possible [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.1.6. DIAGNOSIS

* **Contrast-enhanced spiral CT** or **MRI:** More sensitive/specific than TEE [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **TEE:** Useful but less sensitive than cross-sectional imaging [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.1.7. TREATMENT

**Type A Dissection:**
* **Surgical repair:** Close intimal tear, prevent false lumen expansion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Address complications:** Cerebral/coronary malperfusion, tamponade, AV disruption [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Procedure:** Composite graft ± coronary reimplantation, AV replacement, cerebral vessel anastomosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Stent placement** in proximal descending aorta becoming common [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Type B Dissection:**
* **Medical management** in absence of organ malperfusion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Endovascular stents** if intervention required [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.1.8. ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS

⚠️ **Pre-Induction Assessment:**
* **LV function** assessment [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Pericardial tamponade** evaluation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **AV competency** assessment [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Use **transthoracic echocardiography** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

💡 **Management Priorities:**
* **Sympathetic surge suppression** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Maintain adequate perfusion** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Prevent hypertension and tachycardia** during laryngoscopy/sternotomy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.1.9. HYPOTHERMIC CIRCULATORY ARREST

**Indication:** AD involving aortic arch [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Technique:**
* **Cooling to 18°C:** Allows ~30 minutes of circulatory arrest [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Cerebral protection adjuncts:**
  * **Retrograde cerebral perfusion** via SVC cannula [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
  * **Selective antegrade perfusion** via axillary artery or cerebral vessels [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Benefits:** Flush embolic matter, remove ischemic metabolites [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.1.10. TABLE 39-10: ACUTE AORTIC DISSECTION HEMODYNAMIC GOALS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

| Parameter | Goal | Comment |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Preload | Normal | If tamponade, ↑ preload |
| Afterload | ↓ | With anesthetics, analgesics, vasodilators; keep systolic BP <100-120 mmHg |
| Contractility | ↓ | β-blockers unless ventricular dysfunction |
| Rate | ↓ to <60-80 bpm | Use β-blocker; ensure contractility is adequate |
| Rhythm |  | Control ventricular response if atrial fibrillation present |
| M O2 | Normal | Compromised if dissection involves coronary vessels |

**Bottom Line:** Aortic dissection is a surgical emergency requiring immediate control of blood pressure and heart rate while preparing for complex surgical repair with potential hypothermic circulatory arrest.

## 4.2. AORTIC ANEURYSM

**Definition:** Aortic segment >4 cm in maximal diameter in average size patient [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 4.2.1. CLASSIFICATION

* **Morphology:** Saccular, fusiform, or diffuse [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Location:** Aortic root, ascending aorta, arch, descending aorta [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.2.2. CLINICAL PRESENTATION

* **Usually asymptomatic** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Large aneurysms:** May compress airway or recurrent laryngeal nerve [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Ascending aorta dilation:** May cause AI [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.2.3. DIAGNOSIS

* **Gold standard:** Contrast-enhanced CT and MRA [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.2.4. SURGICAL INDICATIONS

* **Ascending aortic aneurysms >5.5 cm** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Risk for rupture increases abruptly at 6 cm** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.2.5. SURGICAL OPTIONS

**Isolated Ascending Aorta:**
* **Simple supracoronary composite graft** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**With AI but Normal AV:**
* **Valve-sparing procedure (David procedure)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Dilated Root or Pathologic AV:**
* **Composite aortic repair (Bentall procedure):** Composite graft with mechanical or biologic prosthetic AV [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Descending Thoracic Aorta:**
* **Endovascular stent grafts** most common [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **Paraplegia risk:** 13-17% due to lumbar artery interruption [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 4.2.6. ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS

**Management priorities similar to aortic dissection (Table 39-10):**
* **Decrease strain on aorta** by reducing contractility and MAP [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Hypothermic circulatory arrest** if arch involvement [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Endovascular Repair:**
* **CSF drainage** to improve spinal cord perfusion pressure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Maintain CSF pressure <10 mmHg** during and after stent deployment [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Aortic aneurysm management focuses on reducing aortic wall stress while preparing for complex surgical interventions that may require specialized techniques like hypothermic circulatory arrest.

# 5. HEART FAILURE
<!-- EPA-10:K8:heart_failure EPA-10:K8:lcos -->

> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Circulatory Assist|Part B: Circulatory Assist]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Initial medical management of specific conditions|Initial Medical Management - Cardiogenic Shock]]

**Heart failure represents a growing clinical challenge with significant implications for anesthetic management.**

By 2030, 1 in 33 Americans will suffer from HF, 9 million patients will seek care related to HF and the cost will increase to an estimated 70 billion dollars [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. The increasing prevalence has been attributed to aging population and prolonged survival in patients with cardiovascular disorders [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

## 5.1. DEFINITION, CLASSIFICATION, AND STAGING OF HEART FAILURE

**Definition:** Progressive clinical syndrome characterized by reduced cardiac performance, related to impaired ventricular filling and/or systolic blood ejection [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 5.1.1. DIAGNOSIS

#### 5.1.1.1. TABLE 39-11: DIAGNOSIS OF HEART FAILURE [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

| Assessment Modality | Comments |
|-------------------|----------|
| History and Physical Examination | **Symptoms:** Dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea or PND, abdominal swelling, fatigue, recent weight gain<br>**Signs:** Audible S3, lung rales, pedal edema/ascites, raised JVP, +ve hepatojugular reflex |
| Modified Framingham Clinical Criteria | **Major:** PND, orthopnea, elevated JVP, S3, cardiomegaly on CXR, pulmonary edema on CXR, weight loss ≥4.5 kg in 5 days<br>**Minor:** Bilateral leg edema, dyspnea on exertion, nocturnal cough, hepatomegaly, pleural effusion, tachycardia >120 bpm<br>**Diagnosis requires:** 2 major or 1 major + 2 minor criteria |
| Laboratory Evaluation | BNP, liver and renal function tests, electrolytes, TSH, CBC, urinalysis, HbA1c, lipid profile |
| Other Modalities | Echocardiography, CXR, stress testing, left and right heart catheterization |

### 5.1.2. CLASSIFICATION BY EF

* **HFrEF:** EF ≤40% (Heart Failure with reduced EF) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **HFpEF:** EF ≥50% (Heart Failure with preserved EF) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **HFimEF:** Previously EF ≤40%, improved to >40% (Heart Failure with improved EF) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **HFmrEF:** EF 41-49% (Heart Failure with mildly reduced EF) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 5.1.3. STAGING SYSTEM

#### 5.1.3.1. TABLE 39-12: STAGING OF HEART FAILURE [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

| Stage | Definition | Examples |
|-------|------------|----------|
| A | At high risk for HF. No evidence of structural heart disease or HF symptoms | Patients with hypertension or diabetes mellitus |
| B | Signs or symptoms of HF absent (pre-HF, asymptomatic). Evidence of structural heart disease present | Asymptomatic valvular disease such as AS with LVH |
| C | Patient reports prior or current HF symptoms (Symptomatic). Evidence of structural heart disease present | Symptomatic valvular disease. Patient with degenerative MR with dyspnea, fatigue, or chest pain during exercise |
| D | Refractory, advanced or end-stage HF necessitating specialized interventions | NYHA Class IV patient with recurrent hospitalizations for HF decompensation |

💡 **Key Differences:**
* **NYHA Classification:** Can revert to lower functional class with treatment
* **Staging System:** Patients cannot revert to previous stage (progressive disease model)

### 5.1.4. TEMPORAL CLASSIFICATION

* **Chronic HF:** Symptoms for months to years [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Stable HF:** Unchanged symptoms on optimal GDMT for ≥1 month [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **New-onset/De novo HF:** Previously asymptomatic patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Acute HF syndrome:** Decompensated HF requiring urgent therapy/hospitalization [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Advanced/Refractory HF:** NYHA III-IV despite optimal GDMT with recurrent episodes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 5.1.5. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

#### 5.1.5.1. HFREF

* **Dysregulated neurohormonal response** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Activation:** Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone and sympathetic nervous systems [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Results:** Sodium/fluid retention, ventricular remodeling, vasoconstriction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 5.1.5.2. HFPEF  

* **Microvascular endothelial inflammation and dysfunction** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Results:** Fibrosis and ventricular hypertrophy → ↑ LVEDP, pulmonary hypertension, AF [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Heart failure classification encompasses etiology, ejection fraction, staging, and temporal factors, requiring comprehensive assessment for optimal management.

## 5.2. CARDIOGENIC SHOCK

**Definition:** Characterized by persistent hypotension, hypoperfusion, and low cardiac index [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Leading Causes:** Acute coronary syndromes and progressive HF [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 5.2.1. TABLE 39-13: DEFINITION OF CARDIOGENIC SHOCK [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

| Criteria | Definition |
|----------|------------|
| **Hemodynamic** | • SBP <90 mmHg or MAP <60 mmHg for ≥30 min or catecholamines to maintain SBP >90 mmHg or MAP >60 mmHg<br>• Other parameters: CI <2.2 L/min/m², PCWP >15 mmHg, CPO <0.6 watts, Shock index (HR/SBP) >1, RV shock (PAPI <1, CVP/PCWP >0.6) |
| **Hypoperfusion** | At least one: Altered mental status, cold/clammy skin, urine output <30 mL/h, lactate >2.0 mmol/L |

### 5.2.2. PROGRESSION STAGES

#### 5.2.2.1. TABLE 39-14: STAGES OF CARDIOGENIC SHOCK PROGRESSION [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

| Stage | Features | Biochemical/Hemodynamic Parameters |
|-------|----------|-----------------------------------|
| **A: At Risk** | At risk but no current signs/symptoms | Normal lactate, normotensive (SBP ≥100), CI ≥2.5, CVP <10, M O2 sat ≥65% |
| **B: Beginning** | Relative hypotension or tachycardia without hypoperfusion | Normal lactate, elevated BNP, SBP <90 OR MAP <60, PR ≥100, CI ≥2.2, M O2 sat ≥65% |
| **C: Classical** | Hypotension and hypoperfusion. May require inotropes/vasopressors and MCS (excluding ECMO) | Lactate ≥2, doubling of serum Cr, increased LFTs, elevated BNP, SBP <90 OR MAP <60 with drugs needed, CI <2.2, PCWP >15, RAP/PCWP ≥0.8 |
| **D: Doom/Deteriorating** | Refractory to therapies, deteriorating rapidly. Consider ECMO | Stage C and deteriorating. Multiple vasopressor/inotropes OR addition of MCS device needed |
| **E: Extremis** | Cardiac arrest with ongoing CPR and/or ECMO CPR | No SBP/MAP in absence of ongoing resuscitation, PEA or refractory VT/VF, persistent hypotension despite maximal support |

**Bottom Line:** Cardiogenic shock represents a spectrum from at-risk to extremis states, requiring staged interventions and potential mechanical circulatory support.

## 5.3. MECHANICAL CIRCULATORY SUPPORT
<!-- EPA-10:K4:mcs EPA-10:K4:ecmo EPA-10:K4:iabp -->

💡 **Key Advantage over Vasoactive Medications:**
* **Vasoactive drugs:** Increase M O2, worsen ischemia, increase myocardial work, risk malignant arrhythmias [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **MCS:** Reduces LV stroke work and M O2 while providing adequate perfusion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 5.3.1. MCS DEVICE CATEGORIES

**By Implantation Method:**
* Percutaneous vs Surgical

**By Duration:**
* **Short term:** Days to weeks (bridge to recovery)
* **Durable:** Months to years (bridge to transplant, destination therapy)

**By Ventricle Supported:**
* Left, right, or biventricular support

### 5.3.2. SHORT-TERM MCS DEVICES

#### 5.3.2.1. LV ASSISTANCE

* **IABP** (Intra-aortic balloon pump)
* **Impella** (2.5, CP, 5, 5.5)  
* **Tandem Heart** (TH)

#### 5.3.2.2. RV ASSISTANCE  

* **Impella RP**
* **TH Right Atrium-Pulmonary Artery**
* **pRVAD** (percutaneous right ventricular assist device)

#### 5.3.2.3. BIVENTRICULAR ASSISTANCE

* **VA ECMO** (venous-arterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation)
* **Combinations:** LV-RV Impella devices, LV Impella + TH RA-PA

### 5.3.3. INTRA-AORTIC BALLOON PUMP (IABP)

**Design:** 25-cm long balloon on 90-cm vascular catheter, helium gas inflation/deflation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

![Figure 39-9 IABP deflates at commencement of systole (left) and inflates at beginning of diastole (right).](https://4c37f570e5dd-4080-9df0-ac4ec6c67e0c.ngrok-free.app/img/fig39-9.png)

**Mechanism - Synchronized Counter-pulsation:**
* **Inflation:** During diastole (T wave-P wave interval) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Deflation:** During systole (R wave-T wave interval) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

💡 **Effects:**
* **Diastolic augmentation:** Elevates aortic diastolic BP → ↑ coronary perfusion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Afterload reduction:** Presystolic deflation lowers systemic diastolic pressure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 5.3.3.1. IABP DETAILED SPECIFICATIONS

**Key Components:**
* Console
* Catheter (8-9.5Fr) and inflatable balloon (25-50ml)

**Indications in Cardiac Surgery:**

**Preoperative:**
* Severe LV systolic dysfunction
* Ongoing angina/arrhythmias
* Critical left main stenosis

**Postoperative:**
* Cardiogenic Shock (LCOS)

**Contraindications:**
* Significant atherosclerosis of aorta/iliac/femoral artery
* Aortic incompetence
* Aortic aneurysm
* Aortic dissection

**Insertion:**
* In most cases, IABP is placed through the common femoral artery
* Alternate access sites: subclavian or axillary artery access
* Balloon pumps manufactured in multiple sizes from 20cc to 50cc
* Appropriate size chosen based on patient's height

**Physiological Effects:**
* Balloon inflation in diastole increases aortic diastolic pressure → increases coronary perfusion pressure
* Balloon deflation in early systole decreases afterload, reduces myocardial work and causes modest increase in cardiac output

#### 5.3.3.2. IABP TRIGGERING

**Inflation and deflation can be triggered by:**
* ECG waveform or arterial pressure tracing
* **Inflation:** Mid point of T wave
* **Deflation:** Peak of R wave
* Internal trigger: used when there is no cardiac output
* Atrial fibrillation trigger
* Pacer trigger

**Complications:**
* Aortic dissection
* Hematoma
* Bleeding
* Bowel Ischaemia
* Stroke/Embolism
* Ischaemic limb
* Renal impairment
* Thrombosis

⚠️ **Limitations:** 
* Use declining as other devices provide more robust cardiac output augmentation and greater LVEDP reduction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 5.3.4. LV IMPELLA

**Design:** Continuous, nonpulsatile, axial flow Archimedes screw pump [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

![Figure 39-10 Impella device decompresses the left ventricle by withdrawing blood from the cavity and ejecting it into the ascending aorta.](https://4c37f570e5dd-4080-9df0-ac4ec6c67e0c.ngrok-free.app/img/fig39-10.png)

💡 **Advantages:**
* **Immediate LV unloading** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Decreased LVEDP and wall tension** → ↓ M O2 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Increased MAP and coronary perfusion** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Independent of cardiac rhythm** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Greater flow augmentation than IABP** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Mechanism:** Aspirates LV blood and expels into aorta, providing near ideal conditions for LV recovery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 5.3.5. VA ECMO

**Configuration:** Venous cannula (femoral/internal jugular) → centrifugal pump → membrane oxygenator → arterial return (femoral/axillary) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

![Figure 39-11 In VA ECMO, deoxygenated blood is removed from the inferior vena cava, oxygenated and then propelled into the aorta with the help of a centrifugal pump.](https://4c37f570e5dd-4080-9df0-ac4ec6c67e0c.ngrok-free.app/img/fig39-11.png)

⚠️ **Important Differences from Impella:**
* **Nonphysiologic retrograde perfusion** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Can increase total peripheral resistance** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **May increase LV wall tension** → paradoxical ↑ LVEDP [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Can worsen subendocardial ischemia** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Risk of intracavitary thrombus** from LV blood pooling [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

💡 **LV Venting Solutions:**
* Surgical or percutaneous vent placement
* IABP or Impella devices used with VA ECMO [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**ECPR (Extracorporeal CPR):** VA ECMO established within 20 minutes of cardiac arrest [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 5.3.6. COMPLICATIONS OF MCS DEVICES

* **Hemolysis** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Bleeding** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Infection** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Thrombocytopenia** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Distal limb ischemia** (femoral/axillary access) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 5.3.7. LONG-TERM LVADS

**Current devices:** Provide nonpulsatile continuous flow via centrifugal (HeartWare, Heartmate III) or axial (Heartmate II) pumps [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Indications:**
* **Bridge to transplantation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Destination therapy** (not transplant candidates) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 5.3.7.1. HEMODYNAMIC CONSIDERATIONS

* **Nonpulsatile flow:** Attenuates systolic/diastolic pressure components [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Monitor MAP** for end-organ perfusion adequacy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Optimal MAP range:** 70-90 mmHg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Flow governed by:** Preload (LV pressure) and afterload (MAP) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **"Suction Events":**
* Occur when LV filling inadequate (poor RV function) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Result:** Suctioning of interventricular septum at inflow connection [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Effects:** Ventricular irritability, compromised RV ejection [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

💡 **RV Support Strategies:**
* **Inotropic agents:** Epinephrine, dobutamine, milrinone [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Inhaled agents:** Nitric oxide, iloprost [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Goal:** Maintain low PVR and optimize RV function [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 5.3.8. TABLE 39-15: DURABLE LVAD PATIENTS FOR NONCARDIAC SURGERY [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Pre-operative Planning:**
* Communicate with Heart Failure/LVAD team
* Assess type of LVAD, baseline parameters, anticoagulation status
* Consider invasive monitoring for long/complex procedures

**Hemodynamic Goals:**
* MAP 70-90 mmHg range, optimal RV and LV filling
* Monitor adequacy with cerebral oximetry or lactate/SvO2

**Intraoperative Considerations:**
* Minimize PVR: Avoid hypoxemia, hypercarbia, high intrathoracic pressures
* Support RV function with inotropes and inhaled agents
* Reduced anesthetic drug doses often required

**Bleeding Management:**
* TEG/ROTEM guided transfusion
* Bleeding related to anticoagulation and device-induced hemolysis/thrombocytopenia

**Emergency Situations:**
* Rule out: Volume issues, suction events, RV failure, device malfunction, tamponade, sepsis, ARDS, pneumothorax, bleeding

**Bottom Line:** Mechanical circulatory support devices provide superior hemodynamic support compared to vasoactive medications by reducing myocardial work while maintaining perfusion, but require specialized management considerations.

# 6. CARDIOPULMONARY BYPASS
<!-- EPA-10:K4:cpb EPA-10:K4:extracorporeal_circuit -->

> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Cardiopulmonary bypass techniques|Part B: Cardiopulmonary Bypass Techniques]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Haematological issues|Haematological Issues]]

**Cardiopulmonary bypass represents one of the most complex technological systems in medicine, requiring comprehensive understanding of its components, physiology, and management.**

CPB circuitry replaces heart and lung function during cardiac surgery to: provide bloodless and motionless surgical field, oxygenate and ventilate, maintain organ perfusion and oxygen delivery, manage temperature, and provide anesthesia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

* **Venous drainage cannulae** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* **Mechanical pump** to propel blood [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* **Arterial return cannula** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 6.1.2. ADDITIONAL COMPONENTS

* **Cardioplegia delivery system** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Venting cannulae** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* **Volatile agent vaporizers** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 6.1.3. CPB CIRCUIT DESIGN AND BIOCOMPATIBILITY

**Circuit Materials:**
* **Tubing:** Polyvinylchloride tubing and polycarbonate connectors
* **Biocompatible coatings:** Improve biocompatibility, reduce complement, leukocyte, and platelet activation, lessen release of proinflammatory mediators
* **Heparin-coated circuits:** Allow safe use of lower heparin doses, potentially associated with less bleeding

**Circuit Activation:**
* **Potent coagulation activator:** Generates factor Xa and thrombin, contributes to systemic inflammatory response and ischemia/reperfusion injury
* **Anticoagulation essential:** Minimize coagulation activation, thrombus formation, and fibrinolysis

### 6.1.4. VENOUS CANNULAE AND CANNULATION STRATEGIES

**Cannulation Sites:** SVC, IVC, RA, coronary sinus [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Cannula Types:**
* **Dual/multistage cannula:** Multiple apertures in single cannula [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
  * **Used for:** CABG, AV, aortic surgery (RA not opened) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
  * **Risk:** Kinking during heart manipulation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Bicaval cannulation:** Individual single-stage cannulae [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
  * **Required for:** Mitral and tricuspid valve surgery (RA opened) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.1.4.1. VENOUS CANNULA DESIGN SPECIFICS

**A:** Tapered, "two-stage" RA–IVC cannula
**B:** Straight, wire-wound "lighthouse" tipped cannula for RA or separate cannulation of SVC or IVC
**C:** Right-angled, metal-tipped cannula for cannulation of SVC or IVC

**Alternative Approaches:**
* **Femoral vein cannulation:** For MICS or redo surgery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **TEE guidance:** For multistage cannula advancement into SVC [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Complications:** Bleeding, arrhythmias, malposition → venous congestion/inadequate drainage [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 6.1.5. VENOUS RESERVOIR

**Function:** Collect blood drained via gravity [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].
* **Position:** 40-70 cm below heart level [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Capacity:** 4-5 L [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Features:** Equipped with low-volume alarms [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Enhancement:** Vacuum may augment gravity drainage [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Critical Safety Considerations:**
* **Receives blood** from patient via one or two venous cannulas in RA or SVC + IVC
* **Gravity drainage:** Blood flows to reservoir by gravity
* **Height differential:** Driving force proportional to difference in height between patient and reservoir
* **Siphon effect:** Priming machine creates siphon effect
* **Air lock prevention:** Entrainment of air can produce air lock preventing blood flow
* **Critical fluid level:** If reservoir empties, air can enter main pump → fatal air embolism

### 6.1.6. MECHANICAL PUMPS

#### 6.1.6.1. ROLLER PUMPS (LEGACY)

* **Mechanism:** Rotating arms with rollers compress tubing [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Disadvantages:** Blood shearing, plastic emboli, cavitation, disconnection risk [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Current use:** Limited to pump suckers/venting rather than systemic flow [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.1.6.2. CENTRIFUGAL PUMPS (CURRENT STANDARD)

* **Mechanism:** Impeller creates pressure drop → centrifugal blood propulsion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Advantages:** Less blood trauma, lower rupture risk, lower air emboli risk, no tubing wear [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Limitation:** Flow decreases with increased afterload → requires flow meter [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Design Features:**
* **Centrifugal pump:** Creates vortex within polycarbonate structure
* **Flow rate:** Depends on cone's revolutions per minute
* **Roller pump:** Two rollers compress tubing and move blood forward

### 6.1.7. OXYGENATORS

#### 6.1.7.1. EVOLUTION

* **1980s:** Bubble oxygenators (gas bubbled through blood) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Current:** Membrane oxygenators [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.1.7.2. MEMBRANE OXYGENATORS

* **Design:** Hollow-fiber capillaries of microporous polypropylene [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Function:** Blood flows externally, gas flows through hollow bundles [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Gas exchange:** Simple diffusion across thin membrane (like native lungs) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Advanced Design Features:**
* **Low resistance capillary fibers:** Made of poly-methyl-pentene (PMP)
* **Gas permeability:** Allows gas permeability and facilitates intraluminal gas transfer
* **Concurrent blood flow:** Around fiber bundle for optimal exchange

**Gas Control:**
* **PaO2:** Vary FiO2 [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **PaCO2:** Vary total gas flow ("sweep rate") [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Volatile anesthetics:** Move freely across membrane into blood [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Gas Delivery System:**
* **Mixer/gas blender:** Allows FiO2 control
* **Oxygen/air mixture:** Regulates partial pressure of oxygen
* **Fresh gas flow:** Determines partial pressure of carbon dioxide
* **CO2 removal:** Reduced fresh gas flow → less CO2 washout → increased PCO2

### 6.1.8. HEAT EXCHANGERS

**Function:** Counter-current devices circulating heated or cooled water around oxygenator fibers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].
* **Precise temperature control** during cooling and rewarming phases [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Design Integration:**
* **Integrated unit:** Heat exchanger integrated into oxygenator unit
* **External heater-cooler:** Pumps temperature-controlled water through heat exchanger
* **Separation barrier:** Blood and water separated by polypropylene or stainless-steel surface
* **Heat transfer efficiency:** Facilitates efficient heat transfer between blood and water
* **Temperature precision:** Water temperature precisely controlled → careful patient temperature regulation

### 6.1.9. ARTERIAL CANNULAE AND CANNULATION STRATEGIES

#### 6.1.9.1. CENTRAL APPROACH (STANDARD)

* **Site:** Ascending aorta [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Assessment:** Palpation for plaques, epiaortic ultrasound [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.1.9.2. ARTERIAL CANNULA DESIGN

**A:** Tapered, bevel-tipped cannula with molded flange near tip
**B:** Angled, thin-walled, metal-tipped cannula with molded flange for securing to aorta
**C:** Angled, diffusion-tipped cannula designed to direct systemic flow in four directions to avoid "jetting effect"
**D:** Integral cannula/tubing connector and luer port for deairing

#### 6.1.9.3. PERIPHERAL APPROACH (ALTERNATIVE)

**Sites:** Femoral, subclavian, axillary, innominate arteries [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**Indications:**
* Ascending aorta plaques/atheroma [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* RV or aorta abutting sternum (redo surgery) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* MICS procedures [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Emergency CPB establishment [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Complications:**
* **Plaque dislodgement** with embolization [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Aortic dissection** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Malpositioning** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Aortic Dissection Signs:**
* Low systemic arterial pressure
* High arterial line pressure (>300 mmHg)
* Loss of venous drainage
* Bluish aortic discoloration
* TEE confirmation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 6.1.10. ARTERIAL LINE FILTER AND PRESSURE MONITOR

**Function:** Trap gaseous and non-gaseous emboli (tissue debris, thrombi, fat particles) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].
* **Monitoring:** Arterial line pressure proximal to filter (detect occlusion) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Filter specifications:** Usually 40μM screen filters inserted in arterial line or integrated with oxygenator/heat exchanger

### 6.1.11. ADDITIONAL MONITORING COMPONENTS

**Continuous In-line Monitoring:**
* **Technology:** Spectrophotometric optical fluorescence and reflectance-based systems
* **Parameters:** Up to 11 critical blood gas parameters with lab-quality accuracy
* **Real-time results:** Near real-time display, temperature corrected
* **Sensor placement:** Around blood-filled tubing of CPB circuit
* **Measurements:** pH, PCO2, PO2, potassium, oxygen saturation, hematocrit, hemoglobin, temperature

**Bubble Detector:**
* **Function:** Alarms when bubbles sensed in cardioplegia line or arterial line

**Low Level Alarm:**
* **Function:** Senses when venous reservoir volume reaches minimum running levels

### 6.1.12. PUMP PRIME

**Purpose:** Fill and de-air CPB circuit [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Components:**
* **Crystalloid base** with additions [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Albumin:** Decrease fluid extravasation/edema [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Mannitol:** Diuretic effect [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Electrolytes:** Calcium (prevent hypocalcemia), magnesium (antiarrhythmic) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Heparin:** Anticoagulation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Priming Solutions:**
* **Base solution:** Balanced electrolyte solution (Lactated Ringer's or Plasmalyte)
* **Average priming volume:** ~1000-1500 mL
* **Colloid additions:** Albumin may be added
* **Blood priming:** Used if patient's hematocrit is low

**Volume Considerations:**
* **Average adult prime:** ~1.5 L based on BSA [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Benefits of hemodilution:** Decreases viscosity, counteracts hypothermia effects [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Target hematocrit on CPB:** 21-24% in adults [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.1.12.1. PRIME VOLUME REDUCTION TECHNIQUES

**Retrograde Autologous Priming (RAP):**
* Patient's blood displaces crystalloid priming solution [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* External reservoir excluded from circuit [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Substantially decreases priming volume [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Mini Extracorporeal Circuits:**
* Shorter tubing (80-150 cm vs standard) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Reduced prime volume (0.5-0.8 L) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Decreased surface area and blood usage [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** CPB components work together as an integrated system to replace cardiopulmonary function, requiring detailed understanding of each element's function and potential complications.

## 6.2. ANTICOAGULATION
<!-- EPA-10:K3:coagulation_profile EPA-10:EQ2:heparin_protamine -->

**Rationale:** Contact between patient's blood and CPB components activates coagulation cascade → widespread thrombosis without anticoagulation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 6.2.1. UNFRACTIONATED HEPARIN

**Mechanism:** Indirect anticoagulant binding to antithrombin III (AT) via specific pentasaccharide sequence [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. Heparin binding results in 1,000-fold stronger AT inhibition of thrombin (IIa) and factor Xa [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 6.2.1.1. DETAILED MECHANISM OF ACTION

**Normal Circumstances:**
* Antithrombin III (ATIII) inactivates thrombin (factor IIa) and factor Xa
* This process occurs at a slow rate

**With Heparin Administration:**
* Heparin binds reversibly to ATIII → almost instantaneous inactivation of factors IIa and Xa
* Heparin-ATIII complex also inactivates factors IX, XI, XII and plasmin
* Mechanism is ATIII-dependent
* Acts mainly by accelerating neutralization of activated coagulation factors by antithrombin
* Antithrombotic effect well correlated to inhibition of factor Xa

**Pharmacokinetics:**
* **Dose:** 300-500 units/kg IV [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Onset:** <5 minutes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Half-life:** ~90 minutes (normothermic), increases with hypothermia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

💡 **Important Properties:**
* **Glycosaminoglycan (GAG) family** - MW 3000-30000 Daltons
* **Dose range:** 300-400iu/kg for CPB
* **Off pump CABG:** Dose varies from full anticoagulation to minimal dose
* **Administration:** IV via central line or peripheral line with adequate flushing
* **Not thrombolytic:** Prevents progression of existing clots by inhibiting further clotting - lysis relies on endogenous thrombolytics

### 6.2.2. MONITORING

#### 6.2.2.1. ACTIVATED CLOTTING TIME (ACT)

* **Method:** Whole blood + contact phase activators (celite or kaolin) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Normal values:** 80-160 seconds [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Target for CPB:** >400-480 seconds [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Procedure:**
* **Baseline ACT:** Checked after IA line insertion or after induction
* **Fresh blood:** Added to tube containing surface activator high in silicate (celite or kaolin)
* **Contact activation:** Results in activation of contact pathway of coagulation
* **Detection:** Conversion of fibrinogen to fibrin by thrombin detected mechanically or through electrogenic assay

**Factors Affecting ACT:**
* **Prolong:** Hypothermia, hemodilution, severe thrombocytopenia, hypo/dysfibrinogenemia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Resistance:** AT deficiency (acquired or congenital) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.2.2.2. HEPARIN CONCENTRATION MONITORING

* **Safe levels:** 3-3.5 IU/mL [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Method:** Automated protamine titration assay [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 6.2.3. HEPARIN RESISTANCE
<!-- EPA-10:EQ2:heparin_resistance -->

**Definition:** Inability to achieve desired ACT or decreased slope on Heparin Dose-Response (HDR) curve after adequate heparin dosage

**Common Definition:** Inability to achieve ACT >480s despite administration of >500iu/kg unfractionated heparin

**Incidence:** 4% to 26% of patients

#### 6.2.3.1. MECHANISMS OF HEPARIN RESISTANCE

**Congenital ATIII Deficiency:**
* **Reduced AT levels**
* **Reduced synthesis/stability:** Secondary to gene mutations
* **Functionally defective AT:** Mutations leading to reduced activity

**Acquired ATIII Deficiency:**
* **Decreased synthesis:** Liver disease, malnutrition
* **Increased clearance:** Nephrotic syndrome
* **Increased consumption:** Heparin therapy
* **Upregulated hemostatic system:** Sepsis, infective endocarditis, DIC, DVT, PE, ECMO, IABP

**Non-ATIII Mediated:**
* **Increased heparin binding:** To other proteins, cells, non-endothelial surfaces
* **High platelet count:** >300,000 cells/mm³ (PF4 activation - strong heparin inhibitor)
* **Low albumin:** <35 g/dL (albumin exhibits heparin-like action)
* **Preoperative hypovolemia:** Dehydration → increased concentration of heparin-binding molecules

⚠️ **Antithrombin Deficiency:**
* **Problem:** Failure to achieve target ACT despite large heparin doses (>600 units/kg) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Treatment:** AT concentrate administration (up to 1,000 units) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.2.3.2. MANAGEMENT OF HEPARIN RESISTANCE

* **Additional heparin doses** or alternative anticoagulants
* **ATIII supplementation** via ATIII concentrate (first line)
* **Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)** administration

### 6.2.4. HEPARIN-INDUCED THROMBOCYTOPENIA (HIT)
<!-- EPA-10:EQ2:hit -->

**HIT-I:**
* Mild, transient platelet count decrease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Within first few days of exposure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**HIT-II:**
* **Severe autoimmune-mediated** platelet decrease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Mechanism:** PF4/heparin antibodies activate platelets [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Risk:** Clinically devastating thrombosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Management:** Wait until anti-PF4 antibodies undetectable before elective surgery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Alternative Anticoagulants for HIT:**
* **Bivalirudin and argatroban:** Direct thrombin inhibitors [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Use:** Active HIT requiring urgent/emergent cardiac surgery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Anticoagulation with unfractionated heparin is essential for CPB, requiring careful monitoring and management of potential complications including AT deficiency and HIT.

## 6.3. BLOOD CONSERVATION IN CARDIAC SURGERY

**Rationale:** Blood products are finite, expensive, and carry risks (infection, incompatibility, TRALI, TACO) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 6.3.1. PREOPERATIVE STRATEGIES

#### 6.3.1.1. RISK IDENTIFICATION

**High-risk factors:** Advanced age, preoperative anemia, abnormal coagulation status [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 6.3.1.2. ANEMIA MANAGEMENT

* **Iron deficiency:** Supplemental iron therapy days to weeks before surgery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Non-iron deficiency:** Erythropoietin-stimulating agents ± iron supplementation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.3.1.3. MEDICATION MANAGEMENT

* **Dual antiplatelet therapy, warfarin, NOACs:** Clear timing instructions for discontinuation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Laboratory/POC testing:** Determine residual anticoagulant effects [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 6.3.2. INTRAOPERATIVE STRATEGIES

#### 6.3.2.1. ANTIFIBRINOLYTIC AGENTS

* **Agents:** Epsilon-aminocaproic acid (EACA), tranexamic acid [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Mechanism:** Inhibit fibrinolysis by binding plasminogen [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Effect:** Decreased post-CPB bleeding and transfusion needs [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.3.2.2. ACUTE NORMOVOLEMIC HEMODILUTION (ANH)

* **Technique:** Remove 1-2 units whole blood before heparinization [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Rationale:** Exclude patient's blood from CPB effects [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Benefit:** Return active platelets and clotting factors post-CPB [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **ANH Contraindications:**
* Preoperative anemia
* High-grade left main CAD
* Severe aortic stenosis
* Hemodynamically unstable patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.3.2.3. RETROGRADE AUTOLOGOUS PRIMING (RAP)

* **Benefits:** Decreases CPB hemodilution, reduces extravascular lung water [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Combination with ANH:** Helps preserve adequate hematocrit [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Management:** May require vasoconstrictors or Trendelenburg positioning [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.3.2.4. MINIMALLY INVASIVE EXTRACORPOREAL CIRCULATION (MIECC)

* **Features:** Shorter tubing, closed CPB circuits [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Benefits:** Reduced priming volume, decreased blood transfusions [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.3.2.5. TRANSFUSION GUIDELINES

* **RBC transfusion threshold:** ≤7.5 g/dL [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Viscoelastic testing:** TEG/ROTEM to guide blood component therapy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 6.3.3. CELL SALVAGE

**Process:** Shed mediastinal blood processed to achieve hematocrits up to 70% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**Limitation:** Platelets and coagulation factors removed during washing [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Contraindications:**
* Admixture with topical hemostatic agents
* Sterile water or toxic solutions (alcohol, hydrogen peroxide, povidone-iodine) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Relative:** Infection, malignancy (case-by-case basis) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 6.3.4. MODIFIED ULTRAFILTRATION

**Mechanism:** Semipermeable membrane separates plasma water from cellular components using hydrostatic pressure differential [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Benefits:**
* **Hemoconcentration** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Reduced postoperative bleeding** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Decreased circulating inflammatory mediators** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Blood conservation requires a multimodal approach including preoperative optimization, intraoperative techniques, and judicious transfusion practices guided by objective measures.

## 6.4. MYOCARDIAL PROTECTION
<!-- EPA-10:K5:cardioprotection -->

**Goal:** Minimize myocardial metabolic function during CPB to prevent ischemic damage [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 6.4.1. THREE-COMPONENT STRATEGY

1. **Hypothermia** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
2. **Cardioplegia induction** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
3. **LV decompression** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 6.4.2. HYPOTHERMIA

**Metabolic Effect:** ~8% decrease in cellular metabolism per °C reduction below 37°C [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. At 28°C, metabolic rate falls to 50% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Categories:**
* **Mild hypothermia:** >28°C [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Moderate hypothermia:** 20-28°C [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Deep hypothermia:** <20°C (for aortic arch, pulmonary endarterectomy) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Benefits:** Myocardial and neurologic protection through reduced O2 consumption, preserved high-energy phosphates, inhibited excitatory neurotransmitter release [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 6.4.3. CARDIOPLEGIA

**Composition:** Cold (10-15°C), hyperkalemic (8-20 mEq/L) solutions [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**Types:** Crystalloid or blood-crystalloid solutions [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**Effect:** Supranormal K+ concentration precipitates diastolic cardiac arrest [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 6.4.3.1. CARDIOPLEGIA CLASSIFICATION

**Based on Ion Composition:**
* **Extracellular solutions:** Reflect extracellular environment
* **Intracellular solutions:** Reflect intracellular environment

#### 6.4.3.2. COMMON CARDIOPLEGIA SOLUTIONS

**Del Nido Cardioplegia:**
* **Initially developed:** For infants and pediatric surgery
* **Base solution:** Plasmalyte A (similar electrolyte composition to ECF)
* **Ratio:** Crystalloid to Blood ratio of 4:1
* **Additives:**
  * Mannitol 20%: 16.3 mL
  * Magnesium sulphate 50%: 4 mL
  * Sodium bicarbonate 8.4%: 13 mL
  * Potassium chloride (2 mEq/mL): 13 mL
  * Lidocaine 1%: 13 mL
* **Temperature:** Delivered at 8-12°C
* **Duration:** Single dose lasts up to 90 minutes

**St Thomas Solution:**
* **Popular crystalloid cardioplegia** among cardiac surgeons
* **Limitation:** Must be administered repeatedly at short intervals (20 mins)
* **Constituents:**
  * Na+: 110 mmol/l
  * K+: 16 mmol/l
  * Mg2+: 16 mmol/l
  * Ca2+: 1.2 mmol/l
  * NaHCO3-: 10 mmol/l

**HTK Solution (Histidine-Tryptophan-Ketoglutarate):**
* **Components:**
  * Sodium: 15 mmol/L
  * Potassium: 9 mmol/L
  * Magnesium: 4 mmol/L
  * Calcium: 0.015 mmol/L
  * Ketoglutarate/glutamic acid: 1 mmol/L
  * Histidine: 198 mmol/L
  * Mannitol: 30 mmol/L
  * Tryptophan: 2 mmol/L
* **Protection duration:** Up to 120 minutes

#### 6.4.3.3. DELIVERY METHODS

**Antegrade:**
* Into aortic root (proximal to cross-clamp) at 60-100 mmHg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Direct coronary ostial injection (if significant AI or CAD) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Retrograde:**
* Via coronary sinus catheter at 200-400 mL/min to venous pressure 30-50 mmHg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Used with antegrade** for maximal protection, especially with severe CAD [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 6.4.3.4. TIMING AND VARIATIONS

**Cold Cardioplegia:**
* **Standard:** Every 20-30 minutes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Single shot:** Del Nido solution (1:4 blood:crystalloid ratio) for short cross-clamp times [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Warm/Normothermic Cardioplegia:**
* **Delivery:** Must be continuous [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Benefits:** Better postoperative cardiac indices [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Onset of Arrest:**
* **Antegrade:** 30-60 seconds [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Retrograde:** 2-4 minutes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Washout Effect:** Cardioplegia washout over time leads to resumption of electrical activity [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 6.4.4. LV DECOMPRESSION

**Importance:** LV distention increases wall tension and oxygen consumption → poor myocardial protection [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Monitoring:** TEE surveillance essential [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

⚠️ **High-Risk Situations:**
* **Severe AI:** Antegrade cardioplegia may leak into LV instead of coronaries [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Results:** LV distention, lack of electrical arrest [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 6.4.5. TOPICAL COOLING

**Additional protection** via bathing heart with cold (ice) solution [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Bottom Line:** Myocardial protection requires coordinated use of hypothermia, cardioplegia, and LV decompression, with careful attention to delivery techniques and monitoring for adequate protection.

**Comorbid Conditions:**
* **Diabetes mellitus:** Note HBA1c and diabetic control

* **Exclude:** GI, renal, hepatic, neurological, metabolic, or hematological disease/bleeding disorders

### 7.1.3. MEDICATION MANAGEMENT

**Continue until day of surgery:**
* **Cardiovascular medications** (most) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Aspirin and β-blockers** for CABG surgery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Variable practice:**
* **RAAS blockers** (ACEIs, ARBs, ARNIs): Guidelines vary by institution [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 7.1.3.1. DETAILED MEDICATION MANAGEMENT

**Diabetic Drugs:**
* **Oral hypoglycaemic agents and insulin:** Omit on day of surgery
* **Sliding scale:** Can be started on ward
* **SGLT2 inhibitors:** Stop 72 hrs prior to surgery (risk of euglycaemic diabetic ketoacidosis - EDKA)

**Anti-hypertensive Medications:**
* **Generally continued** unless patient hemodynamically unstable
* **ACEi and ARBs:** Sometimes withheld on day of surgery (risk of perioperative hypotension, increased risk of AKI and myocardial injury - check with consultant)

**Anticoagulation Management:**
* **Oral anticoagulants:** Clear discontinuation timing (apixaban, dabigatran, fondaparinux, warfarin) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Warfarin patients:** Perioperative bridging protocols [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Specific Anticoagulant Timing:**
* **IV heparin infusions:** Stop 4-6hrs prior to surgery
* **LMWHs:** Stop 12-24hrs before surgery
* **Warfarin (VKA):** Stop 3-5 days before surgery, recheck INR
* **DOAC (e.g. Rivaroxaban):** Normally stopped 48hrs pre-op

**Anti-platelet Drugs:**
* **Normally directed by surgical team**
* **Aspirin:** May be continued or stopped 5-7 days before surgery in patients at high risk for bleeding
* **Clopidogrel:** Stopped 5 days pre-op
* **Ticagrelor:** Stop 3 days pre-op
* **Consider platelet function tests** (e.g. platelet mapping)

### 7.1.8. PREMEDICATION AND ANXIOLYSIS

**Modern practice:** Minimal/no sedation until regulatory completion and multidisciplinary huddle [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Optimal sedation level:** Mild to moderate to alleviate hypertension, tachycardia, coronary vasospasm [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

⚠️ **Avoid deep sedation** in hemodynamically tenuous patients susceptible to myocardial depressant effects [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Empathy-based communication** plays important role in allaying fears and worries [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

## 7.2. MONITORING
<!-- EPA-10:K3:monitoring_tools -->


### 7.2.1. STANDARD ASA MONITORING

* **Pulse oximetry** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Electrocardiography** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Noninvasive blood pressure** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 7.2.2. ELECTROCARDIOGRAM

**Configuration:** 5-electrode system (left-arm, left-leg, precordial, right-arm, right-leg) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Display leads:** V5 and II for detection of arrhythmias and ~90% of ischemic episodes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 7.2.2.1. ECG LOCALIZATION OF ISCHEMIA [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* **Leads I, aVL, V5, V6:** Lateral LV wall (left circumflex/LAD territory)
* **Leads II, III, aVF:** Inferior LV wall (RCA territory)  
* **Leads V1-V4:** Anteroseptal and anterior LV wall (LAD territory)

### 7.2.3. TEMPERATURE MONITORING

**Sites:**
* **Core:** Bladder, nasopharynx, tympanum, pulmonary artery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Peripheral:** Skin, rectum (lag behind core temperature) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Clinical relevance:** Patients cooled to mild-moderate hypothermia during CPB then rewarmed [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 7.2.4. ARTERIAL BLOOD PRESSURE

#### 7.2.4.1. SITE SELECTION CRITERIA [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* Accessibility, convenience
* Lack of proximal stenosis
* Rate of complications
* Avoid dominant hand
* Accommodate surgical needs

**Surgical considerations:**
* **Left radial artery graft planned:** Use right brachial/radial artery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Descending aorta procedures:** Use right brachial/radial (left subclavian may be clamped) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 7.2.4.2. POST-CPB PRESSURE DISCREPANCY

* **Radial artery pressure may under-represent central pressure by up to 30 mmHg** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Mechanism:** Decreased radial diameter, vasoconstriction, or vasospasm [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Solutions:** Direct palpation by surgeon, femoral artery catheterization, direct aortic needle [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 7.2.5. CENTRAL VENOUS PRESSURE AND PAC

#### 7.2.5.1. CVP FUNCTIONS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* Safe cardioactive drug infusion
* RAP/CVP measurement
* CVP waveform analysis
* Large volume resuscitation

**Limitations:** CVP not accurate representation of cardiac preload or LV filling pressure, especially with severe LV failure, pulmonary hypertension, or reduced LV compliance [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 7.2.5.2. PAC ADVANTAGES [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* Comprehensive hemodynamic data (stroke volume, cardiac output, PVR, SVR)
* Mixed venous blood sampling
* Hemodynamic management facilitation
* Pacing capabilities

**Usage factors:** Patient risk, practice type, geography, surgeon preference [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

⚠️ **PAC Complications:**
* **Pulmonary artery perforation/infarction** (rare but serious) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Risk factors:** Distal migration, balloon over-inflation, prolonged inflation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Prevention:** Optimal balloon volume, balloon deflation after positioning, retract few cm before CPB [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 7.2.6. ECHOCARDIOGRAPHY
<!-- EPA-10:K3:tee EPA-10:EQ2:echocardiography -->

**Recommendation:** Perioperative TEE for all open heart and thoracic aortic surgery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**CABG:** Not mandatory but encouraged [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 7.2.6.1. PRE-CPB TEE USES [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* Confirm preoperative diagnosis
* Evaluate aortic atherosclerotic disease (alter cannulation strategy)
* Rule out new findings (persistent left SVC affecting retrograde cardioplegia)
* Guide volume resuscitation

#### 7.2.6.2. POST-CPB TEE USES [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* Assess de-airing adequacy
* Evaluate surgical intervention results
* Detect inadvertent consequences (new RWMA, RV failure, worsening valve regurgitation, AD)
* Assess ventricular filling (LV compliance worsens post-CPB)

### 7.2.7. NEUROLOGIC MONITORING
<!-- EPA-10:K3:cerebral_monitoring EPA-10:K5:neuroprotection -->

**Goal:** Assess adequacy of cerebral oxygen delivery during CPB [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Techniques:**
* **Bispectral index monitoring** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Electroencephalography** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Evoked potentials** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Near-infrared spectroscopy** (cerebral oximetry) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Transcranial Doppler** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 7.2.7.1. NEUROLOGIC COMPLICATIONS

**Types:**
* **Ischemic strokes:** Vastly outnumber hemorrhagic, tend to be focal, likely from macroembolization [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Cognitive dysfunction:** Likely from microembolization, hypoperfusion, inflammation, silent infarctions [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Risk factors:**
* Advanced age (>70 years) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Pre-existing cerebrovascular disease (carotid stenosis >80%) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* History of stroke, peripheral vascular disease [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Ascending aortic atheroma, diabetes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Surgical risk factors:**
* CPB duration, intracardiac procedures [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Aortic pressure, excessive warming [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* Perioperative hypotension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 7.2.7.2. CEREBRAL OXIMETRY

**Principle:** Based on Beer-Lambert law and optical spectrometry [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**Normal values:** 60-80% regional cerebral saturation (rScCO2) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Treatment thresholds:**
* **Absolute decrease:** rScCO2 ≤50% [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Relative decrease:** ≥20% from baseline [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Interventions:**
* Reposition malpositioned CPB cannulae
* Increase pump flow, augment MAP
* Alter ventilation for normocarbia
* Increase inspired O2 for hypoxemia
* Deepen anesthesia level
* Transfuse blood (maintain Hgb ≥8 g/dL) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

💡 **Evidence:** Standardized protocol-based treatment may decrease postoperative cognitive dysfunction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Bottom Line:** Comprehensive monitoring including neurologic assessment is essential for optimal outcomes in cardiac surgery.

## 7.3. ANESTHETIC DRUG SELECTION

### 7.3.1. FAST-TRACK CARDIAC ANESTHESIA EVOLUTION

**Historical context:** High-dose opioid technique of 1980s provided cardiovascular stability but prolonged mechanical ventilation and resource utilization [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Modern approach:** Short-acting, fast-emergence drugs to facilitate early tracheal extubation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 7.3.1.1. CONTEMPORARY REGIMEN COMPONENTS

* **Induction:** Etomidate or propofol [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Amnesia:** Volatile anesthetics or propofol-based TIVA [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Supplementation:** Short-acting benzodiazepines [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Depth monitoring:** MAC ≥0.8 pre-CPB, volatile concentration in oxygenator exhaust line [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 7.3.2. ENHANCED RECOVERY AND FAST-TRACKING

#### 7.3.2.1. ERAS PRINCIPLES

**Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS):** Term encompassing strategies aimed at reducing surgical stress response and optimizing patient recovery through evidence-based, multidisciplinary approach.

**Benefits of ERAS:**
* Reduced length of hospital stay
* Lower rates of complications
* Faster return to normal activities
* Increased patient satisfaction
* Reduced opioid consumption

**ERAS Interventions:**
* Pre-operative patient education
* Early ambulation
* Minimizing opioids
* Multimodal pain management
* Carbohydrate-rich drink before surgery

### 7.3.3. VOLATILE ANESTHETICS (PREFERRED)

💡 **Advantages:**
* **Associated with favorable postoperative morbidity and mortality outcomes** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Cardioprotective effects** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 7.3.3.1. CPB PHARMACOKINETIC CHANGES

* **Hemodilution:** Decreases blood/gas partition coefficient [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Hypothermia:** Increases blood/gas partition coefficient [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Net effect:** Often counterbalance each other [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 7.3.4. INDUCTION DRUGS AND CONSIDERATIONS

**Common Induction Agents:**
* **Propofol:** Commonly used
* **Alternatives:** Various combinations based on consultant preference

**Hemodynamic Goals During Induction:**
* **Hemodynamic stability:** Avoid extremes of heart rate and blood pressure
* **Bradycardia tolerated:** As long as MAP and cardiac output not compromised (increased diastolic time for coronary perfusion)
* **Maintain adequate perfusion pressure:** Based on coronary, cerebrovascular, renal, and other organ systems

**Endotracheal Intubation:**
* **Ensure adequate depth and analgesia** during stimulating events
* **Preferred tube:** Portex Blueline Suction above the Cuff Tube (Size 7.0 for females, 8.0 for males)

### 7.3.5. MUSCLE RELAXANTS

**Preferred agents:** Intermediate-acting (rocuronium, vecuronium, atracurium, cis-atracurium) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**Management:** Neuromuscular monitoring-guided re-dosing [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Important for ERAS:**
* **Stop infusion early** or avoid further doses before ICU transfer
* **Especially relevant** for early extubation and fast tracking
* **Muscle relaxation important** to prevent shivering during hypothermia and patient movement

### 7.3.6. MULTIMODAL ANALGESIA

**Components:**
* **Low-dose opioids** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Regional anesthetic blocks** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Ketamine infusions** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Lidocaine infusions** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 7.3.6.1. OPIOID CONSIDERATIONS DURING CPB

* **Hemodilution:** Reduces total propofol and opioid concentration [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Reduced protein concentration:** Increases unbound fraction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Hypothermia:** Decreases metabolism, prolongs elimination half-lives [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Remifentanil:** Can decrease 30% per 5°C temperature drop (temperature-dependent elimination) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Analgesia During CPB:**
* **Provided with opioid boluses** (e.g. fentanyl, morphine) or infusions (e.g. remifentanil)
* **With Fast Track Surgery:** Opioid doses kept to minimum, multimodal analgesia used

### 7.3.7. ANESTHETIC DEPTH CONSIDERATIONS

**Variable intensity periods:**
* **High stimulation:** Tracheal intubation, skin incision, sternotomy, pericardiotomy, aortic manipulation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Requires:** Rapidly adjustable anesthetic depth [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Modern cardiac anesthesia uses short-acting drugs with multimodal techniques, emphasizing volatile anesthetics for their cardioprotective properties while facilitating early recovery.

## 7.4. OPERATING ROOM PREPARATION AND SETUP

### 7.4.1. COMMUNICATION AND TEAMWORK

**Healthcare Providers Involved:**

* Perfusionist

#### 7.4.1.1. COMMUNICATION GUIDELINES

* **Open communication:** Indispensable for patient safety and optimal outcomes
* **Empower team members:** All should voice concerns and observations
* **WHO surgical safety checklist** implementation
* **Speak-up culture:** Foster robust environment for safety
* **Debriefs and data management:** For continuous improvement

### 7.4.2. EQUIPMENT AND MONITORING SETUP

#### 7.4.2.1. ANESTHESIA MACHINE AND EQUIPMENT

* **Check Anaesthesia Machine & Equipment**
* **Hotline/Underbody Bair Hugger**
* **Defibrillator Machine**
* **Platelet Agitator**
* **TEG Machine**

#### 7.4.2.2. TEE PREPARATION

* **TEE Machine and TEE Probe** (Do not use unless consultant present)

#### 7.4.2.3. INFUSION PUMPS

* **Check Infusion Pumps**
* **Inotropes/Vasopressor Infusions:** Prepared by AU Nurse - check drug and program for each pump
* **Potential infusions:** Adrenaline/Noradrenaline, Sodium Nitroprusside, GTN, Insulin, Milrinone, others according to consultant

#### 7.4.2.4. RESUSCITATION DRUGS

* **Atropine:** 600mcg
* **Adrenaline:** 10mcg/ml & 100mcg/ml in 10ml syringe
* **Phenylephrine:** 100mcg/ml (Dilute 10mg in 100ml 0.9% N/Saline bag)
* **Ephedrine:** 6mg/ml

#### 7.4.2.5. INDUCTION DRUGS (AS PER CONSULTANT)

* **Propofol**
* **Non-depolarising muscle relaxant:** Rocuronium/Atracurium
* **Fentanyl and/or other opioid**

#### 7.4.2.6. OTHER ESSENTIAL DRUGS

* **Anti-fibrinolytic:** Tranexamic Acid – 1g on induction
* **Antibiotic Prophylaxis:** On induction
  * Cefazolin 2g OR
  * Vancomycin 1g for penicillin allergy OR
  * Cefazolin & Vancomycin for MRSA
* **Analgesia:** Morphine/Paracetamol
* **Heparin:** 5000iu/ml (300-400iu/kg)

### 7.4.3. MONITORING AND PATIENT PREPARATION

#### 7.4.3.1. STANDARD MONITORING

* **Standard ASA Monitoring**
* **ECG:** Lead II and V; Additional 3 lead ECG for TEE machine/Defibrillator
* **SpO2:** May need 2 probes for radial harvest
* **NIBP:** Right arm
* **BIS/Cerebral Oximetry**
* **Defibrillator Pads:** For MICS/MIDCAB/Redo Surgery

#### 7.4.3.2. VASCULAR ACCESS

* **Large bore IV Access:** 1 or 2
* **Arterial Line:** Radial artery preferred - check for radial artery harvest
* **Quad lumen CVP line:** Right IJ preferred (Pre-induction/post-induction)

#### 7.4.3.3. POST-INDUCTION SETUP

* **TEE with bite block** inserted post induction
* **Foley Catheter**
* **Nasal and rectal temperature probes** post induction

### 7.4.4. PATIENT POSITIONING

#### 7.4.4.1. CONVENTIONAL STERNOTOMY

* **Position:** After induction with all invasive lines, catheter, temperature probe in situ
* **Supine position:** Thorax and whole body exposed from neck to ankles
* **Pillow:** Under knees
* **Shoulder roll:** Under upper back to elevate chest and extend neck
* **Arms:** Both tucked after being rolled in gel pads; left arm on arm board if left radial harvest

**Key Points:**
* Ensure drips running after arms tucked
* Check IA line trace
* Check SpO2 probe and trace
* Connect all infusions to central line - ensure connections secure and pumps programmed/working
* Ensure easy access to central line and peripheral line ports
* Protect all 3-way taps in contact with patient with gauze
* Protect pressure points
* ECG leads/Defib pads covered with Tegaderm
* Ensure head and neck not excessively hyperextended
* **Access to patient difficult once surgery commences**

#### 7.4.4.2. MINIMALLY INVASIVE DIRECT CORONARY ARTERY BYPASS (MIDCAB)

**MIDCAB:** Minimally invasive approach via left anterior mini thoracotomy, surgeon accesses heart from left hemithorax, usually off pump, mainly for 1-2 grafts.

**Positioning:**
* **Supine** with left chest elevated to right by 20-30 degrees
* **Roll** placed below lateral left chest and left buttock
* **Left arm** tucked below left chest to expose lateral part
* **External defibrillator pads required** for MIDCAB surgery

**Key Points:**
* **Brachial plexus injury possible** - left shoulder lowered can cause excessive retraction
* **Ensure defibrillator pads** placed for all redo surgery and minimally invasive surgery
* **Re-check DLT position** before and after positioning patient

**Bottom Line:** Systematic preparation and communication are essential for safe cardiac anesthesia, with particular attention to equipment setup, drug preparation, and optimal patient positioning.

## 7.5. INTRAOPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

**Principle:** Anesthesiology team familiarity with surgical steps helps anticipate needs and prepare accordingly [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 7.5.1. PHASES OF CONVENTIONAL ON-PUMP CABG

#### 7.5.1.1. MANAGEMENT BEFORE REVASCULARIZATION

**Phases Include:**
* Sternotomy
* Graft Harvesting
* Anti-coagulation (Heparinization)
* Cannulation
* Preparation for CPB/Pre CPB Checklist
* On Bypass Checklist

#### 7.5.1.2. MANAGEMENT DURING REVASCULARIZATION

**Phases Include:**
* Off Pump Surgery vs On Pump Surgery
* Conduct of CPB
* Monitoring During CPB

#### 7.5.1.3. MANAGEMENT AFTER REVASCULARIZATION

**Phases Include:**
* Weaning & separation from CPB & Checklist
* Haemostasis and coagulation management
* Chest closure
* Preparation for transfer to ICU

### 7.5.2. STERNOTOMY

**Highly stimulating event:** Ensure adequate analgesia, especially if preparation/draping prolonged - common to administer fentanyl 50-100mcg pre-sternotomy.

**"Lungs Off or Stop Ventilation":** Avoid potential pleural tear from sternal saw - disconnect circuit from ETT, restart when instructed.

**Hypotension Management:** Can occur with heart/pericardial manipulation - closely observe surgeon, treat with:
* Phenylephrine
* Fluid bolus
* Trendelenburg position

### 7.5.3. GRAFT HARVESTING

**Concurrent harvesting:** LIMA and saphenous vein typically harvested simultaneously.

#### 7.5.3.1. LEFT INTERNAL MAMMARY ARTERY (LIMA) DISSECTION

* **Table manipulation:** Elevate table, rotate to left, reduce tidal volumes (increase rate) for surgeon exposure
* **Similar maneuvers** for less common RIMA dissection
* **Heparin usually administered** before clamping LIMA pedicle to avoid thrombosis
* **Papaverine injection:** Often injected retrograde by surgeon - occasionally causes hypotension

#### 7.5.3.2. LEFT RADIAL ARTERY HARVEST

* **Can be harvested:** Open or endoscopically
* **Left arm:** Abducted and placed on arm board
* **Post-harvest:** Arm tucked again
* **Superior patency:** Radial artery conduits have better long-term patency than saphenous vein grafts

#### 7.5.3.3. SAPHENOUS VEIN HARVEST

* **Technique:** Open or endoscopic
* **Usually harvested** from one leg

### 7.5.4. CONVENTIONAL ON-PUMP CABG SEQUENCE

**Preparation phase:**
* **Conduit harvesting:** Saphenous vein grafts, internal mammary artery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Approach:** Median sternotomy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **IMA mobilization:** Pedicled (proximal origin intact) or free graft [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Ventilation:** Low tidal volume, frequent holds for exposure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Heparinization and cannulation:**

* **Ensure surgeon and perfusionist present:** Cardiac arrest on induction possible - may require emergency sternotomy/CPB
* **Monitor setup:** All monitors connected, appropriate alarm limits set, invasive monitoring lines zeroed

#### 7.5.6.2. INDUCTION AND INTUBATION

* **Goal:** Smooth transition with minimal hemodynamic perturbations [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Unstable patients:** May require preinduction vasopressors/inotropes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* **High stimulation periods:** Skin incision, sternotomy, pericardiotomy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* **TEE:** New RWMA, mitral regurgitation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* **PAC:** Increased LV filling pressure [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

# 8. CARDIOPULMONARY BYPASS MANAGEMENT
<!-- EPA-10:K4:cpb EPA-10:EQ2:cpb_management -->

> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Cardiopulmonary bypass techniques|Part B: Cardiopulmonary Bypass Techniques]]

**Cardiopulmonary bypass management during revascularization requires understanding of physiologic principles and systematic monitoring.**

CPB is designed to perform four major functions: (1) oxygenation and carbon dioxide elimination, (2) circulation of blood and organ perfusion, (3) systemic cooling and rewarming, and (4) diversion of blood from the heart to provide a bloodless surgical field.

## 8.1. CPB INITIATION AND MANAGEMENT

### 8.1.1. INITIATION CHECKLIST

* **Heparin:** 400 IU/kg, ACT >400-480 seconds [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Cannulation:** Cavoatrial and aortic [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Confirmation of adequacy:**
  * Venous drainage (no CVP/PA pulsatility) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
  * Arterial flow (appropriate pressures) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
  * Gas exchange (ABG parameters) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
  * Anesthesia depth (BIS monitoring) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 8.1.2. PREPARATION FOR CPB

**Once aortic and venous cannula inserted and ACT >480s, CPB can be instituted.**

**Checklist Approach:**
* **Clear communication** between anesthesiologist, perfusionist, surgeon, nursing staff
* **Ensure heparin administered** and check ACT >480s
* **Aortic and venous cannula** in situ and connected to arterial and venous limbs of CPB circuit
* **Check position** of arterial cannula - TEE, normal pressure swing and line pressure
* **Ensure no air bubbles** in arterial limb of CPB circuit or in aortic cannula
* **Ensure no air lock** in venous limb
* **Ensure adequate paralysis and anesthesia depth**
* **Withdraw PAC** by 3-5cm if used, deflate balloon
* **TEE position:** Neutral position and unlocked, "freeze" mode activated to prevent heating probe tip
* **Monitor functionality:** All monitors, invasive monitoring lines, drips in working order

### 8.1.3. INITIATION SEQUENCE

**Surgeon signals readiness:** "GO ON BYPASS"

**Perfusionist actions:**
* **Release venous clamp:** Allow blood drainage from patient into CPB machine venous reservoir
* **Gradually increase flow rate:** Roller pump returns blood via arterial cannula to aorta
* **Establish flow rate:** 2.2-2.8 L/min/m²
* **Signal achievement:** "FULL FLOW ACHIEVED" when adequate flow rate achieved

### 8.1.4. POST-INITIATION CHECKLIST

**Once patient on Full Flow, quick check:**
* **Venous drainage adequacy:** Blood draining into CPB venous reservoir, no air lock
* **Heart emptying:** No distention of heart
* **SVC/IVC obstruction check:** No elevated CVP, facial flushing, eye swelling
* **Arterial return adequacy:** Adequate flow rate achieved, blood bright red and well oxygenated in arterial limb/cannula
* **MAP achievement:** Maintained between 60-70mmHg
* **Ventilation cessation:** Stop mechanical ventilation, set appropriate alarm limits
* **Anesthetic maintenance:** Ensure anesthetic gas or IV agents running

**Full CPB parameters:**
* **Flow:** 2.2-2.4 L/min/m² or 50-60 mL/kg/min [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Ventilation:** Withheld [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **MAP:** Initially 30-40 mmHg (hemodilution), adjusted to 50-80 mmHg [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

## 8.2. ON-PUMP CABG CONDUCT

### 8.2.1. CARDIOPLEGIA AND CARDIAC ARREST

**Once full flow achieved:** Heart can be arrested by cardioplegia delivery

**Process:**
* **Aortic cross-clamp placed** by surgeon
* **Antegrade or retrograde cardioplegia** (or both) delivered
* **Asystole observed** on ECG as cardioplegia distributes to myocardium
* **Heart arrested in diastole**

**Cooling employed:** Patient typically cooled to 32-34°C for organ protection

### 8.2.2. SURGICAL SEQUENCE

**Distal anastomoses first:**
* **LIMA to LAD anastomosis** performed initially
* **Other distal anastomoses** completed

**Rewarming phase:**
* **Aortic cross-clamp released** once distal anastomoses completed
* **Patient rewarming** ensues

**Proximal anastomoses:**
* **Aortic side clamp used** for anastomosis of grafts to ascending aorta

## 8.3. ANESTHESIA MANAGEMENT DURING CPB

### 8.3.1. ANESTHETIC MAINTENANCE OPTIONS

**Volatile Anesthetic:**
* **Isoflurane/Sevoflurane/Desflurane**
* **Requires mounting vaporizer** onto gas inlet line to oxygenator
* **Confirmation:** Disconnect gas analysis line from airway circuit, reconnect to oxygenator outlet
* **Scavenging:** Ensure appropriate scavenging of oxygenator outlet

**TIVA (Total Intravenous Anesthesia):**
* **Combination:** Opiates and sedatives by infusion
* **Propofol:** Typical rates 3-6 mg/kg/h or target plasma concentration 2-4 μg/mL
* **Advantages:** Simplicity, less myocardial depression, no need for oxygenator scavenging

### 8.3.2. OXYGENATION AND GAS EXCHANGE

**Gas Control During CPB:**
* **Mixer/gas blender:** Controls FiO2
* **Fresh gas flow:** Determines PCO2 removal
  * **Reduced flow:** Less CO2 washout → increased PCO2
* **Oxygen/air mixture:** Regulates PO2

## 8.4. MONITORING AND MANAGEMENT DURING CPB

### 8.4.1. TABLE 39-17: CPB MONITORING CHECKLIST [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Laboratory Values:**
* ACT (adequate heparinization), ABGs (acidosis assessment)
* Hematocrit, electrolytes (Na+, K+, ionized Ca2+), glucose

**Anesthetic Monitors:**
* Discontinue ventilation, monitor anesthetic depth

**Pressure Management:**
* **Hypotension causes:** Inadequate venous return, pump issues, arterial cannula problems, decreased vascular tone, monitor malfunction
* **Hypertension causes:** High flow, misdirected cannula, vasoconstriction, light anesthesia

**Perfusion Assessment:**
* Flow and pressure adequacy, acidosis, mixed venous oxygen saturation
* Temperature, urine output

**Patient/Field Observation:**
* Heart distention/fibrillation, skin cyanosis/temperature, movement, signs of light anesthesia

### 8.4.2. HEMODYNAMIC MANAGEMENT

#### 8.4.2.1. CARDIAC OUTPUT AND MAP

**Cardiac Output:** Determined by pump flow rate set by perfusionist
* **During CPB:** CO usually maintained between 2.2-2.8 L/m²/min
* **With hypothermia:** Pump flow rate can be further reduced
* **Metabolic targets:** DO2i at 280 mL/min/m² and CI at 2.4 L/min/m²

**MAP Management:** MAP = CO × SVR
* **2019 EACTS/EACTA/EBCP guidelines:** Maintain MAP 50-80 mmHg

**MAP <50mmHg Management:**
* Ensure adequate pump flow
* Check line pressures, venous return, Hct
* **Vasoconstrictors:** Phenylephrine, Noradrenaline, Vasopressin, Steroids

**MAP >80mmHg Management:**
* Ensure adequate anesthesia, muscle relaxation, analgesia
* **Vasodilators:** GTN Infusion, Sodium Nitroprusside

#### 8.4.2.2. MARKERS OF END ORGAN PERFUSION

* **Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation**
* **Oxygen Consumption**
* **Lactate Levels**
* **Urine Output**
* **Cerebral Oximetry**

### 8.4.3. ANTICOAGULATION MONITORING

* **Unfractionated heparin** used for anticoagulation during CPB
* **ACT checked every 30 minutes** on CPB
* **ACT maintained >400-480s** while on CPB
* **Off pump surgery:** ACT checked every 30-45 minutes

### 8.4.4. TEMPERATURE MANAGEMENT

**Body temperature regulation** via heat exchanger in CPB circuit

**Cooling Benefits:**
* **Organ protection**
* **Reduced metabolic demands**

**Rewarming Process:**
* **Gradual temperature increase** to normothermia before separation

### 8.4.5. ACID-BASE AND ELECTROLYTE MANAGEMENT

**Glucose Control**

**Acid-Base Balance**

**Electrolyte Optimization:**
* **Calcium:** Maintain appropriate levels
* **Potassium:** Monitor and adjust
* **Magnesium:** Antiarrhythmic properties

## 8.5. OFF-PUMP CABG CONSIDERATIONS

### 8.5.1. OFF-PUMP CABG MANAGEMENT

**Performed on beating heart without CPB**

#### 8.5.1.1. KEY VIGILANCE POINTS

**Hemodynamic Instability Causes:**

**1. Surgical Manipulation and Heart Positioning:**
* Reduced stroke volume, reduced MAP, increased CVP and RVEDP
* Reduced coronary artery blood flow → ischemia
* Impaired drainage of blood from atria into ventricles
* Distortion of MV or TV leading to valvular regurgitation
* Mechanical/suction stabilizers can impede LV regional wall motion & contractility

**2. Myocardial Ischemia During Anastomosis:**
* **Temporary occlusion** of target coronary artery used by some surgeons
* **Creates precise anastomosis** in bloodless surgical field

#### 8.5.1.2. MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES FOR HEMODYNAMIC INSTABILITY

* **Relative bradycardia** with adequate preload preferred
* **Repositioning:** Heart/repositioning stabilizers
* **Trendelenburg positioning:** 20 degrees head down
* **Pharmacologic support:** Inotropes/vasopressors
* **Ischemia monitoring:** ECG/TEE for RWMA
* **Coronary shunt:** Small shunt into coronary artery maintains myocardial perfusion, stabilizes hemodynamic state
* **IABP consideration**
* **Conversion to On Pump CABG**

**Bottom Line:** CPB management requires systematic approach to monitoring, hemodynamic control, and readiness for both on-pump and off-pump techniques.

# 9. SEPARATION FROM CPB AND POST-CPB MANAGEMENT
<!-- EPA-10:EQ2:weaning_cpb EPA-10:K4:pacemaker -->

> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Postoperative Issues|Part B: Postoperative Issues]]

**Separation from cardiopulmonary bypass represents a critical transition requiring systematic preparation and management.**

The main goal of weaning from CPB is smooth transition from mechanical pump-assisted circulation to spontaneous heart activities with sufficient blood flow to maintain systemic circulation.

## 9.1. PREPARATION FOR SEPARATION

### 9.1.1. WEANING FROM CPB CHECKLIST: WAAARRRRMM

**W - Warm:** Nasopharyngeal temperature 37°C and core temperature ~35.5°C

**A - Anesthesia:** Most patients remain intubated and sedated with controlled postoperative mechanical ventilation

**A - Adjuvant drugs:** Adequate supply of muscle relaxants, antiarrhythmic agents, inotropic agents ready

**A - Air:** Removed by ventilation and heart massage, assisted by TEE visualization

**R - Rhythm:** Normal sinus rhythm ideal, but temporary epicardial pacing employed if sinus rhythm cannot be achieved

**R - Rate:** Heart rate between 80-90 beats/minute

**R - Resistance:** Hypotension with normal/elevated bypass flow rate or cardiac output indicates low SVR → need for vasopressors

**R - Respiration:** Adequate ventilation and oxygenation

**M - Metabolism:** Calcium (1.09-1.3 mmol/L), potassium (4-5.5 mmol/L), hemoglobin (≥7 mg/dL), acid-base status normalized

**M - Monitoring:** All monitors functioning and volume on pulse oximeter audible

### 9.1.2. TABLE 39-18: PRE-SEPARATION CHECKLIST [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Laboratory:** Hematocrit, ABGs, electrolytes (K+, ionized Ca2+)

**Anesthetic/Machine:**
* Lung compliance evaluation, expanded ventilated lungs
* Vaporizers off, alarms on

**Monitors:**
* Normothermia, ECG assessment, re-zeroed transducers
* Arterial and filling pressures, recorder ready

**Patient/Field:**
* Visual heart assessment (contractility, size, rhythm)
* De-airing confirmed (Lead II, TEE)
* LV vent clamped/removed, caval snares released
* No major bleeding sites

### 9.1.3. TEMPORARY EPICARDIAL PACING WIRES

**Indication:** Required only in patients at high risk of pacing dependence

**Risk Factors:**
* Advanced age
* Valvular surgery
* Poor left ventricular function
* Structural heart disease
* Diabetes mellitus
* Preoperative beta-blocker or digoxin use
* History of arrhythmias

#### 9.1.3.1. PACING WIRE CONFIGURATIONS

**Options:**
* **Ventricular wires only**
* **Atrial and ventricular wires** (most common)

**Types:**
* **Unipolar or bipolar**

#### 9.1.3.2. PACEMAKER CHECK PROCEDURE

**Prerequisites:** Ensure normothermia and normal K+ levels

**Steps:**
1. **Connect pacing leads** to pacemaker box
2. **Set rate:** 80-90bpm, output A and V to 10mV
3. **Check for capture:** Pacing spikes, P wave and QRS complex
4. **Check underlying rhythm:** Gradually reduce pacemaker rate or press pause button
5. **Check SENSING threshold** for A and V leads
6. **Check CAPTURE threshold** for both A and V leads

#### 9.1.3.3. SENSING THRESHOLD CHECK

**Procedure:**
1. Put pacemaker in VVI, AAI, or DDD mode
2. Set rate much lower than patient's native rate
3. Set output to minimum setting
4. Observe sense indicator
5. Slowly decrease sensitivity (increase mV value) until pacemaker stops detecting endogenous activity
6. Slowly increase sensitivity (decrease mV value) until pacemaker detects every endogenous event
7. **Sensing threshold:** Minimal sensitivity value where pacemaker senses every P-wave or QRS interval

#### 9.1.3.4. CAPTURE THRESHOLD CHECK

**Procedure:**
1. **Set rate:** At least 10 beats per minute above patient's intrinsic rate
2. **Increase output:** Slowly increase in mA until pacing spike visible on monitor
3. **Check for capture:** Look for widened QRS complex and broad T wave after each spike (electrical capture), check for ejection on IA trace (mechanical capture)
4. **Set output:** Double the capture threshold for safety margin

#### 9.1.3.5. PACING MODES

**Emergency/CPB Separation:**
* **VOO/DOO:** Asynchronous modes for separation from CPB, pacing dependence during diathermy, emergency situations

**Post-CPB Management:**
* **AAI:** Useful if no AV block - patients with sinus bradycardia
* **DDD/DDI:** Used in presence of high grade AV block

## 9.2. WEANING PROCESS

### 9.2.1. SEPARATION TECHNIQUE

**Incremental Process:**
* **Heart filling:** Gradually clamp venous return line
* **Monitor:** CVP and observe heart for overdistention
* **TEE guidance:** Look at LV filling (preload), LV and RV contractility
* **Ejection begins:** As LV fills, arterial trace becomes pulsatile
* **Flow reduction:** Arterial pump flow gradually reduced from 100% to "off CPB" in 25% increments

#### 9.2.1.1. "A TO H" MNEMONIC [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**A - Anesthesia:** Adequate depth (MAC or BIS), functioning monitors, re-zero hemodynamic monitors

**B - Breathe:** Recruitment maneuvers, lung protective ventilation

**C - Circulation:** Optimize heart rate/rhythm, pressures, initiate inotropes/vasopressors as needed. Pacing to 70-100 bpm if needed

**D - De-air:** Ensure complete air removal

**E - Electrolytes:** Optimize calcium (1.09-1.3 mmol/L), potassium (4-5.5 mEq/L), pH >7.3

**F - Foster warmth:** Adequate rewarming, consider heated breathing circuit

**G - Go over issues:** Communicate bleeding, coagulation, transfusion needs, vasoactive medications, MCS requirements

**H - Hemoglobin:** Adequate levels (≥7 g/dL)

#### 9.2.1.2. WEANING PROCESS

**Technique:** Incremental venous cannula occlusion with measured CPB flow decreases [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Assessment parameters:**
* **Hemodynamics:** MAP, CO, PA pressure, CVP [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **TEE:** Direct heart visualization [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Visual inspection:** Contractility, rhythm, ventricular filling [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 9.2.2. OPTIMIZATION STRATEGIES

**TEE Guidance:**
* **Optimize preload** and myocardial contractility
* **Preload increase:** 50-100ml boluses via aortic cannula

**Hemodynamic Support:**
* **Poor LV contractility:** May require inotropes, mechanical support with IABP or ECMO if cannot wean off CPB
* **Vasoplegia and low SVR:** Treat with vasoconstrictors such as phenylephrine and noradrenaline

## 9.3. COMPLICATIONS AND MANAGEMENT

**Ischemia:**
* Inadequate myocardial protection, intraoperative MI
* Reperfusion injury, coronary spasm
* Coronary embolism (air, thrombus, calcium)

* Nongraftable vessels, diffuse CAD

**CPB-Related Factors:**

* **Phenylephrine:** 1-3 μg/kg/min [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 9.3.3. CORONARY AIR EMBOLISM MANAGEMENT

**Signs:** Diffuse ST elevation, arrhythmias, RV dysfunction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Treatment:**
* Revert to CPB
* Unload LV
* Higher perfusion pressure
* **Goal:** Expel air bubbles from coronary circulation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

## 9.4. POST-CARDIOTOMY COMPLICATIONS

### 9.4.1. LOW CARDIAC OUTPUT SYNDROME (LCOS)
<!-- EPA-10:K8:lcos -->

**Definition:** Post-cardiotomy cardiogenic shock defined by:
* **Cardiac index <2.0 L/min/m²**
* **Systolic BP <90 mmHg** (or need for vasopressors ≥90 mmHg)
* **PCWP >16-18 mmHg**
* **Oliguria**

**Incidence:** Can affect up to 6% of cardiac surgical patients

**Risk Factors:**
* Age, Female Sex
* Combined CABG + Mitral Valve Surgery
* Heart Failure
* Emergency Surgery & Re-Operation
* Pre-Existing Co-Morbidities
* Chronic Renal Failure

#### 9.4.1.1. LCOS MANAGEMENT

**Goals:**
* Increase tissue oxygen delivery
* Reduce tissue oxygen demand

**Strategies:**
* **Address reversible causes** of shock
* **Ensure adequate hemodynamic monitoring** (TEE or advanced invasive monitors in ICU)
* **Optimize preload**
* **Optimize myocardial contractility and function**
* **Address elevated SVR or vasoplegia**
* **Pharmacologic support:** Inotropes, Vasoconstrictors, Vasodilators, Inodilators
* **Institute early mechanical support**
* **Consider Delayed Sternal Closure**

### 9.4.2. VASOPLEGIC SYNDROME
<!-- EPA-10:K8:vasoplegia -->

**Definition:** Distributive shock due to significant drop in vascular resistance after CPB

**Incidence:** 5-44% of cardiovascular surgery patients

**Risk Factors:**
* Heart failure with low ejection fraction
* Renal failure
* Preoperative ACE inhibitor use
* Prolonged aortic cross-clamp
* Left ventricular assist device surgery

#### 9.4.2.1. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

**Multifactorial mechanism:**
* **Surgical trauma** and **CPB circuit exposure**
* **Ischemia-reperfusion** promotes systemic inflammatory response
* **Cytokine release:** IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α with vasodilating properties
* **NO production increase** → decreased vascular resistance and reduced vasopressor responsiveness

**Additional mechanisms:**
* **Lowering of plasma vasopressin**
* **Adrenergic receptor desensitization**
* **ATP-dependent potassium (KATP) channel activation**

#### 9.4.2.2. VASOPLEGIA MANAGEMENT

**Primary Treatment:**
* **Fluid resuscitation**
* **Conventional vasopressors:** Catecholamines and vasopressin

**Alternative/Rescue Therapy:**
* **Alternative vasopressors:** Angiotensin 2, methylene blue, hydroxocobalamin
* **Anti-inflammatory strategies:** Corticosteroids
* **Note:** Insufficient evidence for strong recommendations

**Bottom Line:** Separation from CPB requires systematic preparation using established checklists, with readiness to manage complications including LCOS and vasoplegic syndrome through pharmacologic and mechanical support.

# 10. POSTOPERATIVE MANAGEMENT AND COMPLICATIONS


> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Postoperative Issues|Part B: Postoperative Issues]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Haematological issues|Haematological Issues]]

**Postoperative cardiac surgery management requires vigilant monitoring and rapid intervention for life-threatening complications.**

The acuity and complexity of cardiac surgical procedures present challenges including continued bleeding, coagulopathy, cardiac tamponade, organ failure, atrial fibrillation, respiratory failure, and pain management [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. Enhanced recovery after cardiac surgery (ERACS) guidelines encourage expedient discharge for appropriate patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

## 10.1. PROTAMINE ADMINISTRATION AND HEPARIN REVERSAL
<!-- EPA-10:EQ2:heparin_protamine -->

### 10.1.1. PROTAMINE CHARACTERISTICS

**Properties:**
* **Small, nuclear, basic, arginine-rich** positively charged proteins
* **Similarities to histones**

**Actions:**
* **Primary:** Neutralization of anticoagulation effect of unfractionated heparin
* **Secondary effects:** Anti-coagulant properties via anti-platelet effects and interference with coagulation factors
* **May reduce clot strength** and promote fibrinolysis

### 10.1.2. DOSING AND ADMINISTRATION

**Dosing:**
* **Range:** 1:1 to 0.8:1 ratio
* **Standard:** 0.8mg Protamine for each 100iu heparin
* **High protamine to heparin ratio** may cause increased bleeding and transfusion

**Administration Protocol:**
* **Draw protamine only when required**
* **Wait for instructions** from surgeon before administration
* **Administer by slow IV injection**
* **Inform perfusionist and surgeon** when administration started
* **Communicate progress:** When 50% given and at completion
* **Rate:** Ideally <5mg/min

### 10.1.3. PROTAMINE REACTIONS

#### 10.1.3.1. TYPE 1: HYPOTENSION RELATED TO RAPID ADMINISTRATION
* **Mechanism:** Histamine release from mast cells
* **Rate-dependent** reaction
* **Response:** Usually responds to fluid loading and vasoconstrictors
* **Risk factors:** Poorly tolerated by patients with poor LVEF and hypovolemia
* **Prevention:** Slow protamine infusion

#### 10.1.3.2. TYPE 2: ALLERGIC REACTIONS
* **A) True Anaphylactic Reaction:** IgE mediated
* **B) Immediate Anaphylactoid Reaction**
* **C) Delayed Anaphylactoid Reaction**

#### 10.1.3.3. TYPE 3: CATASTROPHIC PULMONARY HYPERTENSION
* **Mechanism:** IgG/complement mediated

## 10.2. HEMOSTASIS AND BLEEDING MANAGEMENT

### 10.2.1. POST-PROTAMINE ASSESSMENT

**ACT Check:** 5 minutes after full protamine dose
* **If ACT still elevated:** Additional 50mg protamine dose

**Blood Product Requirements:**
* **Routine CABG:** Blood products normally not required for most cases

### 10.2.2. RISK FACTORS FOR BLOOD PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS

**Patient Factors:**
* Advanced age
* Female sex
* Lower BMI
* Low Hematocrit
* Emergency Surgery
* Redo Surgery
* Prolonged CPB
* Co-morbidities (CKD)

### 10.2.3. BLEEDING CLASSIFICATION AND THRESHOLDS

**Some blood loss expected:** 0.5-1.0ml/kg/hr in first 6 hrs often acceptable

**Excessive bleeding defined as:**
* **Drain output >8-10ml/kg/hr** OR
* **Drain outputs:**
  * >500ml in first hour
  * >400ml/hr for 2 hrs
  * >300ml/hr for 3 hrs
  * >200ml/hr for 4 hrs

#### 10.2.3.1. MEDICAL VS SURGICAL BLEEDING

**Medical Bleeding Causes:**
* Residual heparin/heparin rebound
* Coagulopathy
* Platelet dysfunction
* Fibrinolysis

**Surgical Bleeding Sources:**
* Anastomotic sites (suture lines)
* Side branches of arterial or venous conduits
* Substernal soft tissues, sternal suture sites, bone marrow, periosteum
* Raw surfaces from previous surgery
* Pericarditis

**Management Principle:** Bleeding not responding to blood products usually prompts immediate surgical re-exploration.

### 10.2.4. PATIENT BLOOD MANAGEMENT GUIDELINES

#### 10.2.4.1. PREOPERATIVE OPTIMIZATION

* **Optimize Hb/Anemia**
* **Anticoagulant/antiplatelet management:**
  * Appropriate cessation timing
  * Platelet Function Testing
* **Check for Traditional Chinese Medicine usage**

#### 10.2.4.2. INTRAOPERATIVE STRATEGIES

* **Use anti-fibrinolytics:** Tranexamic acid
* **Goal-directed transfusion algorithms:** ROTEM/TEG guided coagulopathy management
* **Cell Saver utilization**
* **Acute Normovolemic Haemodilution and Retrograde Priming**
* **Minimize Haemodilution**

#### 10.2.4.3. POSTOPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

* **Normothermia maintenance**
* **Acid/Base Balance optimization**
* **Continued bleeding vigilance:** Early reopening if indicated
* **Management algorithms** for bleeding
* **Transfusion trigger:** Hb 7-8g/dl

### 10.2.5. THROMBOELASTOGRAPHY (TEG) GUIDED MANAGEMENT
<!-- EPA-10:K3:teg_rotem EPA-10:EQ2:teg_rotem -->

**TEG Function:**
* **Viscoelastic hemostatic assay** measuring global properties of whole blood clot formation under low shear stress
* **Shows interaction** of platelets with coagulation cascade (aggregation, clot strengthening, fibrin cross-linking, fibrinolysis)

**It is reasonable to give 1 unit platelets** for routine CABG if:
* Surgeon requests it
* Surgical field looks oozy
* Patient hasn't stopped anti-platelet medications

**Blood product administration otherwise guided by ROTEM or TEG6**

**In NUH practice:**
* **Factor concentrates (Octaplex)** and **fibrinogen concentrate** usually administered guided by ROTEM/TEG
* **Preference over FFPs and cryoprecipitate**

## 10.3. RE-EXPLORATION FOR BLEEDING

### 10.3.1. INCIDENCE AND INDICATIONS

**Overall reexploration rate:** ~4.2% of cardiac surgical patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**Bleeding-specific:** 3.9% return for bleeding [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Triggers for reexploration:**
* **Hemodynamic instability** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Anemia** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Persistent chest tube output >200 mL/hr** despite correction of coagulopathy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Consequences of delay:** Cardiac tamponade → decreased cardiac output, compromised organ perfusion, poor long-term prognosis [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 10.3.2. ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS FOR RE-EXPLORATION

**Emergency re-exploration considerations:**
* **Avoid SVR compromise:** Ketamine often induction agent of choice [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Surgical readiness:** Prepped chest, gowned team before induction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Post-sternotomy:** Expect significant BP increase [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Risk:** Acute BP elevation may cause additional bleeding if surgical anastomoses were challenging due to diseased tissues [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

## 10.4. CARDIAC TAMPONADE
<!-- EPA-10:EQ2:cardiac_tamponade -->

**May occur early** (within first 24hrs) or **late** (5-7days post-op)

**Early tamponade:** Secondary to surgical bleeding or coagulopathy

### 10.4.1. CARDIAC TAMPONADE PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

**Mechanism:** Elevated pericardial pressure compresses cardiac chambers, falsely elevating measured intracardiac pressures [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 10.4.1.1. CHAMBER COMPRESSION PATTERN

* **Most affected:** Chambers with lowest intracardiac pressure (RA, LA, RV) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Diagnostic gold standard:** Chamber collapse on echocardiography [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 10.4.1.2. HEMODYNAMIC COMPENSATION

* **Cardiac output:** Limited stroke volume → tachycardia primary component [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **SVR:** Significantly increased to enhance venous return and RV filling [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 10.4.1.3. VENTRICULAR INTERDEPENDENCE

**Mechanical ventilation:**
* **Inspiration:** ↑ intrathoracic pressure → impedes RV, augments LV filling [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Diastolic flow:** ↓ tricuspid, ↑ mitral flow [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Spontaneous ventilation:** Reciprocal pattern occurs [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 10.4.2. RECOGNITION AND DIAGNOSIS

**Early recognition saves lives**

**Clinical Signs (may be non-specific):**
* **Low cardiac output state**
* **Tachycardia and hypotension**
* **Cool peripheries/increasing SVR**
* **Raised JVP/CVP**
* **Acid/base derangement, increasing lactate levels**
* **Sudden cardiovascular collapse/cardiac arrest**

**Point of Care Cardiac Ultrasound/TEE Findings:**
* **Pericardial collection**
* **Right atrial systolic collapse**
* **Right ventricular diastolic collapse**
* **Paradoxical motion of interventricular septum**
* **Swinging heart and enlarged, non-pulsatile inferior vena cava**
* **Reciprocal variation in ventricular size**
* **Trans-mitral and trans-tricuspid doppler-flow velocity paradoxus**

### 10.4.3. MANAGEMENT

**Immediate sternotomy and surgical re-exploration**
* **May require bedside chest opening** in ICU
* **Correction of coagulopathy/surgical cause** for bleeding

### 10.6.1. TRANSFER TO ICU

**Standard Transfer Process:**
* **Patients transferred to CTICU** post-operatively
* **Propofol infusion** for sedation during transport
* **Dexmedetomidine** can be used for fast track extubation candidates
* **Avoid additional muscle relaxant doses** prior to transfer

* **Handover to ICU:** Vital communication with nursing and medical staff - template available

### 10.6.2. MECHANICAL VENTILATION AND EXTUBATION

**Routine Management:**
* **Mechanical ventilation continued** 4-6 hours
* **Sedation and weaning** during this period

**Extubation Criteria:**
* **Adequate oxygenation and gas exchange:** Minimal mechanical ventilatory support required
* **Hemodynamically stable:** Minimal inotropic/vasopressor support
* **Normal organ perfusion indicators:** Acid/base balance, urine output, lactate levels, mental status
* **Normothermia**
* **Acceptable drain output:** Minimal bleeding and coagulopathy

### 10.6.3. EARLY EXTUBATION CANDIDATES

**Simple lesions:** No ventricular incisions (ASD, VSD) can often be extubated at conclusion or shortly after in ICU.

**High-Risk for Ventilatory Failure:**
* **Age <6 months** (pediatric)
* **Complex surgeries** with long CPB/circulatory arrest time
* **Down syndrome**
* **Pulmonary hypertension** requiring preoperative ventilatory support
* **Postoperative cardiovascular/pulmonary complications**
* **High inotropic support requirement**

## 10.9. DEEP STERNAL WOUND INFECTION (DSWI)
<!-- EPA-10:K4:dswi EPA-10:EQ3:wound_complications -->

**Deep sternal wound infection (DSWI), also termed mediastinitis, is a devastating complication following median sternotomy with significant morbidity, mortality, and healthcare costs.**

### 10.9.1. DEFINITION AND EPIDEMIOLOGY

**Definition:** DSWI encompasses clinical or microbiological evidence of infected presternal tissue and **sternal osteomyelitis** with or without mediastinal sepsis and sternal instability [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Epidemiology:**
* **Incidence:** 0.5-5% following cardiac surgery (varies by center and patient population)
* **Mortality:** 9-29% (highest with true mediastinitis: 82% survival vs 94-95% without DSWI)
* **Hospital stay:** Mean 33 days vs 9 days without DSWI
* **Healthcare costs:** Approximately $211,000 vs $82,000 per patient

[Source: Surgery Journal 2025; PMC 2018]

### 10.9.2. CLASSIFICATION SYSTEMS

#### 10.9.2.1. CDC CLASSIFICATION
<!-- EPA-10:K4:cdc_classification -->

**Distinguishes two categories:**
* **Deep Incisional Surgical Site Infection (diSSI):** Involves suprasternal tissues only
* **Organ-Space Mediastinitis:** Infection of substernal area or sternal bone

**CDC Diagnostic Criteria** (requires at least one):
* Organism identified from mediastinal tissue/fluid by culture or nonculture methods
* Evidence of mediastinitis on anatomic/histopathologic examination
* Patient with fever (>38C), chest pain, or sternal instability PLUS purulent drainage or mediastinal widening on imaging

#### 10.9.2.2. EL OAKLEY-WRIGHT CLASSIFICATION

**Five types based on risk factors and timing post-surgery:**

| Type | Timing | Risk Factors | Notes |
|------|--------|--------------|-------|
| **I** | <=2 weeks | None | Early, no comorbidities |
| **II** | 2-6 weeks | None | Intermediate timing |
| **IIIa** | <=2 weeks | >=1 risk factor | Early with comorbidity |
| **IIIb** | 2-6 weeks | >=1 risk factor | Intermediate with comorbidity |
| **IVa/b** | Any | Failed therapy | Failed previous treatment |
| **V** | >6 weeks | Any | Late presentation |

**Risk factors included:** Diabetes, obesity, COPD, immunosuppression

#### 10.9.2.3. PAIROLERO AND ARNOLD CLASSIFICATION

**By timing of presentation:**
* **Type I:** Early separation (first days) - usually mechanical dehiscence
* **Type II:** Fulminant mediastinitis (first weeks) - acute infection
* **Type III:** Chronic infection (months to years) - indolent course

### 10.9.3. RISK FACTORS FOR DSWI
<!-- EPA-10:K4:dswi_risk_factors -->

#### 10.9.3.1. PREOPERATIVE FACTORS

| Factor | Evidence |
|--------|----------|
| **Diabetes mellitus** | OR 2.0; Poor glycemic control increases risk |
| **Obesity (elevated BMI)** | OR 1.076 per unit BMI increase |
| **Chronic lung disease/COPD** | OR 2.47 |
| **Advanced age** | OR 1.055 per year |
| **Female sex** | Independent predictor |
| **Smoking** | Impaired wound healing |
| **Renal failure** | Immune dysfunction |
| **Steroid use** | Immunosuppression |
| **NYHA class >=3** | Reflects poor functional status |

#### 10.9.3.2. INTRAOPERATIVE FACTORS

* **Bilateral Internal Mammary Artery (BIMA) Grafting:**
  - Higher DSWI risk than single IMA (OR 1.30)
  - **Skeletonized technique reduces risk:** 1.3% vs 11.1% pedicled (P=0.03)
  - Risk mainly in **severe chronic diabetes**
* **Prolonged operative time**
* **Prolonged CPB time**
* **Concomitant cardiac procedures**
* **Increased number of grafts**

#### 10.9.3.3. POSTOPERATIVE FACTORS

* **Prolonged mechanical ventilation**
* **Re-exploration for bleeding** (major predictor)
* **Blood product transfusion**
* **Prolonged inotropic support**

### 10.9.4. MICROBIOLOGY
<!-- EPA-10:K4:dswi_microbiology -->

**DSWI is predominantly caused by gram-positive organisms (89%):**

| Organism | Frequency |
|----------|-----------|
| **Coagulase-negative staphylococci** | 56% |
| **Staphylococcus aureus (MSSA)** | 24% (45% in some series) |
| **MRSA** | 16% |
| **Gram-negative organisms** | 10-17% |
| **Streptococci** | 5% |

**Important considerations:**
* Gram-negative and fungal organisms more common in **treatment failures** and **prolonged therapy**
* Positive blood cultures (especially S. aureus) may identify sternal osteomyelitis as infection source
* **Biofilm formation** on sternal wires complicates eradication

### 10.9.5. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND DIAGNOSIS
<!-- EPA-10:K4:dswi_diagnosis -->

#### 10.9.5.1. EARLY PRESENTATION (within 30 days)

| Sign | Frequency |
|------|-----------|
| **Wound dehiscence** | 75% |
| **Wound discharge** | 69% |
| **Sternal instability** | 51% |
| **Fever** | 29% |

#### 10.9.5.2. CHRONIC PRESENTATION

* Delayed wound healing
* **Sternocutaneous fistula**
* Persistent drainage
* Low-grade fever

#### 10.9.5.3. LABORATORY FINDINGS

* **WBC, CRP, ESR:** Elevated but nonspecific post-cardiac surgery
* **Blood cultures:** Positive in 18-57% of acute cases
* **Wound cultures:** Essential for targeted therapy

#### 10.9.5.4. IMAGING MODALITIES

| Modality | Findings/Advantages |
|----------|---------------------|
| **Chest X-ray** | Mediastinal widening, air-fluid levels, pneumomediastinum |
| **CT scan** | Superior for extent assessment; fluid collections, abscess, extension |
| **MRI** | Early osteomyelitis detection; bone marrow changes on T1/T2 |
| **PET-CT** | Localizing disease, monitoring treatment response |

### 10.9.6. PREVENTION STRATEGIES
<!-- EPA-10:K4:dswi_prevention -->

#### 10.9.6.1. ANTIMICROBIAL PROPHYLAXIS

* **Agent:** Cefazolin or cefuroxime (first/second generation cephalosporin)
* **Timing:** Within **60 minutes before incision**
* **Duration:** Continue **<=48 hours** postoperatively
* **Redosing:** If procedure exceeds 4 hours
* **Alternatives:** Vancomycin for Type 1 penicillin allergy or high MRSA prevalence

#### 10.9.6.2. GLYCEMIC CONTROL

* **Target:** Blood glucose **<180 mg/dL (10 mmol/L)**
* **Duration:** Intraoperatively and first **24 hours postoperatively** (longer if extended ICU stay)
* **Method:** **Continuous insulin infusion** superior to intermittent subcutaneous (DSWI 0.8% vs 2.0%, P=0.01)
* **Evidence Level:** STS/EACTS Guidelines Class I, Level B

#### 10.9.6.3. ADDITIONAL PREVENTION MEASURES

| Intervention | Evidence |
|--------------|----------|
| **Intranasal mupirocin** | Two RCTs show benefit in reducing DSWI |
| **MRSA screening and decolonization** | Standard of care in many centers |
| **Topical vancomycin paste** | Meta-analysis: substantial reduction in DSWI |
| **Gentamicin-collagen sponges** | Significant risk reduction |
| **Surgical technique:** Skeletonized IMA harvest | Preserves sternal blood supply |
| **External support vests (Posthorax)** | Reduced reoperation 3.9% to 0.6% |

### 10.9.7. MANAGEMENT OF DSWI
<!-- EPA-10:K4:dswi_management EPA-10:S3:wound_management -->

#### 10.9.7.1. SURGICAL MANAGEMENT

**Principles:**
* **Early and sufficient surgical debridement** is mandatory
* **Remove all necrotic tissue** and infected sternal wires
* **Assess sternal viability** for reconstruction options

**Treatment Options:**

| Approach | Description | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| **Traditional** | Debridement, primary closure, mediastinal catheter irrigation | Older technique |
| **VAC/NPWT** | Negative Pressure Wound Therapy | First-line in many centers |
| **Muscle flap** | Pectoralis major advancement/turnover | Good blood supply, coverage |
| **Omental flap** | Greater omentum transposition | >95% success rate |
| **Titanium plating** | Rigid sternal fixation | SternaLock, Sternal Talon systems |

#### 10.9.7.2. NEGATIVE PRESSURE WOUND THERAPY (NPWT/VAC)

**Mechanism:**
* Drains bacteria, debris, and exudates
* Enhances microcirculation
* Promotes tissue granulation
* Reduces wound oedema

**Protocol:**
* **Pressure:** 75-125 mmHg continuous negative pressure
* **Dressing changes:** Twice weekly until cultures negative
* **Criteria for VAC removal:** Negative bacterial culture, afebrile, falling CRP, healthy granulating wound

**Outcomes:**
* Shorter ICU and hospital stay
* Faster wound healing
* Reduced reinfection rates
* Average hospitalization 25 +/- 20 days
* Mortality approximately 15%

**Complications:**
* Major complications in **7.2%** of patients
* **Bleeding** from CABG grafts (most serious)
* Right ventricular rupture from sharp sternal edges
* Secondary infection (VAC foam may harbour organisms)

**Important considerations:**
* VAC foam can be reservoir for organisms (97% of foams culture positive)
* Pressure <120 mmHg may cause foil dismantling

#### 10.9.7.3. ANTIMICROBIAL THERAPY

**Empiric Therapy:**
* Initiate after microbiological sampling
* **Target coverage:** CoNS, S. aureus (including MRSA if prevalent), Gram-negatives, anaerobes
* **Agents:** Piperacillin-tazobactam, imipenem, or meropenem +/- vancomycin/daptomycin

**Duration:** **6-12 weeks total** (2 weeks IV, then oral)

**Targeted Therapy by Organism:**

| Organism | IV Phase (2 weeks) | Oral Phase |
|----------|-------------------|------------|
| **MSSA** | Flucloxacillin +/- fosfomycin | Rifampicin + fluoroquinolone/cotrimoxazole |
| **MRSA** | Daptomycin or vancomycin +/- fosfomycin | Rifampicin-based combination |
| **Enterococci** | Ampicillin + gentamicin | Based on sensitivities |
| **Gram-negatives** | Piperacillin-tazobactam/meropenem | Ciprofloxacin |
| **Candida** | Caspofungin/anidulafungin 1-2 weeks | Fluconazole suppression >=1 year |

**Key points:**
* **Rifampicin-containing regimens** improve outcomes for susceptible staphylococcal osteomyelitis
* Rifampicin has **antibiofilm activity** critical for wire-associated infection
* Monitor: Leukocytes, CRP, renal function, liver enzymes twice weekly

### 10.9.8. ANAESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS FOR RE-OPERATION
<!-- EPA-10:S3:reop_anaesthesia -->

**Patients requiring re-operation for DSWI present significant anaesthetic challenges:**

#### 10.9.8.1. PATIENT FACTORS

* Often **septic/haemodynamically unstable**
* May have **multi-organ dysfunction**
* Prolonged ICU stay with **deconditioning**
* Previous cardiac surgery with **altered anatomy**
* Potential **coagulopathy** from sepsis

#### 10.9.8.2. PROCEDURAL CONSIDERATIONS

**VAC Dressing Changes:**
* May require **adequate analgesia/sedation** in ICU
* Some patients tolerate with local anaesthesia
* Consider ketamine sedation for haemodynamic stability

**Debridement/Flap Surgery:**
* Often **prolonged procedures** (3-6+ hours)
* **Significant blood loss** anticipated
* Have blood products immediately available
* Consider **cell salvage** if infection controlled

**Specific Risks:**
* **Bleeding from CABG grafts** - 7.2% major complication rate with VAC
* **Right ventricular injury** from sharp sternal edges
* **Cardiac tamponade** if pericardium opened

#### 10.9.8.3. ANAESTHETIC TECHNIQUE

* **Induction:** Ketamine-based to maintain SVR; expect compromised patients
* **Maintenance:** Consider haemodynamic goals similar to primary cardiac surgery
* **Monitoring:** Arterial line, central venous access, consider TOE if cardiac function concerns
* **Blood pressure management:** Avoid acute hypertension that may stress anastomoses or grafts
* **Temperature:** Maintain normothermia for coagulation and immune function
* **Vasopressor/inotrope support:** Often required

#### 10.9.8.4. MULTIDISCIPLINARY TEAM

**Optimal outcomes require:**
* Cardiothoracic surgeons
* Plastic/reconstructive surgeons
* Infectious disease specialists
* Clinical microbiologists
* Intensivists
* Cardiac anaesthesiologists

**Bottom Line for DSWI:** Deep sternal wound infection requires early recognition, aggressive surgical debridement, appropriate antimicrobial therapy targeting likely organisms (predominantly staphylococci), and may involve complex reconstruction. Prevention through glycemic control, appropriate antibiotic prophylaxis, and surgical technique is paramount.

# 11. MINIMALLY INVASIVE CARDIAC SURGERY

> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Anaesthesia considerations/technique for cardiac surgical procedures|Part B: Anaesthesia Considerations - Minimally Invasive Techniques]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Interventional cardiology|Interventional Cardiology]]

**Minimally invasive cardiac surgery represents the evolution toward reduced surgical trauma while maintaining optimal outcomes.**

MICS prioritizes avoidance of complete median sternotomy, using smaller incisions (thoracotomy, mini-sternotomy, transverse sternotomy) with or without CPB and robotic assistance [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. Suggested benefits include less pain, decreased scarring, and shorter recovery times [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

## 11.1. MIDCAB AND OPCAB

### 11.1.1. MINIMALLY DIRECT CORONARY ARTERY BYPASS (MIDCAB)

**Approach:** LIMA harvesting and LAD anastomosis via limited left thoracotomy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Anesthetic requirements:**
* **Lung isolation:** Double lumen ETT or single lumen + bronchial blocker [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Positioning:** Supine with 30° left hemithorax elevation, left arm tucked [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Preparation:** Chest and groins prepped for potential sternotomy/femoral bypass [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Procedure:**
* **Access:** Anterolateral thoracotomy (4th/5th left intercostal space) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Stabilization:** Pressure or vacuum-assisted myocardial stabilizers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Anticoagulation:** Heparin (target ACT >300 seconds) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Completion:** Protamine, hemostasis, left pleural chest tube [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Applications:** One or two vessel CABG (LIMA to LAD or composite grafts) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 11.1.1.1. MIDCAB ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS

**Lung Isolation Required:**
* **Double lumen tube:** Commonly left sided DLT
* **Alternative:** Single lumen tube with bronchial blocker

**Positioning Specifics:**
* **Patient positioned supine** with left chest elevated to right by 20-30 degrees
* **Roll placed** below lateral left chest and left buttock
* **Left arm tucked** below left chest to expose lateral part
* **External defibrillator pads required**

**Key Points:**
* **Brachial plexus injury possible:** Left shoulder lowered can cause excessive retraction
* **Ensure defibrillator pads placed** for all redo and minimally invasive surgery
* **Re-check DLT position** before and after positioning

### 11.1.2. OFF-PUMP CORONARY ARTERY BYPASS (OPCAB)

**Rationale:** Achieve revascularization while minimizing deleterious CPB effects (stroke, renal injury) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Approach:** Median sternotomy without CPB or aortic cross-clamping [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 11.1.2.1. TECHNICAL CONSIDERATIONS

**Preparation:**
* **Generous pericardiotomy:** Facilitate cardiac mobilization and vessel access [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Heparin:** ACT 300 seconds or institutional preference [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Vessel Access Strategy:**
* **Anterior wall (LAD):** Relatively easy access [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Inferior/lateral walls:** Require heart verticalization [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Verticalization challenges:** Apex at zenith, compromised forward ejection and stroke volume [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Stabilization devices:**
* **Examples:** Octopus/Medtronic, Ultima II/Guidant, Acrobat-i/Macquet [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Function:** Compress ventricles for local immobility [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Grafting order (typical):** Anterior → inferior → lateral wall vessels [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 11.1.2.2. MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES

**Hemodynamic support:**
* **Epicardial pacing wires:** Target HR 80 bpm to increase stroke volume [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Intracoronary shunts:** Mitigate ongoing ischemia during coronary occlusion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Conversion to CPB:** For technical difficulties or refractory instability [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 11.1.2.3. TABLE 39-21: OPCAB ANESTHETIC CONSIDERATIONS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Monitoring and Access:**
* Invasive arterial BP, central venous access
* Consider PAC when LV function poor or severe MR present
* TEE essential unless contraindicated
* ECG and TEE may not detect ischemia during verticalization

**Temperature Management:**
* Prioritize normothermia: warming devices, heated circuits, optimize room temperature

**Hemodynamic Challenges:**
* Related to heart positioning, stabilizer application, ischemia, atrial compression, compromised preload
* Use Trendelenburg, volume administration, vasoactive medications

**Specific Concerns:**
* RV dysfunction and compromised graft patency → persistent hemodynamic instability
* Ensure immediate CPB and MCS availability

#### 11.1.2.4. OPCAB OUTCOMES

💡 **Benefits:** Decreased transfusion rates, reduced renal injury, lower resource utilization [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

⚠️ **Limitations:** Higher incomplete revascularization rates, technically challenging [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Stroke rates:** Similar to on-pump CABG despite absence of CPB-associated emboli [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Bottom Line:** MIDCAB and OPCAB techniques offer alternatives to conventional CABG but require specialized anesthetic management and immediate backup capabilities for conversion to CPB.

## 11.2. ROBOTIC VALVULAR SURGERY

### 11.2.1. MICS MITRAL VALVE SURGERY

**Evolution:** From right anterior lateral minithoracotomy to robotic approaches with five small port incisions [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Essential requirements:**
* **Right lung isolation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Peripheral CPB:** Femoral artery and vein access [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Superior drainage:** Right internal jugular vein SVC cannula [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 11.2.2. MICS AORTIC VALVE SURGERY

**Approach:** Right mini anterior thoracotomy lateral to sternum (4th intercostal space) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Advantages:**
* **Anterior AV position:** Accessible through 3-cm incision [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Right lung isolation required** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Peripheral CPB cannulation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **No superior SVC cannula needed** (anterior AV position allows adequate visualization) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Robotic and MICS valvular surgery requires lung isolation and peripheral CPB but offers potential benefits of reduced surgical trauma.

## 11.3. PERCUTANEOUS APPROACHES

### 11.3.1. TRANSCATHETER AORTIC VALVE REPLACEMENT (TAVR/TAVI)
<!-- EPA-10:K6:tavi -->

**Evidence:** PARTNER trials demonstrated noninferiority to surgical AV replacement [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Technique:** Expandable AV delivered via femoral artery, deployed with balloon inflation or self-expansion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 11.3.1.1. ANESTHETIC MANAGEMENT

* **Standard approach:** Monitored anesthesia care [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Alternative:** General anesthesia (institutional preference) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **Major Risks:**
* **Structural injury:** AV annular disruption, AD, ventricular tear, femoral artery injury [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Stroke:** Calcium debris showering from native valve [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Debris protection devices:** Carotid artery "straining" devices available [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Emergency preparedness:** Pre-determined rescue strategies essential for catastrophic events [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 11.3.2. PERCUTANEOUS MITRAL VALVE INTERVENTIONS

#### 11.3.2.1. MITRACLIP

**Indication:** High surgical risk MR patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Technique:** Edge-to-edge grasping of pathologic mitral valve regions [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Evidence:** Successful outcomes in both degenerative and functional MR [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 11.3.2.2. ANESTHETIC MANAGEMENT

* **General anesthesia required** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **TEE guidance:** Essential for interventional cardiologist placement [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Access:** Femoral vein with trans-septal crossing [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Monitoring:** Echocardiographic and fluoroscopic guidance for clip positioning and MR impact assessment [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Percutaneous valve interventions require careful anesthetic planning with emergency preparedness for rare but catastrophic complications, while providing options for high-risk surgical patients.

# 12. PEDIATRIC CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE

> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|Part B: S6 Cardiac Surgery]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S4 Paediatric Anaesthesia|S4 Paediatric Anaesthesia]]

**Pediatric cardiac anesthesia represents one of the most challenging subspecialties due to complex congenital lesions and unique physiologic considerations.**

## 12.1. INTRODUCTION AND CLASSIFICATION

**Incidence:** Stable worldwide at 17.9 per 1,000 live births, but prevalence continues to increase [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Complexity:** High heterogeneity of cardiac lesions, best understood by following blood pathway for systemic and pulmonary circulation ("anatomy dictates physiology") [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 12.1.1. TABLE 39-22: PHYSIOLOGIC EFFECTS OF CONGENITAL CARDIAC LESIONS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Volume overload (ventricle or atrium) resulting in increased pulmonary blood flow:**
* **Atrial septal defect:** ↑ flow, ↓ pressure
* **Ventricular septal defect:** ↑ flow, ↑ pressure  
* **Patent ductus arteriosus:** ↑ flow, ↑ pressure
* **Endocardial cushion defect:** ↑ flow, ↑ pressure

**Cyanosis resulting from obstruction to pulmonary blood flow:**
* **Tetralogy of Fallot**
* **Tricuspid atresia**
* **Pulmonary atresia**

**Pressure overload to the ventricle:**
* **Aortic stenosis**
* **Coarctation of the aorta**
* **Pulmonary stenosis**

**Cyanosis due to a common mixing chamber:**
* **Total anomalous venous return**
* **Truncus arteriosus**
* **Double outlet right ventricle**
* **Single ventricle**

**Cyanosis due to separation of the systemic and pulmonary circulation:**
* **Transposition of the great vessels**

**Bottom Line:** CHD classification by physiologic impact helps predict hemodynamic consequences and guide anesthetic management.

## 12.2. PREOPERATIVE EVALUATION

### 12.2.1. HISTORY

#### 12.2.1.1. INFANT HF MANIFESTATIONS

* **Feeding difficulties** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Easy fatigability** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Lethargy** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Labored breathing** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Failure to thrive** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 12.2.1.2. OLDER CHILD HF MANIFESTATIONS

* **Easy fatigability** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Shortness of breath** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Dyspnea on exertion** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 12.2.1.3. ASSESSMENT PARAMETERS

* **Age and weight** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Baseline oxygen saturation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Medications** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Supplemental oxygen requirement** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Associated comorbidities** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Previous surgical history and cardiac catheterization data** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 12.2.2. PHYSICAL EXAMINATION

**Uncompensated lesions signs:**
* **Failure to thrive** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Diaphoresis** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Tachycardia** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Rales and/or jugular venous distension** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Extremity assessment:**
* **Cyanosis, clubbing, edema, pulse volume** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Blood pressure measurement in arms and legs** (rule out coarctation) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

💡 **Important considerations:**
* **Previous Blalock-Taussig shunt or subclavian flap:** Upper extremity pulses may be reduced/absent on shunt side [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Extracardiac anomalies:** May be present in up to 20% of CHD patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 12.2.3. LABORATORY EVALUATIONS

#### 12.2.3.1. CYANOTIC LESIONS

* **Polycythemia:** Consequence of bone marrow stimulation (erythropoietin release) from arterial desaturation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Complications:** Hyperviscosity, peripheral sludging, reduced oxygen delivery [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Risk factors:** Dehydration from fasting, hypothermia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Hematocrit >70%:** Consider preoperative electrophoresis if symptomatic hyperviscosity [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 12.2.3.2. COAGULATION ISSUES

* **Polycythemia effects:** Low-grade DIC, fibrinolysis activation, platelet degranulation, coagulation factor consumption [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Newborn factors:** Inadequate coagulation factors (hepatic immaturity) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **CPB effects:** Significant coagulation factor dilution, platelet dysfunction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Required tests:** Platelet count, PT, PTT [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 12.2.3.3. OTHER CONSIDERATIONS

* **Diuretic therapy:** Risk for hypokalemia (especially with digitalis) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Infants with CHF:** Risk for hypoglycemia and hypocalcemia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Previous blood exposure:** Increased risk of abnormal antibodies to blood antigens [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 12.2.4. CARDIAC EVALUATIONS

**Echocardiography:** Delineates most cardiac anatomy, permits noninvasive measurement of ventricular size/function, cardiac output, valve dysfunction severity [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Cardiac catheterization:** Reserved for poor echo windows or unclear findings [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Advanced imaging:** Cardiac MRI, 3D printed heart models for complex anatomy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 12.2.4.1. CHEST RADIOGRAPH ASSESSMENT [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

* **Cardiac position, size, shape**
* **Abnormal vessels, right aortic arch**
* **Scimitar syndrome** (right lung/PA hypoplasia)
* **Aberrant pulmonary vessels, abnormal bronchi position**
* **Vascular rings, associated pulmonary abnormalities**

**ECG:** Rate and rhythm abnormalities assessment [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Bottom Line:** Comprehensive preoperative evaluation requires assessment of physiologic impact, associated anomalies, and careful preparation for perioperative complications.

## 12.3. INTRAOPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

### 12.3.1. PREMEDICATION

**Goal:** Calm child without loss of protective airway reflexes or hemodynamic compromise [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**Purpose:** Facilitate parent separation, reduce perioperative anxiety [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 12.3.2. MONITORING

**Standard monitors plus:**
* **Peripheral and central temperature monitoring** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Invasive blood pressure** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **CVP monitoring** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **LAP monitoring** (via surgeon-placed catheter in selected cases) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **TEE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

⚠️ **TEE considerations:** Airway or aortic arch compression can occur in smaller patients (<4 kg) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 12.3.3. ANESTHETIC MANAGEMENT

#### 12.3.3.1. AGENT SELECTION

**Primary agents:** Inhalational agents for induction and maintenance [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Special considerations:**
* **Poor ventricular function:** May need IV access preinduction, avoid/limit myocardial depressants [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Pulmonary hypertension:** May need IV access preinduction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Critical SVR/PVR dependence:** May need IV access preinduction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 12.3.3.2. PHYSIOLOGIC GOALS

**Balance:** Total pulmonary blood flow (Qp) to total systemic blood flow (Qs) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**Maintain:** Contractility, heart rate, systemic perfusion [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 12.3.3.3. SPECIFIC LESION CONSIDERATIONS

**Left-to-right shunt lesions (VSD, AV canal):**
* **Vigilance required** during induction while oxygenating and ventilating [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Right-to-left shunt lesions (TOF):**
* **Slow inhalation induction** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **De-air IV catheters and lines** (prevent paradoxical air embolism) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 12.3.4. NEONATAL CONSIDERATIONS

**Distinct myocardial characteristics:**
* **Limited preload reserve** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Afterload mismatch** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Reduced ventricular compliance and contractile mass** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Increased interventricular dependence** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Incomplete autonomic sympathetic system** with dominant parasympathetic innervation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 12.3.5. CPB IN PEDIATRICS

**Significant stress response:** Neonates and infants undergoing deep hypothermic CPB [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**Neuromuscular blocking:** Marked reduction in requirements during hypothermic bypass [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 12.3.5.1. COAGULOPATHY MANAGEMENT

**Contributing factors:**
* **RBC and clotting factor dilution** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Clotting cascade activation and consumption** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Fibrinolytic pathway activation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Treatment:** Aminocaproic acid or tranexamic acid to attenuate CPB-associated coagulopathy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 12.3.5.2. CPB SEPARATION

**Requirements:** Some patients need pharmacologic and/or pacing support [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**Pulmonary hypertension:** Addition of inhaled pulmonary vasodilators may be beneficial [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Bottom Line:** Pediatric cardiac anesthesia requires understanding of unique neonatal physiology, lesion-specific considerations, and specialized CPB management.

## 12.4. POSTOPERATIVE MONITORING

### 12.4.1. LOW CARDIAC OUTPUT SYNDROME (LCOS)

**Incidence:** ~25% of children experience decreased cardiac index (<2 L/min/m²) within 6-18 hours postoperatively [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 12.4.1.1. CONTRIBUTING FACTORS

* **Ventricular dysfunction** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Change in loading conditions** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **SVR and PVR changes** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Ventriculotomy** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Myocardial edema** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Refractory arrhythmias** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Residual lesions** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 12.4.1.2. MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES

* **Close monitoring:** Tissue oxygenation assessment (mixed venous oxygen saturation, lactate trends) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Cardiovascular function assessment** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Ventricular preload optimization** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Judicious inotropic agent use** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Mechanical ventilation:** Decrease oxygen demand and afterload [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Rare cases:** MCS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 12.4.2. PULMONARY HYPERTENSION CRISIS

**RV sensitivity:** Extremely sensitive to afterload increases [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].  
**Consequence:** Acute pulmonary hypertension may lead to severe circulatory compromise and cardiac arrest [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 12.4.2.1. CLINICAL FEATURES

* **Rapid PA pressure increase** (exceeding systemic pressure) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Abrupt EtCO2 decrease** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Hypoxemia** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Hypotension** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Increased CVP** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Bronchospasm** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 12.4.2.2. MANAGEMENT

* **100% oxygen** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Moderate hyperventilation** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Epinephrine and calcium** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Inotropes** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Inhaled pulmonary vasodilators** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Correction of metabolic and respiratory acidosis** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

💡 **Importance:** Close monitoring, early recognition, and timely intervention vital for preventing cardiac dysfunction, LCOS, and hemodynamic collapse [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

## 12.5. SPECIAL POPULATIONS

### 12.5.1. ADULTS WITH CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE (ACHD)

**Demographics:** Current distribution of age at death approximates general population [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]. Number of ACHD exceeds children with CHD in US [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Perioperative challenge:** Despite survival benefits, perioperative death rates higher than peers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 12.5.1.1. COMMON ACHD AFTER REPAIR/PALLIATION

* **Atrial septal defects** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Ventricular septal defects** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Tetralogy of Fallot** (after transannular patch) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Common atrioventricular canal defects** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Coarctation of the aorta** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Transposition of the great arteries** (after atrial/arterial switch) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Congenitally corrected transposition** (after double switch) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Anesthetic considerations:** Depends on residual lesion amount, functional status, and surgical procedure complexity [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

**Specialist care:** CHD anesthesiologists better suited for moderate/severe residual lesions, significant pulmonary hypertension, single ventricle patients [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

## 12.6. TRACHEAL EXTUBATION AND RECOVERY

### 12.6.1. EARLY EXTUBATION CANDIDATES

**Simple lesions:** No ventricular incisions (ASD, VSD) can often be extubated at conclusion or shortly after in ICU [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 12.6.2. HIGH-RISK FOR VENTILATORY FAILURE

* **Age <6 months** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Complex surgeries** with long CPB/circulatory arrest time [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Down syndrome** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Pulmonary hypertension** requiring preoperative ventilatory support [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Postoperative cardiovascular/pulmonary complications** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **High inotropic support requirement** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

### 12.6.3. SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS

#### 12.6.3.1. FONTAN PHYSIOLOGY

* **Physiology:** Passive pulmonary circulation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Key principle:** Decreasing PVR paramount, very dependent on adequate ventilation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Challenge:** Positive pressure ventilation has deleterious effect on pulmonary blood flow [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Benefit:** Pain-free spontaneous respiration enhances hemodynamic performance [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 12.6.3.2. ALTERNATIVE VENTILATORY SUPPORT

**Infants:** Nasal CPAP can be employed instead of mechanical ventilation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

### 12.6.4. REGIONAL ANESTHESIA

**Multimodal analgesia component:** Supplement intraoperative anesthesia, provide postoperative analgesia, facilitate early extubation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]].

#### 12.6.4.1. TECHNIQUES

* **Caudal (epidural) opioids** for coarctation repair or PDA ligation [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Intercostal nerve blocks** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Ultrasound-guided blocks:** Bilateral paravertebral, erector spinae plane, transverse thoracic muscle plane [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

#### 12.6.4.2. BENEFITS

* **Decreased stress response** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Improved pulmonary and gastrointestinal function** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]
* **Potential cost reduction** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#S6 Cardiac surgery and interventional cardiology|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Pediatric cardiac surgery recovery requires specialized monitoring for LCOS and pulmonary hypertension crisis, with individualized approaches to ventilatory support and multimodal analgesia to optimize outcomes.

# 13. BASIC PERIOPERATIVE TEE AND CARDIAC POCUS

> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#Monitoring: specific issues|Part B: Monitoring - Echocardiography]]

**Basic perioperative TEE represents an essential monitoring and diagnostic tool for cardiac anesthesia.**

## 13.1. BASIC PERIOPERATIVE TEE EXAMINATION

**The basic perioperative TEE exam typically involves evaluation of cardiac function using 11-16 TEE views.**

### 13.1.1. STANDARD TEE VIEWS FOR CARDIAC ANESTHESIA

**Basic examination should assess:**
* **Volume status evaluation**
* **Air detection in heart chambers**
* **Eyeball assessment of contractility**
* **Regional wall motion analysis**
* **Valvular function assessment**

### 13.1.2. TEE COMPETENCY REQUIREMENTS

**For Entrustment Based Discussion:**
* **Knows how to evaluate volume status**
* **Recognize air in the heart chambers**
* **Eyeball contractility of the heart surgery**
* **Demonstrate different views** and explain findings
* **Interpret findings** in clinical context

### 13.1.3. CLINICAL APPLICATIONS

**Pre-CPB Uses:**
* **Confirm preoperative diagnosis**
* **Evaluate aortic atherosclerotic disease** (alter cannulation strategy)
* **Rule out new findings** (persistent left SVC affecting retrograde cardioplegia)
* **Guide volume resuscitation**

**Post-CPB Uses:**
* **Assess de-airing adequacy**
* **Evaluate surgical intervention results**
* **Detect inadvertent consequences** (new RWMA, RV failure, worsening valve regurgitation, AD)
* **Assess ventricular filling** (LV compliance worsens post-CPB)

**Bottom Line:** Basic TEE skills are essential for cardiac anesthesia practice and require systematic training and competency assessment.
