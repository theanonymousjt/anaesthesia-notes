# 1. RENAL ANATOMY AND PHYSIOLOGY

> **Syllabus**: [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|Part A: Renal System]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C5 Perioperative Medicine|Part B: C5 Perioperative Medicine]] | [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#1. Medical Knowledge|Part C: Medical Knowledge]]

## 1.1. GROSS ANATOMY

**The kidneys are paired retroperitoneal organs located in the paravertebral gutters that serve vital roles in fluid, electrolyte, and waste management.**

* Two normal kidneys are reddish-brown organs with an ovoid outline [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The medial margin is deeply indented with a vertical cleft (the hilus) that transmits structures entering and leaving the kidney [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The hilus lies approximately at the level of the first lumbar vertebra [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Kidneys lie in the paravertebral gutters, behind the peritoneum
* The right kidney rests slightly lower than the left due to the presence of the liver [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* At its upper end, the ureter dilates to form the renal pelvis, which passes through the hilus [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The renal pelvis is continuous with several short funnel-like tubes (calyces) that unite it with the kidney parenchyma [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The kidneys are solid, 'bean-shaped' retroperitoneal organs located at vertebral levels T12 to L3 [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* From inside to outside, the kidney is surrounded by the renal capsule, perirenal fat, renal fascia and pararenal fascia [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

### 1.1.1. ANATOMICAL RELATIONS AND INNERVATION

* The renal blood vessels lie anterior to the pelvis of the kidney, though some branches may pass posteriorly [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Kidney pain sensation is conveyed to spinal cord segments T10-L1 by sympathetic fibers [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Sympathetic innervation is supplied by preganglionic fibers from T8 to L1 [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The vagus nerve provides parasympathetic innervation [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* S2-S4 spinal segments supply the ureters [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

### 1.1.2. CAPSULAR AND FASCIAL ANATOMY

* Each kidney is enclosed in a thick, fibrous capsule [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The capsule is surrounded by a fatty capsule that fills the space inside the loosely applied renal (Gerota) fascia [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* During development, the kidney first forms in the pelvis and then ascends to its final position [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* An accessory renal artery from the aorta may be found entering the lower pole of the kidney [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* "Horseshoe kidney" occurs when rudimentary kidneys fuse during development, remaining a pelvic organ due to the inferior mesenteric artery holding it in place [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

### 1.1.3. RELATED UROLOGIC STRUCTURES

* The bladder is located in the retropubic space [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Receives sympathetic innervation from T11-L2 (pain, touch, temperature sensations) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Bladder stretch sensation is transmitted via parasympathetic fibers from S2-S4 [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Parasympathetics provide most of the bladder's motor innervation [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Prostate, penile urethra, and penis also receive sympathetic and parasympathetic fibers from T11-L2 and S2-S4 segments [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The pudendal nerve provides pain sensation to the penis via the dorsal nerve of the penis [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Sensory innervation of the scrotum is via cutaneous nerves projecting to lumbosacral segments [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Testicular sensation is conducted to lower thoracic and upper lumbar segments [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The female urethra is much shorter than the male urethra (4 vs. 20 cm), but sources of autonomic innervation are the same [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

### 1.1.4. VASCULAR ANATOMY

* The blood supply to the kidneys is carried by the renal arteries, paired arteries that arise directly from the aorta [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The right renal artery is longer, as the aorta is positioned slightly to the left of the midline [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Sometimes there are also additional accessory arteries [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Venous drainage of the kidneys is through the renal veins, which drain directly into the inferior vena cava (IVC) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The left renal vein is longer than the right owing to the position of the IVC to the right of the midline [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** The kidneys have a complex anatomy with rich innervation patterns that influence both renal function and clinical symptoms of renal disease. The retroperitoneal location, segmental blood supply, and close association with important autonomic pathways have significant implications for surgical approaches and anesthetic management.

## 1.2. ULTRASTRUCTURE

**The kidney's internal structure is organized into cortex and medulla regions, containing approximately one million tightly packed nephrons.**

* Inspection of the cut surface reveals:
  * The paler cortex adjacent to the capsule [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The darker, conical pyramids of the kidney medulla [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Pyramids are radially striated and covered with cortex [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The cortex extends into the kidney as the renal columns [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* In cross-section, the kidney contains:
  * An outer renal cortex [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * An inner renal medulla, interrupted by renal columns (extensions of the cortex) that penetrate deep into the renal medulla [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Towards the hilum, minor calyces coalesce to form major calyces, which merge to form the renal pelvis and finally the ureter [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

### 1.2.1. NEPHRON STRUCTURE

* Each nephron consists of:
  * A tuft of capillaries (the glomerulus) invaginated into the blind, expanded end (glomerular corpuscle) of a long tubule [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The tubule leaves the renal corpuscle to form the proximal convoluted tubule in the cortex [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * This leads into the straight tubule, which loops down into the medullary pyramid (loop of Henle) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The tubule then returns to the cortex to become continuous with the distal convoluted tubule [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The distal tubule opens into a collecting duct common to multiple nephrons [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The functional unit of the kidney is the nephron, of which there are around 1,000,000 per kidney [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* A nephron consists of:
  * The glomerulus, a network of capillaries located in the renal cortex whose role is the filtration of plasma [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The fluid is collected in Bowman's capsule and passes along a series of tubes [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The proximal convoluted tubule (PCT) – a twisting tubule within the renal cortex where the majority of the filtered products are reabsorbed [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The loop of Henle (LOH) – the tubule straightens and then enters the medulla to become the thin descending limb [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The limb undergoes a hairpin bend to continue as the thin and then the thick ascending limb of the LOH [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The main role of the LOH is to generate a longitudinal osmotic gradient in the renal medulla, which allows controlled water reabsorption from the collecting ducts [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The distal convoluted tubule (DCT) – the thick ascending limb of the LOH returns to the renal cortex to form the DCT, the site of regulated reabsorption [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The collecting duct (CD) – the DCT becomes the CD, before forming a minor calyx [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * The CD is an important site of water reabsorption [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

### 1.2.2. COLLECTING SYSTEM

* Collecting tubules from each lobe of the kidney discharge urine into the calyceal system via renal papillae [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* These papillae are located at the entrance of each pyramid into the calyx proper [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The parenchyma of each kidney contains approximately 1 × 10^6 nephrons [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

### 1.2.3. JUXTAGLOMERULAR APPARATUS

* The distal convoluted tubule comes into close contact with the afferent glomerular arteriole [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The modified cells of each form the juxtaglomerular apparatus [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* This complex serves as a physiologic feedback control mechanism [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* It contributes to the precise control of intraand extrarenal hemodynamics [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The DCT folds back to lie anatomically very close to its corresponding glomerulus [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* At this point are located a group of specialised cells that form the juxtaglomerular apparatus, consisting of three components [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]:
  * Granular cells, located within the wall of the afferent arteriole, whose role is renin secretion [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Macula densa cells, located at the junction of the DCT and the thick ascending limb of the LOH [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Macula densa cells sense tubular Na+ and Cl‾ concentration [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Extra-glomerular mesangial cells. These interact with the macula densa via a purinergic signalling mechanism to control the granular cells and the vascular smooth muscle of the afferent arterioles [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

### 1.2.4. VASCULAR ULTRASTRUCTURE

* The renal artery enters at the hilum and divides multiple times [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Arcuate arteries run along the boundary between cortex and outer medulla [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Interlobular arteries branch from arcuate arteries toward the outer kidney surface [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* These give rise to numerous afferent arterioles, each leading to a single glomerular capillary tuft [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The filtration barrier includes:
  * Fenestrated negatively charged capillary endothelial cells [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Tubular epithelial cells (podocytes) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * A basement membrane separating these cell layers [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Normally, about 25% of plasma elements pass into Bowman capsule [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Only cells and proteins >60-70 kDa cannot cross the filtration barrier [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Abnormalities can lead to:
  * Nephrotic syndrome (proteinuria >3.5 g/24 hr) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Glomerulonephritis (hematuria and proteinuria) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

### 1.2.5. PERITUBULAR CIRCULATION

* Glomerular capillaries exit Bowman capsule and merge to form the efferent arteriole [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* This branches into peritubular capillaries that nourish the tubules [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The kidney has two capillary beds joined in series by arterioles - a unique arrangement [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Blood supply to the entire tubular system comes from the glomerular efferent arteriole [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Some peritubular capillaries (the vasa recta) descend into the medulla to parallel the loops of Henle [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The vasa recta then return in a cortical direction and empty into the cortical veins [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The arterial blood supply of the nephron is unique [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]:
  * The renal arterial tree divides as usual to give afferent arterioles, which in turn divide to give rise to the glomerular capillaries [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * These capillaries then unite to form efferent arterioles [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * By varying the relative resistances of the afferent and efferent arterioles, glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure, which is the main driving force for glomerular filtration, can be modified [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Glomerular filtration is therefore controllable [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The vasa recta are an additional set of arterioles that arise from the efferent arterioles, whose role is to supply blood to the renal medulla [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The vasa recta also have an unusual feature: they descend with the ascending limb of the LOH and ascend with the descending limb, providing a countercurrent flow of blood [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* This countercurrent arrangement is required to generate the high solute concentration gradients of the renal medulla [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** The kidney's intricate ultrastructure features specialized filtration barriers, a unique dual capillary bed arrangement, and the juxtaglomerular apparatus, all of which are critical to normal renal function. Understanding this architecture is essential for appreciating how pathological processes and anesthetic agents can affect kidney function. The unique vascular arrangement creates a well-vascularized cortex but a relatively poor blood supply to the medulla, which is important for maintaining concentration gradients.

## 1.3. CORRELATION OF STRUCTURE AND FUNCTION

**The kidneys perform diverse functions including filtration, regulation of fluid and electrolyte balance, and hormone production despite comprising only 0.4% of body weight.**

* Kidney tissue makes up only 0.4% of body weight but receives 25% of cardiac output [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* This makes kidneys the most highly perfused major organs in the body [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* This high perfusion facilitates plasma filtration at rates of 125-140 mL/min in young adults [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The kidney has an array of functions that can be classified as [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]:
  * Homeostasis of blood composition, including:
	* Regulation of plasma volume and electrolyte concentration
	* Control of plasma osmolarity
	* Removal of waste products and drugs or their metabolites
	* Gluconeogenesis: the kidney is a major gluconeogenic organ
	* Control of the metabolic aspects of acid-base balance
  * Endocrine roles:
	* Erythropoietin synthesis, which in turn controls erythrocyte production (peritubular interstitial fibroblasts)
	* Activation of vitamin D to 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol (1 alpha-hydroxylase in epithelial cells of glomerulus)
	* Secretion of renin, the first hormone of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone axis (JGA)

### 1.3.1. FUNCTIONAL ROLES

* **Waste filtration:** Elimination of nitrogenous and other metabolic wastes [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* **Endocrine and exocrine activities:** Production of hormones and other substances [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* **Immune and metabolic functions:** Participating in immune response and metabolism [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* **Homeostasis maintenance:** Regulation of fluid and electrolyte balance [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

### 1.3.2. SPECIFIC REGULATORY FUNCTIONS

* Tight regulation of extracellular solutes:
  * Sodium
  * Potassium
  * Hydrogen
  * Bicarbonate
  * Glucose [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Metabolic functions:
  * Generation of ammonia and glucose [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Elimination of nitrogenous and other metabolic wastes [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Excretion of urea, creatinine, bilirubin, and other uremic toxins [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Hormonal functions:
  * Secretion of circulating hormones that influence red blood cell generation [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Regulation of calcium homeostasis [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Control of systemic blood pressure [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

### 1.3.3. FILTRATION AND REABSORPTION

* The kidney fulfills its dual roles by:
  * Filtering large amounts of fluid and solutes from the blood [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Secreting waste products into the tubular fluid [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Selectively reabsorbing essential substances [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Comorbid disease, surgery, and anesthesia can significantly affect these normal filtration and reabsorption processes [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** The kidney's diverse functions extend far beyond waste elimination to include crucial roles in fluid and electrolyte balance, acid-base regulation, and hormonal control. This functional diversity explains why kidney dysfunction can have widespread systemic effects and why preserving kidney function is a key perioperative priority. Despite making up only a small percentage of body weight, kidneys require a disproportionately large blood supply to perform their critical functions.

## 1.15. RENAL REPLACEMENT THERAPY IN THE MANAGEMENT OF CKD AND AKI

**Renal failure may occur acutely or insidiously – there are a multitude of causes, and the pathogenesis is complex. The end result is a loss of the essential functions of the kidney, most notably failure of [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]:**
* Clearance of toxic substances, leading to uraemia [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Electrolyte homeostasis, leading to life-threatening hyperkalaemia [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Water excretion, leading to fluid overload [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Acid excretion, leading to acidaemia [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

**Management of renal failure involves artificially performing the key functions of the kidney. Methods of renal replacement therapy (RRT) are [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]:**
* Peritoneal dialysis. Hyperosmolar dialysate solution is infused into the peritoneal cavity. 'Filtration' is achieved by using the peritoneum as a semipermeable membrane. Water is reabsorbed by making use of the osmolar gradient across the peritoneum. Diffusion along concentration gradients across the peritoneum allows clearance of toxic substances and correction of electrolyte abnormalities [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Intermittent haemodialysis and continuous haemofiltration. Whilst conceptually simple, these methods are inherently complex, involving extracorporeal circuits and an artificial semipermeable membrane. 'Filtration' occurs within an artificial 'kidney', a cellulose or synthetic semipermeable membrane. Like the native kidney, this membrane selectively 'sieves' the blood, allowing small molecules (H2O, electrolytes and waste products) to pass through, but retaining cells and large proteins in the blood. Depending on the type of RRT, simple diffusion, convection or a combination of the two may be utilised. Reabsorption is achieved by returning electrolyte-rich, pH-balanced fluid to the blood [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Renal transplant. The best method of replacing the function of a failed native kidney is through transplantation of a donor kidney [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Chronic kidney disease is a complex multisystem disorder that significantly complicates perioperative management. The uremic syndrome affects virtually every organ system and requires careful attention to fluid status, electrolyte balance, and acid-base homeostasis. Drug selection and dosing must be adjusted based on understanding altered pharmacokinetics in renal failure, with particular caution needed for medications eliminated primarily by the kidneys or those with active metabolites.

# 2. DIURETIC DRUGS: EFFECTS AND MECHANISMS





## 2.1. PHYSIOLOGIC BASIS OF DIURETIC ACTION

**Diuretics work by interfering with sodium reabsorption at different sites along the nephron, with distinct mechanisms and effects.**

* Diuretics are grouped according to site and mechanism of action [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Under normal conditions, <1% of filtered Na+ load enters the urine (FENa <1%) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The Na+/K+-ATPase pump on the basolateral surface (blood side) of kidney tubular cells is primarily responsible for:
  * Active pumping of Na+ out of cells into blood in exchange for K+ [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Creating an electrochemical gradient (2 K+ in for every 3 Na+ out) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Causing Na+ to enter the luminal (urine) side of the cell [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Different tubular segments have different luminal "systems" for Na+ influx [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* These systems are the sites where different diuretics work [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

## 2.2. PROXIMAL TUBULE DIURETICS

**Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors block sodium reabsorption in the proximal tubule, causing alkaline diuresis.**

* In the proximal tubule, a specialized luminal transporter exchanges H+ for Na+ [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* This results in:
  * Sodium reabsorption [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Acidification of urine [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The excreted H+ combines with bicarbonate (HCO3−) in the tubule to form carbonic acid: H+ + HCO3− → H2CO3 [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Carbonic acid converts to water and carbon dioxide: H2CO3 → H2O + CO2 [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* This reaction is catalyzed by carbonic anhydrase [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The same enzyme allows the reverse reaction within tubular cells:
  * Converts H2CO3 to HCO3− and H+ [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Generates more H+ for countertransport with Na+ [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Releases bicarbonate that passes into circulation [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

#### 2.2.1.1. CARBONIC ANHYDRASE INHIBITORS

* Inhibit the enzyme carbonic anhydrase [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Net effect:
  * Sodium and bicarbonate that would have been reabsorbed remain in urine [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Results in alkaline diuresis [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Long-term effects:
  * Patients may initially develop metabolic acidosis [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Compensatory processes in tubules accommodate effects [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Rarely causes sustained problems with chronic use [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Useful in:
  * Contraction alkalosis from aggressive loop diuretic use [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Reducing PaCO2 and improving PaO2 with little blood pH change [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Treatment of mountain sickness [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Open-angle glaucoma [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Increasing respiratory drive in central sleep apnea [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

## 2.3. OSMOTIC DIURETICS

**Osmotic diuretics are freely filtered substances that remain in the tubule to oppose water reabsorption.**

* Substances such as mannitol that are:
  * Freely filtered at the glomerulus [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Poorly reabsorbed by the kidney tubule [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Cause an osmotic diuresis [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Mechanism:
  * In water-permeable segments (proximal tubule and loop of Henle), fluid reabsorption occurs [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Filtered mannitol is concentrated [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Eventually oncotic pressure in tubular fluid resists further fluid reabsorption [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Additional effect: Mannitol draws water from cells into plasma and effectively increases RBF [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

#### 2.3.1.1. CLINICAL USES AND LIMITATIONS

* Widely used for increased intracranial pressure [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Used as strategy to prevent AKI [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Evidence for benefit:
  * Animal studies were promising [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Apart from kidney transplantation, no evidence suggests effectiveness for prevention or treatment of AKI [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Even in cadaveric kidney transplant recipients, data supporting benefit is modest [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* In a controlled trial with mild chronic renal failure, less effective than hydration alone for prevention of contrast-associated nephropathy [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

#### 2.3.1.2. COMPLICATIONS

* Mannitol therapy is not without risk [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Shifts water between fluid compartments affecting:
  * Plasma and intracellular electrolyte concentrations [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Can cause hyponatremia and hypochloremia [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Intracellular increases in K+ and H+ [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Patients with normal kidney function quickly correct these changes [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Patients with kidney impairment may develop:
  * Significant circulatory overload with hemodilution and pulmonary edema [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Hyperkalemic metabolic acidosis [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Central nervous system depression [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Severe hyponatremia requiring urgent hemodialysis [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

## 2.4. LOOP DIURETICS

**Loop diuretics are potent agents that block sodium-potassium-chloride cotransport in the thick ascending limb.**

* The electrochemical gradient from Na+/K+-ATPase drives transport of one Na+, one K+, and two Cl− ions into tubule cells from tubular fluid [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* The thick ascending limb is water-impermeable, so solute reabsorption:
  * Concentrates the interstitium [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Dilutes the tubular fluid [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Loop diuretics (furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide) directly inhibit the electroneutral transporter [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* This prevents salt reabsorption [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Since 25% of filtered NaCl is normally reabsorbed in the loop of Henle, loop diuretics cause:
  * Large salt load to pass to the distal convoluted tubule [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Beyond the extra reserve of this segment to reabsorb [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Large volumes of dilute urine [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

#### 2.4.1.1. CLINICAL USES

* First-line treatment for acute decompensated congestive heart failure [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Benefits:
  * Reduce left ventricular filling pressures [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Very effectively relieve symptoms:
	* Congestion [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
	* Pulmonary edema [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
	* Extremity swelling [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
	* Hepatic congestion [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * No proven mortality benefit [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

#### 2.4.1.2. ADVERSE EFFECTS

* Hypokalemia [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Hyponatremia [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* AKI [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Heart failure patients with atrial fibrillation on digitalis may develop hypokalemia-induced dysrhythmias when also given furosemide [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Ototoxicity, especially with furosemide in patients with kidney insufficiency [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

## 2.5. DISTAL CONVOLUTED TUBULE DIURETICS

## 2.6. DISTAL (COLLECTING DUCT) ACTING DIURETICS

**Potassium-sparing diuretics act in the collecting duct to block sodium channels or inhibit aldosterone, preserving potassium.**

* Unlike proximal segments, NaCl absorption in collecting duct cells is not electroneutral [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* A net electrical gradient is maintained by:
  * Na+/K+-ATPase [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Na+ ion channels in luminal membranes [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* This makes the tubule lumen negatively charged with respect to blood [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Normally causes K+ secretion into the tubular lumen through K+-specific ion channels [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

#### 2.6.1.1. POTASSIUM-SPARING DIURETICS

* **Direct sodium channel blockers:**
  * Amiloride and triamterene [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Directly inhibit luminal Na+ entry [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Block the mechanism causing K+ secretion [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Result in K+-"sparing" effect [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Also inhibit H+ secretion [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* **Aldosterone antagonists:**
  * Spironolactone and eplerenone [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Competitively inhibit aldosterone [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Aldosterone is normally released in response to:
	* Angiotensin II [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
	* Hyperkalemia [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Aldosterone stimulates Na+ reabsorption and K+ excretion by collecting duct [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Inhibiting this effect causes mild natriuresis and K+ retention [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

#### 2.6.1.2. CLINICAL USES

* K+-sparing diuresis (e.g., in patients on digitalis or with hypokalemic alkalosis) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Treatment of disorders with secondary hyperaldosteronism:

* Spironolactone improves survival in patients with:
  * Volume overload [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

#### 2.6.1.3. ADVERSE EFFECTS

## 2.7. DOPAMINERGIC AGONISTS

**Dopaminergic agents can induce natriuresis at low doses but have limited clinical utility for renal protection.**

* Intravenous infusion of low-dose dopamine (1-3 μg/kg/min) is natriuretic [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Primary mechanisms:
  * Modest increase in GFR [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Reduction in proximal Na+ reabsorption mediated by dopamine type 1 (DA1) receptors [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Fenoldopam is a selective DA1 receptor agonist with little cardiac stimulation [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* At higher doses, dopamine's pressor response benefits hypotensive patients [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Little or no kidney effect in critically ill or septic patients [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

  * Impaired endocrine and immunologic system function [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Blunting of ventilatory drive [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Increased risk of post-cardiac surgery atrial fibrillation [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

## 2.8. SGLT2 INHIBITORS

**SGLT2 inhibitors are newer agents that block glucose reabsorption in the proximal tubule with beneficial renal and cardiovascular effects.**

* Sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 inhibitors were developed for type 2 diabetes mellitus [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Additional benefits:
  * Weight loss [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Reducing cardiovascular events [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Protective kidney effects [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* Mechanism:
  * Work at proximal tubule level [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Prevent reabsorption of approximately 90% of filtered glucose [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Lower glomerular capillary hypertension and hyperfiltration [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Improve cortical oxygenation [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Reduce tubular glucotoxicity [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* These mechanisms are thought to preserve glomerular filtration rate [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

#### 2.8.1.1. CLINICAL EVIDENCE

* Recent studies show some SGLT2 inhibitors:
  * Slow progression of kidney disease [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Lower risk of kidney failure and death in people with CKD [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Effective regardless of presence or absence of type 2 diabetes [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

#### 2.8.1.2. PERIOPERATIVE CONSIDERATIONS

* AKI can occur with initiation of SGLT2 inhibitor therapy [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Reversible with discontinuation [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* FDA recommendation: Discontinue 3-4 days prior to elective surgery [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * To prevent serious euglycemic ketoacidosis [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
* More studies needed on:
  * Patient populations that would benefit [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]
  * Better understanding of role and timing of perioperative discontinuation [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#1.3 Renal, Cellular, Body Fluids and Electrolytes, Acid Base|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Different classes of diuretics work at specific sites along the nephron through distinct mechanisms. Understanding these mechanisms helps in selecting appropriate agents for specific clinical situations and anticipating adverse effects. Newer agents like SGLT2 inhibitors offer renal protection but require careful perioperative management. Each class has unique indications, contraindications, and side effect profiles that must be considered in the context of individual patient characteristics.
