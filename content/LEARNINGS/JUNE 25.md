# MISTAKES
1. Diluting ephedrine into 5ml instead of 6ml and labelling it as 5mg/ml instead of 6mg/ml.
2. Cannulating a poor vein with a large bore IV x2
	1. Vein had inadequate runway
	2. Vein was actually too small
3. Not taking a pre-anaesthesia BP before induction
4. Remember to change the fresh gas flow after intubation - reduce the flows and use as low FiO2 as possible (usually people do 30% O2 + 2L of flow)
5. Letting go of inserted ETT - never let go if not inflated!
6. Not keeping bite block in when inserting oral airway
7. Not going down ACLS pathway for unstable bradycardia and instead using ephedrine 

# TECHNIQUES
1. Donald's approach to a slow induction in patients with anticipated haemodynamic instability
	1. Going slow on induction and then giving a small bolus of paralytic (to help relax the patient) instead of giving a larger dose of induction agent will help avoid the complication of hypotension from induction agent while allowing the relaxation required for 
2. Eugene's LMA technique
	1. Prof Eugene inserts his finger into the sleeve of the LMA; and he slides that finger along the palate
	2. Your finger must go pretty deep
3. General LMA habits from consultant
	1. CK Sim and Constance does not like to dirty their fingers
	2. Deborah likes to do scissors 
	3. Most cons like to use left hand to flip the tip of the LMA down
	4. In general, get pass the teeth first, and then subsequently flip the tip of the LMA down (or if you are using Eugene's finger sleeve method, slide along hard palate)
4. Kevin's Suprainguinal (actually Bow-tie) technique
	1. Some principles of PNB does apply for all PNB cases
		1. No need to scrub for PNB insertion, just sterile gloves
		2. First do screening US unsterile
		3. Then open up the PNB set
		4. Pour chlorhex 
		5. Pour sterile water in 
			1. Used to create volume in volume dependent plane blocks
			2. Used to prepare LA (generally prepared as 0.3% - i.e. 3mg/10ml; ropivacaine comes as 1% (10mg/ml) and bupivacaine comes as 0.5% (5mg/ml) - thus for ropivacaine draw 3ml and dilute to 10ml and for bupivacaine draw 6ml and dilute to 10ml. 
		6. Clean and drape the area
		7. Pour the gel into the US sleeve and ask the nurse to drop the US in
		8. Pull the sleeve of the US and ensure even tight spread, and use the rubber band to secure it
		9. Using left hand holding US, left hand must rest on patient surface for stabilization
		10. Always ensure critical structure is in mid-line regardless of in-plane or out-of-plane approach
		11. Angle 45 degrees, dont bend needle (happens if too superficial)
		12. Find needle on US and deposit when at appropriate location
		13. For bow tie, look for spread towards the iliacus (medial spread)
5. Kevin's Extubation on Remi
	1. Kept remi on to reduce bucking and coughing, allowing for deep extubation
6. Constance's finding difficult vein technique
	1. Hand resting slightly off table, slight pull of gravity
	2. BP cuff on, start inflating
	3. Double tourniquet upon cuffing
	4. Look at dorsum of hand
7. Deborah's spinal technique
	1. Position patient first!! Then mark the site of insertion (Iliac crest L4 vert or L4/5 interspace)
	2. In contrast to LA, intrathecal opioids actually diffuse across the entire CSF and is not dependent on volume.
	3. Open up the spinal set, and then open up the equipment into it
	4. Pour the chlorhex into the bowl (for disinfecting)
	5. Spinal needle of choice - 25G pencil point used that day and LA/opioid/lidocaine opened into the tray
	6. Pour normal saline into tray as well
	7. Dilute opioid using normal saline w the 10ml syringe
		1. If 10mg/ml of morphine diluted to total 10ml to give 1mg/ml > draw up to 0.3ml for 300mcg
		2. If 50mcg/ml of fentanyl (and each vial has 2ml) diluted to total 10ml to give 10mcg/ml 
	8. Eventual volume for intrathecal administration should be around 2ml (although not volume dependent)
	9. Draw lidocaine into syringe and connect needle
	10. Clean and drape patient
	11. And then drape the patient using the clear sheet, white tape side up
	12. Left hand align with iliac crest, right hand w lidocaine needle advance in midline, perpendicular (watch for tendency to drift and go off course)
	13. Create wheal first
	14. Then advance some more and deposit lidocaine around tract
	15. Then remove lidocaine syringe, leave the needle there
	16. Take the introducer needle, while removing the LA needle, take not of insertion and enter exact path (may need adjustments if hitting bony structures) - introducer needle should also go all the way near end of hub
	17. If smooth, insert spinal needle should go all the way until hub > remove needle from sheath, should see clear CSF > wait until CSF reaches near hub > connect LA/opioid syringe > aspirate for CSF swirl and then deposit.
	18. Pull entire thing out at one go, nurse will spread
8. Intubation
	1. Hold Bougie and ETT distally and like a pencil

# GENERAL WISDOM
1. Prevent hypothermia actively by warming OT to 23C at start of case
2. Always minimize patient exposure while under GA; use force air warmer
3. Aim of OT anaesthesia - to ensure every cell gets oxygenated
4. There is more to TTE than just the EF. 
5. Monitor targets
	1. SpO2 > 96%; it is a problem if SpO2 is not high when on supplemental O2 when under GA, so intervene early. Ensure pulse ox waveform is legible first.
	2. HR 60-100 (patient baseline)
	3. BP 20% of patient baseline, but in general keep MAP >65
	4. RR - used as a basis of titration if on SIMV or PSV mode, hence some cons actually choose to give analgesic at the end of the case
	5. Minute ventilation - 70-110ml/kg/min (can use 100 as an estimate)
	6. TV - 7ml/kg
		1. But then I am not sure if this takes into account the compliance of the breathing circuit
	7. ETCO2
		1. ETCO2 lags behind PaCO2; thus normal PaCO2 of 35-45, actually is 30-40 ETCO2. In patients where ICP is important, maintain 30-35; else can allow permissive hypercarbia up to ETCO2 of 45.
		2. Also ensure baseline reaches 0
	8. BIS - 40-60 + DSA (low beta signature (red), high alpha and delta signature (red - but may appear yellow in elderly))
		1. Left hand column is the frequency
			1. 13-30Hz (Beta), 8-13Hz (Alpha), 4-8Hz (Theta), 0-4Hz (Delta)
		2. Color corresponds to increased signature (red is higher, blue is lower)
		3. Increased heat signature in alpha suggest potential responses to pain (random red speckles)
	9. EEG waveform under GA is expected to be higher amplitude but lower frequency, in contrast to when awake which is lower amplitude but higher frequency (more chaotic like VF)
	10. ECG - creating a static snapshot of ECG is good practice for comparison
	11. Peak airway pressure (Ppeak) - in supine patient with normal compliant patient, should not be more than 20
	12. End tidal gas concentration (O2 and anaesthetic gas)
		1. O2 
			1. Most cons I see run about 2L/min of 30-40% SpO2 (to conserve soda lime as very low flows consume it), some cons like Jian Hong and Bryan run carrier gas flows at 30% SpO2 (as minimal as possible) at 0.5L/min. 
			2. Rmb to PreO2 at start and end of case to 85% - washout the nitrogen, increases FRC oxygen content (apneic time in normal patient 8min if FRC is 100% O2)
	13. Volatiles - it seems most don't run at 1 MAC, perhaps it is a part of experience? Some run at 0.7-0.8 MAC instead.
6. Calculating ideal body weight and lean body weight
	1. Ideal body weight estimated = height - 100
	2. Adjusted body weight = 0.4 * (ABW - IBW (estimated)) + IBW (estimated)
7. Putting in thoracic impedance to monitor RR w ETCO2 during MAC to see if there is chest movement w airway obstruction Vs apnea
