# 1. REGIONAL ANESTHESIA: GENERAL PRINCIPLES

## 1.1. INTRODUCTION TO REGIONAL ANESTHESIA

> **Syllabus**: [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|Part A: Local Anesthetics]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|Part B]] | [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|Part C]]

**Regional anesthesia provides location-specific anesthesia and analgesia with multiple benefits.**

* **Peripheral nerve blocks (PNBs)**: *Techniques targeting specific nerves or plexuses* [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]. Can serve as:
  * Primary surgical anesthetic [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * Supplement to general anesthesia (analgesia + muscle relaxation) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * Prolonged postoperative analgesia method [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Advantages over parenteral analgesics** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Better analgesia
  * Fewer side effects (e.g., PONV (Postoperative Nausea and Vomiting))
  * Improved patient satisfaction
  * Earlier mobilization
  * Cost-effective surgical services
* ⚠️ **Safety considerations**: Success depends on accurate local anesthetic delivery near the target nerve [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]].
  * Inherent failure rate exists even with experienced providers [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]].
  * Rare risks: systemic toxicity, infection, bleeding, nerve injury [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]].
* **Technological advances** improving PNB success [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Electrical nerve stimulation (NS) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * Ultrasound (US) imaging [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Foundation requirements** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Solid understanding of anatomy and common variations
  * Knowledge of nerve localization techniques

**Bottom Line:** Regional anesthesia provides targeted anesthesia and analgesia with significant benefits over general anesthesia and systemic analgesics, though successful and safe application requires thorough anatomical knowledge and technical expertise.

## 1.2. EQUIPMENT AND SETUP

> **Syllabus**: [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|Part B]] | [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6. Systems-Based Practice|Part C: Safety Systems]]

### 1.2.1. BLOCK ROOM REQUIREMENTS

* **Block room concept** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Dedicated presurgical area for PNB placement
  * Allows sufficient "soak time" before entering OR
  * ***Soak time***: *Time required for local anesthetic to diffuse across nerve membranes, block action potentials, and produce clinical effect* [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]
* **Essential block room components** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Sufficient space for procedures, monitoring, resuscitation
  * Clearly identifiable, accessible supplies (organized cart)
  * Monitoring equipment (ECG, NIBP, SpO2, EtCO2)
  * Oxygen delivery system
  * Emergency airway management tools
  * Suction equipment
  * Adequate lighting
  * Cardiovascular resuscitation equipment (crash cart)
  * LAST (Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity) treatment supplies (**INTRALIPID**)
* **Equipment organization** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Organized storage cart containing:
	* Block supplies (needles, nerve stimulators, trays, dressings)
	* Local anesthetics
	* Resuscitation drugs
	* Ultrasound machine with various probes
  * **Specialty tray components**:
	* Sterile skin preparation items (e.g., chlorhexidine)
	* Draping materials
	* Marking pen and ruler for landmark identification
	* Needles/syringes for skin infiltration (e.g., 1% **LIDOCAINE**)
	* Specific block needles (insulated, echogenic) and catheters
  * **Medication availability**:
	* Sedatives, hypnotics, IV anesthetics for patient comfort (e.g., **MIDAZOLAM**, **FENTANYL**, **PROPOFOL**) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* 💡 **Clinical Pearl**: Use short-acting medications with high safety margin, titrate carefully [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6. Systems-Based Practice|ⓘ]]
	* Emergency drugs: **ATROPINE**, **EPINEPHRINE**, **PHENYLEPHRINE**, **EPHEDRINE**, **PROPOFOL**, **MIDAZOLAM**, **SUCCINYLCHOLINE**, **INTRALIPID** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* ⚠️ Laminated LAST resuscitation guidelines kept with **INTRALIPID** [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6. Systems-Based Practice|ⓘ]]

### 1.2.2. MONITORING

* **Standard requirements** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * ECG (Electrocardiogram)
  * Noninvasive blood pressure (NIBP)
  * Pulse oximetry (SpO2)
  * End-tidal CO₂ (EtCO2) for sedated patients
  * Regular consciousness checks (vasovagal episodes common)
* **Toxicity monitoring** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * No reliable real-time blood level detection techniques [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]
  * **EPINEPHRINE** as pharmacologic marker (indirect indicator of intravascular injection - ↑HR, ↑BP) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * ⚠️ **Systemic toxicity timing** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]:
	* Rapid IV injection → toxicity within ~2 minutes
	* Delayed absorption → toxicity around ~20 minutes
	* Monitor patient closely for at least 30 minutes post-PNB
* **Discharge criteria (from block area/PACU)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Stable vital signs following regional anesthesia
  * If block unregressed:
	* Protection for anesthetized limb (e.g., sling, immobilizer)
	* Complete instructions to patient/family on limb protection and signs of complications
	* Proper orders for inpatients ensuring limb protection
* 🏥 **Clinical Practice**: Postoperative perineural infusions require physician follow-up or Acute Pain Service monitoring [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Pediatric considerations** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Blocks often administered under general anesthesia/deep sedation
  * Additional monitoring crucial (US, NS, injection pressure monitoring)
  * Children cannot reliably articulate discomfort or cooperate

### 1.2.3. PREMEDICATION AND SEDATION

* **Keys to success** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Patient selection and education
  * Appropriate sedation/analgesia for patient comfort and cooperation
* **Medication options** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **PROPOFOL**
  * **MIDAZOLAM**
  * **FENTANYL**
  * **KETAMINE**
  * **REMIFENTANIL**
  * **ALFENTANIL**
  * Combinations of above
* 💡 **Clinical Pearl**: Titrate drugs carefully to patient factors, nerve block procedure, and anticipated surgery length [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

### 1.2.4. DOCUMENTATION

* **Preblock checklists**: Ensure correct patient, site, procedure (Time Out) [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6. Systems-Based Practice|ⓘ]]
* **Document** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Preoperative conditions (especially neurologic deficits)
  * Risk-benefit discussion
  * Informed consent obtained
  * Detailed PNB procedure note (technique, drugs, dose, volume, response, complications)
  * *Importance*: Quality assurance, research, medicolegal reasons

**Bottom Line:** A properly equipped block area with comprehensive monitoring, appropriate medications, and thorough documentation is essential for safe and effective regional anesthesia procedures.

## 1.3. BLOCK PERFORMANCE TECHNIQUES

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#3.1 Physics|Part A - Physics]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|Part B - Nerve localization techniques]] | [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|Part C - Procedural skills]]

### 1.3.1. NERVE STIMULATION (NS) TECHNIQUE

* **Historical context**: Introduced in 1960s [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Basic principle**: Low-current electrical impulses stimulate motor fibers → muscle twitch indicates nerve proximity without needle contact or patient pain [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#3.1 Physics|ⓘ]]
* **Theoretical advantage**: Reduced nerve injury risk (unproven) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Catheter applications**: Stimulating catheters can help confirm placement accuracy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ **Limitation**: Relies on physiologic responses that vary widely between individuals [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Practical guidelines** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Improved nerve depolarization*:
	* Connect positive (red) pole as ground electrode (skin patch)
	* Connect negative (black) lead to insulated needle (**cathodal preference**)
	* Ground electrode location less critical with constant-current stimulator
  * *Threshold indicators*:
	* Optimal needle proximity: Motor response at **0.3-0.5 mA** threshold [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* ⚠️ Threshold of **<0.2 mA**: High risk of intraneural injection - **AVOID INJECTION**, withdraw needle slightly [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * *Injection technique*:
	* Once satisfactory threshold obtained, inject 1-3 mL local anesthetic [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* Watch for motor twitch disappearance ("Raj test" or "disappearance sign") [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* *Mechanism*: Physical nerve displacement or electrical field changes by injectate [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#3.1 Physics|ⓘ]]
	* Injected solutions affect current thresholds:
	  * Conducting solutions (**LOCAL ANESTHETIC**, saline) → ↓ current density → ↑ current threshold needed for twitch [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#3.1 Physics|ⓘ]]
	  * Non-conducting solutions (**D5W**) → ↑ current density → maintains/augments twitch (useful for hydrodissection) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#3.1 Physics|ⓘ]]

	  ![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image.png_af073a.png_5d1a4f.png_b2a419.png)

	* Inject remainder of dose in divided aliquots (e.g., 5 mL increments) with frequent aspiration [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

### 1.3.2. ULTRASOUND (US) IMAGING TECHNIQUE

* **Advantages** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Visualizes nerve size, depth, location relative to adjacent structures
  * Shows needle movement and tip position in real-time
  * Allows visualization of local anesthetic spread
  * Facilitates catheter placement adjustment
  * Reduces number of needle passes
  * Enables use of lower local anesthetic volumes
  * Helps avoid critical structures (vessels, pleura, peritoneum)
* **Imaging principles** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#3.1 Physics|ⓘ]]:
  * Images reflect anatomic contours based on *acoustic impedance* differences [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#3.1 Physics|ⓘ]]
  * Sound waves reflect at interfaces between tissues with different impedance [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#3.1 Physics|ⓘ]]
  * *Image appearance (Echogenicity)* [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#3.1 Physics|ⓘ]]:
	* High impedance/dense (bone, fascia, tendons, nerve epineurium) → bright (**hyperechoic**) + acoustic shadowing (bone)
	* Low impedance (muscle, nerve fascicles) → gray (**hypoechoic**)
	* Minimal impedance (fluid in vessels, cysts, local anesthetic spread) → black (**anechoic**)
  * *Frequency selection* [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#3.1 Physics|ⓘ]]:
	* **Higher frequencies** (e.g., 10-15 MHz) → better superficial spatial resolution (e.g., supraclavicular block)
	* **Lower frequencies** (e.g., 2-5 MHz) → better deep structure penetration (e.g., sciatic nerve block)
  * *Probe selection*: Based on block location and depth (linear vs. curved) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * *Important functions*: Depth, Gain, Focus, Doppler effect (helps distinguish arteries/veins) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#3.1 Physics|ⓘ]]
* **Practical guidelines** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Probe sterility options*:
	* Sterile sleeve covers for real-time/dynamic US guidance
	* Sterile transparent dressing (e.g., Tegaderm) acceptable for single-injection blocks
  * *Visualization tips*:
	* Use sufficient sterile gel for optimal skin-probe acoustic coupling
	* Identify reliable anatomic landmarks (bone, vessel, muscle) with known nerve relationship
	* Trace nerve proximally/distally to optimal block location
  * *Needle alignment techniques*:
	* **"In-plane" (IP)**: Align needle shaft parallel to the long axis of the US probe beam [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	  * Provides view of entire needle shaft and tip (ideal)
	  * Tip: Place target nerve near edge of US screen to allow visualization of needle shaft approaching
	* **"Out-of-plane" (OOP)**: Align needle perpendicular/transverse to the US probe beam [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	  * Shows needle tip as a hyperechoic dot (cross-section). Harder to visualize entire shaft

	![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_1.png_bc56b3.png_de7da1.png_9b9858.png)

  * *Injection visualization*:
	* Inject small test dose (1-2 mL) of **LOCAL ANESTHETIC** or **D5W** to confirm appropriate needle tip location and spread pattern [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* Look for circumferential spread around the nerve ("donut sign") [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* Use **D5W** with combined US/NS technique to maintain accurate motor responses (doesn't abolish twitch like LA) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * 💡 **Clinical Pearl**: **Combined US/NS technique** offers enhanced safety [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* Primary nerve identification via US
	* Nerve stimulator set at 0.3-0.5 mA
	* Acts as an alert if insulated needle tip comes too close to nerve (<0.2 mA response), even if visualization is suboptimal

### 1.3.3. EQUIPMENT (NEEDLES AND CATHETERS)

* **Needles** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **"Short bevel"** (30-45 degrees) or **"B bevel"**: Theoretically reduce nerve penetration/injury potential vs. long bevel needles
  * **"Pencil-point"** needles: Also designed to minimize nerve trauma by spreading fibers rather than cutting
  * *Single-injection PNBs*: Typically 22-24 gauge insulated needles with short bevels
  * *Echogenic needles*: Special coatings/grooves/dimples enhance acoustic reflectivity for better US visualization

### 1.3.4. PRESSURE MONITORING AND SAFETY

* **Practical tips** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * ⚠️ **High injection pressures (>15-20 psi)**, especially into nerve fascicles, are associated with nerve damage [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * *Pressure-limiting approaches*:
	* Subjective feel ("syringe feel" - unreliable) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* Disposable in-line injection pressure monitors (objective measurement) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* Compressed air injection technique (CAIT) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
	  * Draw excess air (e.g., 3-5 mL) into LA syringe
	  * Compress air bubble by ~50% during injection
	  * Maintains pressure around ~15 psi (760 mmHg), below threshold for nerve injury risk (~25 psi or 1300 mmHg). Based on Boyle's law

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_2.png_ec4939.png_854576.png_36cbcd.png)

* **Catheters** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Continuous-infusion catheter kits*:
	* Standard non-stimulating polyamide catheter
	* Insulated Tuohy needle (epidural-style) with NS capability for placement
  * *Specialized options*:
	* **Stimulating catheters**: Electrode in tip allows NS confirmation of final catheter tip location after needle removal [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* **Catheter-over-needle assemblies**: Inserted as a single unit (like an IV catheter) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	  * Avoids threading problems
	  * Potentially more stable and leakproof at skin site
  * *Maintenance requirement*: Strict sterility of catheter and insertion site during placement and infusion [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6. Systems-Based Practice|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Modern regional anesthesia techniques combine ultrasound guidance, nerve stimulation, specialized needles, and pressure monitoring to maximize block success and safety. These technologies help identify target nerves, avoid critical structures, confirm appropriate needle placement, and prevent injection-related nerve injury.

## 1.4. POST-BLOCK MANAGEMENT

> [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|Part B - Post-block monitoring]] | [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6. Systems-Based Practice|Part C - Patient safety]]

### 1.4.1. BLOCK ASSESSMENT

* **Sensory monitoring tools** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Cold stimuli (ice, alcohol swab) - assesses small C and A-delta fibers
  * Touch/Pinprick (pinwheel, blunt needle tip, monofilament) - assesses A-beta and A-delta fibers
  * 🔑 HY: Most assessment methods are subjective and assess different fiber types. Compare to contralateral side
  * *Pain rating scales*:
	* 0-10 Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) (0="no pain"; 10="worst pain imaginable")
	* Wong-Baker FACES Pain Rating Scale (helpful for children/non-verbal patients)
	* Specialized scales for dementia/cognitive impairment (e.g., PAINAD - based on physical/verbal cues)
* **Motor block assessment** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Bromage score (common for lower extremity epidural/spinal) or modified versions for PNBs
  * Graded scale: lowest=full movement range; highest=complete block/no movement against gravity/resistance
  * Subjective method relying on assessor's judgment and patient cooperation

### 1.4.2. DISCHARGE CRITERIA

* Well-controlled pain (e.g., NRS < 4) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* Standard level of pain relief achieved before discharge [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* Discussion of specific block-related risks and management plan [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6. Systems-Based Practice|ⓘ]]
* *For patients discharged with a still-anesthetized extremity* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * ⚠️ **In-depth instruction** about risks (falls, burns, pressure injury, compartment syndrome) and prevention strategies [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6. Systems-Based Practice|ⓘ]]
  * Ensure clear understanding by patient and caregivers [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6. Systems-Based Practice|ⓘ]]
  * Examples: Avoiding open heat/cold sources, careful positioning, using assistive devices (sling, crutches) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * Provide written instructions including expected block duration, typical side effects, signs of complications, and 24-hour contact information for emergencies [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6. Systems-Based Practice|ⓘ]]

### 1.4.3. COMPLICATION AVOIDANCE

* **Complication rates**: Generally low but potentially severe [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * LAST-related seizures: ~1-8 per 10,000 PNBs (decreasing with US) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * Clinically significant nerve injury: <0.1-1% (transient paresthesias more common) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * Severe chronic pain syndromes (e.g., CRPS): Rare [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Preventive measures* [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#6. Systems-Based Practice|ⓘ]]:
  * Meticulous technique (US/NS guidance, pressure monitoring, aspiration)
  * Attention to unusual patient responses (severe pain) or resistance during injection
  * Appropriate patient selection and risk discussion
  * Follow-up communication/assessment before and after discharge

**Bottom Line:** Post-block care requires systematic assessment of block efficacy, thorough patient education about block-related precautions, and vigilant monitoring for complications. For ambulatory patients with prolonged blocks, detailed discharge instructions and follow-up protocols are essential to prevent injuries and detect complications early.

## 1.5. PATIENT SELECTION

> [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|Part B - Patient factors]] | [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|Part C - Patient assessment]]

### 1.5.1. SUITABLE CANDIDATES

* Patients scheduled for surgery where PNB can provide primary anesthesia or postoperative analgesia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Extremity surgery (upper and lower)
  * Thoracic surgery (e.g., breast, thoracotomy - PVB, ESP, Intercostal)
  * Abdominal surgery (e.g., hernia repair, C-section - TAP, QL, Ilioinguinal)
  * Perineal surgery (Pudendal)

### 1.5.2. CONTRAINDICATIONS

* **Absolute** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Patient or guardian refusal
  * Infection at the site of needle insertion
  * True allergy to local anesthetic class (rare - check specifics)
* **Relative** (Risk vs. Benefit Assessment) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Systemic anticoagulation or severe coagulopathy (follow ASRA guidelines)
  * Severe systemic infection (sepsis)
  * Pre-existing neurologic disease in the distribution of the block
* **Controversial** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Pre-existing neurologic disease*:
	* Safety data limited, especially for PNBs vs. neuraxial
	* Theoretical risk of compounding existing deficits or difficulty distinguishing block complication from underlying disease progression
	* ⚠️ Important: Document pre-procedure neurologic deficits thoroughly and have detailed risk-benefit discussion with patient
* 💡 **Clinical Pearl**: Always follow applicable guidelines (ASA for monitoring, ASRA for anticoagulated patients) [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Patient selection involves evaluating surgical site, individual patient factors, and potential contraindications. While absolute contraindications are few, several relative contraindications require careful risk-benefit assessment. Pre-existing neurologic disease in the block distribution requires particular attention and documentation.

Patient Care and Procedural Skills|Part C - Systemic toxicity management]]

### 1.6.1. DRUG SELECTION PRINCIPLES

* **Choice depends on**: Desired onset, duration, density (sensory vs. motor block), potential for toxicity [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]
* **Commonly used LAs for PNBs** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]:
  * Short-acting: **LIDOCAINE**, **MEPIVACAINE**
  * Intermediate-acting: (Less common for single shot PNB)
  * Long-acting: **BUPIVACAINE**, **ROPIVACAINE**, **LEVOBUPICAINE**
  * Exparel (**LIPOSOMAL BUPIVACAINE**): Extended duration formulation
* **Concentration guidelines** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]:
  * Use the **lowest effective concentration** to achieve desired block characteristics
  * Lower concentrations often sufficient for PNB vs. epidural/spinal
	* Examples: 1-1.5% **LIDOCAINE**; 0.2-0.5% **BUPIVACAINE**/**ROPIVACAINE**
  * Neural toxicity appears concentration-dependent (and dose-dependent)
  * Lower concentrations allow larger volumes for spread without exceeding toxic dose limits
  * No evidence that prolonged nerve exposure to *appropriate* concentrations causes neurotoxic injury
* **Volume**: Highly variable depending on block type, technique (US vs. NS), desired spread. US guidance generally allows for volume reduction [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

### 1.6.2. SYSTEMIC TOXICITY FACTORS (LAST)

* **Common causes** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]:
  * Accidental intravascular injection (most common cause of severe LAST)
  * Systemic absorption of excessive dose, even if injected correctly (especially with highly vascular sites or large total dose)
* **Absorption variation by site** (influences peak plasma levels - highest risk to lowest) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]:
  * 📋 **MNEMONIC**: "ICE-BS": **I**ntravenous > **T**racheal > **I**ntercostal > **C**audal > **P**aravertebral > **E**pidural > **B**rachial Plexus > **S**ciatic/Femoral > **S**ubcutaneous
  * *In other words*: Blocks in highly vascular areas (Intercostal, Paravertebral) lead to faster absorption and higher peak levels
* **Duration variation by site** (equivalent doses, related to clearance) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]:
  * Epidural space: Shorter duration (e.g., 3-4 hours for **BUPIVACAINE**)
  * Arm (Brachial Plexus): Moderate duration (e.g., 12-18 hours)
  * Sciatic nerve: Longest duration (e.g., 18-36+ hours)
* **Epinephrine addition** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]:
  * *Concentration*: Typically 1:200,000 (5 mcg/mL) to 1:400,000 (2.5 mcg/mL)
  * *Advantages*:
	* Vasoconstriction → ↓ systemic absorption → lower peak blood levels → ↑ safety margin
	* Prolongs block duration (more significant for shorter-acting LAs like **LIDOCAINE**)
	* Acts as intravascular injection marker (↑HR, ↑BP if >5-15 mcg injected IV)
  * ⚠️ **Contraindicated** in blocks involving areas with potential end-arterial circulation (digits, penis, earlobe, tip of nose) - risk of ischemia
  * ⚠️ **Contraindicated** in intravenous regional anesthesia (**BIER BLOCK**)
* **Safety requirements** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Calculate maximum safe dose based on patient weight and specific LA
  * Use US guidance to avoid vessels
  * Aspirate frequently before and during injection
  * Inject incrementally (e.g., 3-5 mL aliquots)
  * Maintain verbal contact with patient if awake/sedated
  * Immediate availability of oxygen, suction, monitoring, resuscitation equipment, and **INTRALIPID**
  * Even small doses may produce significant side effects if injected intravascularly or into sensitive regions (e.g., vertebral artery near interscalene block)

### 1.6.3. LAST MANAGEMENT

* **Recognition**: Biphasic - CNS excitation (agitation, tinnitus, metallic taste, seizures) → CNS depression (coma, respiratory arrest) +/- Cardiovascular effects (arrhythmias, hypotension, cardiac arrest - esp. **BUPIVACAINE**) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]
* **Immediate Management** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  1. **STOP INJECTING** local anesthetic
  2. **Call for help**
  3. **Airway management**: Maintain airway, provide 100% oxygen, assist ventilation. Prevent hypoxia and acidosis (worsen LAST)
  4. **Seizure suppression**: Benzodiazepines (**MIDAZOLAM**, **DIAZEPAM**) preferred. Small doses of **PROPOFOL** or **THIOPENTAL** okay, but avoid large doses due to myocardial depression. Avoid **SUCCINYLCHOLINE** unless needed for intubation (muscle activity increases acidosis)
  5. **Manage arrhythmias/hypotension**: Standard ACLS, but modify:
	 * Avoid vasopressin, calcium channel blockers, beta-blockers, **LIDOCAINE**
	 * Use smaller doses of **EPINEPHRINE** (e.g., < 1 mcg/kg)
	 * Amiodarone preferred for ventricular arrhythmias
  6. **Lipid Emulsion Therapy (INTRALIPID 20%)**: Administer EARLY, especially with cardiovascular instability [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	 * *Mechanism*: "Lipid sink" theory - creates expanded lipid phase in plasma, partitioning LA away from target receptors. May also have direct positive cardiac effects [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]
	 * **Dosing (Adults > 70kg)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
	   * **Bolus**: 100 mL (approx 1.5 mL/kg) IV over 2-3 minutes
	   * **Infusion**: 200-250 mL IV over 15-20 minutes
	   * *Repeat bolus* once or twice if cardiovascular stability not restored
	   * *Continue infusion* for at least 10 minutes after stability achieved
	   * **Maximum dose**: Approx 12 mL/kg total
	 * **Dosing (Adults < 70kg or Children)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
	   * **Bolus**: 1.5 mL/kg IV over 2-3 minutes
	   * **Infusion**: 0.25 mL/kg/min
	   * Adjust based on response, max dose ~12 mL/kg
	 * ⚠️ Use **20% lipid emulsion**, not 10% or propofol emulsion
	 * Primary goal remains **prevention** of LAST
  7. **Cardiopulmonary bypass**: Consider if refractory cardiac arrest [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Local anesthetic selection requires balancing onset, duration, and potential toxicity. LAST, though rare, is a potentially fatal complication that requires immediate recognition and a structured management approach including lipid emulsion therapy. Prevention through careful technique and dose calculation remains paramount.

## 1.7. NERVE DAMAGE AND OTHER COMPLICATIONS

> [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|Part B - Mechanisms of nerve injury]] | [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|Part C - Complication management]]

### 1.7.1. MECHANISMS OF INJURY

* **Direct needle trauma**: Transection or impalement of nerve fascicles [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Intraneural injection**: Injection within the epineurium but outside fascicles (less damaging) vs. **intrafascicular injection** (high risk of severe injury) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **High injection pressures**: >15-20 psi applied intrafascicularly → mechanical disruption, ischemia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Local anesthetic toxicity**: Concentrated solutions or prolonged exposure (less common with standard clinical doses/concentrations). Preservatives in multi-dose vials may be neurotoxic [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#2.2 Core Anaesthetic Drugs|ⓘ]]
* **Ischemia**: From direct vascular injury, hematoma compression, or vasoconstrictors (**EPINEPHRINE**) in susceptible areas [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Inflammation/Infection**: Direct spread from skin or contaminated equipment [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

### 1.7.2. INJURY PATTERNS

* Needle trauma without injection: Often results in transient paresthesia, lesser magnitude injury [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* Major sequela of severe injury: Endoneurial ischemia, inflammation, axonal damage [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Recovery patterns**: Variable [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * Many resolve spontaneously over days to weeks (neuropraxia)
  * Some take months for partial/full recovery (axonotmesis - requires slow peripheral nerve regeneration ~1mm/day)
  * Some result in permanent deficits (neurotmesis)
* **Hematoma**: Compression around peripheral nerve generally less significant than in enclosed spaces (e.g., epidural/subarachnoid), but can still cause transient neuropathy [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

### 1.7.3. NON-BLOCK COMPLICATIONS (DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS)

* Nerve injury may result from factors related to the surgical procedure itself [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Patient positioning (stretch, compression)
  * Surgical retractors or direct surgical trauma
  * Tourniquet application (ischemia, direct pressure)
  * Inflammation/swelling post-surgery
* These may not be apparent until block regresses [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|ⓘ]]
* Essential to consider these factors when evaluating post-procedure neurologic symptoms. Address patient concerns and differentiate cause [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Multiple mechanisms can lead to nerve injury during regional anesthesia. While severe injuries are rare, understanding these mechanisms helps implement preventive strategies. When evaluating post-block neurologic symptoms, it's crucial to consider both block-related and surgical factors in the differential diagnosis.

### 2.1.1. TRIGEMINAL NERVE (CN V)

* **Major sensory nerve of the face** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.1 Head and Neck (Including Airway)|ⓘ]]
* Originates from semilunar (Gasserian) ganglion [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.1 Head and Neck (Including Airway)|ⓘ]]
* Three major divisions [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.1 Head and Neck (Including Airway)|ⓘ]]:
  * ***Ophthalmic division (V1)***: Exits via superior orbital fissure
	* *Terminal branches*: Supraorbital, Supratrochlear (forehead, upper eyelid, scalp)
	* *Landmark*: Supraorbital nerve exits through palpable **supraorbital notch/foramen**
  * ***Maxillary division (V2)***: Exits via foramen rotundum. Purely sensory
	* *Terminal branch*: Infraorbital nerve (midface, lower eyelid, side of nose, upper lip)
	* *Landmark*: Infraorbital nerve exits through **infraorbital foramen**
  * ***Mandibular division (V3)***: Exits via foramen ovale. Mixed sensory and motor (muscles of mastication)
	* *Terminal sensory branches*: Mental (lower lip, chin), Lingual (anterior 2/3 tongue sensation), Auriculotemporal (temple, TMJ)
	* *Landmark*: Mental nerve exits through **mental foramen**
* 🔑 **Key Relationship**: Supraorbital, infraorbital, and mental foramina lie approximately in the same sagittal plane, roughly 2.5 cm lateral to the midline (pupillary line) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.1 Head and Neck (Including Airway)|ⓘ]]

### 2.1.2. CERVICAL PLEXUS (C1-C4 VENTRAL RAMI)

* Located deep to sternocleidomastoid (SCM) muscle, anterior to levator scapulae and middle scalene [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.1 Head and Neck (Including Airway)|ⓘ]]
* *Sensory branches (Superficial Cervical Plexus)*: Emerge from behind the posterior border of the SCM near its midpoint (Erb's point) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.1 Head and Neck (Including Airway)|ⓘ]]

## 2.2. REGIONAL NERVE ANATOMY - SPINE

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|Part A - Spinal cord anatomy]] | [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|Part B - Neuraxial blocks]] | [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. ### 2.2.1. SPINAL NERVES

* **31 pairs**: 8 Cervical (C1-C8), 12 Thoracic (T1-T12), 5 Lumbar (L1-L5), 5 Sacral (S1-S5), 1 Coccygeal (Co1) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Formed by union of ventral (motor/anterior) and dorsal (sensory/posterior) roots within the intervertebral foramen [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Mixed nerves containing motor, sensory, and sympathetic fibers [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Each divides into [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * *Ventral ramus*: Larger, supplies anterior/lateral trunk and limbs (forms plexuses)
  * *Dorsal ramus*: Smaller, supplies paravertebral muscles and posterior skin

### 2.2.2. ANATOMIC RELATIONSHIPS & NAMING

* C1 nerve exits *above* C1 vertebra [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* C2-C7 nerves exit *above* their corresponding numbered vertebra (e.g., C7 nerve exits C6-C7 foramen) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* C8 nerve exits *below* C7 vertebra (between C7 and T1) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Thoracic, Lumbar, Sacral nerves exit *below* their corresponding numbered vertebra (e.g., L4 nerve exits L4-L5 foramen) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ This difference in naming convention is crucial for accurate block level identification [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

### 2.2.3. PARAVERTEBRAL SPACE (PVS)

* Wedge-shaped potential space adjacent to vertebral bodies [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* *Boundaries (Thoracic)* [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * *Medial*: Vertebral body, intervertebral disc, intervertebral foramen (communicates with epidural space)
  * *Anterolateral*: Parietal pleura
  * *Posterior*: Superior costotransverse ligament (SCTL), transverse process, rib neck
* *Contents*: Spinal nerve roots (ventral/dorsal rami), sympathetic chain/ganglia, intercostal vessels, fat [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

### 2.2.4. VERTEBRAL PROCESS ORIENTATION & LANDMARKS

* *Spinous Processes* [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * C7: Vertebra prominens (most prominent cervical)
  * T7: Level with inferior angle of scapulae
  * L4: Level with iliac crests (Tuffier's line)
* *Transverse Processes (TPs)* [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Thoracic: TPs project posteriorly and inferiorly. Tip of TP is significantly *cephalad* to its own spinous process in mid-thoracic region. Approx 2.5 cm lateral to spinous process
  * Lumbar: TPs project more laterally. Tip of TP is roughly at the *same level* as its own spinous process

**Bottom Line:** Understanding spinal nerve anatomy, especially the relationships between vertebral structures and nerve exits, is essential for performing paravertebral blocks and interpreting dermatomal distributions. The paravertebral space provides access to spinal nerves before they divide into rami, making it a valuable target for truncal analgesia.

### 2.3.2. BRACHIAL PLEXUS ORGANIZATION

* **Origin**: Ventral rami of **C5, C6, C7, C8, T1** spinal nerves (Roots) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* **Organizational structure** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * 📋 **MNEMONIC**: "**R**andy **T**ravis **D**rinks **C**old **B**eer" → **R**oots, **T**runks, **D**ivisions, **C**ords, **B**ranches (Terminal Nerves)
* *Roots (5)*: Emerge between anterior and middle scalene muscles in the neck [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* *Trunks (3)*: Formed above the clavicle in the posterior triangle of the neck [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
  * Superior (C5-C6)
  * Middle (C7)
  * Inferior (C8-T1)
* *Divisions (6)*: Each trunk divides into anterior and posterior divisions behind the clavicle [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
  * Anterior divisions → supply flexor compartments
  * Posterior divisions → supply extensor compartments
* *Cords (3)*: Formed in the axilla, named by relation to the axillary artery (2nd part) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
  * **Lateral Cord**: Anterior divisions of Superior and Middle trunks (C5-C7). Lies lateral to artery
  * **Medial Cord**: Anterior division of Inferior trunk (C8-T1). Lies medial to artery
  * **Posterior Cord**: Posterior divisions of all three trunks (C5-T1). Lies posterior to artery
* *Terminal Branches (5 major)*: Arise from the cords around the axillary artery (3rd part) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
  * From Lateral Cord: **Musculocutaneous nerve**, Lateral root of Median nerve
  * From Medial Cord: **Ulnar nerve**, Medial root of Median nerve, Medial cutaneous nerves of arm/forearm
  * From Posterior Cord: **Axillary nerve**, **Radial nerve**

### 2.3.3. TERMINAL NERVES - COURSE & KEY LOCATIONS

* **MUSCULOCUTANEOUS NERVE (C5-C7)** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Origin: Lateral cord
  * Course: Pierces coracobrachialis muscle, runs between biceps and brachialis
  * Terminal Branch: Lateral cutaneous nerve of forearm (sensory)
  * *Block Relevance*: Often leaves sheath early; needs specific targeting in axillary/infraclavicular blocks
* **AXILLARY NERVE (C5-C6)** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Origin: Posterior cord
  * Course: Passes posteriorly through quadrangular space with posterior circumflex humeral artery. Supplies deltoid, teres minor
  
* **RADIAL NERVE (C5-T1)** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Origin: Posterior cord (largest branch)
  * Course: Runs in spiral groove of humerus with deep brachial artery → crosses elbow anterior to lateral epicondyle → divides in forearm
  * *Block Relevance*: Vulnerable in spiral groove. Supplies extensors of arm/forearm/wrist/fingers
  
* **MEDIAN NERVE (C5-T1)** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Origin: Formed by roots from Lateral (C5-C7) and Medial (C8-T1) cords
  * Course: Runs with brachial artery in arm → crosses elbow medial to artery → passes between heads of pronator teres → runs between FDS/FDP muscles → through carpal tunnel
  * *Block Relevance*: Close relationship to brachial/axillary artery is key US landmark
  
* **ULNAR NERVE (C8-T1)**: (*C7 contribution variable*) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
  * Origin: Medial cord
  * Course: Runs medial to brachial artery → passes posterior to medial epicondyle in cubital tunnel → runs between FCU/FDP muscles → superficial to flexor retinaculum at wrist (Guyon's canal)
  * *Block Relevance*: Palpable behind medial epicondyle. Close relationship to ulnar artery in distal forearm

### 2.3.5. VARIATIONS IN DISTRIBUTION

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

* On the motor side, the ulnar nerve may encroach on median territory and vice versa [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* The ulnar nerve may supply part or even all of the thenar muscles, or the median nerve may innervate adductor pollicis and the 1st dorsal interosseous muscle in addition to the three muscles of the thenar eminence [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* The radial nerve never innervates the intrinsic muscles of the hand [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* On the sensory side, either the radial or the ulnar supply to the dorsum of the hand may enlarge at the expense of the other component [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* The ulnar supply to the dorsum may even, on occasion, be completely deficient [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* On the palmar aspect, the ulnar nerve may extend its area to innervate the whole of the ring finger or even reach the ulnar side of the middle finger. In other cases, it may be confined to the sensory supply of only the 5th finger [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

### 2.3.6. NERVE BLOCKS AT ELBOW AND WRIST

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]] [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* The ulnar, radial and median nerves can be blocked at the elbow or wrist [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* These blocks are often performed to supplement patchy brachial plexus blocks, but do not last as long as plexus blocks [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Occasionally used to provide anesthesia for operations on the hand, and for analgesia for dressing changes [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

**Elbow Blocks** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
* The ulnar nerve is blocked 1–3 cm cephalad to where it passes behind the medial epicondyle of the elbow
* The median nerve lies just medial to the brachial artery
* The radial nerve lies about 2 cm lateral to the biceps tendon
* The cutaneous branches of the musculocutaneous nerve can be blocked by subcutaneous infiltration between the biceps tendon and the lateral epicondyle

**Wrist Blocks** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
* Most anesthetists use a 27G hypodermic needle with a 60° bevel and paresthesiae to locate the nerves
* The ulnar nerve can be blocked just lateral to the tendon of flexor carpi ulnaris or just posterior to it
* The median nerve can be blocked in the midline between the tendons of flexor carpi radialis and palmaris longus
* The cutaneous branches of the radial nerve are blocked by subcutaneous infiltration at the base of the thumb

### 2.3.7. SEGMENTAL INNERVATION OF UPPER LIMB

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

**Cutaneous Supply** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
1. C3 and C4 supply the upper shoulder region (supraclavicular nerves)
2. C5 supplies the deltoid region and the lateral aspect of the arm
3. C6 supplies the lateral forearm and the thumb
4. C7 supplies the hand and the middle three fingers
5. C8 supplies the 5th finger and medial side on the hand and lower forearm
6. T1 supplies the medial side of the lower arm and upper forearm
7. T2 supplies the upper arm on its medial side (via the intercostobrachial nerve)

**Muscular Supply** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
1. C5 supplies the abductors and lateral rotators of the shoulder
2. C6, C7 and C8 supply the adductors and medial rotators of the shoulder
3. C5 and C6 supply the elbow flexors
4. C7 and C8 supply the elbow extensors
5. C6 supplies the pronators and supinators of the forearm
6. C6 and C7 supply the long flexors and extensors of the wrist
7. C7 and C8 supply the long flexors and extensors of the fingers
8. T1 supplies the intrinsic muscles of the hand

**Bottom Line:** The brachial plexus has a complex but consistent organization from roots to terminal nerves that can be targeted at multiple levels. Understanding the plexus formation, cord arrangements around the axillary artery, and course of terminal nerves enables effective block placement at interscalene, supraclavicular, infraclavicular, or axillary approaches.

## 2.4. REGIONAL NERVE ANATOMY - TRUNK

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]] [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]] [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|ⓘ]]

### 2.4.1. INTERCOSTAL NERVES (T1-T11)

* Ventral rami of thoracic spinal nerves. Do not form a plexus [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Course: Run in the **costal groove** on the inferior aspect of their corresponding rib, between the internal and innermost intercostal muscles [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Neurovascular bundle order (superior to inferior): **V**ein, **A**rtery, **N**erve (**VAN**) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Give off lateral cutaneous branch near midaxillary line [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Continue anteriorly to supply abdominal wall muscles (T7-T11, thoracoabdominal nerves) and skin [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* *Block Relevance*: Target nerve in costal groove near angle of rib for block. High vascular uptake [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

**Typical Intercostal Nerves (T3-T6)** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
* Enter their intercostal spaces across the anterior aspect of the corresponding superior costotransverse ligament to lie below the intercostal vessels
* First between the posterior intercostal membrane and the pleura, then at rib angles, between the internal intercostal and innermost intercostal muscles
* Near the margin of the sternum, pass in front of the internal thoracic vessels and transversus thoracis (sternocostalis), pierce the internal intercostal muscle, anterior intercostal membrane and the overlying pectoralis major
* Terminal branches become anterior cutaneous nerves of the thorax

**Special Intercostal Nerves** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
* **T1**: Sends a large contribution to the brachial plexus across the front of the neck of the 1st rib. Remaining part has no lateral cutaneous branch
* **T2**: Lateral cutaneous branch (intercostobrachial nerve) crosses the axilla to supply the skin over the medial aspect of the upper arm. Inevitably destroyed in radical mastectomy

### 2.4.2. SUBCOSTAL NERVE (T12)

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

* Ventral ramus of T12. Runs inferior to 12th rib [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Runs along the lower border of the 12th rib behind the lateral arcuate ligament, behind the kidney and colon [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Passes between transversus abdominis and internal oblique and has similar course to lower intercostal nerves [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Lateral cutaneous branch descends without branching to supply skin over lateral aspect of buttock [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Supplies the pyramidalis muscle (within lowest part of rectus sheath) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

### 2.4.3. ILIOHYPOGASTRIC NERVE (T12-L1) & ILIOINGUINAL NERVE (L1)

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]] [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Arise from lumbar plexus (upper part) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Course: Emerge lateral border of psoas major → run across quadratus lumborum → pierce transversus abdominis near ASIS → run between internal oblique and transversus abdominis muscles (**TAP plane**) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* *Iliohypogastric*: Supplies skin over hypogastric region and lateral gluteal region [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* *Ilioinguinal*: Traverses inguinal canal, supplies skin of upper medial thigh, root of penis/scrotum or mons pubis/labium majus [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* *Block Relevance*: Targeted in TAP blocks and specific ilioinguinal/iliohypogastric blocks for inguinal hernia repair [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

### 2.4.4. GENITOFEMORAL NERVE (L1-L2)

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

* Arises from lumbar plexus [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Course: Pierces psoas major muscle anteriorly [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Divides into [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * *Genital branch*: Enters inguinal canal, supplies cremaster muscle, scrotal/labial skin
  * *Femoral branch*: Passes under inguinal ligament, supplies skin of femoral triangle

**Bottom Line:** Understanding thoracoabdominal wall innervation is critical for truncal blocks. Intercostal nerves follow predictable paths in the intercostal space and can be targeted for analgesia of the chest wall. Abdominal wall innervation involves nerves running in specific fascial planes that can be accessed with TAP, rectus sheath, and ilioinguinal/iliohypogastric blocks.

## 2.5. REGIONAL NERVE ANATOMY - LOWER EXTREMITY

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]] [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]] [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|ⓘ]]

### 2.5.1. LUMBAR PLEXUS (L1-L4, SOMETIMES T12)

* Formed within the **psoas major muscle** by ventral rami of L1-L4 (contribution from T12 variable, L4 contributes to both lumbar and sacral plexuses via lumbosacral trunk) [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* **Major Branches** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * **ILIOHYPOGASTRIC NERVE (T12-L1)** - *See Trunk Anatomy*
  * **ILIOINGUINAL NERVE (L1)** - *See Trunk Anatomy*
  * **GENITOFEMORAL NERVE (L1-L2)** - *See Trunk Anatomy*
  * **LATERAL FEMORAL CUTANEOUS NERVE (LFCN) (L2-L3)**:
	* Purely sensory
	* Course: Emerges lateral border of psoas → crosses iliacus → passes under inguinal ligament near ASIS (anterior superior iliac spine) → supplies skin of anterolateral thigh
	* *Block Relevance*: Targeted for meralgia paresthetica, thigh skin grafts. Variable origin/course
  * **FEMORAL NERVE (L2-L4)**:
	* Largest branch of lumbar plexus
	* Course: Emerges lateral border of psoas → passes deep to inguinal ligament lateral to femoral artery → divides in femoral triangle
	* *Supplies*: Anterior thigh muscles (quadriceps femoris, sartorius, pectineus), skin of anterior thigh and medial leg/foot (via saphenous nerve)
	* *Block Relevance*: Key nerve for knee surgery. Landmark: Lateral to femoral artery
  * **OBTURATOR NERVE (L2-L4)**:
	* Course: Emerges medial border of psoas → passes through obturator foramen into medial thigh
	* Divides into anterior and posterior branches
	* *Supplies*: Medial thigh muscles (adductors), skin of medial thigh, hip and knee joint sensation
	* *Block Relevance*: Often blocked for hip/knee surgery analgesia, TURBT (prevents adductor reflex)

### 2.5.2. SACRAL PLEXUS (L4-S4)

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

* Formed anterior to the **piriformis muscle** by the lumbosacral trunk (L4-L5) and ventral rami of S1-S4 [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* Located deep in the pelvis [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* **Major Branches** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * **SCIATIC NERVE (L4-S3)**:
	* Largest nerve in the body. Actually two nerves bundled together (Tibial and Common Peroneal divisions)
	* Course: Exits pelvis via greater sciatic foramen (usually inferior to piriformis muscle) → runs deep in posterior thigh between adductor magnus and hamstrings → typically bifurcates in distal thigh/popliteal fossa
	* *Supplies*: Posterior thigh muscles (hamstrings), all muscles below the knee (via branches), skin of posterior leg and most of foot
	* *Block Relevance*: Provides anesthesia/analgesia for most of lower leg/foot. Multiple block approaches (posterior, anterior, popliteal)
  * **PUDENDAL NERVE (S2-S4)**:
	* Main sensory nerve of perineum. Motor to external anal/urethral sphincters
	* Course: Exits via greater sciatic foramen → loops around sacrospinous ligament → re-enters pelvis via lesser sciatic foramen → travels in pudendal canal (Alcock's canal)
	* *Block Relevance*: Perineal surgery, chronic pelvic pain, labor analgesia
  * **Superior Gluteal Nerve (L4-S1)**: Supplies gluteus medius/minimus
  * **Inferior Gluteal Nerve (L5-S2)**: Supplies gluteus maximus
  * **Posterior Femoral Cutaneous Nerve (S1-S3)**: Sensory to posterior thigh skin. Travels close to sciatic nerve initially

### 2.5.3. TERMINAL NERVES OF LOWER EXTREMITY - COURSE & KEY LOCATIONS

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

* **SCIATIC NERVE BIFURCATION** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Divides into Tibial Nerve (medial) and Common Peroneal Nerve (lateral). Usually occurs in distal third of thigh or upper popliteal fossa
* **TIBIAL NERVE (L4-S3)** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Course: Descends through popliteal fossa (medial to common peroneal, superficial to popliteal vessels initially, then crosses deep) → runs deep to gastrocnemius/soleus in posterior compartment of leg → passes posterior to medial malleolus through tarsal tunnel → divides into medial and lateral plantar nerves
  * *Block Relevance*: Popliteal block, ankle block (posterior tibial nerve)
* **COMMON PERONEAL (FIBULAR) NERVE (L4-S2)** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Course: Follows medial border of biceps femoris tendon → winds around neck of fibula → divides into superficial and deep peroneal nerves
  * *Block Relevance*: Vulnerable at fibular neck. Popliteal block. Branches blocked at ankle
	* **Superficial Peroneal Nerve**: Supplies lateral compartment muscles (eversion), skin of anterolateral leg and dorsum of foot (except 1st web space)
	* **Deep Peroneal Nerve**: Supplies anterior compartment muscles (dorsiflexion, toe extension), skin of first web space. Runs with anterior tibial artery
* **SAPHENOUS NERVE (L3-L4)** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Terminal sensory branch of the **Femoral Nerve**
  * Course: Travels with femoral artery through adductor canal → emerges between sartorius/gracilis → runs down medial leg alongside great saphenous vein → supplies skin of medial leg, ankle, and foot arch
  * *Block Relevance*: Needs separate block for complete below-knee anesthesia (not covered by sciatic block). Adductor canal block, ankle block
* **SURAL NERVE (S1-S2)** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Purely sensory. Formed by branches from Tibial and Common Peroneal nerves
  * Course: Descends between heads of gastrocnemius → runs near lesser saphenous vein → passes posterior to lateral malleolus → supplies lateral foot and ankle skin
  * *Block Relevance*: Ankle block

### 2.5.4. LUMBAR PLEXUS FORMATION AND DISTRIBUTION

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

**Formation** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
* Derived from anterior primary rami of L1, L2, L3 and part of L4
* About 50% of subjects receive contribution from T12
* May be prefixed (lowest contribution from L3) or post-fixed (extending to L5)
* Assembles in front of transverse processes of lumbar vertebrae within psoas major
* L1 (sometimes with T12) divides into upper and lower divisions
* Upper division gives iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal nerves
* Lower division joins with branch from L2 to form genitofemoral nerve
* Rest of L2 with L3 and L4 contribution divide into dorsal and ventral divisions
* Dorsal divisions of L2-L3 form lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh
* Dorsal divisions of L2-L4 form femoral nerve
* Ventral divisions join to form obturator nerve and accessory obturator nerve (when present)

**Relationship to Psoas Major** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
* Obturator nerve emerges on medial border
* Genitofemoral nerve pierces muscle to lie on anterior surface
* Remaining nerves emerge from lateral border

![Lumbar plexus relationship to psoas](PNB_pdf_extracted_pages_42-77.pdf_p7_img1_d04310.png)

**Femoral Nerve Distribution** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
* Largest nerve of the lumbar plexus (L2-L4)
* Provides motor and sensory innervation to anterior compartment of thigh
* Emerges from lateral margin of psoas, passes between psoas and iliacus, enters thigh beneath inguinal ligament
* At femoral triangle, lies a finger's breadth lateral to femoral artery
* Divides into anterior and posterior divisions with following branches:

**Anterior Division**:
* Muscular branches to pectineus and sartorius
* Cutaneous branches: intermediate and medial cutaneous nerves of thigh

**Posterior Division**:
* Muscular branches to quadriceps femoris
* Cutaneous branch - saphenous nerve
* Articular branches to hip and knee

![Femoral nerve distribution](PNB_pdf_extracted_pages_42-77.pdf_p9_img1_39c91e.png)

**Obturator Nerve Distribution** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
* Emerges from medial border of psoas at pelvic brim
* Passes along side wall of pelvis, lateral to internal iliac vessels and ureter
* Enters obturator canal above and anterior to obturator vessels
* Divides into anterior and posterior branches

**Anterior Branch**:
* Passes above obturator externus
* Supplies adductor longus, gracilis, and often adductor brevis
* Sends articular branch to hip
* Forms subsartorial plexus with medial cutaneous and saphenous branches of femoral nerve

**Posterior Branch**:
* Pierces and supplies obturator externus
* Supplies adductor magnus and sometimes adductor brevis
* Descends along adductor canal to popliteal fossa, ending by supplying knee joint

### 2.5.5. SACRAL PLEXUS FORMATION AND DISTRIBUTION

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

**Formation** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
* Formed from contribution of L4, entire L5, S1, S2, S3, and part of S4
* The coccygeal plexus receives rest of S4, S5, and coccygeal nerve
* L4 and L5 fuse to form lumbosacral trunk at medial border of psoas
* The trunk passes over pelvic brim and joins S1 in front of sacroiliac joint
* S1-S4 emerge through anterior sacral foramina
* Roots join to form wide band which splits into sciatic and pudendal nerves

**Anatomical Relations in Pelvis** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
* Lies on posterior wall of pelvic cavity, behind pelvic fascia on anterior surface of piriformis
* In front lie internal iliac vessels, ureter, sigmoid colon (left) or ileum (right)
* Plexus is pierced by four important vessels:
  1. Iliolumbar vessels (between L4 and L5 roots)
  2. Superior gluteal vessels (between lumbosacral trunk and S1 or S1-S2)
  3. Inferior gluteal vessels (between S1-S2 or S2-S3)
  4. Internal pudendal vessels (between sciatic and pudendal nerves)

![Sacral plexus relations in pelvis](PNB_pdf_extracted_pages_42-77.pdf_p16_img1_6d6475.png)

**Sciatic Nerve** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
* Largest peripheral nerve in body (L4, L5, S1-3)
* Actually composed of two nerves (tibial and common peroneal) within common sheath
* Usually splits at apex of popliteal fossa, but division may occur more proximally
* Occasionally the components separate at origin, with common peroneal piercing piriformis
* Course: Exits posterior pelvic wall through greater sciatic foramen below piriformis, enters buttock slightly medial to midpoint between ischial tuberosity and greater trochanter, descends vertically down midline of back of leg to popliteal fossa
* Branches include motor branches to hamstrings, adductor magnus (ischial portion), articular branch to hip joint, and terminals (common peroneal and tibial nerves)

* Line from midpoint between posterosuperior iliac spine and ischial tuberosity, curving slightly outward to point midway between greater trochanter and ischial tuberosity, then continuing vertically down midline of posterior thigh

### 2.5.6. SEGMENTAL INNERVATION OF LOWER LIMB

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]

**Cutaneous Innervation** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
1. L1, L2 and L3 supply the front of the thigh from above down
2. L4 supplies the anteromedial aspect of the leg
3. L5 supplies the anterolateral aspect of the leg but also extends on to the medial side of the foot
4. S1 supplies the lateral side of the foot and the sole
5. S2 supplies the posterior surface of the leg and thigh
6. S3 and S4 supply the buttock and perianal region

**Muscular Innervation** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
1. L2 and L3 supply the flexors, adductors and medial rotators of the hip
2. L3 and L4 supply the extensors, abductors and lateral rotators of the hip
3. L3 and L4 supply the extensors of the knee
4. L5 and S1 supply the flexors of the knee
5. L4 and L5 supply the dorsiflexors of the ankle
6. S1 and S2 supply the plantar flexors of the ankle
7. L4 supplies the ankle invertors
8. L5 and S1 supply the ankle evertors

**Bottom Line:** The lower extremity is innervated by two major plexuses - lumbar (supplying anterior/medial thigh via femoral and obturator nerves) and sacral (supplying posterior thigh and most of leg/foot via sciatic nerve). Complete lower extremity anesthesia typically requires blocks targeting both plexuses, with the exception of distal procedures where ankle blocks may suffice.

## 2.6. ULTRASOUND LANDMARKS FOR NERVE LOCALIZATION

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]] [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]] [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|ⓘ]]

### 2.6.1. UPPER EXTREMITY LANDMARKS

* **Interscalene block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Target*: Roots/Trunks (C5-C7 primarily)
  * *Landmarks*: Between anterior and middle scalene muscles at C6 level (cricoid cartilage). Nerves appear as hypoechoic circles ("traffic lights"). Subclavian artery deep/inferior
* **Supraclavicular block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Target*: Trunks/Divisions
  * *Landmarks*: Superior and posterior to subclavian artery, lateral to first rib. Nerves appear as hypoechoic cluster ("bunch of grapes"). Pleura deep to first rib
* **Infraclavicular block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Target*: Cords
  * *Landmarks*: Deep to pectoralis muscles, surrounding axillary artery inferior to clavicle/coracoid process. Cords appear hyperechoic relative to artery. (Lateral, Posterior, Medial cords)
* **Axillary block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Target*: Terminal Nerves (Median, Ulnar, Radial, Musculocutaneous)
  * *Landmarks*: Surrounding axillary artery in axilla. Nerves appear as distinct hyperechoic structures. Musculocutaneous nerve often separate in coracobrachialis muscle

### 2.6.2. PERIPHERAL NERVE BLOCKS (UPPER EXTREMITY)

> [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Radial nerve at spiral groove/elbow** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Landmarks*: Between brachialis/brachioradialis (elbow) or adjacent to humerus/deep brachial artery (spiral groove). Hyperechoic
* **Median nerve at elbow/forearm** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Landmarks*: Medial to brachial artery at elbow; between FDS/FDP muscles in forearm. Hyperechoic, honeycomb appearance
* **Ulnar nerve at elbow/forearm** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Landmarks*: Cubital tunnel (medial epicondyle); adjacent to ulnar artery in forearm. Hyperechoic, honeycomb appearance

### 2.6.3. LOWER EXTREMITY LANDMARKS

> [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Lumbar plexus block (Psoas Compartment)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Target*: Lumbar plexus roots within psoas muscle
  * *Landmarks*: Deep to psoas muscle, anterior to transverse processes (L3-L4). Difficult visualization. "Shamrock sign" with US
* **Femoral block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Target*: Femoral nerve
  * *Landmarks*: Lateral to femoral artery, inferior to inguinal ligament, superficial to iliopsoas muscle, deep to fascia iliaca. Hyperechoic, triangular/oval shape. (NAVEL - Nerve, Artery, Vein, Empty space, Lymphatics from lateral to medial)
* **Sciatic block (Gluteal/Subgluteal)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Target*: Sciatic nerve
  * *Landmarks*: Between ischial tuberosity and greater trochanter, deep to gluteus maximus. Large, hyperechoic, flat/oval structure
* **Popliteal block (Sciatic bifurcation)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Target*: Tibial and Common Peroneal nerves
  * *Landmarks*: Proximal to popliteal crease where sciatic nerve bifurcates, typically lateral/superficial to popliteal artery/vein. Nerves hyperechoic
* **Adductor Canal Block (Saphenous nerve)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Target*: Saphenous nerve primarily
  * *Landmarks*: Mid-thigh, deep to sartorius muscle, anterolateral to femoral artery within the canal. Nerve small, hyperechoic

### 2.6.4. ANKLE BLOCKS

> [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Tibial (Posterior Tibial) block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Target*: Tibial nerve
  * *Landmarks*: Posterior to posterior tibial artery, behind medial malleolus. Hyperechoic, honeycomb
* **Deep Peroneal block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Target*: Deep peroneal nerve
  * *Landmarks*: Lateral to anterior tibial artery, between tibialis anterior and EHL tendons at ankle crease level. Small, hyperechoic
* **Superficial Peroneal, Sural, Saphenous**: Often blocked via subcutaneous infiltration based on landmarks, but US can identify nerves/associated vessels [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Ultrasound has revolutionized peripheral nerve blocks by allowing direct visualization of target nerves and surrounding structures. Understanding the sonographic appearance of nerves (typically hyperechoic in cross-section with honeycomb internal architecture) and their relationship to key vascular, muscular and bony landmarks is essential for successful ultrasound-guided regional anesthesia.

## 2.7. ANATOMIC LANDMARKS AND PRACTICAL CONSIDERATIONS

> [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]] [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]] [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|ⓘ]]

* **KEY BONY LANDMARKS**: Clavicle, Ribs, Spinous/Transverse Processes, ASIS, Iliac Crest, Pubic Tubercle, Greater Trochanter, Ischial Tuberosity, Epicondyles, Malleoli [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]
* **VASCULAR RELATIONSHIPS** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * **Clinical Practice**: Major nerves often travel with corresponding arteries/veins ("neurovascular bundles"). Arteries are reliable US landmarks (pulsatile, non-compressible)
  * Use Doppler US to identify vessels and avoid puncture
* **FASCIAL PLANES** [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Local anesthetic spreads within fascial compartments (e.g., Psoas compartment, Fascia Iliaca compartment, TAP plane, Plexus sheaths)
  * Septations within sheaths may necessitate multiple injections or larger volumes for complete block
* **ULTRASOUND APPEARANCE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Nerves typically appear **hyperechoic** (bright epineurium) with **hypoechoic** fascicles inside (honeycomb pattern in cross-section). Appearance varies with location and nerve size
  * *Anisotropy*: Nerve appearance changes significantly with US beam angle; keep probe perpendicular to nerve for best image
* **Clinical Pearl**: Always consider common anatomic variations when performing blocks, especially if encountering difficulty. US helps identify variations in real-time [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Understanding anatomical relationships, especially between nerves and adjacent structures (vessels, muscles, bones), is the foundation for successful regional anesthesia. Ultrasound allows visualization of these relationships but requires knowledge of expected sonoanatomy and the ability to recognize variations.

# 3. SPECIFIC TECHNIQUES FOR REGIONAL ANESTHESIA

## 3.1. HEAD AND NECK REGIONAL ANESTHESIA

> [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]] [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|ⓘ]]

**APPLICATIONS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Procedures**: Ophthalmic, neurologic, ENT, plastic, endocrine (thyroid/parathyroid) surgeries
* **Pain Management**: Acute (post-op) and chronic pain syndromes (e.g., trigeminal neuralgia, occipital neuralgia, cervicogenic headache). Diagnostic blocks
* **Anesthetic Technique**: Can be sole anesthetic, supplement to GA, or primarily for postoperative analgesia
* ⚠️ **Airway Concerns**: Often performed without a definitive airway; requires careful patient selection, monitoring, and preparedness for airway management
* **Localization Methods**: Primarily anatomic landmarks. Paresthesia elicitation common. NS/US less common but use is growing

### 3.1.1. TRIGEMINAL NERVE BLOCKS (CN V)

> [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

**PREPARATION** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Aseptic technique (antiseptic solution, sterile equipment)
* Needles: Typically 24-27 gauge, 25-40 mm length
* Syringes: 1-5 mL
* Patient position: Usually supine
* All described blocks use extraoral route

**SEMILUNAR (GASSERIAN) GANGLION BLOCK** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Target*: Central ganglion where V1, V2, V3 originate
* *Indication*: Primarily trigeminal neuralgia (often neurolytic block)
* *Technique*: Technically challenging, usually performed under fluoroscopic guidance by pain specialists. Rarely by OR anesthesiologists

**SUPERFICIAL TRIGEMINAL NERVE BRANCH BLOCKS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Targets*: Terminal branches exiting facial foramina
* *Landmark Principle*: Supraorbital, infraorbital, and mental foramina lie in same sagittal plane (~2.5 cm lateral to midline/pupil). Easily palpable or located by US

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_12.png_b33e41.png_8bc3c3.png_a2c7d8.png)

**1. Supraorbital Nerve Block (V1 Branch)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Target*: Supraorbital nerve
* *Landmark*: Supraorbital notch/foramen at superior medial orbital rim. Palpable or US identified
* *Technique*: Insert needle just superior/lateral to notch, advance slightly. Aspirate, inject slowly
* *Anesthesia*: Ipsilateral forehead, scalp to vertex
* *Dose*: 1-3 mL LA

**2. Supratrochlear Nerve Block (V1 Branch)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Target*: Supratrochlear nerve (medial branch)
* *Landmark*: Superior medial angle of orbit
* *Technique*: Superficial infiltration at landmark. Often blocked concurrently with supraorbital
* *Anesthesia*: Medial forehead near midline
* *Dose*: 1-2 mL LA. Required if anesthesia needs to cross midline

**3. Infraorbital Nerve Block (V2 Branch)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Target*: Infraorbital nerve
* *Landmark*: Infraorbital foramen (~1 cm below midpoint of inferior orbital rim). Palpable depression or identified by US (hypoechoic discontinuity in hyperechoic bone)
* *Technique*: Insert needle ~0.5 cm below anticipated foramen, direct slightly cephalad. Contact bone, withdraw slightly. Aspirate, inject slowly
* *Anesthesia*: Midface: lower eyelid, side of nose, upper lip
* *Dose*: 2-3 mL LA

**4. Mental Nerve Block (V3 Branch)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Target*: Mental nerve
* *Landmark*: Mental foramen, inferior to second premolar tooth, midway between upper/lower mandible borders. Palpable or US identified
* *Technique*: Mental canal angles medially/inferiorly. Insert needle ~0.5 cm superior/lateral to foramen, direct towards it. Aspirate, inject slowly at opening
* *Anesthesia*: Lower lip, chin on ipsilateral side
* *Dose*: 1-3 mL LA
* Warning: Avoid injection directly *into* canal to reduce nerve injury risk

**CLINICAL PEARLS - TRIGEMINAL BLOCKS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Local Anesthetic Choice**:
  * Short duration (diagnosis, minor repair): 1% **LIDOCAINE** or **MEPIVACAINE**
  * Long duration (post-op analgesia, chronic pain): 0.25-0.5% **BUPIVACAINE** or 0.5-0.75% **ROPIVACAINE**
* **Volume Guidance**:
  * Surgical anesthesia: 2-5 mL per nerve
  * Diagnostic/therapeutic/infant blocks: 0.5-1 mL per nerve
* **Post-block**: Apply local compression to minimize hematoma risk
* **Facial Lacerations**: Peripheral blocks offer effective alternative to painful local infiltration. May need supplemental infiltration for incomplete block
* **Infraorbital nerve block for cleft lip repair**: Effective postoperative analgesia. Landmark palpation difficult in neonates; US helpful
* **Craniotomy Procedures**: Scalp blocks (supraorbital, supratrochlear, auriculotemporal, greater/lesser occipital) significantly reduce postoperative pain and opioid requirements
  * *Supraorbital variation*: Nerve may exit as single trunk or separate medial/lateral branches. Ensure adequate spread for procedures like stereotactic frame pin placement
* **Aging Consideration**: Mandibular resorption makes mental foramen appear more superior on ramus in older patients

### 3.1.2. MAXILLARY NERVE BLOCK (V2)

> [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

**INDICATIONS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* When infraorbital block is inadequate (more proximal anesthesia needed)
* Procedures involving maxillary teeth, palate, nasal cavity
* Trigeminal neuralgia (V2 distribution)

**APPROACHES** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Lateral (Extraoral) Approach to Pterygopalatine Fossa**:
  * *Patient Position*: Supine, head turned slightly away; or sitting with mouth slightly open
  * *Landmarks*: Zygomatic arch, mandibular notch (depression below zygoma between coronoid/condylar processes)
  * *Technique (Below Zygomatic Arch)*:
	1. Mark lowest point of mandibular notch ("X"). Create skin wheal
	2. Insert 60-90 mm needle perpendicular to skin, angled slightly superiorly and anteriorly (towards pterygopalatine fossa)
	3. Advance until contact with lateral pterygoid plate (bone, ~4-5 cm deep). Note depth
	4. Withdraw slightly, redirect more anteriorly and superiorly, advance needle ~1 cm past plate into fossa
	5. Confirm location by eliciting paresthesia (upper teeth, nose, lip) or using fluoroscopy/US if available
	6. Warning: **Highly vascular area**: Aspirate frequently. Inject 3-5 mL LA incrementally

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_13.png_d0f659.png_ed5831.png_8184f6.png)

* **Suprazygomatic Maxillary Nerve (SZMN) Block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Indication*: Common for post-cleft palate repair analgesia, pediatric adenotonsillectomy. Targets V2 in pterygopalatine fossa via infratemporal fossa
  * *Landmark Technique (Pediatric)*:
	1. Insert 22G, 5 cm needle perpendicular at frontozygomatic angle (lateral canthus level)
	2. Contact greater wing of sphenoid (~10-15 mm deep)
	3. Redirect caudally, anteriorly, medially into infratemporal fossa
	4. Advance towards pterygopalatine fossa until gentle osseous contact (~35-45 mm deep)
	5. Inject LA: `Dose: 0.2 mL/kg` (e.g., 0.5% **ROPIVACAINE**) up to max 5 mL per side. Dilute to 5 mL total volume if needed
  * *Ultrasound-Guided Technique*:
	6. High-frequency linear probe in infrazygomatic area, angled 45° cephalad
	7. Visualize maxillary artery/vein deep in fossae. Nerve rarely seen
	8. Needle approach often OOP. Observe tissue expansion with injection

	![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_14.png_48b3bd.png_236bae.png_4f6be7.png)

  * *Clinical Pearls (SZMN)*:
	* Targets a fossa, not directly perineural
	* 5 mL volume may exceed fossa capacity, causing spread
	* Provides numbness in V2 distribution
	* Warning: Does NOT block glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX) - preserves airway reflexes

**CLINICAL PEARLS - MAXILLARY BLOCK** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Warning: Watch for LA spread to orbit (orbital pain, vision changes) - stop injection, withdraw needle if occurs
* Can be combined with mandibular block for V2/V3 trigeminal neuralgia

### 3.1.3. MANDIBULAR NERVE BLOCK (V3)

> [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

**INDICATIONS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Dental procedures (inferior alveolar nerve)
* Surgery on mandible, floor of mouth, anterior tongue
* Trigeminal neuralgia (V3 distribution)
* Temporomandibular joint (TMJ) dysfunction

**SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Warning: Only trigeminal branch with significant motor component (muscles of mastication). Block causes temporary inability to chew effectively

**PROCEDURE (LATERAL EXTRAORAL APPROACH)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Patient Position*: Supine, face turned away
* *Landmarks*: Same as maxillary block (mandibular notch below zygoma)
* *Technique*:
  1. Insert 60-90 mm needle at mandibular notch ("X") perpendicular to skin (NO cephalad angulation needed unlike V2 block)
  2. Advance slowly until contact with lateral pterygoid plate (~4-5 cm deep). Note depth
  3. Withdraw needle to skin, redirect slightly caudad (~10-15°) to pass *below* the transverse process
  4. Advance needle an additional **1.5-2 cm** deeper than the depth of the transverse process. Needle tip should now be in infratemporal fossa
  5. Elicit paresthesia in mandibular nerve distribution (lower teeth, lip, tongue sensation) or target **0.3-0.5 mA** with NS
* *Injection*:
  * After negative aspiration
  * Inject 3-5 mL LA incrementally

**CLINICAL PEARLS - MANDIBULAR BLOCK** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Anesthesia of auriculotemporal nerve (supplies TMJ, temple) may be delayed
* Facial nerve (CN VII) palsy possible with large volumes (usually transient)
* Consider alternative intraoral techniques (Gow-Gates, Akinosi-Vazirani) for dental procedures or if extraoral approach fails

### 3.1.4. CERVICAL PLEXUS BLOCKS (C1-C4)

**INDICATIONS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Surgery on lateral/anterior neck:
  * Carotid endarterectomy (CEA)
  * Thyroidectomy/Parathyroidectomy
  * Lymph node dissection
  * Clavicle fracture repair (supraclavicular branches)
* Analgesia for neck/shoulder pain

### 3.1.5. DEEP CERVICAL PLEXUS BLOCK

* *Target*: Ventral rami of C2, C3, C4 as they emerge from transverse processes. Blocks both sensory and motor components (including phrenic nerve partially) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Patient Position*: Supine, head turned ~45° away, slight neck extension [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Landmarks* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Mastoid process
  * Chassaignac's tubercle (anterior tubercle of C6 transverse process - most prominent)
  * Line connecting mastoid and C6 tubercle (approximates location of TPs)
  * Palpate TPs of C2, C3, C4 along this line (approx 1.5 cm intervals, C2 is ~1.5 cm below mastoid). Mark points ~0.5-1 cm posterior to TP tips
* *Technique (Classic Multi-injection)* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  1. Infiltrate skin at C2, C3, C4 marks
  2. Insert three 22G, 3.5-5 cm needles perpendicular to skin, angled slightly caudad (~30°) and posteriorly
  3. Advance slowly until **contact with transverse process** (bone)
  4. Withdraw needle 1-2 mm (to exit periosteum)
  5. Aspirate carefully (vertebral artery risk!)
  6. Inject LA at each level:
	 * Surgical block: 3-5 mL per level (Total 10-15 mL)
	 * Analgesia/Diagnostic: 2-3 mL per level
* *Technique (Single Injection - Winnie)*: Inject larger volume (10-15 mL) at C3 or C4 level. Less precise [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Ultrasound Guidance* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Identify TPs and nerve roots posterior to SCM. Nerves appear hypoechoic between anterior/posterior tubercles. Inject LA around roots. Can visualize vertebral artery medially

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_15.png_5634aa.png_9a11dd.png_738578.png)

**CLINICAL PEARLS - DEEP CERVICAL PLEXUS BLOCK** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Paresthesia* during needle placement associated with higher success
* *Carotid Endarterectomy*: Often combined with superficial block. Benefit over superficial alone is debated. May still need surgeon infiltration at carotid bifurcation (glossopharyngeal nerve)
* ⚠️ **Life-threatening complications possible**:
  * **Intravascular injection** into vertebral artery (runs through transverse foramina C1-C6)
  * **Subarachnoid/Epidural injection**: If needle advanced too medially/deeply through intervertebral foramen. Dural sleeves extend further along cervical roots. → High spinal/Total spinal
  * Requires careful aspiration, incremental injection, close monitoring (minimum 30-60 min)
* ⚠️ **Phrenic nerve palsy** (C3, C4, C5 contribution): **UNIVERSAL** with effective deep block. Causes hemidiaphragmatic paresis
  * **Contraindicated** in patients with severe respiratory disease or contralateral phrenic palsy
  * **Bilateral deep blocks absolutely contraindicated**
* *Other common side effects*:
  * Horner syndrome (ptosis, miosis, anhydrosis) due to sympathetic chain block
  * Recurrent laryngeal nerve block → Hoarseness

### 3.1.6. SUPERFICIAL CERVICAL PLEXUS BLOCK

* *Target*: Sensory branches (lesser occipital, great auricular, transverse cervical, supraclavicular) as they emerge from behind SCM. Blocks skin/superficial structures only. **Does NOT reliably block phrenic nerve** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Patient Position*: Same as deep block [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Landmarks*: Midpoint of the posterior border of the SCM muscle (Erb's point) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Technique* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  1. Identify midpoint of posterior SCM border
  2. Insert needle subcutaneously at this point
  3. Inject LA in a fan-like distribution along the posterior border, 2-4 cm superiorly and 2-4 cm inferiorly from midpoint, staying superficial to deep cervical fascia
  * *Dose*: 10-20 mL LA (e.g., 0.25-0.5% **BUPIVACAINE** or **ROPIVACAINE**). `Pediatric Dose: 0.1-0.2 mL/kg`
* *Ultrasound Guidance*: Visualize SCM, inject LA in plane between SCM and levator scapulae/scalene muscles (sub-SCM plane) or more superficially [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_16.png_9a059c.png_0ebe01.png_ecfdde.png)

**CLINICAL PEARLS - SUPERFICIAL CERVICAL PLEXUS BLOCK** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Safer alternative to deep block, especially when phrenic nerve function is critical
* Sufficient for many superficial neck procedures (e.g., lymph node biopsy, minimally invasive parathyroidectomy when combined with local infiltration)
* US guidance may improve consistency but blind technique often effective
* Thyroid surgery possible under superficial block + anterior field block (requires surgeon cooperation/modified technique)
* ⚠️ **Complications still possible** (though less frequent than deep block):
  * Phrenic nerve paresis (less common/complete than deep block, but can occur with deep spread)
  * Recurrent laryngeal nerve block (hoarseness)
  * Intravascular injection (external jugular vein nearby)

### 3.1.7. OCCIPITAL NERVE BLOCKS

**OVERVIEW** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Targets*: Greater Occipital Nerve (GON - C2 dorsal ramus) and Lesser Occipital Nerve (LON - C2/C3 ventral rami)
* *Indications*:
  * Diagnosis/treatment of occipital neuralgia, cervicogenic headaches, migraines, cluster headaches
  * Analgesia for posterior scalp procedures (e.g., craniotomy closure, otoplasty)
* Rarely used as primary surgical anesthetic

**PROCEDURE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Patient Position*: Sitting or prone, head slightly flexed
* *Landmarks*:
  * External occipital protuberance (EOP)
  * Superior nuchal line (SNL)
  * Occipital artery pulsation (medial to GON)
  * **Greater Occipital Nerve (GON)**: Located ~1/3 of the way from EOP to mastoid process along SNL, typically ~2-2.5 cm lateral to midline, just medial to occipital artery pulsation. Nerve emerges from trapezius/semispinalis capitis here
  * **Lesser Occipital Nerve (LON)**: Located further lateral along SNL, ~2/3 of the way from EOP to mastoid, or ~2.5 cm lateral to GON location. Emerges posterior to SCM
* *Technique*:
  1. Identify landmarks. Palpate occipital artery
  2. Insert short, fine needle (25-27G, 25 mm) perpendicular to skin at GON location (medial to artery). Advance until contact with bone (occiput)
  3. Withdraw 1-2 mm. Aspirate
  4. Inject LA:
	 * Diagnostic: 0.5-1 mL (e.g., 1% **LIDOCAINE**)
	 * Therapeutic/Analgesic: 1-3 mL (e.g., 0.25-0.5% **BUPIVACAINE**/**ROPIVACAINE** +/- steroid)
  5. Repeat for LON if needed (more lateral injection)
  6. Paresthesia not required but may occur
* *Ultrasound Guidance*: Can visualize GON adjacent to occipital artery between semispinalis/trapezius muscles. LON harder to see. Allows targeted injection [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

**CLINICAL PEARLS - OCCIPITAL BLOCKS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* 🏥 **Otoplasty Analgesia**: Block LON and Great Auricular nerve (branch of superficial cervical plexus) with subcutaneous infiltration from angle of mandible towards mastoid process. Reduces opioid needs/PONV
* **Headache Management**: GON block is common treatment. May need to block supraorbital/auriculotemporal nerves as well for comprehensive coverage
* **Complications**: Rare. Local hematoma possible
  * ⚠️ Avoid advancing needle too deep or angled superiorly near foramen magnum (risk with long needles). US guidance improves safety for deeper/suboccipital approaches

**Bottom Line:** Head and neck blocks provide effective anesthesia and analgesia for a variety of procedures. While primarily landmark-based, ultrasound guidance is increasingly utilized for improved precision. Understanding the location of superficial cranial and cervical nerves in relation to foramina, vascular structures, and muscular landmarks is essential for successful block placement. Careful technique is required given the proximity to critical structures, particularly with deep cervical blocks.

## 3.2. BRACHIAL PLEXUS BLOCKS FOR UPPER EXTREMITY


### 3.2.1. SYLLABUS CORRELATION

* **Part B:** Brachial plexus blocks, indications, techniques [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]].
* **Part C:** Block performance, safety considerations [[mmed_anaes_part_c_syllabus#2. Patient Care and Procedural Skills|ⓘ]].



### 3.2.2. ANATOMICAL APPROACH LOCATIONS

* **Brachial plexus can be blocked at multiple levels** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  1. **Interscalene**: Roots/Trunks (Neck) - Best for shoulder/proximal arm
  2. **Supraclavicular**: Trunks/Divisions (Above clavicle) - "Spinal of the arm," good for entire arm except possibly medial aspect (T1)
  3. **Infraclavicular**: Cords (Below clavicle) - Good for entire arm, catheter placement
  4. **Axillary**: Terminal Nerves (Axilla) - Best for elbow, forearm, hand. Misses musculocutaneous often
* **Ultrasound (US)** is now the primary guidance modality, often combined with Nerve Stimulation (NS) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* ⚠️ Clear needle visualization and understanding of surrounding anatomy (vessels, pleura, nerves) is essential for safety [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Approach selection depends on**: Surgical site, patient factors, provider experience, desired block characteristics (e.g., catheter vs single shot) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

### 3.2.3. INTERSCALENE BLOCK

**CLINICAL APPLICATIONS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* 🔑 **Primary indication**: Surgery on the **shoulder** and **proximal humerus**
* Provides excellent anesthesia/analgesia for C5-C6 distribution
* May provide incomplete anesthesia for hand/forearm (often spares C8-T1 roots/inferior trunk)
* Low interscalene approach (targeting C7) may improve distal coverage

**PATIENT POSITIONING** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Supine or semi-sitting (beach chair)
* Head turned slightly (~30-45°) to contralateral side
* *Tips to accentuate landmarks*: Patient reach for ipsilateral knee, slight head elevation, deep breath (tenses scalenes)

**LANDMARKS (TRADITIONAL/NS)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Interscalene groove**: Palpable groove between anterior and middle scalene muscles
* *Level*: Cricoid cartilage (C6 vertebral level)
* Posterior border of clavicular head of sternocleidomastoid (SCM) muscle
* External jugular vein often crosses the groove superficially
* C6 transverse process (Chassaignac's tubercle) may be palpable deep in the groove

**NERVE STIMULATION (NS) TECHNIQUE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Needle*: 22G, 35-50 mm insulated needle
* *Insertion Point*: Interscalene groove at level of cricoid cartilage (C6)
* *Direction*: **Strictly perpendicular** to skin in all planes OR directed slightly **medially, caudally, and posteriorly** ("down and back") towards C6 transverse process
  * ⚠️ **CRITICAL**: Avoid excessive cephalad or anterior angulation to prevent entry into vertebral artery or epidural/spinal space via neural foramen
* *Depth*: Plexus is usually very superficial (0.5 - 2 cm)
* *Nerve Localization*:
  * Initial current: 0.8-1.0 mA
  * **Target Response**: Motor twitch of **deltoid (axillary n.), biceps/brachialis (musculocutaneous n.), pectoralis major (upper trunk branches), or forearm/hand** at **0.3-0.5 mA**
  * **AVOID**: Diaphragmatic twitch (phrenic nerve stimulation - needle too anterior) or Trapezius twitch (spinal accessory nerve or dorsal scapular nerve - needle too posterior). Redirect accordingly
* *Injection*:
  * After negative aspiration for blood/CSF
  * Inject LA (e.g., 15-30 mL of 0.5% **ROPIVACAINE** or **BUPIVACAINE**) incrementally. Volume depends on goal (surgical anesthesia vs analgesia). US allows lower volumes

**ULTRASOUND-GUIDED (US) TECHNIQUE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Probe*: High-frequency linear probe (10-15 MHz)
* *Scanning*: Transverse orientation at level of cricoid cartilage. Identify SCM, slide probe posteriorly over anterior scalene to visualize interscalene groove
* *Sonographic Appearance ("Traffic Light Sign")*:
  * **Roots/Trunks (C5, C6, C7)** appear as round/oval **hypoechoic** structures nestled between hyperechoic anterior and middle scalene muscles. C5 often smallest/most superficial, C7 largest/deepest
  * Anterior scalene muscle (ASM) is anterior/medial. Middle scalene muscle (MSM) is posterior/lateral
  * Carotid artery & Internal Jugular Vein medial to ASM. Vertebral artery deep/medial to ASM. Phrenic nerve often visible on surface of ASM
* *Needling*:
  * Typically **In-Plane (IP)** approach from lateral-to-medial
  * Needle: 22G, 50 mm echogenic needle
  * Aim needle tip into the interscalene groove, posterior to ASM, anterior to MSM, ideally positioning tip among the nerve roots/trunks
  * NS confirmation (0.3-0.5 mA) recommended
* *Local Anesthetic Spread*:
  * Inject LA incrementally, visualizing spread around the target nerve structures within the fascial sheath. Goal is circumferential spread
  * *Volume*: 10-20 mL often sufficient with US guidance

**CLINICAL PEARLS - INTERSCALENE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Long-acting LAs provide 10-18 hours of analgesia
* Continuous catheter placement effective for extended analgesia (e.g., total shoulder arthroplasty). Catheter tip ideally placed near C5/C6 roots. Securing catheter challenging due to neck movement (tunneling helps)
* Avoid injecting if stimulating current < 0.2 mA (risk of intraneural injection)
* Low volume (5-10mL) targeted blocks may reduce side effects but potentially shorter duration/less dense block

**COMPLICATIONS - INTERSCALENE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* ⚠️ **Phrenic Nerve Block**: **Essentially 100% incidence** with traditional volumes. Causes ipsilateral hemidiaphragmatic paralysis. Usually well-tolerated in healthy patients but risky in severe pulmonary disease. US-guided low volume techniques may reduce incidence/severity
* **Horner Syndrome**: Common due to LA spread to cervical sympathetic chain (stellate ganglion). Usually benign
* **Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve Block**: Hoarseness. Less common
* **Spinal/Epidural Injection**: Rare but devastating if needle directed too medially/deeply into intervertebral foramen
* **Vertebral Artery Injection**: Rare, risk of seizure/CNS toxicity even with small LA volume. Artery lies deep/medial to roots
* **Pneumothorax**: Very rare with proper technique (pleura is much lower)
* **Nerve Injury**: To roots or long thoracic/dorsal scapular nerves (pass through middle scalene)

### 3.2.4. SUPRACLAVICULAR BLOCK

**CLINICAL APPLICATIONS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Provides anesthesia/analgesia for the **entire upper extremity below the shoulder**. "Spinal of the arm"
* Excellent for elbow, forearm, and hand surgery
* May spare medial cutaneous nerve of arm (intercostobrachial T2)

**PATIENT POSITIONING** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Supine, head turned ~45° to contralateral side. Arm adducted. Small pillow under head/shoulders may help

**LANDMARKS (TRADITIONAL/NS)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Clavicle (midpoint)
* Interscalene groove superiorly
* Subclavian artery pulsation (may be palpable superior/posterior to clavicle midpoint)
* Block performed just superior to clavicle where plexus is most compact

**NERVE STIMULATION (NS) TECHNIQUE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Needle*: 22G, 35-50 mm insulated needle
* *Insertion Point*: ~1 cm superior and posterior to clavicle midpoint, in line with interscalene groove
* *Direction*: Needle aimed **caudally**, parallel to midline (or slightly medially/posteriorly), "walking" down towards first rib
  * ⚠️ **CRITICAL**: Maintain shallow angle relative to chest wall to avoid pneumothorax. Needle should NOT be directed medially towards lung apex
* *Nerve Localization*:
  * Initial current: 0.8-1.0 mA
  * **Target Response**: Any motor twitch of the **hand or forearm** (flexion/extension of fingers/wrist) at **0.3-0.5 mA**. Proximal twitches (shoulder/arm) less reliable
  * First rib contact may be felt as endpoint (plexus lies on rib)
* *Injection*:
  * After negative aspiration
  * Inject 20-30 mL LA incrementally

**ULTRASOUND-GUIDED (US) TECHNIQUE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Probe*: High-frequency linear probe (10-15 MHz)
* *Scanning*: Transverse or slightly oblique orientation in supraclavicular fossa, just superior to clavicle. Start laterally, scan medially
* *Sonographic Appearance ("Corner Pocket")*:
  * **Subclavian Artery**: Round, anechoic, pulsatile structure sitting atop the **First Rib** (hyperechoic line with posterior acoustic shadow)
  * **Brachial Plexus (Trunks/Divisions)**: Appears as a cluster of **hypoechoic** round structures ("bunch of grapes") located **lateral and posterior/superior** to the subclavian artery
  * **Pleura**: Hyperechoic line deep to the first rib. Visualize clearly!
  * Subclavian vein is usually medial/anterior/inferior to artery (may not be in view)
* *Needling*:
  * Typically **In-Plane (IP)** approach from **lateral-to-medial**
  * Needle: 22G, 50 mm echogenic needle
  * Insert needle lateral to plexus, advance medially towards the "corner pocket" formed by the subclavian artery and first rib
  * Aim needle tip deep/posterior to the plexus cluster, adjacent to the artery/rib junction
  * NS confirmation helpful
* *Local Anesthetic Spread*:
  * Inject LA incrementally into the corner pocket. Visualize LA lifting the plexus cluster superiorly and spreading medially/laterally around nerves
  * *Volume*: 15-25 mL often sufficient

**CLINICAL PEARLS - SUPRACLAVICULAR** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* 🔑 HY: Considered the most efficient block for entire arm due to compact nature of plexus here
* ⚠️ **Highest risk of pneumothorax** among brachial plexus blocks due to proximity of pleura. US guidance significantly reduces this risk by allowing visualization of pleura and rib
* Always visualize pleura and first rib before and during needle insertion. Measure skin-to-pleura distance
* Lateral-to-medial IP approach is generally preferred for safety (avoids directing needle towards pleura/vessels)
* Phrenic nerve block incidence lower than interscalene (~40-60%), but still significant
* Catheter placement possible, but securing can be difficult

**COMPLICATIONS - SUPRACLAVICULAR** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* ⚠️ **Pneumothorax**: Most significant risk. Higher risk on right side, tall thin patients. Delayed presentation possible
* **Phrenic Nerve Block**: Common
* **Subclavian Artery/Vein Puncture**: Hematoma. US reduces risk
* **Nerve Injury**: Possible
* **Horner Syndrome**: Possible
* **Brachial Plexitis**: Rare inflammatory reaction

### 3.2.5. INFRACLAVICULAR BLOCK

**CLINICAL APPLICATIONS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Provides anesthesia/analgesia for the **entire arm below the shoulder**, including musculocutaneous nerve distribution
* Excellent choice for elbow, forearm, hand surgery
* Good site for **continuous catheter placement** due to stable location deep to muscles

**PATIENT POSITIONING** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Supine, head turned slightly away
* Arm position options:
  * Adducted at side, hand on abdomen (allows easy observation of hand twitches for NS)
  * Abducted 90°, elbow flexed, hand behind head (may bring cords closer to artery, stretch pectoralis muscles)

**LANDMARKS (TRADITIONAL/NS - Coracoid Approach)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Coracoid Process**: Bony prominence palpated inferior to lateral clavicle (in deltopectoral groove)
* Clavicle midpoint
* Puncture site typically 2 cm medial and 2 cm inferior to coracoid process tip

**NERVE STIMULATION (NS) TECHNIQUE (Coracoid Approach)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Needle*: 22G (or 18-20G for catheter), 50-100 mm insulated needle
* *Insertion Point*: 2 cm medial and 2 cm inferior to coracoid tip
* *Direction*: Needle directed **posteriorly**, perpendicular to chest wall, or angled slightly laterally towards axilla
  * ⚠️ Avoid medial angulation to prevent pneumothorax
* *Depth*: Cords are typically 4-7 cm deep. Contact with axillary artery may occur
* *Nerve Localization*:
  * Initial current: 1.0-1.5 mA
  * **Target Response**: Motor twitch of **hand or forearm** (finger/wrist flexion/extension) at **0.3-0.5 mA**. Elbow flexion (musculocutaneous) indicates needle too lateral/superficial
  * May require seeking responses from multiple cords (lateral, medial, posterior) by slightly redirecting needle
* *Injection*:
  * After negative aspiration
  * Inject 25-35 mL LA incrementally. Single injection often sufficient if good distal twitch obtained, but multi-injection technique targeting different cords may improve success

**ULTRASOUND-GUIDED (US) TECHNIQUE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Probe*: Low-frequency curved (2-5 MHz) or linear probe (5-10 MHz)
* *Scanning*: Parasagittal orientation, medial and inferior to coracoid process, below clavicle
* *Sonographic Appearance*:
  * Identify **Pectoralis Major** and **Pectoralis Minor** muscles superficially
  * Deep to muscles, locate **Axillary Artery** (round, anechoic, pulsatile) and **Axillary Vein** (compressible, often medial/inferior to artery)
  * **Brachial Plexus Cords**: Appear as **hyperechoic** structures clustered around the axillary artery (usually 2nd/3rd part)
	* **Lateral Cord**: Typically superior/lateral to artery (~9-11 o'clock position)
	* **Medial Cord**: Typically inferior/medial to artery (~4-6 o'clock position, near vein)
	* **Posterior Cord**: Typically posterior/inferior to artery (~6-8 o'clock position, often hardest to see due to artery shadow)
  * 🔑 HY: Cords are hyperechoic here, unlike hypoechoic roots/trunks more proximally. Location relative to artery is variable ("U-shape" around artery is common goal)
* *Needling*:
  * Typically **In-Plane (IP)** approach from **cephalad-to-caudad**
  * Needle: 22G (or 17-18G Tuohy for catheter), 80-100 mm echogenic needle
  * Insert needle cephalad to probe, advance through pectoralis muscles towards posterior aspect of axillary artery
  * Target needle tip **posterior to the axillary artery** (near posterior cord)
  * NS confirmation highly recommended due to variability and importance of posterior cord block
* *Local Anesthetic Spread*:
  * Inject LA incrementally posterior to artery. Visualize LA spreading circumferentially around the artery, enveloping all three cords ("U-shape" or "donut")
  * May require slight needle repositioning to ensure spread reaches all cords
  * *Volume*: 20-30 mL often sufficient

**CLINICAL PEARLS - INFRACLAVICULAR** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Reliably blocks musculocutaneous nerve
* Lower risk of phrenic nerve block and Horner syndrome compared to interscalene/supraclavicular
* Excellent site for continuous catheters - stable position, comfortable for patient. Aim catheter tip posterior to artery. Intermittent boluses often needed for complete coverage if catheter lies near only one cord
* US guidance makes multi-injection approaches targeting specific cords safer and more feasible if needed

**COMPLICATIONS - INFRACLAVICULAR** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Vascular Puncture**: Axillary artery/vein puncture possible. Hematoma. US reduces risk
* **Pneumothorax**: Possible if needle directed too medially. Lower risk than supraclavicular. US guidance minimizes risk
* **Nerve Injury**: Possible

### 3.2.6. AXILLARY BLOCK

**CLINICAL APPLICATIONS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Provides anesthesia/analgesia for procedures at the **elbow, forearm, and hand**
* Does NOT reliably anesthetize the shoulder or upper arm
* 🔑 **Musculocutaneous nerve block often requires separate injection**

**ANATOMICAL CONSIDERATIONS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Targets terminal nerves (Median, Ulnar, Radial, Musculocutaneous) surrounding the axillary artery in the axilla
* **Musculocutaneous nerve** typically leaves the plexus sheath proximal to the axilla and lies within the **coracobrachialis muscle** or between coracobrachialis and biceps
* Relative positions around artery are variable but commonly:
  * Median: Superior/Anterior (Lateral on screen)
  * Ulnar: Medial/Inferior (Medial on screen)
  * Radial: Posterior/Inferior (Deep on screen)
  * Musculocutaneous: Lateral/Superior, separate from main bundle
* Fascial septations may exist, requiring targeted injection near each nerve

**PATIENT POSITIONING** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Supine, arm abducted ~90°, elbow flexed, hand placed behind or near head. External rotation of shoulder

**LANDMARKS (TRADITIONAL/NS)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Axillary Artery**: Palpate pulsation high in the axilla, medial to coracobrachialis muscle, lateral to pectoralis major tendon insertion
* Nerves surround the artery

**NERVE STIMULATION (NS) TECHNIQUE (Multi-injection)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Needle*: 22-24G, 35-50 mm insulated needle
* *Insertion Point*: Just superior or inferior to the axillary artery pulsation
* *Direction*: Aim towards specific nerves based on desired motor response
* *Nerve Localization*: Requires identifying and stimulating **at least 3 nerves** (Median, Ulnar, Radial) plus a separate block for Musculocutaneous
  * Median: Finger flexion (digits 2-3), thumb opposition, wrist flexion/pronation
  * Ulnar: Finger flexion (digits 4-5), thumb adduction, ulnar deviation
  * Radial: Finger/wrist extension, supination
  * Musculocutaneous: Elbow flexion (biceps twitch). Requires separate injection into coracobrachialis
  * Target threshold: 0.3-0.5 mA for each nerve
* *Injection*:
  * Inject 5-10 mL LA at each identified nerve location (Median, Ulnar, Radial)
  * Inject 5-8 mL LA near musculocutaneous nerve (often found by seeking biceps twitch within coracobrachialis muscle superior/lateral to artery)

**ULTRASOUND-GUIDED (US) TECHNIQUE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Probe*: High-frequency linear probe (10-15 MHz)
* *Scanning*: Transverse orientation high in the axilla, perpendicular to artery course
* *Sonographic Appearance*:
  * **Axillary Artery**: Round, anechoic, pulsatile, centrally located. Axillary vein(s) often nearby, compressible
  * **Terminal Nerves**: Appear as distinct **hyperechoic**, round/oval structures surrounding the artery. Often honeycomb pattern visible
  * **Musculocutaneous Nerve**: Usually seen as a separate hyperechoic structure lateral/superior to the artery, often nestled between coracobrachialis and biceps muscles
  * Identify tendons (latissimus dorsi/teres major posteriorly, pectoralis major anteriorly) and muscles (coracobrachialis/biceps laterally, triceps medially) as surrounding landmarks
* *Needling*:
  * Typically **In-Plane (IP)** approach. Can be from superior or inferior aspect
  * Needle: 22-24G, 50 mm echogenic needle
  * Systematically target each nerve (Median, Ulnar, Radial) with the needle tip
  * Inject small volume (3-7 mL) of LA adjacent to each nerve, visualizing circumferential spread
  * Perform separate injection targeting the Musculocutaneous nerve
* *Alternative "Perivascular" Technique*: Inject larger volume (20-30 mL) into the sheath posterior or superior to the artery, relying on volume spread to reach nerves. May be less reliable than targeted multi-injection

**CLINICAL PEARLS - AXILLARY** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* 🔑 HY: **Musculocutaneous nerve blockade is essential** for complete forearm anesthesia (lateral forearm skin) and elbow flexion surgery. Must be targeted specifically
* US-guided multi-injection targeting each nerve is generally considered the most reliable approach
* **Intercostobrachial nerve (T2)** supplies skin of medial upper arm/axilla. Block with subcutaneous ring of 5-10 mL LA in upper medial arm if needed for tourniquet tolerance or axillary incision
* Safest brachial plexus block approach regarding major complications (pneumothorax, phrenic palsy). Main risk is vascular puncture or nerve injury
* Catheter placement possible but challenging to secure

**COMPLICATIONS - AXILLARY** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Vascular Puncture/Hematoma**: Most common complication
* **Nerve Injury**: Possible, especially with multiple needle passes or intraneural injection
* **Systemic Toxicity (LAST)**: Possible with intravascular injection or large volume absorption

### 3.2.7. TERMINAL UPPER EXTREMITY NERVE BLOCKS (ELBOW/WRIST)

**CLINICAL VALUE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Rescue blocks**: Supplement incomplete proximal brachial plexus block
* **Selective anesthesia/analgesia**: For specific hand/wrist procedures
* **Diagnostic blocks**
* US guidance significantly improves accuracy and safety

**RADIAL NERVE BLOCK (ELBOW)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Supplies*: Extensors of forearm/wrist/fingers, skin of posterior arm/forearm, dorsal-lateral hand
* *Location*: Anterior aspect of elbow, lateral to biceps tendon, between brachialis and brachioradialis muscles, before dividing into superficial/deep branches
* *US Technique*:
  * High-frequency linear probe, transverse view at elbow crease
  * Identify nerve as triangular/oval hyperechoic structure lateral to brachial artery/median nerve, often near radial recurrent artery. Trace proximally from wrist if needed
  * IP approach, inject 3-5 mL LA around nerve
* *NS Technique*: Seek wrist/finger extension at 0.3-0.5 mA

**MEDIAN NERVE BLOCK (ELBOW/FOREARM)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Supplies*: Forearm flexors/pronators, thenar muscles, sensation to palmar aspect of lateral 3.5 fingers and dorsal fingertips
* *Location*: Medial to brachial artery at elbow crease; deeper between FDS/FDP muscles in mid-forearm
* *US Technique*:
  * High-frequency linear probe, transverse view
  * Identify nerve as hyperechoic honeycomb structure adjacent to brachial artery (elbow) or between muscles (forearm)
  * IP approach, inject 3-5 mL LA around nerve
* *NS Technique*: Seek thumb/finger flexion/opposition or wrist flexion at 0.3-0.5 mA
* 💡 **Clinical Pearl**: Avoid wrist block due to risk of increasing carpal tunnel pressure. Forearm block preferred if wrist block needed

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_22.png_8d38a5.png_a2a69e.png_ccba2c.png)

**ULNAR NERVE BLOCK (ELBOW/FOREARM)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Supplies*: Intrinsic hand muscles (except thenar), FCU, medial FDP, sensation to medial 1.5 fingers (palmar/dorsal)
* *Location*: Behind medial epicondyle in cubital tunnel (elbow); adjacent to ulnar artery between FCU/FDP muscles in mid-distal forearm
* *US Technique*:
  * High-frequency linear probe, transverse view
  * Identify nerve as hyperechoic honeycomb structure in cubital tunnel or next to ulnar artery
  * IP approach, inject 3-5 mL LA around nerve
* *NS Technique*: Seek 4th/5th finger flexion or ulnar deviation at 0.3-0.5 mA
* 💡 **Clinical Pearl**: Forearm block may reduce risk of nerve injury/neuritis compared to elbow block in cubital tunnel (confined space). Small volumes (1-4mL) if blocking at elbow

### 3.2.8. INTRAVENOUS REGIONAL ANESTHESIA (BIER BLOCK)

**PROCEDURE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

1. **IV Access**: Place 20-22G IV catheter in operative hand/forearm (dorsum preferred). Secure well. Place separate IV in contralateral arm for meds/fluids
2. **Tourniquet**: Apply **double-cuffed pneumatic tourniquet** to upper arm. Test both cuffs for function
3. **Exsanguination**: Elevate arm for 1-2 minutes. Apply Esmarch bandage tightly from distal to proximal to exsanguinate limb
4. **Proximal Cuff Inflation**: Inflate **PROXIMAL** cuff rapidly to pressure **~100 mmHg above systolic BP** (or minimum 250-300 mmHg for upper extremity)
5. **Confirm Occlusion**: Check for loss of radial pulse. Remove Esmarch bandage
6. **LA Injection**: Inject calculated dose of **PRESERVATIVE-FREE 0.5% LIDOCAINE** slowly over 90-120 seconds through IV catheter
   * *Dose*: Typically 40-50 mL for average adult forearm/hand surgery (approx 3 mg/kg, but volume is key)
   * ⚠️ **DO NOT USE BUPIVACAINE** (high cardiotoxicity risk if tourniquet fails)
   * ⚠️ **DO NOT ADD EPINEPHRINE**
7. **Catheter Removal**: Remove IV catheter after injection and apply pressure
8. **Anesthesia Onset**: Usually within 5-10 minutes
9. **Tourniquet Pain**: Usually occurs after 30-45 minutes under proximal cuff
10. **Distal Cuff Inflation**: Before deflating proximal cuff, inflate **DISTAL** cuff (now over anesthetized skin)
11. **Proximal Cuff Deflation**: Once distal cuff is confirmed inflated, deflate **PROXIMAL** cuff. This relieves tourniquet pain
12. **Tourniquet Deflation (End of Surgery)**:
	* ⚠️ **MINIMUM INFLATION TIME = 20-30 minutes** (allows tissue binding of LA, reduces peak systemic levels upon release)
	* If < 30 min: Deflate-reinflate cycles (e.g., deflate 10s, reinflate 1 min, repeat 2-3x) may reduce systemic bolus
	* If > 30-40 min: Single deflation usually safe
	* Monitor patient closely during and immediately after deflation for signs of LAST

**MECHANISM OF ACTION (MOA)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Primary mechanism: LA diffuses from vasculature into surrounding tissues, blocking peripheral nerves and nerve endings. Some central effect possible

**CLINICAL PEARLS - BIER BLOCK** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Simple, rapid onset, dense surgical block for short procedures (< 45-60 min) on forearm/hand
* Muscle relaxation is good. Rapid recovery after tourniquet deflation
* ⚠️ **Major risk is LAST** if tourniquet fails or is deflated prematurely. Requires meticulous attention to tourniquet function and timing
* Requires dedicated provider monitoring throughout. Resuscitation equipment (**INTRALIPID**) must be immediately available
* Less commonly used for lower extremity due to larger LA doses required and higher tourniquet pressures/pain

**COMPLICATIONS - BIER BLOCK** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* ⚠️ **Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST)**: Seizures, cardiac arrest
* **Tourniquet Pain**: Very common
* **Nerve Injury/Compartment Syndrome**: Possible from prolonged high tourniquet pressure

**Bottom Line:** Brachial plexus blocks offer effective anesthesia and analgesia for upper extremity procedures. The choice of approach (interscalene, supraclavicular, infraclavicular, or axillary) depends on the surgical site, desired block characteristics, and patient factors. Ultrasound guidance, often combined with nerve stimulation, has significantly improved block success and safety by allowing direct visualization of neural and surrounding structures. Each approach has specific advantages and potential complications that must be considered when selecting the optimal block.

### 3.3.2. PENILE BLOCK

**BLOCK FUNDAMENTALS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Purpose*: Anesthesia/analgesia for procedures on the glans and shaft of the penis (e.g., circumcision, hypospadias repair)
* *Innervation*: **Dorsal nerves of the penis** (terminal branches of the pudendal nerve, S2-S4). Run bilaterally beneath Buck's fascia on the dorsal aspect (10 and 2 o'clock positions) alongside dorsal arteries/vein

**TECHNIQUE: DORSAL PENILE NERVE BLOCK (Landmark)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Patient Position*: Supine
* *Landmarks*: Base of the penis, pubic symphysis
* *Needle*: 25-27G, short needle (e.g., 0.5-1 inch)
* *Approach*:
  1. Retract penis gently downward
  2. Insert needle just below the pubic symphysis at the 10 o'clock position, perpendicular to skin. Advance until slight resistance of Buck's fascia felt (or gentle contact with pubic bone, then withdraw slightly). Avoid deep injection into corpora
  3. Aspirate carefully (dorsal vessels nearby)
  4. Inject LA slowly: `Dose: 0.1 mL/kg per side` (infants/children) or 2-5 mL per side (adults) of plain LA (e.g., 0.25-0.5% **BUPIVACAINE**)
  5. Repeat on contralateral side (2 o'clock position)
* *Alternative: Circumferential Ring Block*: Subcutaneous infiltration around the entire base of the penis. May provide more complete block but uses larger volume

**ULTRASOUND-GUIDED APPROACH** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Probe*: High-frequency linear probe
* *Scanning*: Transverse view at dorsal base of penis
* *Sonoanatomy*: Identify corpora cavernosa, Buck's fascia (hyperechoic line), dorsal arteries (Doppler helpful), and hypoechoic dorsal nerves lateral to arteries beneath fascia
* *Approach*: In-plane or OOP. Inject LA into the space beneath Buck's fascia adjacent to the nerves bilaterally
* *Benefits*: May improve efficacy, reduce volume needed, potentially decrease vascular puncture risk

⚠️ **CRITICAL WARNING**: **EPINEPHRINE-containing solutions are ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED** due to risk of penile ischemia/necrosis (end-arterial circulation). Use PLAIN local anesthetic only [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

### 3.3.3. LOWER EXTREMITY BLOCKS OVERVIEW

**INDICATIONS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Primary anesthesia or postoperative analgesia for hip, thigh, knee, lower leg, ankle, and foot surgery
* Alternative when neuraxial anesthesia is contraindicated or undesirable
* Selective anesthesia for one limb
* Requires blocking components of both **Lumbar Plexus** (anterior/medial thigh) and **Sacral Plexus** (posterior thigh, entire leg/foot below knee, except medial strip)
* Common combinations:
  * Femoral + Sciatic = Entire leg (except medial strip below knee)
  * Femoral + Sciatic + Obturator = Entire leg (more complete medial thigh)
  * Adductor Canal + Popliteal Sciatic = Knee and below (motor sparing focus)
  * Ankle Block = Foot surgery

**ANATOMICAL CONSIDERATIONS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Nerves are often larger and deeper than upper extremity nerves
* Fascial planes are important for spread (e.g., fascia iliaca, psoas compartment)
* US guidance is standard of care for most blocks

### 3.3.4. LUMBAR PLEXUS BLOCK (PSOAS COMPARTMENT BLOCK)

**BLOCK FUNDAMENTALS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Target*: Lumbar plexus roots (L1-L4) located within the substance of the psoas major muscle, posterior to the muscle belly but anterior to the transverse processes
* *Anatomical Coverage*: Blocks Femoral, Lateral Femoral Cutaneous, and Obturator nerves (variable). Genitofemoral nerve also blocked. Provides anesthesia/analgesia to anterior, lateral, and medial thigh, hip joint, knee joint, and medial leg/foot via saphenous nerve
* *Clinical Applications*: Hip fracture surgery, total hip arthroplasty (often combined with sciatic block), anterior thigh surgery

**NERVE STIMULATION TECHNIQUE (Posterior Approach - Capdevila)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Patient Position*: Lateral decubitus, operative side up. Hip slightly flexed, knee slightly flexed
* *Landmarks*:
  1. Iliac crest
  2. Spinous processes (Identify L4 at level of iliac crests)
  3. Posterior Superior Iliac Spine (PSIS)
  4. Draw line connecting iliac crests (Intercristal line ~L4)
  5. Draw line parallel to spine through PSIS
  6. Draw line perpendicular to spine from L4 spinous process laterally
  7. *Insertion Point ("X")*: Junction of lateral 1/3 and medial 2/3 of the distance between L4 spinous process and the PSIS line, on the L4 transverse line. (Alternative: 3-5 cm lateral to L4 spinous process)

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_25.png_1fd003.png_313331.png_7d0d52.png)

* *Needle*: 18-22G, 100-150 mm insulated needle
* *Approach*:
  1. Insert needle perpendicular to skin at "X"
  2. Advance slowly until contact with **L4 transverse process** (typically 4-8 cm deep). Note depth
  3. Withdraw needle to skin, redirect slightly caudad (~10-15°) to pass *below* the transverse process
  4. Advance needle an additional **1.5-2 cm** deeper than the depth of the transverse process. Needle tip should now be in psoas compartment
* *Nerve Localization*:
  * Initial current: 1.5 mA
  * **Target Response**: **Quadriceps contraction (patellar twitch)** indicating femoral nerve stimulation at **0.3-0.5 mA**. Obturator response (adductor twitch) also acceptable but less common. LFCN response (tensor fascia lata twitch) indicates needle too lateral
* *Injection*:
  * After negative aspiration (low risk of vascular/CSF puncture with posterior approach)
  * Inject 20-30 mL LA incrementally. Resistance should be low

**ULTRASOUND-GUIDED TECHNIQUE (Posterior Approach - "Shamrock Sign")** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Probe*: Low-frequency curved probe (2-5 MHz)
* *Scanning*: Transverse orientation at L3-L4 level, ~4-5 cm lateral to midline
* *Sonoanatomy*: Identify hypoechoic transverse process with posterior shadow. Medial is vertebral body/lamina. Lateral is quadratus lumborum/erector spinae muscles. **Psoas muscle** is large hypoechoic structure anterior to TP. **Lumbar plexus** appears as **hyperechoic** structures within the posterior aspect of the psoas muscle, near the junction with the transverse process ("shamrock" appearance with TP as stem, psoas/QL/ESM as leaves). Kidney may be visible anteriorly/laterally (especially on left, higher levels)
* *Needling*:
  * Typically **In-Plane (IP)** approach from posterior-to-anterior
  * Advance needle through erector spinae/quadratus lumborum towards the posterior aspect of the psoas muscle where hyperechoic plexus elements are seen
  * Target injection into the fascial plane within the psoas containing the plexus
  * NS confirmation often used due to depth and sometimes indistinct US image
* *Local Anesthetic Spread*: Visualize LA spreading within the posterior psoas compartment around the hyperechoic plexus elements
* *Volume*: 20-30 mL

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_26.png_4ded25.png_3f3836.png_ad0c76.png)

**CLINICAL PEARLS - LUMBAR PLEXUS BLOCK** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Provides comprehensive anterior/medial thigh coverage. Obturator block inconsistent
* Catheter placement possible for prolonged analgesia (e.g., hip fracture)
* ⚠️ **Contraindicated in anticoagulated patients** due to risk of large retroperitoneal hematoma (psoas compartment is vascular, non-compressible space)
* *Potential Complications*:
  * **Retroperitoneal Hematoma**: Most significant risk
  * **Intravascular Injection**: Lumbar vessels nearby
  * **Kidney Puncture**: Especially with high approaches (L1-L2)
  * **Intraneural Injection**
  * **Epidural/Subarachnoid Spread**: Possible if needle directed too medially towards intervertebral foramen. Results in bilateral block, potential high spinal. More common with anterior approaches
  * **Quadriceps Weakness**: Significant motor block. Fall risk

### 3.3.5. TERMINAL NERVES OF LUMBAR PLEXUS BLOCKS

### 3.3.6. FEMORAL NERVE BLOCK

**BLOCK FUNDAMENTALS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Target*: Femoral nerve (L2-L4) located in the femoral triangle, lateral to the femoral artery, below the inguinal ligament
* *Anatomical Coverage*: Anterior thigh muscles (quadriceps), skin of anterior thigh, knee joint (partially), medial leg/foot skin (via saphenous nerve)
* *Clinical Applications*: Knee surgery (arthroscopy, TKA - often combined with sciatic/adductor canal), femur fracture, anterior thigh procedures. Analgesia for hip fracture

**NERVE STIMULATION TECHNIQUE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Patient Position*: Supine, leg neutral or slightly externally rotated
* *Landmarks*:
  * Inguinal ligament
  * Femoral artery pulsation (palpate below midpoint of inguinal ligament)
  * *Insertion Point*: ~1-1.5 cm lateral to the femoral artery pulse, just below the inguinal ligament (~1-2 cm)
* *Needle*: 22G, 50 mm insulated needle
* *Approach*: Insert needle perpendicular to skin or angled slightly cephalad (30°)
* *Nerve Localization*:
  * Initial current: 1.0 mA
  * **Target Response**: **Quadriceps contraction ("patellar twitch" or "dancing patella")** at **0.3-0.5 mA**. Sartorius twitch indicates needle too superficial/lateral. Adductor twitch indicates needle too medial (near obturator/femoral branches to pectineus)
* *Injection*:
  * After negative aspiration (femoral artery/vein nearby!)
  * Inject 15-20 mL LA incrementally

**ULTRASOUND-GUIDED TECHNIQUE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Probe*: High-frequency linear probe (6-13 MHz)
* *Scanning*: Transverse orientation just below inguinal ligament, centered over femoral artery
* *Sonoanatomy ("NAVEL" from Lateral to Medial)*:
  * **N**erve: **Femoral Nerve** appears as a **hyperechoic**, triangular or oval structure, lateral to the artery. Often has fascicular/honeycomb appearance. Lies superficial to iliopsoas muscle, deep to fascia lata and fascia iliaca
  * **A**rtery: **Femoral Artery** is round, pulsatile, non-compressible
  * **V**ein: **Femoral Vein** is medial to artery, compressible, often larger
  * **E**mpty Space / **L**ymphatics
* *Needling*:
  * Typically **In-Plane (IP)** approach from **lateral-to-medial**
  * Needle: 22G, 50-80 mm echogenic needle
  * Insert needle lateral to nerve, advance medially in the plane between fascia iliaca and iliopsoas muscle
  * Aim needle tip just lateral or deep to the nerve
  * NS confirmation can be used
* *Local Anesthetic Spread*:
  * Inject LA incrementally, visualizing spread around the nerve, often lifting it off the iliopsoas muscle. Circumferential spread ideal
  * *Volume*: 10-20 mL often sufficient

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_27.png_1630d0.png_4d2d5c.png_83c2ce.png)

**FASCIA ILIACA COMPARTMENT BLOCK (FICB)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Concept*: Indirect femoral nerve block (also potentially blocks LFCN, obturator partially) by injecting large volume LA into the potential space *deep to the fascia iliaca*, allowing proximal spread towards lumbar plexus roots within the psoas compartment
* *Landmark Technique*:
  * Identify ASIS and pubic tubercle. Divide inguinal ligament into thirds
  * *Insertion Point*: 1 cm caudal to the junction of the lateral 1/3 and medial 2/3 of the inguinal ligament
  * Insert blunt/short-bevel needle perpendicular to skin. Feel for **two distinct "pops"**: 1st = fascia lata, 2nd = fascia iliaca
  * Inject large volume (30-40 mL) LA after negative aspiration. Low resistance expected
* *Ultrasound-Guided Technique*:
  * Linear probe below inguinal ligament, identify fascia lata (superficial) and fascia iliaca (deeper bright line superficial to iliopsoas muscle). Femoral nerve/artery may be seen medially
  * Insert needle IP lateral to nerve, pierce fascia lata then fascia iliaca
  * Inject large volume (30-40 mL) LA, visualizing spread within the plane deep to fascia iliaca, lifting it off the iliopsoas. LA should spread medially and laterally

**CLINICAL PEARLS - FEMORAL / FASCIA ILIACA** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Femoral block causes significant **quadriceps weakness** → High fall risk. Patient education crucial
* FICB may provide broader coverage (LFCN) than targeted femoral block, potentially better for hip fracture analgesia. Reliability of obturator block with FICB is low
* US-guided femoral block is precise. Lateral-to-medial approach keeps needle away from vessels initially
* Consider adductor canal block as motor-sparing alternative for knee analgesia
* Catheter placement common for prolonged analgesia (TKA, femur fracture). Place tip adjacent to nerve under US guidance

### 3.3.7. LATERAL FEMORAL CUTANEOUS NERVE (LFCN) BLOCK

**BLOCK FUNDAMENTALS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Target*: Lateral Femoral Cutaneous Nerve (L2-L3), purely sensory
* *Anatomical Coverage*: Skin of the anterolateral thigh
* *Clinical Applications*: Meralgia paresthetica (diagnosis/treatment), analgesia for thigh skin graft harvest. Supplement to femoral block for tourniquet pain

**TECHNIQUE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Patient Position*: Supine
* *Landmarks*: Anterior Superior Iliac Spine (ASIS). Nerve typically crosses under inguinal ligament ~1-2 cm medial to ASIS, between layers of fascia lata or deep to it. Course is highly variable
* *Nerve Stimulation Technique*:
  * Insert 22-25G, 35-50 mm needle ~1-2 cm medial and 1-2 cm inferior to ASIS
  * Direct slightly laterally. Seek paresthesia over lateral thigh at low current (0.5 mA). Often difficult to elicit
  * Inject 5-10 mL LA as a fan-shaped infiltration in the expected area if no response
* *Ultrasound-Guided Technique*:
  * High-frequency linear probe, transverse view just medial/inferior to ASIS
  * Identify ASIS (bright bone). Scan medially to find Sartorius muscle origin and Tensor Fascia Lata (TFL) muscle laterally
  * **LFCN** appears as small (often multiple branches), **hyperechoic** structure(s) in the fascial plane **between Sartorius and TFL muscles**, superficial to iliacus muscle. Variable depth
  * IP approach, inject 3-5 mL LA around the identified nerve(s)

### 3.3.8. OBTURATOR NERVE BLOCK

**BLOCK FUNDAMENTALS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Target*: Obturator nerve (L2-L4) as it exits the obturator foramen and divides into anterior/posterior branches in the medial thigh
* *Anatomical Coverage*: Adductor muscles of medial thigh, skin of distal medial thigh, sensory innervation to hip and knee joints
* *Clinical Applications*:
  * Supplement to femoral/sciatic block for TKA/THA (adductor spasm relief, joint analgesia)
  * Prevent adductor muscle contraction (obturator reflex) during transurethral resection of bladder tumor (TURBT) near lateral bladder wall
  * Diagnosis/treatment of groin pain, adductor spasticity

**NERVE STIMULATION TECHNIQUE (Pubic Tubercle Approach)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Patient Position*: Supine, leg slightly abducted and externally rotated
* *Landmarks*: Pubic tubercle
* *Needle*: 22G, 80-100 mm insulated needle
* *Approach*:
  1. Insert needle 1.5 cm lateral and 1.5 cm inferior to pubic tubercle
  2. Advance perpendicular to skin until contact with **inferior pubic ramus** (bone). Note depth
  3. Withdraw needle slightly, redirect slightly laterally and cephalad, advance needle **2-4 cm past** the bone into the obturator canal/foramen region
* *Nerve Localization*:
  * Initial current: 1.5 mA
  * **Target Response**: **Adductor muscle contraction** (thigh adduction) at **0.3-0.5 mA**
* *Injection*:
  * After negative aspiration (obturator artery/vein nearby!)
  * Inject 10-15 mL LA incrementally

**ULTRASOUND-GUIDED TECHNIQUE (Interfascial Approach)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Probe*: High-frequency linear probe (or curved for deeper view)
* *Scanning*: Transverse orientation high on the medial thigh, inferior to inguinal crease. Identify femoral artery/vein laterally. Scan medially
* *Sonoanatomy*: Identify sequential muscle layers from superficial to deep: **Pectineus**, **Adductor Longus**, **Adductor Brevis**, **Adductor Magnus**. Obturator vessels often visible with Doppler between these layers
  * **Anterior Branch of Obturator Nerve**: Lies in the fascial plane **between Adductor Longus (superficial) and Adductor Brevis (deep)**. Appears hyperechoic
  * **Posterior Branch of Obturator Nerve**: Lies in the fascial plane **between Adductor Brevis (superficial) and Adductor Magnus (deep)**
* *Needling*:
  * IP approach, typically from anterior/medial
  * Target injection into the fascial plane containing the anterior branch (between AL/AB)
  * Inject 5-10 mL LA. This often spreads sufficiently to block both branches
  * Alternatively, perform separate injection into the deeper plane (between AB/AM) for posterior branch if needed (another 5-10 mL)
  * Use Doppler to avoid vessels

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_28.png_c88ecc.png_396bc3.png_5f0350.png)

**CLINICAL PEARLS - OBTURATOR** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* US-guided interfascial approach is generally preferred now - safer and potentially more reliable than blind NS technique
* Blocking only the anterior branch may be sufficient for TURBT adductor reflex prevention. Both branches needed for complete adductor block/joint analgesia
* Contribution to knee joint sensation is variable

### 3.3.9. SAPHENOUS NERVE BLOCK (TERMINAL BRANCH OF FEMORAL NERVE)

**BLOCK FUNDAMENTALS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Target*: Saphenous nerve (L3-L4), purely sensory
* *Anatomical Coverage*: Skin on medial aspect of leg, ankle, and foot arch. Contributes sensory fibers to medial knee joint
* *Clinical Applications*: Supplement to sciatic block for complete below-knee anesthesia/analgesia (e.g., ankle fracture, foot surgery). Medial knee pain. Great saphenous vein stripping/ligation

**TECHNIQUES** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

**1. Transsartorial Approach (Below Knee - Landmark/NS)**

* *Patient Position*: Supine, knee slightly flexed
* *Landmarks*: Medial femoral condyle, tibial tuberosity. Nerve runs medial to sartorius tendon insertion
* *Needle*: 22-25G, 35-50 mm needle
* *Approach*: Insert needle just posterior to sartorius tendon below medial condyle. Seek paresthesia down medial leg/ankle
* *Injection*: 5-10 mL LA as fan-shaped infiltration. Often unreliable

**2. Adductor Canal Block (Mid-Thigh - US Guided)** - *Preferred method* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Concept*: Block saphenous nerve (and potentially nerve to vastus medialis, articular branches) within the adductor canal in the mid-thigh. More proximal, reliable, and provides some knee analgesia. Motor sparing (does not block main femoral nerve branches to quadriceps)
* *Patient Position*: Supine, leg slightly abducted and externally rotated
* *Probe*: High-frequency linear probe
* *Scanning*: Transverse orientation on anteromedial aspect of mid-thigh (~midpoint between ASIS and patella)
* *Sonoanatomy*: Identify **Sartorius muscle** (large, superficial, "tailor's muscle"). Deep to sartorius, identify **Femoral Artery** (pulsatile, use Doppler). **Saphenous nerve** is typically located **anterolateral** to the artery, appearing as a small, **hyperechoic** structure, deep to sartorius, superficial to vastus medialis/adductor longus fascia which forms the floor/roof of the adductor canal. Nerve to vastus medialis may also be seen nearby
* *Needling*:
  * Typically **In-Plane (IP)** approach from anterolateral or anteromedial
  * Advance needle through sartorius muscle towards the space adjacent to the femoral artery where the saphenous nerve lies
  * Target injection into the potential space around the nerve/artery, deep to sartorius
* *Local Anesthetic Spread*: Visualize LA spreading around the artery and nerve within the canal
* *Volume*: 10-20 mL LA

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_29.png_b6664c.png_74abe1.png_623754.png)

**3. Ankle Block Approach** - *See Ankle Block section* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

**CLINICAL PEARLS - SAPHENOUS / ADDUCTOR CANAL** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Adductor canal block is primarily a **sensory block**. Preserves quadriceps strength, making it ideal for TKA analgesia protocols focused on early mobilization
* May provide less complete knee analgesia than femoral block but with significantly lower fall risk
* Often combined with popliteal sciatic block or IPACK block for comprehensive TKA analgesia

### 3.3.10. SCIATIC NERVE BLOCK

**BLOCK FUNDAMENTALS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Target*: Sciatic nerve (L4-S3), the largest nerve in the body. Composed of Tibial and Common Peroneal divisions within a common epineural sheath
* *Anatomical Coverage*: Provides anesthesia/analgesia for the entire lower leg and foot **EXCEPT** the medial strip of skin supplied by the saphenous nerve (femoral branch). Also supplies posterior thigh muscles (hamstrings)
* *Clinical Applications*: Surgery on the knee (posterior aspect), lower leg, ankle, and foot. Often combined with femoral or saphenous block for complete limb coverage

**APPROACHES** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Multiple approaches target the nerve at different points along its course from the pelvis down to the popliteal fossa. Choice depends on patient position, surgical site, and provider preference
* US guidance is standard

### 3.3.11. POSTERIOR SCIATIC NERVE BLOCK: CLASSICAL GLUTEAL (LABAT) APPROACH

* *Target*: Sciatic nerve high in the gluteal region as it emerges below piriformis muscle
* *Patient Position*: Lateral decubitus (Sims position), operative side up, hip flexed ~45°, knee flexed ~90°
* *Landmarks*:
  1. Greater Trochanter (GT)
  2. Posterior Superior Iliac Spine (PSIS)
  3. Draw line connecting PSIS and GT
  4. *Insertion Point*: Midpoint of this line, then drop perpendicular line ~3-5 cm inferiorly. (Alternatively: line from GT to sacral hiatus, insertion at midpoint). Landmarks can be unreliable, especially in obese patients

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_30.png_1f329f.png_2e5745.png_c4a7ff.png)

* *Nerve Stimulation Technique* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Needle: 22G, 100-120 mm insulated needle
  * Insert perpendicular to skin. Nerve is deep (5-10 cm)
  * Target Response: Any motor response in the foot or ankle (plantarflexion/inversion = Tibial; dorsiflexion/eversion = Common Peroneal) at 0.3-0.5 mA. Hamstring twitch indicates needle too superficial or stimulation of posterior femoral cutaneous nerve
  * Inject 15-25 mL LA
* *Ultrasound-Guided Technique* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Probe: Low-frequency curved probe (2-5 MHz)
  * Scanning: Transverse orientation between GT and Ischial Tuberosity (IT). Identify gluteus maximus muscle superficially. Sciatic nerve is deep to gluteus maximus, appearing as large, flat/oval, **hyperechoic** structure. Piriformis muscle may be seen deep/superior to nerve. Inferior gluteal artery often nearby
  * Needling: IP approach, typically lateral-to-medial. Advance through gluteus maximus towards nerve. Inject LA around nerve

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_31.png_8643e7.png_4eae56.png_adc453.png)

* *Clinical Pearls*: High block, covers entire sciatic distribution including hamstrings. Deep needle insertion required. US guidance highly recommended for safety and accuracy

### 3.3.12. POSTERIOR SCIATIC NERVE BLOCK: SUBGLUTEAL APPROACH

* *Target*: Sciatic nerve lower in the gluteal region, below the gluteus maximus fold, between GT and IT. Nerve is usually more superficial here than classical approach
* *Patient Position*: Lateral decubitus (Sims) or Prone
* *Landmarks*: Greater Trochanter (GT), Ischial Tuberosity (IT). Subgluteal crease
* *Nerve Stimulation Technique* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Draw line connecting GT and IT
  * *Insertion Point*: Midpoint of this line
  * Needle: 22G, 80-120 mm insulated needle
  * Insert perpendicular. Nerve depth ~4-8 cm
  * Target Response: Foot/ankle motor response at 0.3-0.5 mA
  * Inject 15-25 mL LA
* *Ultrasound-Guided Technique* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Probe: Low-frequency curved or high-frequency linear (if thin patient)
  * Scanning: Transverse orientation, centered on midpoint line between GT and IT, just below gluteal crease
  * Sonoanatomy: Sciatic nerve appears as large, oval/round, **hyperechoic** structure, superficial to quadratus femoris muscle, deep to gluteus maximus tendon/fascia
  * Needling: IP approach, lateral-to-medial or medial-to-lateral. Inject LA circumferentially around nerve
* *Clinical Pearls*: Often easier to perform than classical approach, nerve more superficial. Good choice for US guidance. Still provides hamstring block

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_32.png_fad0a7.png_6e7c2c.png_79fe0a.png)

### 3.3.13. POSTERIOR POPLITEAL SCIATIC BLOCK

* *Target*: Sciatic nerve just proximal to its bifurcation into Tibial and Common Peroneal nerves, within the popliteal fossa
* *Anatomical Coverage*: Entire lower leg and foot (except saphenous distribution). **Spares hamstring muscles**
* *Clinical Applications*: Foot and ankle surgery, lower leg surgery below tibial tubercle. Common component of TKA analgesia (combined with adductor canal/femoral)
* *Patient Position*: Prone or Lateral decubitus (operative side up, dependent leg flexed, operative leg extended)
* *Landmarks*: Popliteal fossa crease. Biceps femoris tendon (lateral), Semitendinosus/Semimembranosus tendons (medial)
* *Nerve Stimulation Technique* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Insertion Point*: ~5-7 cm proximal to popliteal crease, at midpoint between biceps and semitendinosus tendons (or slightly lateral to midline)
  * Needle: 22G, 50-100 mm insulated needle
  * Insert perpendicular or angled slightly cephalad (30-45°)
  * Target Response: Foot/ankle motor response (either Tibial or Common Peroneal) at 0.3-0.5 mA
  * Inject 20-30 mL LA
* *Ultrasound-Guided Technique* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Probe: High-frequency linear probe (6-13 MHz)
  * Scanning: Transverse orientation starting at popliteal crease. Identify popliteal artery/vein (vein often superficial/lateral to artery). Scan proximally. Tibial nerve (TN) is typically lateral/superficial to vessels at crease. Common Peroneal nerve (CPN) is more lateral. As probe moves proximally, TN and CPN converge to form the **Sciatic Nerve**, which moves away (more superficial/lateral) from the vessels
  * *Sonoanatomy (Proximal to bifurcation)*: Sciatic nerve appears as large, oval/round, **hyperechoic** structure, often with visible internal fascicles. Lies lateral to popliteal vessels, between hamstring muscles, superficial to femur
  * *Needling*:
	* Typically **In-Plane (IP)** approach from **lateral-to-medial**
	* Insert needle lateral to nerve, advance medially, aiming tip adjacent to nerve within the common epineural sheath (paraneural injection)
	* NS confirmation often used to confirm which division (Tibial vs Peroneal) is closer, or if intraneural
* *Local Anesthetic Spread*: Inject LA incrementally, visualizing circumferential spread ("donut sign") around the sciatic nerve within its sheath
* *Volume*: 15-25 mL often sufficient

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_33.png_5169cb.png_ca8895.png_e335e4.png)

* *Clinical Pearls* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Very common and effective block for foot/ankle surgery
  * US guidance significantly improves success and safety (avoids vessels)
  * Lateral IP approach keeps needle away from vessels
  * Blocking slightly proximal to bifurcation ensures coverage of both divisions. Injection distal to bifurcation (targeting TN and CPN separately) may lead to faster onset but risk missing one division
  * Excellent site for continuous catheter placement

### 3.3.14. ANTERIOR SCIATIC NERVE BLOCK

* *Target*: Sciatic nerve deep in the anterior thigh, medial to femur, posterior to adductor magnus
* *Clinical Applications*: For patients who cannot be positioned prone or lateral (e.g., trauma, hip fracture). Provides similar coverage to posterior approaches (below knee, posterior thigh)
* *Patient Position*: Supine
* *Landmarks (Traditional)*: Complex, involves lines drawn from ASIS, pubic tubercle, greater trochanter. Less commonly used now
* *Nerve Stimulation Technique* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Needle: 22G, 100-150 mm insulated needle
  * Insertion point high on anterior thigh, medial to sartorius/femoral vessels
  * Advance posteriorly, slightly laterally, until contact with femur. Withdraw, redirect medially/posteriorly past femur. Nerve is deep (8-12 cm)
  * Target Response: Foot/ankle motor response at 0.3-0.5 mA
  * Inject 20-30 mL LA
* *Ultrasound-Guided Technique* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Probe: Low-frequency curved probe (2-5 MHz)
  * Scanning: Transverse orientation on anterior thigh, distal to lesser trochanter level. Identify femur laterally. Scan medially. Identify adductor muscles
  * Sonoanatomy: Sciatic nerve appears as large, **hyperechoic**, oval structure deep in thigh, posterior to adductor magnus muscle, medial to femur shaft
  * Needling: IP approach, typically anterior-to-posterior, medial to femur. Requires long needle and careful maneuvering deep in thigh
  * Inject LA around nerve
* *Clinical Pearls*: Technically challenging block due to depth and surrounding structures. US guidance essential if performed. Combined US/NS recommended. Consider posterior approach if possible

### 3.3.15. IPACK (INFILTRATION BETWEEN POPLITEAL ARTERY AND CAPSULE OF KNEE) BLOCK

**BLOCK FUNDAMENTALS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Concept*: Not a specific nerve block, but an interspace infiltration targeting articular sensory branches supplying the **posterior knee capsule**. These branches arise from tibial, common peroneal, and posterior division of obturator nerves
* *Purpose*: Provide analgesia for posterior knee pain after TKA, complementing anterior knee blocks (femoral, adductor canal) which cover anterior capsule
* *Benefit*: Primarily sensory block, **motor sparing** (does not reliably block main tibial/peroneal nerves). Avoids foot drop associated with popliteal sciatic block

**ULTRASOUND-GUIDED TECHNIQUE** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Patient Position*: Supine with knee slightly flexed (pillow underneath) OR Prone/Lateral
* *Probe*: High-frequency linear or low-frequency curved probe (depending on patient habitus)
* *Scanning*: Transverse orientation just proximal to knee joint line, either medially or laterally, aiming towards popliteal fossa. Identify femur condyles, popliteal artery/vein (use Doppler)
* *Target Space*: The potential space **between the popliteal artery (anteriorly) and the posterior aspect of the distal femur / knee capsule (posteriorly)**
* *Needling*:
  * IP approach, typically from medial or lateral side
  * Advance needle towards the target space, posterior to the popliteal artery
  * ⚠️ **CRITICAL**: Visualize needle tip clearly and keep it posterior to the artery at all times. Avoid vascular puncture. Sciatic/tibial nerve is usually posterior/lateral to this space - avoid injecting near it
* *Injection*:
  * Inject 10-20 mL LA (e.g., 0.25% **ROPIVACAINE** or **BUPIVACAINE**) into this space. Visualize LA spreading along the posterior aspect of the femur/capsule, separating it from the artery. Inject slowly while withdrawing needle slightly

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_35.png_f1c7ba.png_b485f9.png_b3f563.png)

**CLINICAL PEARLS - IPACK** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Valuable adjunct for multimodal TKA analgesia, specifically targeting posterior knee pain
* Combine with Adductor Canal Block for comprehensive motor-sparing knee analgesia
* Technique requires careful US visualization to avoid vascular puncture and sciatic/tibial nerve block

### 3.3.16. ANKLE BLOCK

**BLOCK FUNDAMENTALS** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Target*: Five nerves supplying sensation to the foot, blocked at the level of the malleoli
* *Anatomical Coverage*: Entire foot distal to the malleoli
* *Clinical Applications*: Foot surgery (bunionectomy, hammertoe correction, forefoot trauma), diabetic foot debridement. Analgesia for ankle fractures
* *Nerves Blocked*:
  * **Deep Nerves (require precise localization)**:
	1. **Posterior Tibial Nerve** (branch of Tibial)
	2. **Deep Peroneal Nerve** (branch of Common Peroneal)
  * **Superficial Nerves (often blocked via subcutaneous infiltration)**:
	3. **Saphenous Nerve** (branch of Femoral)
	4. **Superficial Peroneal Nerve** (branch of Common Peroneal)
	5. **Sural Nerve** (formed from Tibial & Common Peroneal branches)

**TECHNIQUES** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Patient Position*: Supine, foot slightly elevated. May need slight internal/external rotation for access
* *Needle*: 25-27G, 1-1.5 inch needle
* *LA*: Typically 3-5 mL per nerve (Total 15-25 mL) of intermediate/long-acting LA (e.g., 1-1.5% **LIDOCAINE**/**MEPIVACAINE**, or 0.25-0.5% **BUPIVACAINE**/**ROPIVACAINE**)
* ⚠️ **AVOID EPINEPHRINE** due to risk of distal ischemia
* US guidance improves localization of deep nerves and associated vessels

**1. Posterior Tibial Nerve Block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Supplies*: Sole of the foot (heel, plantar surface, toes). Motor to intrinsic foot muscles
* *Landmarks*: Medial malleolus, posterior tibial artery pulsation (palpable behind medial malleolus). Nerve lies just posterior to artery within tarsal tunnel
* *US Technique*:
  * High-frequency linear probe, transverse view posterior to medial malleolus
  * Identify posterior tibial artery (use Doppler). Nerve is **hyperechoic honeycomb** structure immediately posterior/lateral to artery. Flexor retinaculum superficial
  * IP or OOP approach. Inject 3-5 mL LA adjacent to nerve
* *Landmark/NS Technique*: Insert needle just posterior to artery pulsation. Seek paresthesia to sole or toe flexion (intrinsic muscles). Inject 5 mL LA

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_36.png_140588.png_2b6de2.png_afd7e2.png)

**2. Deep Peroneal Nerve Block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Supplies*: First web space skin (between big toe and 2nd toe). Motor to toe extensors (EDB)
* *Landmarks*: Anterior ankle crease level, between tendons of Extensor Hallucis Longus (EHL - medially) and Extensor Digitorum Longus (EDL - laterally). Anterior tibial artery pulsation often palpable here. Nerve lies just lateral to artery
* *US Technique*:
  * High-frequency linear probe, transverse view at anterior ankle crease
  * Identify anterior tibial artery (use Doppler) between EHL/Tibialis Anterior tendons medially and EDL laterally. Nerve is small, **hyperechoic** structure lateral to artery, deep to fascia
  * OOP approach often easiest due to surrounding tendons. Inject 3-5 mL LA adjacent to nerve, lateral to artery
* *Landmark/NS Technique*: Insert needle just lateral to anterior tibial artery pulsation (or lateral to EHL tendon if artery not palpable). Advance to contact tibia, withdraw slightly. Seek paresthesia to 1st web space or EDB contraction (subtle). Inject 3-5 mL LA

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_37.png_b87763.png_217d7b.png_e8b263.png)

**3. Saphenous Nerve Block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Supplies*: Skin over medial malleolus and medial arch of foot
* *Landmarks*: Anterior to medial malleolus, near great saphenous vein (often visible)
* *Technique*: Subcutaneous infiltration of 3-5 mL LA in a line anterior to the medial malleolus, from tibial crest towards Achilles tendon. US can identify vein/nerve for targeted injection

**4. Superficial Peroneal Nerve Block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Supplies*: Skin over dorsum of foot (except 1st web space) and toes
* *Landmarks*: Nerve becomes subcutaneous ~5-10 cm proximal to lateral malleolus, anterior to fibula
* *Technique*: Subcutaneous infiltration of 5-7 mL LA in a line ("bracelet") across the anterior ankle from the tibial crest towards the lateral malleolus. US can identify nerve branches superficial to fascia for targeted injection

**5. Sural Nerve Block** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Supplies*: Skin over lateral malleolus, lateral aspect of foot and 5th toe
* *Landmarks*: Between lateral malleolus and Achilles tendon. Near lesser saphenous vein
* *Technique*: Subcutaneous infiltration of 3-5 mL LA in the groove between the lateral malleolus and Achilles tendon. US can identify nerve near lesser saphenous vein for targeted injection

**CLINICAL PEARLS - ANKLE BLOCK** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Reliable anesthesia for foot surgery when all 5 nerves blocked
* Onset typically 15-30 minutes
* US guidance particularly helpful for posterior tibial and deep peroneal nerves
* Complications rare: hematoma, transient paresthesia. Avoid intraneural/intratendinous injection

### 3.3.17. CONTINUOUS CATHETER TECHNIQUE

**CHALLENGES** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* Catheter dislodgment/migration
* Leakage at insertion site
* Kinking or occlusion
* Infection risk with prolonged use
* Requires dedicated pump and management (e.g., Acute Pain Service)

**CATHETER-OVER-NEEDLE APPROACH** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Concept*: Similar to placing an IV catheter. Needle is inside catheter during placement, then removed, leaving catheter in place
* *Potential Benefits*: May reduce leakage, potentially more stable fixation at skin site
* *Availability*: Specialized kits available (e.g., Contiplex C). Gaining popularity with US guidance

**PERIPHERAL NERVE STIMULATION (PNS) FOR ACUTE PAIN** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Concept*: Emerging technique using implanted electrical leads near peripheral nerves for **prolonged (days to weeks)** analgesia, primarily for acute post-op or trauma pain. Off-label use of stimulating catheters reported
* *Status*: Requires specialized equipment and expertise. Safety, efficacy, cost-effectiveness still under investigation compared to traditional CPNB or single-shot blocks

**Bottom Line:** Lower extremity blocks provide effective anesthesia and analgesia for numerous surgical procedures. The choice of approach depends on the surgical site, with lumbar plexus components (femoral, obturator, LFCN) covering the anterior thigh and sacral plexus components (sciatic) covering the posterior thigh and most of the leg below the knee. Understanding the complementary nature of these blocks is essential for providing complete lower extremity coverage.

### 3.4.2. TECHNIQUES: GUIDANCE MODALITIES

**1. Ultrasound (US)-Guided** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* **Primary modality** for most peripheral nerve and fascial plane blocks
* *Physics*: Uses high-frequency sound waves (typically 1-20 MHz) emitted/received by transducer. Images generated based on reflection/transmission at tissue interfaces (acoustic impedance mismatch)
* *Terminology*:
  * **Hypoechoic**: Low reflection, appears dark (e.g., muscle, fluid, nerve fascicles)
  * **Hyperechoic**: High reflection, appears bright/white (e.g., bone, fascia, tendons, nerve epineurium, needle)
  * **Anechoic**: No reflection, appears black (e.g., vessel lumen, simple cyst)
  * **Anisotropy**: Appearance of tissues (esp. nerves/tendons) changes dramatically with angle of sound beam. Keep probe perpendicular for best image
* *Probe Selection*:
  * **High Frequency (e.g., 6-15 MHz) Linear Probe**: High resolution, shallow penetration. Best for superficial nerves (brachial plexus, ankle, forearm nerves, pediatric blocks)
  * **Low Frequency (e.g., 2-5 MHz) Curved Probe**: Lower resolution, deeper penetration. Best for deep nerves (sciatic, lumbar plexus, deep abdominal wall blocks)
* *Advantages*: Real-time visualization of anatomy, needle, LA spread; avoidance of structures; potential for lower LA volume

**2. Nerve Stimulation (NS)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Mechanism*: Uses insulated needle with exposed tip connected to nerve stimulator. Delivers electrical current to elicit motor response when near motor nerve fibers
* *Settings*: Constant current output (mA), pulse width (0.05-0.1 ms), frequency (1-2 Hz)
* *Interpretation*:
  * Motor response at < 0.5 mA (typically **0.3-0.5 mA**) suggests close proximity to nerve
  * Motor response at < 0.2 mA suggests potential **intraneural** needle tip location - **withdraw slightly before injecting**
  * Absence of response does not guarantee nerve avoidance (sensory nerves, technical issues)
* *Role*: Often used as **adjunct to US** for confirmation, especially when US view is suboptimal or near critical structures. Primary guidance when US unavailable/unsuitable. Less reliable than US alone for many blocks

**3. Field Block / Infiltration** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

* *Mechanism*: Injection of LA subcutaneously or into specific tissue planes to block terminal cutaneous nerve branches without targeting specific nerve trunks
* *Examples*: Superficial cervical plexus block, intercostobrachial nerve block, ankle block (superficial nerves), surgical site infiltration
* *Technique*: Often landmark-based, involves injecting larger volumes over wider area

### 3.4.3. PARAVERTEBRAL BLOCK (PVB)

* *Target*: Thoracic spinal nerves (ventral and dorsal rami) and sympathetic chain within the **thoracic paravertebral space (TPVS)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Anatomical Coverage*: Ipsilateral, segmental somatic (chest/abdominal wall) and sympathetic blockade over multiple dermatomes depending on volume/spread [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Indications* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Gold standard** regional technique for **thoracotomy** and **major breast surgery** analgesia
  * Rib fracture analgesia
  * Upper abdominal surgery (e.g., cholecystectomy - high thoracic PVB)
* *Borders of TPVS* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Posterior*: Superior costotransverse ligament (SCTL), transverse process, rib neck
  * *Anterior/Lateral*: Parietal pleura
  * *Medial*: Vertebral body, intervertebral disc, intervertebral foramen (communicates with epidural space)
  * *Superior/Inferior*: Heads/necks of ribs
* *Technique (US-Guided)* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Patient Position*: Sitting, Prone, or Lateral
  * *Probe*: Linear probe (can use curved if deep). Parasagittal or Transverse orientation ~2.5 cm lateral to midline
  * *Sonoanatomy*: Identify transverse processes (hyperechoic with shadow). Pleura is hyperechoic line deep to internal intercostal muscle, moves with respiration. TPVS is potential space between SCTL (posterior) and pleura (anterior)
  * *Needling*: IP approach (caudad-to-cephalad or cephalad-to-caudad in parasagittal; lateral-to-medial in transverse). Advance needle through SCTL (may feel pop). Tip should be in TPVS, anterior to SCTL, posterior to pleura
  * *Injection*: Inject LA (e.g., 15-25 mL of 0.25-0.5% **BUPIVACAINE**/**ROPIVACAINE**) visualizing anterior displacement of pleura. Catheter placement common
* *Landmark Technique*: Loss of resistance or "pop" through SCTL. Higher failure/complication rate than US [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_40.png_af707c.png_c56814.png_7ad332.png)

* *Side Effects/Complications* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * ⚠️ **Pneumothorax**: Risk due to pleural proximity. US reduces risk
  * **Hypotension/Bradycardia**: Due to sympathetic blockade (especially multi-level or bilateral blocks - avoid bilateral)
  * **Intravascular Injection**: Intercostal vessels
  * **Epidural/Intrathecal Spread**: Via intervertebral foramen
  * **Nerve Injury**
  * **Hematoma**
* *Contraindications*: Anticoagulation (relative - follow ASRA neuraxial guidelines), infection at site, tumor in PVS [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_41.png_b04f96.png_f9869c.png_99d212.png)

### 3.4.4. INTERCOSTAL NERVE BLOCK

* *Target*: Intercostal nerve within the costal groove on the inferior aspect of the rib [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Anatomical Coverage*: Limited to the dermatome of the specific intercostal nerve blocked. Requires multiple levels for wider coverage [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Indications*: Rib fracture analgesia (limited levels), analgesia for chest tube insertion/removal, minor chest wall procedures. Less common now due to safer alternatives (PVB, ESP) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Technique (Landmark)* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Palpate rib near angle (posteriorly). Insert needle just inferior to rib border, walk off caudad edge. Advance 1-2 mm. Inject 3-5 mL LA per level
* *Technique (US-Guided)* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Linear probe, identify rib. Nerve/vessels (VAN) located in plane between internal and innermost intercostal muscles just deep to inferior rib cortex. Inject LA into this plane
* *Complications* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * ⚠️ **High risk of LAST**: Intercostal space is highly vascular → rapid LA absorption. Use lowest effective dose, consider adding **EPINEPHRINE**
  * ⚠️ **Pneumothorax**: Risk of pleural puncture
  * **Nerve Injury**

### 3.4.5. ERECTOR SPINAE PLANE (ESP) BLOCK

* *Target*: Fascial plane **deep to the Erector Spinae Muscles (ESM)** and **superficial to the tips of the transverse processes** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Mechanism*: Controversial. LA injected into this plane spreads cranially/caudally and potentially anteriorly through costotransverse foramina/connective tissues to reach dorsal and ventral rami of spinal nerves and possibly the paravertebral space. Analgesia likely from blocking dorsal rami (posterior trunk/back) and possibly ventral rami (lateral/anterior chest/abdominal wall) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Anatomical Coverage*: Multi-dermatomal somatic analgesia of posterior, lateral, and sometimes anterior chest/abdominal wall depending on level and volume. May provide some visceral analgesia (mechanism unclear) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Indications*: Rib fractures, thoracotomy analgesia (alternative to PVB), breast surgery, abdominal surgery, spine surgery analgesia, chronic pain. Wide range of applications being explored [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Technique (US-Guided)* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Patient Position*: Sitting, Prone, or Lateral
  * *Probe*: Linear or curved probe depending on depth. Parasagittal orientation ~2-3 cm lateral to midline
  * *Sonoanatomy*: Identify spinous process, move probe laterally to visualize transverse processes (flat hyperechoic surface with shadow). Identify ESM complex superficial to TPs (Trapezius, Rhomboids, Erector Spinae group)
  * *Needling*: IP approach, typically caudad-to-cephalad or cephalad-to-caudad. Advance needle until tip contacts the **bony surface of the transverse process**
  * *Injection*: Inject 20-30 mL LA (e.g., 0.25-0.5% **ROPIVACAINE**) visualizing hydrodissection of the fascial plane deep to ESM, lifting the muscle off the TP. LA should spread cranially/caudally
* *Complications*: Considered relatively safe due to distance from pleura/neuraxis/major vessels. Potential for muscle soreness, hematoma. Pneumothorax very rare. LAST possible with large volumes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

### 3.4.6. SERRATUS ANTERIOR PLANE BLOCK (SAPB)

* ***Definition***: *Ultrasound (US)-guided interfascial block targeting planes around the serratus anterior muscle* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Anatomical location**: Performed in the axillary and lateral chest wall regions [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Position relative to PECS blocks**: More lateral and posterior [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Key feature**: Lacks sympathetic blockade, similar to other interfascial plane blocks [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Anatomical targets & nerves blocked**: Two primary target planes [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Superficial SAPB*: Plane between serratus anterior muscle (deep) and latissimus dorsi muscle (superficial)
	* *Nerves targeted*: Intercostobrachial nerve, lateral cutaneous branches of intercostal nerves (T3-T9), potentially long thoracic and thoracodorsal nerves
  * *Deep SAPB*: Plane between serratus anterior muscle (superficial) and the ribs/external intercostal muscles (deep)
	* *Nerves targeted*: Primarily lateral cutaneous branches of intercostal nerves
* 💡 Clinical Pearl: Similar analgesic efficacy reported for both superficial and deep SAPB injections [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Ultrasound-guided technique** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Patient position**: Typically lateral decubitus or supine with arm abducted
  * **Probe**: High-frequency linear probe (e.g., 13-6 MHz)
  * **Scanning**:
	1. Start scanning near the midclavicular line
	2. Slide probe caudally and laterally towards the midaxillary line
	3. Identify the 4th and 5th ribs in the midaxillary line
	4. **Visualize key structures**:
	  * Latissimus dorsi muscle (most superficial in axilla)
	  * Serratus anterior muscle (deep to latissimus dorsi, overlying ribs)
	  * Ribs (hyperechoic curves with posterior shadowing)
	  * Pleura (hyperechoic line moving with respiration, deep to ribs/intercostal muscles)
	  * Thoracodorsal artery (may be visible superficial to serratus anterior)
  * **Needling**:
	* **Approach**: In-plane, typically directed cephalad-to-caudal or posterior-to-anterior
	* **Target - Superficial SAPB**: Inject Local Anesthetic (LA) into the fascial plane *between* latissimus dorsi and serratus anterior
	* **Target - Deep SAPB**: Inject LA into the fascial plane *between* serratus anterior and the ribs/external intercostal muscles
	* 💡 Clinical Pearl: If performing both superficial and deep injections, inject into the *deep plane first*. Air/LA in the superficial plane can obscure the view of the deep plane [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* **Aspiration**: Always aspirate before injecting to rule out intravascular placement
	* **Rib contact**: The rib serves as a bony backstop for the deep injection, minimizing pleural puncture risk
  * **Local Anesthetic**:
	* **Drug choice**: **ROPIVACAINE** 0.2% - 0.5% commonly used (example: 0.375%) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* **Volume**: 20-40 mL typical for adults [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	  * 🔑 Larger volumes (e.g., 40 mL) may provide better spread compared to smaller volumes (e.g., 20 mL) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Landmark technique** (Less Common) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Procedure**:
	1. Palpate the 5th rib in the midaxillary line
	2. Place index and middle fingers over the rib
	3. Insert needle perpendicular to the skin between the fingers until rib contact
	4. Aspirate, then inject LA
  * ⚠️ **Caution**: Ultrasound guidance is strongly preferred for accuracy and safety

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_44.png_97f318.png_ebca73.png_435c5a.png)

* **Clinical considerations** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Efficacy**: Reduces postoperative pain and opioid consumption in the first 24 hours after thoracic surgery compared to systemic analgesia alone
  * **Safety**: Minimal theoretical risk of pleural and direct neural injury when performed correctly under US guidance (rib acts as backstop)
  * **Catheters**:
	* Can be placed for continuous analgesia
	* 🏥 Clinical Practice: Preoperative catheter placement can be challenging due to potential dislodgement during surgery. Consider postoperative placement

### 3.4.7. INTERPECTORAL (PECS 1) AND PECTOSERRATUS (PECS 2) PLANE BLOCKS

* ***Description***: *US-guided interfascial blocks of the anterior chest wall, often performed sequentially via a single needle insertion point* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Targets** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *PECS 1 (Interpectoral)*: Fascial plane between pectoralis major and pectoralis minor muscles. Targets medial and lateral pectoral nerves
  * *PECS 2 (Pectoserratus)*: Fascial plane between pectoralis minor muscle (anterior) and serratus anterior muscle (posterior) at the level of the 3rd/4th rib. Targets lateral cutaneous branches of intercostal nerves (T2-T6), nerve to serratus anterior (long thoracic), and potentially intercostobrachial nerve
* **Advantages**: Relative ease of performance, favorable safety profile [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Safety**: Considered safe due to superficial targets and rib/pleural barrier deep to the serratus anterior. Hematoma is possible but often manageable with compression [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Indications** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Primary**: Analgesia for breast surgery (mastectomy, lumpectomy, expanders, subpectoral implants)
  * **Other uses**:
	* Pacemaker/Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator (ICD) placement
	* Thoracic port placement/removal
	* Anterior thoracotomy/sternotomy adjunct
	* Rib fracture analgesia (anterior)
	* Shoulder surgery (clavicle, proximal humerus - potentially via pectoral nerve block)
	* Postherpetic neuralgia
* **Ultrasound-guided technique** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Patient position**: Supine, arm slightly abducted (approx. 45 degrees)
  * **Probe**: High-frequency linear probe (e.g., 13-6 MHz)
  * **Scanning**:
	1. Place probe inferolateral to the clavicle in the deltopectoral groove (similar view to infraclavicular block start)
	2. Identify axillary artery/vein deep to pectoral muscles
	3. Slide probe laterally and inferiorly towards the axilla, aiming for the level of the 3rd or 4th rib
	4. **Visualize key structures**:
	  * Pectoralis major muscle (superficial)
	  * Pectoralis minor muscle (deep to major)
	  * Serratus anterior muscle (deep to pectoralis minor, overlying ribs)
	  * Ribs and pleura (deepest structures)
	  * 💡 Clinical Pearl: Identify the thoracoacromial artery (pectoral branch) running in the plane between pectoralis major and minor – this vessel marks the PECS 1 target plane [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * **Needling**:
	* **Approach**: In-plane, typically medial-to-lateral or cephalad-to-caudad
	* **Sequence (Crucial)** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
	  1. Advance needle to the **PECS 2 plane** (between pectoralis minor and serratus anterior). Aspirate and inject LA (e.g., 10 mL)
	  2. *Withdraw* the needle tip into the **PECS 1 plane** (between pectoralis major and minor). Aspirate and inject LA (e.g., 10 mL)
	* **Goal**: Visualize LA spread within the correct fascial planes, avoiding intramuscular injection
  * **Local Anesthetic** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
	* **Drug choice**: **ROPIVACAINE** 0.2-0.25% or **BUPIVACAINE** 0.25% commonly used
	* **Volume**: Total 15-30 mL for adults, typically split between the two planes (e.g., 10 mL PECS 2 + 10 mL PECS 1)
	* 🔑 Optimal dose/concentration/volume is still under investigation

![image.png](PNB_PNB_PNB_image_45.png_da4943.png_6ae7cd.png_686aac.png)

* **Clinical considerations** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Efficacy**: Reduces postoperative pain intensity and opioid consumption after breast surgery compared to systemic analgesia
  * **Potential Complications**:
	* Hematoma (especially if thoracoacromial artery punctured)
	* Infection
	* Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST)
	* Pneumothorax (rare if US guidance used correctly)
	* Nerve injury (long thoracic nerve → winged scapula - rare)
  * ⚠️ Large-scale randomized controlled trials comparing PECS blocks to neuraxial techniques or other blocks (e.g., paravertebral) are limited [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

### 3.4.8. RECTUS SHEATH BLOCK

* ***Description***: *Block targeting the terminal branches of the thoracoabdominal intercostal nerves (T7-T11) as they run within the rectus sheath* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Goal**: Provide somatic analgesia for midline abdominal incisions [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Coverage**: Primarily affects the periumbilical area [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Landmark technique** (Less Common) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Patient position**: Supine
  * **Landmarks**: Umbilicus, lateral border of rectus abdominis muscle (linea semilunaris)
  * **Procedure**:
	1. Identify intersection of linea semilunaris and a horizontal line at the umbilicus level
	2. Insert a short-beveled needle angled ~60° towards the umbilicus
	3. Advance until a "pop" or loss of resistance is felt (penetration of anterior rectus sheath)
	4. Aspirate and inject LA
	5. Repeat on the contralateral side
  * ⚠️ **Caution**: Blind technique with higher risk of vessel puncture or intraperitoneal injection compared to US-guided approach
* **Ultrasound-guided technique** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Patient position**: Supine
  * **Probe**: High-frequency linear probe (e.g., 6-15 MHz)
  * **Scanning**:
	1. Place probe transversely on the anterior abdominal wall, lateral to the umbilicus (or intended incision)
	2. **Visualize key structures**:
	  * Rectus abdominis muscle belly
	  * Anterior rectus sheath (hyperechoic line superficial to muscle)
	  * Posterior rectus sheath (hyperechoic line deep to muscle - *may be absent below arcuate line*)
	  * Peritoneum (hyperechoic line deep to posterior sheath/transversalis fascia)
	  * Linea alba (midline fibrous band - if scanning medially)
	  * Lateral muscles (external oblique, internal oblique, transversus abdominis - if scanning laterally)
	  * ⚠️ **Crucial**: Identify inferior epigastric artery/vein using Color Doppler. These vessels run within the rectus muscle or just deep to it [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * **Needling**:
	* **Needle**: 22-gauge, 50-100 mm (depending on patient habitus)
	* **Approach**: In-plane, typically lateral-to-medial
	* **Target**: Potential space between the posterior aspect of the rectus abdominis muscle and the posterior rectus sheath
	* **Path**: Insert needle laterally, traversing subcutaneous tissue (and potentially oblique muscles if starting far laterally) aiming towards the target space
	* 💡 Clinical Pearl: A lateral approach helps avoid puncturing the rectus muscle belly and the inferior epigastric vessels [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	* **Confirmation**: Inject small volume (1-2 mL) of saline or D5W ("hydrodissection") to confirm correct needle tip placement in the plane. Visualize fluid spread separating muscle from posterior sheath
  * **Local Anesthetic** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
	* **Drug choice**: **ROPIVACAINE** 0.2-0.5% or **BUPIVACAINE** 0.25-0.5%
	* **Volume**: 10-20 mL per side for adults (e.g., 10 mL of 0.5% **ROPIVACAINE**). Pediatric dose example: 0.2 mL/kg per side
	* **Goal**: Visualize LA spread along the posterior aspect of the rectus muscle

### 3.4.9. TRANSVERSUS ABDOMINIS PLANE (TAP) BLOCK

* *Target*: Fascial plane between the **Internal Oblique (IO)** muscle and the **Transversus Abdominis (TA)** muscle. Contains sensory nerves supplying the anterolateral abdominal wall (ilioinguinal, iliohypogastric, lower intercostal nerves T7-T12 depending on injection location) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Anatomical Coverage*: Ipsilateral somatic anesthesia of anterior abdominal wall below the umbilicus (subcostal/lateral TAP) or around umbilicus (posterior TAP). Does NOT provide visceral analgesia [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Indications*: Postoperative analgesia for lower abdominal surgeries (C-section, hysterectomy, hernia repair, appendectomy, prostatectomy) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* *Technique (US-Guided)* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Patient Position*: Supine
  * *Probe*: High-frequency linear probe
  * *Scanning Locations*:
	* **Lateral TAP**: Probe placed transversely in mid-axillary line between iliac crest and costal margin (Triangle of Petit)
	* **Subcostal TAP**: Probe placed obliquely along costal margin
	* **Posterior TAP**: Probe placed more posteriorly towards quadratus lumborum
  * *Sonoanatomy*: Identify three muscle layers superficial to deep: External Oblique (EO), Internal Oblique (IO), Transversus Abdominis (TA). Peritoneum/bowel deep to TA. Target plane is between IO and TA
  * *Needling*: IP approach, typically posterior-to-anterior or anterior-to-posterior. Advance needle until tip lies in the fascial plane between IO and TA
  * *Injection*: Inject 15-20 mL LA (e.g., 0.25% **BUPIVACAINE**/**ROPIVACAINE**) visualizing hydrodissection of the plane
* *Complications*: Rare. Bowel/peritoneal puncture, liver/spleen injury (subcostal), intravascular injection, transient femoral nerve palsy (if LA tracks inferiorly) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

### 3.4.10. QUADRATUS LUMBORUM (QL) BLOCK

* ***Definition***: *An abdominal wall interfascial plane block targeting the thoracolumbar fascia (TLF) surrounding the Quadratus Lumborum (QL) muscle* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Anatomical target**: Local Anesthetic (LA) injected into fascial planes adjacent to the QL muscle [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Key advantages over Transversus Abdominis Plane (TAP) block**: Potential for visceral analgesia, possibly mediated through blockade of sympathetic fibers within the TLF (Thoracolumbar Fascia) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * *TLF contains*: Mechanoreceptors, nociceptors, and sympathetic fibers
* **Dermatomal spread**: Typically T8 to L1, consistently involving iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal nerves [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Block approaches** (Named by LA deposition relative to QL muscle) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * *Lateral QL (QL1)*: Injection into the plane between QL muscle (posterior) and transversus abdominis muscle/aponeurosis (anterior)
  * *Posterior QL (QL2)*: Injection into the plane between QL muscle (anterior) and erector spinae/latissimus dorsi muscles (posterior), within the middle layer of TLF
  * *Anterior QL (QL3 or Transmuscular)*: Injection into the plane between QL muscle (posterior) and psoas major muscle (anterior), within the anterior layer of TLF
* **Ultrasound-guided technique** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Probe selection**: Curvilinear probe (e.g., C5-1 MHz) usually preferred for adults due to target depth
  * **Scanning approach**:
	1. Place probe transversely in the midaxillary line, between the costal margin and iliac crest (similar to TAP block scan)
	2. Slide probe posteriorly, maintaining transverse orientation above the iliac crest
	3. Identify the point where the transversus abdominis muscle tapers off laterally
	4. Visualize the QL muscle deep to the internal oblique and transversus abdominis aponeurosis
  * **Sonoanatomy**:
	* Identify the three muscle layers of the flank: External Oblique (EO), Internal Oblique (IO), Transversus Abdominis (TA)
	* Deep to TA aponeurosis, visualize the QL muscle
	* 💡 **"Shamrock sign"** (viewed from posterior): Useful landmark often seen near the transverse process [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
	  * *Three leaves*: Psoas major muscle (anterior), QL muscle (lateral), Erector Spinae muscle (posterior)
	  * *Stem*: Lumbar transverse process
	* 📋 MNEMONIC: **"PQRST"** - **P**soas, **Q**uadratus lumborum, e**R**ector **S**pinae, **T**ransverse process [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
  * **Needling technique by approach**:
	* *QL1 (Lateral)*:
	  * Target: Fascial plane lateral to QL, between QL and TA aponeurosis
	  * Approach: In-plane, typically posterior-to-anterior. Needle tip placed just anterior to the lateral edge of QL
	  * Tip: Tilting probe slightly caudally can improve visualization of the transversus abdominis aponeurosis
	* *QL2 (Posterior)*:
	  * Target: Fascial plane posterior to QL, between QL and erector spinae/latissimus dorsi. Often targets the Lumbar Interfascial Triangle (LIFT)
	  * Approach: In-plane, posterior-to-anterior or anterior-to-posterior
	  * Goal: Inject LA into the middle layer of TLF, aiming for spread towards the paravertebral space
	* *QL3 (Anterior/Transmuscular)*:
	  * Target: Fascial plane anterior to QL, between QL and psoas major muscle. Requires needle passage *through* the QL muscle
	  * Approach: In-plane, typically posterior-to-anterior (reduces needle path length)
  * **Local Anesthetic** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
	* **Drug choice**: **ROPIVACAINE** 0.2% - 0.375% or **BUPIVACAINE** 0.25% commonly used
	* **Volume**: 15-30 mL per side for adults (e.g., 20 mL)
	* **Injection site**: Target interfascial plane according to chosen approach (QL1, QL2, or QL3)
* **Clinical pearls** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Efficacy**: Studies show reduced pain intensity and opioid consumption post-cesarean delivery and renal surgery compared to systemic analgesia
  * **Shamrock view**: Useful anatomical reference for both QL blocks and lumbar plexus blocks
  * ⚠️ **Risks**: This is a deep block requiring meticulous US visualization. Potential complications include:
	* Peritoneal puncture
	* Bowel injury
	* Kidney injury (especially with lateral/anterior approaches)
	* Vascular puncture (e.g., lumbar arteries - risk of retroperitoneal hematoma, especially if anticoagulated)
  * 🏥 **Clinical consideration**: Quadriceps muscle weakness may occur, particularly after *anterior QL (QL3)* block, likely due to LA spread anteriorly towards the lumbar plexus/fascia iliaca [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

### 3.4.11. ILIOINGUINAL AND ILIOHYPOGASTRIC NERVE BLOCKS

* ***Description***: *Block targeting the ilioinguinal (II) and iliohypogastric (IH) nerves, typically performed near the Anterior Superior Iliac Spine (ASIS)* [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Goal**: Provide somatic analgesia to the inguinal region (e.g., for hernia repair, orchiopexy) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Nerves targeted**: Ilioinguinal nerve (L1) and Iliohypogastric nerve (T12, L1) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Blind technique** (Single-injection fascial click - Less Common) [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Landmarks**: Point 1-2 cm medial and 1-2 cm inferior to the ASIS
  * **Procedure**:
	1. Use a 25-gauge, 36-50 mm blunted hypodermic needle
	2. Insert needle perpendicular to skin
	3. Advance until one or two distinct "pops" or fascial clicks are felt (representing fascia of external oblique and internal oblique). Target is theoretically just deep to internal oblique
	4. Aspirate and inject LA: 10-15 mL for adults (0.3-0.5 mL/kg for children)
	5. Consider additional subcutaneous LA (0.5-1 mL) withdrawal for IH nerve branches
  * ⚠️ **Accuracy concern**: High failure rate. Needle often placed too deep (below transversus abdominis) >40% of the time, even with experienced users [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Ultrasound-guided technique** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Probe selection**: High-frequency linear probe; small footprint ("hockey stick") probe (e.g., 5-10 MHz) is ideal
  * **Probe placement**: Place probe transversely, just medial and slightly superior to the ASIS. Align lateral edge of probe over ASIS initially
  * **Scanning**:
	1. Identify the bony cortex of the iliac crest/ASIS (hyperechoic line with posterior shadowing)
	2. Identify the three abdominal muscle layers superficial to the iliac bone: External Oblique (EO - may be thin/aponeurotic here), Internal Oblique (IO), and Transversus Abdominis (TA)
	3. **Locate nerves**: Scan medially/superiorly from ASIS. II and IH nerves typically lie in the fascial plane *between* the IO and TA muscles
	  * *Sonoanatomy of nerves*: Appear as small, hypoechoic (dark) oval or triangular structures, sometimes with internal hyperechoic dots. May have a hyperechoic rim. Often found close together
  * **Needling**:
	* **Approach**: In-plane approach is preferred, typically medial-to-lateral
	* **Target**: Fascial plane between IO and TA, placing needle tip adjacent to the nerve(s)
	* **Alternative**: Out-of-plane approach possible but generally less preferred for precise nerve visualization
  * **Local Anesthetic** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
	* **Injection strategy**: Can use single injection between nerves or separate injections adjacent to each visualized nerve
	* **Drug choice**: **ROPIVACAINE** 0.2-0.5% or **BUPIVACAINE** 0.25-0.5%
	* **Volume**: Typically lower volumes needed with US guidance compared to blind technique (e.g., 5-10 mL total for adults)
	  * *Pediatric dosing example*: 0.075 mL/kg effective for single US-guided injection
	* **Confirmation**: Visualize LA spread (hypoechoic fluid) surrounding the target nerve(s) within the correct fascial plane
* **Clinical pearls** [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * **Anatomical variation**: II and IH nerves may run together as a common trunk near the ASIS before splitting. US helps identify this variation
  * **Assessment difficulty**: High variability in cutaneous innervation patterns makes clinical assessment of block success challenging based on skin sensation alone
  * **Alternative targeting**: If nerves are hard to separate, inject LA lateral to the most lateral structure (likely II) or medial to the most medial structure (likely IH) within the IO/TA plane
  * **Complications**: Generally related to volume or inaccurate placement
	* Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST)
	* Transient femoral nerve palsy (if LA spreads posteriorly/inferiorly to fascia iliaca)
	* Bowel puncture (rare with US)
  * 🏥 **Value of US guidance**: Significantly improves accuracy of needle placement, allows for lower LA volumes, and reduces risk of complications compared to blind technique

### 3.4.12. OTHER INTERFASCIAL PLANE BLOCKS (BRIEF OVERVIEW)

* **Retrolaminar Block (RLB)**: Injection posterior to the vertebral lamina, deep to erector spinae muscles. Medial to ESP target. Variable spread, less studied than ESP/PVB [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]
* **Intertransverse Process (ITP) Block**: Injection into the "intertransverse tissue complex" between transverse processes, posterior to SCTL. Aims for more direct paravertebral spread than ESP. Multiple variations described. Efficacy compared to ESP/PVB needs more research [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]

**Bottom Line:** Truncal and chest wall blocks provide effective analgesia for thoracic and abdominal procedures by targeting either specific nerves (e.g., intercostal, paravertebral) or fascial planes where sensory nerves travel (e.g., ESP, PECS, TAP, QL). Ultrasound guidance has greatly improved the precision and safety of these blocks. The choice of block depends on the surgical site, desired coverage, and safety considerations including the patient's coagulation status and proximity to critical structures.

### 4.1.2. ANATOMY AND ORGANIZATION

* **The roots of the brachial plexus**  [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Originate from the ventral rami of the spinal nerves C5 to T1
  * Emerge from between the transverse processes of the cervical vertebrae just posterior to the vertebral artery
  * Pass between the anterior and middle scalene muscles in the interscalene groove
* **Location of trunks, divisions and cords**  [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * The trunks lie in the posterior triangle of the neck
  * The divisions lie posterior to the clavicle
  * The cords lie within the axilla

* **Radial nerve**  [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:

### 4.1.4. BLOCK TECHNIQUES AND CONSIDERATIONS

* **Which nerve can be missed by the supraclavicular block?**  [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]] 
  * Suprascapular nerve (C5/6)
	* Supplies sensory innervation to the acromioclavicular joint and 70% of the joint capsule
* **Which nerve does the axillary block commonly miss?**  [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]] 
  * Musculocutaneous nerve
* **Landmark points for a supraclavicular block**  [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]] :
  * With patient semi-supine, head turned away from block site, shoulder down and forearm supinated
	* Palpate the interscalene groove
	  * Follow this distally until the subclavian pulse is felt
	  * This should be near the midpoint of the clavicle
		* 1cm posterior to this is the injection site
		  * There is a pop as the needle passes through the fascia
		  * Caution as the pleura is medial to this
* **Which nerves can be blocked by accident?**  [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]] 
  * Sympathetic chain
	* Horner's syndrome
  * Recurrent laryngeal nerve
  * Phrenic nerve
	* Avoid bilateral supraclavicular nerve blocks, especially in patients with any respiratory disease
* **Infraclavicular block technique**  [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]] :
  * Patient and permission
	* Confirm identity, site, indications and any contraindications
	* Informed consent with explanation of risks, benefits and alternatives
  * Preparation
	* AAGBI monitoring and appropriately skilled assistant with relevant LocSSIP or checklist
	* Anaesthetic machine checked
	* Appropriate IV access and emergency drugs drawn up
	* Ensure all equipment sterile and in date
	* Strict asepsis used, with hat, gloves, gown, mask and drape
  * Positioning
	* Supine with arm abducted and flexed
  * Procedure
	* Linear ultrasound probe
	* Medial to coracoid, perpendicular and inferior to clavicle
	* View of axillary artery and vein
	* Nerve response is hand twitch, if using nerve stimulator
	* 2 injections, deep and lateral to artery
	* Aspirate prior to injection to rule out intravascular injection
  * Paperwork
	* Document in the notes
	* Prescribe drugs given on prescription chart
	* Patient information leaflet

**Bottom Line:** Understanding the complex anatomy of the brachial plexus is crucial for successful upper extremity blocks. The organization from roots to terminal nerves follows a consistent pattern that allows targeting at various levels, each with specific advantages and risks. Different block approaches (interscalene, supraclavicular, infraclavicular, axillary) must be selected based on the surgical site and patient factors.

### 4.2.2. INDICATIONS AND COMPLICATIONS

* **Indications for an intercostal nerve block**  [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Rib fractures
  * Chronic Pain
* **Complications of an intercostal nerve block**  [[MMed_Anaesthesiology_Part_B_Syllabus#C3 Local and Regional Anaesthesia|ⓘ]]:
  * Pneumothorax
  * High systemic absorption - higher risk of local anaesthetic toxicity
  * Respiratory compromise if phrenic nerve palsy

### 4.2.3. ANATOMY

* **Blood supply to the intercostal space**  [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Subclavian artery supplies internal thoracic arteries
	* These supply anterior intercostals
  * Thoracic aorta T3 - T11 and superior intercostal artery supply posterior intercostals
* **Nerve supply to the intercostal space**  [[mmed_anaes_part_a_syllabus#4.3 Neuroanatomy (Central Nervous System and Peripheral Nervous System)|ⓘ]]:
  * Ventral rami of T1 to T11
  * Rami communicantes
  * Collaterals
  * Lateral cutaneous nerves

**Bottom Line:** Intercostal nerve blocks provide effective analgesia for thoracic procedures and rib fractures but carry a higher risk of local anesthetic toxicity due to rapid vascular uptake. Pneumothorax is a significant potential complication that must be considered. Ultrasound guidance has improved safety by allowing direct visualization of the pleura and intercostal neurovascular bundle.
