| **Pre-op**               | Check machine <br>Check for overnight events <br><br>Draw induction tray:<br>- Propofol 20ml <br>- Rocuronium 5ml (green syringe) or 10ml (normal syringe)<br>- Cefazolin 2g <br>- TXA 1g (if not renally impaired) <br>- Big fentanyl (most people prefer in 20ml) <br>- +/- Midazolam (for premedication while inserting IA or CVC)<br><br>Draw emergency drug tray: <br>- Adrenaline in 10mcg/ml and 100mcg/ml <br>- Phenylephrine 100mcg/ml in 100ml NS (1x 20ml syringe and keep, pass rest to perfusionist) <br>- Atropine 1 vial <br>- Ephedrine 5mg/ml<br>- Calcium chloride 1 bottle in 10ml <br><br>Additional drugs<br>- Heparin 300u/kg if heparin naive (put aside and only give when surgeons ask) <br>- If previous heparin for IABP, maybe 5000 more <br>- If no bypass maybe 150-200u/kg <br><br>Chop controlled drug book<br>Program infusion pumps. | **Pre-induction**        | Check if have radial harvest (usually younger patients) <br><br>Set IV plug 14G to 18G on right hand, to be used as a fluid line. Connect it to the hotline. Patient - Plug - Short white extension - Hotline - 75cm extension - 3 way tap - Infusor line - Hartmann's bag<br><br>Bring patient in and give midazolam 1mg \+/- fentanyl 25mcg <br><br>Set IA and CVP after giving LA <br>- If there is left radial artery grafting then set IA on right hand<br>- Take baseline ABG and ACT. Subsequent blood gas can be VBG (or ABG as per consultant's preference)<br><br>CVP<br>- Brown port: CVP transducer line with one blue 3 way. This line will be used to draw ACT/VBG. And administer all meds except protamine and heparin. Protamine is given peripherally and heparin administered <br>- Light blue: Adrenaline + Noradrenaline<br>- White + 3 way: Insulin + GTN + Propofol<br>- Dark blue: free port, to administer heparin, but may be used to run propofol<br><br>Put on BIS and cerebral oximetry<br>An additional pulse ox is required on the L arm if L IA is being harvested                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Induction**            | Pre oxygenate<br><br>Cardiostable induction - ensure noradrenaline or adrenaline are readily available (turned on 0.01mcg/kg/hr) unless patient is very hypertensive. Rocuronium for paralysis.<br><br>High dose fentanyl given to blunt sympathetic response - usually 100-200mcg.<br><br>Induce and secure airway, usually HiLo ETT. Standard ventilator settings.<br>Nasopharyngeal temp probe <br>KIV OGT suck and pull  <br><br>Manage the hemodynamics while TEE is being inserted<br><br>Give cefazolin 2g, TXA 1g<br><br>OTT will put on a drape holder frame just in front of the patient's face.<br>Catch drapes. First layer is a short rectangular drape, just use broad tape to make sure it does not fold back over to surgical area (sterile). Some use a pair of needle holders as we weight. A second larger layer will be draped over, and the frame will extend into a T shape, with the drape covering the entire breadth (like a curtain). This second layer has a pair of tape which I would fold upwards under a lower set of rungs (on the drape holder) to try and paste it as high up as you can. <br><br>Charge all infusion pumps <br><br>Connect defib when passed the cable<br><br>Catch up with charting<br><br>This period after induction is very sensitive to hemodynamic changes (as patient's heart is poor). You need to be extra vigilant. Phenyl ready (as norad in the central line may not have reached the patient). Fluids (crystalloids or colloids) may be administered as well via fluid line.<br><br>Take note of BIS as well.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

| **On CPB**               | When going on bypass and once perfusionist on full flow<br>- Off all alarms and turn off ventilator and sevo<br>- They will target MAP 40-50mmhg during cold period of bypass (cross clamp on) and 50-60mmhg during warm period of bypass (cross clamp off)<br>- ABGs will flow into EPIC (done every hour). <br><br>Perfusionist will replace<br>- Electrolytes<br>- Heparin for ACT<br>- Cardioplegia solution. If delivered retrograde, you will need to perform the below.<br><br>*Retrograde cardioplegia is delivered via a catheter the surgeon places through the right atrium into the *coronary sinus (which normally drains venous blood from the myocardium). The cardioplegia flows backwards through the coronary venous system to protect the myocardium.<br>The problem: if you deliver at too high a pressure, you risk coronary sinus rupture (a serious complication). Too low, and you get inadequate myocardial protection.<br>Target coronary sinus pressure: typically 30–50 mmHg**- The retrograde cardioplegia catheter has a *pressure monitoring lumen<br>*The surgeon hands you the end of this line from the surgical field. You connect it using a male-to-male connector (because both ends are female Luer locks). This connects to a 3-way tap attached to a pressure transducer line. You display this on your monitor — often using a spare channel or repurposing the CVP trace temporarily*<br><br>Anaesthetist will<br>- BSL Insulin bolus/infusion if necessary when BSL \~10 <br>- Give additional cefazolin 2g once and every Q4H after<br>- Take pacing machine out, check battery status and hang on pole<br>- Draw drugs for next case<br><br>When starting to rewarm (usually at the final distal anastomosis)<br>- Standby to order and call for blood products (for when coming off bypass)<br>- Standby TXA 1g, Morphine 10mg to be given when off pump<br>- Prep and load Propofol 50ml w infusor line and drug label (for transfer to ICU)<br>- Standby defibrillator as well - "On" > "Manual mode" > "Sync on or off" > 10-20J charge > **SURGEONS WILL DISCHARGE IT**<br><br>*Rewarming is done early as it takes time, usually when surgeon starts at the final distal anastomosis. Once done, the aorta cross clamp is removed, the heart will then be reperfused with warm blood. The cross clamp prevents the return of the oxygenated arterial inflow from the bypass to fill the left heart via retrograde flow from the aorta. Once released, the heart may fibrillate (VF) transiently which will require immediate defibrillation. The surgeon will continue with the final proximal anastomosis* |

| **Off CPB**              | Draw protamine (keep in bottom drawer if drawn, but some bosses very particular about predrawing so always check)<br>- Ranges from 0.5 to 1mg per 100u of heparin<br><br>When surgeon ready, they will announce to give protamine<br>- to be given through peripheral line very slowly in view of risk of pulmonary hypertension and hypotension) <br>- Announce "1/4" then stop > "1/2" then stop > "3/4" then stop > "All given"; just in case surgeons need to crash back on bypass.<br><br>5 min after protamine given, draw ACT and TEG/Platelet mapping. Correct ACT to baseline ACT value that you drew at the start<br><br>Only AFTER protamine given, then you will give (1) TXA 1g, (2) Morphine.<br>
